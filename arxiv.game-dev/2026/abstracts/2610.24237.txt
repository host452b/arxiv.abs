Title: Usability, User Experience, and Acceptability of the Trail Making Test in Virtual Reality in Younger and Older Adults
Authors: Panagiotis Kourtesis, Dante O'Connor, Sarah E. MacPherson
ID: 2610.24237
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24237

Abstract:
Immersive virtual reality (VR) adaptations of neuropsychological tests change
how a test operates. Before their scores are interpreted, the individuals they
are intended to assess should find them usable, acceptable and tolerable, and
for older adults this evidence is usually missing. This study evaluated the
experience of the Trail Making Test in Virtual Reality (TMT-VR), a head-gaze
adaptation of the Trail Making Test that requires no hand-held controller, in 47
younger (20-38 years) and 44 older adults (64-81 years). After the task,
participants rated usability, pragmatic and hedonic user experience,
acceptability, and cybersickness. The study had three aims: to compare the two
age groups on these ratings; to examine whether the ratings were associated with
age group, gaming and technology experience, and whether cybersickness was
associated with motion sickness susceptibility; and to examine whether the
ratings were related to TMT-VR completion and error-adjusted times. Both groups
rated the TMT-VR as highly usable, acceptable and pleasant to use. Older adults
gave slightly lower usability and pragmatic ratings, but neither the age-group
differences nor the associations with age group or previous experience were
statistically significant after correction. Cybersickness was low, with 38 of
the 91 participants reporting no symptoms; it did not differ between the age
groups and was not associated with motion sickness susceptibility. No
correlation between the ratings and TMT-VR performance survived correction. The
TMT-VR with head-gaze selection is therefore usable, acceptable and well
tolerated by older as well as younger adults. Because the ratings were
favourable across the whole range of TMT-VR scores, slower or less accurate
performance is unlikely to reflect difficulty with the interface.
