Title: EMBERS: Empirically Motivated Robustness Testing of Deep Research Systems via Defect-Guided Fuzzing
Authors: Shuochuan Li, Zan Wang, Dong Wang, Yuhang Jiang, Huajin Cui, Chen Zhang, Gang Wang, Junjie Chen
ID: 2611.03723
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03723

Abstract:
Deep Research (DR) systems extend large language models into autonomous agents
that decompose complex information needs, iteratively search the web, and
synthesize evidence to answer challenging questions. However, their multi-stage
execution may introduce robustness risks, as variations in user questions or
intermediate search inputs may propagate across research stages and lead to
incorrect answers. Existing evaluations focus on task-solving capabilities or
robustness against external evidence disturbances, leaving intrinsic weaknesses
in DR execution insufficiently explored. Thus, we conduct an empirical study of
1,932 execution trajectories from three representative DR systems and identify
nine recurring defect categories across subquestion formulation, online search,
and final reasoning. Guided by these findings, we propose EMBERS, an empirically
motivated, defect-guided fuzzing framework for robustness testing of DR systems.
Starting from correctly answered seed questions, EMBERS generates stage-targeted
test inputs using defect-guided mutation, web-search-assisted fidelity
assurance, and diversity-aware seed maintenance. Experiments across 16 framework
--model--dataset settings show that EMBERS achieves 3.21 times the failure-
discovery throughput of the strongest baseline under identical testing budgets.
Human and LLM-assisted evaluations demonstrate that most generated inputs
preserve the original information need and necessary contextual constraints.
Moreover, model fine-tuning experiments show consistent-answer performance
improvement, highlighting their value for robustness testing and model
improvement.
