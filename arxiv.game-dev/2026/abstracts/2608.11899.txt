Title: Beyond Prompted Agreement: Instruction Effects on Key and Beat Grouping in Text-to-Music Generation
Authors: Yining Wang
ID: 2608.11899
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11899

Abstract:
In music-generation models, an explicit instruction to realize a musical
attribute can paradoxically make it less common. How, then, should we assess
whether a model follows a musical instruction? We use matched inputs that omit
an attribute, specify one target, or specify another target, and compare the
resulting audio. We evaluate key and beat grouping in ACE-Step 1.5, Stable Audio
3 and LeVo2 on 2,304 primary outputs. In Stable Audio 3, a four-beat instruction
reduces detected four-beat occurrence from 96.9% to 56.3%, even though four
beats remain more common than three under their respective instructions. Further
experiments show that rephrasing can improve attainment while reducing the
difference between target instructions, and that ACE-Step shows larger changes
in target occurrence when text instructions retain its native attribute field.
Our findings suggest that evaluating musical instruction following requires both
target attainment and comparisons with generation with an omitted target
instruction and with a different specified target.
