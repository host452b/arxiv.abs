Title: Can LLMs Tell Stories Out of Order? A Corpus and Benchmark for Nonlinear Narrative Generation
Authors: Syed Rifat Raiyan, Md. Tariquzzaman, Md Farhan Ishmam, Tenghao Huang
ID: 2610.21468
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21468

Abstract:
Every story has two orders: the order in which its events happen and the order
in which they are told. A story is nonlinear when the two differ. We introduce a
benchmark of 83 published works that measures whether language models can tell
nonlinear stories. For each work, we annotate one list of events in both orders,
together with a story graph of causal dependencies, character knowledge, and the
questions the telling raises and resolves. Given events in chronological order,
all 19 models tell them, on average, more chronologically than the published
works do; asked for a specific structure, they land within the human range about
half the time. We then compare a model's stories of the same events under
different conditions, using both an automated judge and 71 human readers. Both
prefer the chronological story a model writes by default to the nonlinear one it
writes on request, which readers find harder to follow in 81% of decisive
judgments. Readers also prefer stories written from the story graph to stories
constrained to the published telling order (72% of decisive judgments), a
difference the judge does not detect. Our results suggest that following an
instruction to reorder a story is not yet the same as telling it well out of
order.
