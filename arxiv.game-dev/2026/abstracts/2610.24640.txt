Title: Say It or Grab It? Conversational Control of UI Adaptations in Mixed Reality
Authors: Sina Elahimanesh, Joao Marcelo Evangelista Belo, Isidora Jeknic, Alexander Koller, Anna Maria Feit
ID: 2610.24640
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24640

Abstract:
Mixed reality interfaces must continuously adapt to a user's changing task and
environment, yet adaptation policies are fixed in advance, either preset by the
system or configured by the user. Natural language offers a way to state
changing needs mid-task, but whether users can express interface behavior this
way, and what it gains over direct manipulation, is unknown. We first elicited
how users verbalize adaptation needs (N=19). We then built ConverseMR, which
translates a spoken request into adaptation objectives and constraints and
satisfies them continuously as the user's context changes. In a within-subjects
study (N=24), conversational and direct control proved complementary: direct
manipulation for precision, ConverseMR for reach and expressive breadth, with
their combination rated highest. Spoken commands were rarely final and users
frequently refined and replaced them upon experiencing the result,
characteristic of, what we call, user-led adaptation, where users communicate
adaption needs and the system identifies a solution.
