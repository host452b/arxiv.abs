Title: Torch-DPhys: A Torch-Native, MOOSE-Compatible Framework for Differentiable Physics Simulation and Hybrid Machine Learning Integration
Authors: Akzhol Almukhametov, Yang Liu
ID: 2610.24301
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24301

Abstract:
We present Torch-DPhys, a Torch-native framework implemented in PyTorch for
differentiable physics simulation and hybrid physics-machine learning. It
implements selected thermal-hydraulic modeling concepts and discretizations from
MOOSE's Thermal Hydraulics Module and the System Analysis Module (SAM), with a
preliminary extension to continuum mechanics. Native tensor residuals support
CPU and GPU execution, compilation of selected numerical kernels, and sparse
direct linear solves. Sensitivities are obtained by differentiating executed
numerical operations or through discrete adjoints of converged residual
equations. Verification combines analytical solutions, reference-code
comparisons, and derivative checks. Demonstrations cover plane-strain
elasticity, one- and two-loop thermal-hydraulic transients, and an axisymmetric
molten-salt fast reactor. For the reactor, comparison with SAM-Lite gives a
maximum normalized field discrepancy of 1.13 x 10^-5 over 200 adaptive time
steps, demonstrating agreement between the tested discrete implementations.
Selected transient sensitivities are assessed using tangent-linear calculations
and finite differences on fixed time schedules. Two applications illustrate the
framework's connection between simulation and optimization. A residual-adjoint
parameter-identification calculation recovers nominal pump head and heating
power with relative errors below 0.06% from noiseless synthetic MOOSE
observations. A separate three-loop plant demonstration uses solver-in-the-loop
training of a predictive controller by differentiating trajectory-level
objectives through the thermal-hydraulic simulation. These examples illustrate a
foundation for gradient-based inference, control, and coupled physics-machine
learning models in nuclear engineering.
