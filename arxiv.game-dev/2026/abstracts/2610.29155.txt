Title: 5uGUE: Agentic Hardware-in-the-Loop Fuzzing for 5G Devices and beyond
Authors: Guoqiang Hua, Sakshi Udeshi, Matheus Eduardo Garbelini, Daisuke Mashima, Sudipta Chattopadhyay
ID: 2610.29155
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29155

Abstract:
In this paper, we present 5uGUE, a large language model (LLM)-driven hardware-
in-the-loop agent, which enables the wireless fuzzing of 5G-enabled devices
(such as mobile phones). 5uGUE combines multi-step reasoning with tool-assisted
interaction and integrates into the fuzzing pipeline by leveraging specialized
tools for internet search, 5G protocol analysis, memory management, and test-
case execution. The agent iteratively generates test cases, executes them on
targets, collects runtime feedback, and adaptively refines the fuzzing strategy.
We evaluate 5uGUE on five commercial off-the-shelf (COTS) 5G devices across six
LLMs. The best performing runs for 5uGUE trigger 167 crashes in total (141 novel
and 31 unique) within 60 interaction turns (49 to 133 minutes total runtime). In
contrast, the best runs for the state-of-the-art baseline (5GHOUL) discovers
only 13 crashes in total (10 novel and 6 unique) within 150 minutes. Overall, by
integrating LLM-driven agents, 5uGUE demonstrates significantly better crash
discovery performance (12.8x total, 5.2x unique improvement). Notably, our
experiments identified 46 unique crashes, 44 of which were confirmed by the
vendors but had been previously reported. More importantly, MediaTek confirmed
two previously unknown, high-severity vulnerabilities, one of which was assigned
CVE-2026-20525. Finally, to demonstrate that 5uGUE is agnostic to wireless
protocols, we also extend it to Bluetooth Classic with only minimal code
changes. In our evaluations, 5uGUE finds an average of 6.4 novel crashes and 1.6
unique crashes. In a nutshell, 5uGUE highlights that LLM agents are capable to
operate effectively beyond software-in-the-loop settings, extending autonomous
vulnerability discovery to the proprietary, hardware-bound systems that affect
billions of end-user devices across multiple wireless protocols.
