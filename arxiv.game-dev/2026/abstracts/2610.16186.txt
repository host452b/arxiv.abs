Title: Exploring Mixed Reality for Engagement in Robotics: A Comparison of Robot Control Methods
Authors: Samantha Edvell, Dinh Tung Le, Gavin Paul
ID: 2610.16186
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16186

Abstract:
This paper presents a Mixed Reality (MR) robotics experience that provides an
accessible introduction to interacting with a physical robot. The application
combines a TurtleBot with a virtual animal-tending activity and three control
methods: Joystick, Waypoint, and Path-Drawing. A mixed-methods user study with
10 participants investigated changes in robotics interest and confidence
following the experience and how the control methods influenced engagement,
workload, and preference. Confidence increased following the experience, with
the exploratory analysis indicating a significant pre--post difference, while
the increase in interest was not statistically significant. The control methods
produced clear differences in perceived workload and ease of use, with Path-
Drawing generally more demanding and difficult to use. Joystick was preferred by
eight of ten participants and was associated with better perceived performance
and lower frustration. However, no statistically significant differences were
detected in the other engagement dimensions, suggesting that the most preferred
method was not necessarily more engaging across all aspects of the experience.
These findings highlight the importance of considering both usability and
engagement when designing how users interact with robots in introductory MR
experiences.
