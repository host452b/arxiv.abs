Title: From Formal Methods to Fuzzing and Back
Authors: Alastair F. Donaldson
ID: 2611.12338
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12338

Abstract:
For the first twelve years of my career in computer science I mainly worked on
formal methods. I had been drawn to the field as an undergraduate by its promise
of a way to build software with strong, mathematically based correctness
guarantees. But after many years of struggling to make formal methods scale, I
was seduced by randomised testing---also known as fuzzing. Fuzzing is much less
principled than formal methods, but it just works: it can be readily applied to
very large systems, and it is extremely effective at finding real bugs that
developers care about and fix. In this personal account I trace my path from
formal methods to fuzzing, by way of the GPUVerify project on verifying GPU
kernels and the GraphicsFuzz project on randomised testing of GPU compilers. I
argue that the oracle problem provides an intellectual link between fuzzing and
formal methods, describe my continuing, targeted uses of formal methods in the
domain of GPU programming language design, and discuss work at the boundary
between formal methods and fuzzing. Finally, I reflect on whether generative AI
might be the missing ingredient that allows formal methods to scale, and set out
my plans for large-scale formal methods via the Keystone project, through which
my collaborators and I aim to find out how much of that promise can be realised
in practice.
