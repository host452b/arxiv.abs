Title: Adaptive Budget Allocation for Smart-Contract Fuzzing: Why Online Feedback Fails to Improve Vulnerability Triage
Authors: Yanzi Liu, Jiachi Tong
ID: 2611.22430
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22430

Abstract:
Smart-contract fuzzing campaigns share a finite budget and vary widely in the
execution cost of a first correct-class vulnerability report. Using trace replay
on 143 annotated and 350 bug-injected contracts, we test whether online feedback
improves triage. At a mean budget of 100 executions per contract, uniform
allocation reaches 72% and 70% of the respective clairvoyant optima. All eight
online policies trail uniform on aggregate triage, as do the seven repeated on
the bug-injected corpus. Five online signals yield ranking AUCs of 0.32-0.51 for
imminent reports. Synthetic-oracle experiments show that sufficiently accurate
detection-cost estimates recover more of the gap than detectability rankings.
This motivates an offline probability-curve allocator that predicts first-report
probabilities across budgets and allocates by predicted gain per execution. In a
frozen evaluation on four fresh seeds per corpus, detection-budget AUC improves
over uniform by 5.38% and 4.81% on the two source corpora and by 1.99-6.54% on
three additional public benchmark subsets.
