Title: ZIMPAF & RedPhuzz: High-fidelity Web Application Fuzzing via Branch, Language Construct, and Function Call Monitoring
Authors: Tennov Simanjuntak, Christoph Csallner
ID: 2607.25012
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25012

Abstract:
We present ZIMPAF, runtime interpreter instrumentation, and RedPhuzz, a fuzzer,
to realize high-fidelity fuzzing framework. ZIMPAF implements multi-granular
interceptors to collect traces of branch coverage, errors and exceptions,
functions and language constructs, and input-tainted branches. It identifies
tainted vulnerable functions, marking them as high-value fuzzing targets, and
untainted vulnerable functions using in-situ static analysis called backward
constant probe, allowing them to be skipped, without pre-fuzzing static taint
analysis. The traces are utilized by RedPhuzz to perform targeted function- and
input-level fuzzing that goes beyond traditional coverage-based fuzzing,
supported by three novel mutation strategies: sanitization-aware, input-in-
branch-aware, and data-type-aware mutation. Furthermore, we introduce multi-
stage vulnerability detection that extends beyond error- and exception-based
detection, capable of detecting silent vulnerabilities. We evaluate RedPhuzz's
performance against its predecessor, Phuzz, on 86 test cases across six
benchmark web applications. RedPhuzz detects all vulnerabilities, whereas Phuzz
fails to detect 16. RedPhuzz is 77% faster than Phuzz despite performing more
tasks. ZIMPAF is faster than Phuzz's instrumentation (PCOV and UOPZ) while
writing significantly more data. ZIMPAF achieves 2.1 to 41.22 times higher
throughput than PCOV and UOPZ across five sampled benchmarks, and 0.87 times for
one.
