Title: Collision Avoidance and Collision Detection Strategies to Counteract Large-Scale FMCW Automotive Radar Interference
Authors: Francesco Miccoli, Vittorio Todisco, Fabrizio Cuccoli, Matteo Passeri, Vincent Martinez, Alessandro Bazzi
ID: 2611.13214
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13214

Abstract:
Automotive radars operating in dense traffic environments may experience
significant mutual interference, potentially degrading sensing reliability for
advanced driver assistance systems (ADAS). This issue motivates the development
of techniques to mitigate interference among multiple radar-equipped vehicles
sharing the same spectrum. This paper investigates interference mitigation
strategies for uncoordinated radar networks through system-level simulation. Two
approaches are considered: frame-by-frame collision avoidance (FbF-CA), which
exploits idle intervals to measure the perceived interference and possibly adapt
the operating frequency, and frame-by-frame collision detection (FbF-CD), which
estimates interference during transmission based on observed chirp collisions.
An event-driven radar network simulator is used to model realistic highway
traffic and radar interactions. Results show that collision avoidance provides
higher robustness when a larger bandwidth is available, while collision
detection is more effective under bandwidth-limited conditions. The analysis
highlights the trade-off between collision avoidance and collision detection
mitigation strategies and provides design insights for scalable automotive radar
deployments.
