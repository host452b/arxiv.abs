Title: Automated Parameter Sensitivity Ranking in Audio Synthesis
Authors: Ogulcan Kibar, Baris Ozcan
ID: 2610.38740
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38740

Abstract:
Software synthesizers expose dozens of coupled controls, many of them redundant
or perceptually inert at typical operating points. This paper presents a two-
stage, measure-and-validate framework that ranks parameters by their influence
on the audio output and then checks that ranking against an independent,
variance-based experiment. Every rendered clip is normalized to -23 LUFS and
described by five spectral fingerprints, so that gain shifts cannot masquerade
as timbral change. The Sweep Test scores each parameter by spectral deviation
over a 101-step sweep; the Sobol Test scores it by summed per-cell variance over
a 128-point Sobol excitation. Across five architecturally distinct synthesizers
(TorchSynth, Pyo, Pedalboard, Csound, and FluidSynth) spanning 48 to 76
parameters, the two tests agree at Spearman ρ= 0.82-0.96 (p-values \le 7.2
\times 10^{-15}) on the four engines that render deterministically, and at ρ=
0.32 on the one with process-level render noise--a diagnostic of that engine
rather than of the method. The strong correlations survive reducing the Sobol
budget from 128 to 32 points, and the complete analysis costs a one-time offline
budget of about 66,000 renders.
