Title: Monetary Policy Forecast Errors and Inflation Compensation under Sovereign Stress in Brazil
Authors: Giovana, Tommy
ID: 2610.30487
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30487

Abstract:
This paper examines whether inflation compensation around Brazilian monetary
policy decisions is associated differently with policy forecast errors when
sovereign spreads are relatively elevated. Public Tesouro Direto quotations,
meeting-specific Focus forecasts and a lagged EMBI indicator yield 68 three-year
and 60 five-year event observations. The outcome is a matched-horizon retail
yield proxy; the policy variable is the announced Selic target minus a
preceding-week survey mean. At three years, a 10-basis-point forecast error is
associated with 4.14 basis points less compensation repricing in the high-spread
state than in the low-spread state, but the restricted wild cluster test gives a
two-sided p-value of 0.1018. The estimated total high-state response is -0.42
basis points, with a pointwise 95 percent interval of [-2.25, 1.41]. The five-
year interaction is sensitive to pandemic meetings and changes sign when 2020 is
excluded. A common sample of 43 events does not establish a maturity gradient. A
supplementary comparison on extended windows is also sensitive to the policy
measure. The evidence is therefore inconclusive about state dependence. Survey
timing, sparse maturity support and daily retail quotations prevent these
associations from identifying a structural monetary shock, a pure inflation-risk
premium or fiscal dominance.
