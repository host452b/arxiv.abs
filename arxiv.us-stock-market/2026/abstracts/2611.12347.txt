Title: Fueling Inflation Risks: An Oil Shortage Scenario
Authors: Christiane Baumeister, Todd Clark, Florian Huber
ID: 2611.12347
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12347

Abstract:
Central banks routinely use alternative scenarios to assess risks to the
economic outlook, but these are usually generated from linear models that are
unable to speak to the likelihood of extreme outcomes. We develop a nonlinear
modeling framework that turns a narrative oil market scenario into a fully
probabilistic assessment of the dynamics of oil and gasoline prices, aggregate
inflation, and household inflation expectations. It combines an expert-based
path for a tail event, a nonparametric model of the global oil market, and
nonlinear Phillips curves for the energy, food, and core components of PCE
inflation. We apply it in real time to quantify energy price and inflation risks
of a severe oil shortage caused by the closure of the Strait of Hormuz, modeled
as a rapid drawdown of global petroleum inventories, using only information
available in February 2026. Our analysis shows that once oil reserves hit rock
bottom, oil and gasoline prices rise sharply, with risks tilted to the upside.
Headline PCE inflation peaks near 4.8% in late 2026, and households expect
inflation to rise by about 3 percentage points. Two years out, inflation is
still highly likely to exceed the Fed's 2% target.
