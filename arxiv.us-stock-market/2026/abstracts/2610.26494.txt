Title: Promotion Signaling and Signal Jamming: An Analysis of Job Title Inflation
Authors: Yi Chen, Michael Waldman, Zhenda Yin
ID: 2610.26494
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26494

Abstract:
Starting with Waldman (1984), it has become widely understood that an important
role of promotions in labor markets is that promotions serve as signals of high
worker ability. In this paper we explore the common and growing practice called
job title inflation which serves a signal jamming role that reduces the
signaling value of promotions. We first explore a two-period model and show how
job title inflation can arise in equilibrium and increase social welfare both
because workers directly value impressive sounding job titles and because the
practice reduces job assignment inefficiencies due to signaling. We then
introduce into the model an investment choice for young workers concerning human
capital accumulation and show that job title inflation can decrease rather than
increase welfare in that case. That is, when promotions and the associated wage
increases serve as an important incentive for investing in human capital, the
use of job title inflation can persist even though the practice lowers both
overall efficiency and expected worker utility. We then consider three
extensions: (i) a setting characterized by worker turnover; (ii) a setting
characterized by a wage transparency law; and (iii) a setting characterized by
heterogeneous worker visibility. Also, in addition to showing how job title
inflation can arise as an equilibrium phenomenon that can either raise or lower
welfare, we discuss the extent to which our theoretical analysis matches
evidence concerning the use of job title inflation in real-world labor markets.
