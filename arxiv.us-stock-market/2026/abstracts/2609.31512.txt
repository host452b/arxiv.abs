Title: Tolerable Inflation, Intolerable Uncertainty
Authors: Eric Vansteenberghe
ID: 2609.31512
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31512

Abstract:
How much of an inflation deviation a central bank should tolerate is a well-
defined optimum in the New Keynesian model, but it cannot be identified: optimal
accommodation of supply shocks and an unwarranted drift of the effective
inflation target are observationally equivalent. This impossibility has a cost.
A public that cannot verify the bank's intention prices each expected point of
overshoot, so uncertainty about inflation is flat below the announced target and
rises linearly above it: the observational-equivalence effect. Professional
forecasts, swap and option markets and realized inflation all show this pattern,
with the kink at the announced number. A second and larger effect produces the
same shape: inflation is more volatile when it is high, and in realized
inflation that level effect is mostly energy. Both effects load on the distance
from the target, so the data admit the observational-equivalence effect without
isolating it, and its cost cannot be estimated with the data at hand. We
therefore cannot say how optimal monetary policy should change on its account,
but three policy conclusions survive: in the textbook model, not observing the
slope of the Phillips curve and the composition of shocks never calls for
tolerating less; the announced target remains worth having, because it
compresses beliefs at the target; and the central bank can monitor the point-
years inflation spends above target, on which the effect is charged, and read
inflation uncertainty net of the distance from the target. Normalized
Uncertainty provides that reading. In French loan-level data, raw inflation
uncertainty is unrelated to corporate loan rates after controls, whereas a one-
standard-deviation increase in Normalized Uncertainty is associated with rates
89 basis points higher. The correction also reveals a negative association with
growth across countries and alters euro-area time-series results.
