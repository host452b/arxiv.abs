Title: More Mentions, Less Information? Diagnosing Identity and Alignment in Reddit Embeddings for Volatility Forecasting
Authors: Zhiyu Zheng
ID: 2610.25468
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25468

Abstract:
Since the 2021 GameStop short squeeze, r/WallStreetBets (WSB) has been
associated with episodes of extreme volatility, and its text is increasingly
used to forecast volatility. A model using only large language model (LLM)
embeddings of WSB posts attains an out-of-sample $R^2$ of 0.13 for log realized
variance. That number shows predictive power, but it does not reveal where the
information comes from or how much the text adds beyond a firm's own volatility
history. We answer these questions with a diagnostic protocol that nests
embeddings on a heterogeneous autoregressive (HAR) benchmark, compares them with
text-free firm-identity controls, shuffles the day-to-text pairing within firm-
years to test day-specific alignment, and checks whether gains persist across
periods and coverage groups. We apply the protocol to frozen LLM embeddings in a
panel of 202,145 mentioned stock-days, using retrospective rolling out-of-sample
tests over 2015-2022. The results indicate that most of the standalone fit
reflects firm or firm-year information: on firms seen in training, a text-free
firm baseline reaches $R^2 = 0.32$, shuffling preserves 89-92% of the standalone
fit, and masking ticker strings reduces standalone $R^2$ to -0.005. Adding
embeddings to HAR reduces equal-date MSE by 1.22% (95% CI [0.94, 1.49]). This
small but statistically significant gain survives firm controls and ticker
masking, but is largely eliminated by alignment shuffling. The gain is
concentrated in 2015-2019 and is not distinguishable from zero in 2020-2022.
Expanding coverage by 48% through off-hours and weekend posts adds stock-days
with no reliable positive increment. More mentions therefore do not imply more
incremental forecasting information.
