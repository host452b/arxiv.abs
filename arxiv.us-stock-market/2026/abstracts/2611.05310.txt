Title: Mitigating Return Drag and Regime Whipsaws in Sector Rotation: An Asymmetric Downside-Aware Nested Clustered Optimization Framework
Authors: Supriadi
ID: 2611.05310
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05310

Abstract:
Hierarchical clustering and Nested Clustered Optimization (NCO) mitigate the
mathematical ill-conditioning of classical Markowitz covariance matrices.
However, two structural vulnerabilities persist in equity markets: (1) symmetric
variance penalizes upside and downside return dispersion identically, inducing
acute return drag against soaring momentum sectors; and (2) unconstrained Hidden
Markov Models (HMM) exhibit hyper-active regime switching (whipsaw instability),
incurring excessive turnover and transaction drag. We propose Asymmetric
Downside-Risk Nested Clustered Optimization with Sticky Regime Dynamics (Asym-
Sticky-NCO) for tactical sector rotation across all 11 S&P 500 GICS sectors. The
framework substitutes symmetric covariance with the Bawa-Lindenberg downside
semi-covariance matrix across both hierarchical tree clustering and two-stage
allocation. Furthermore, we introduce a Sticky Gaussian HMM incorporating a
diagonal self-transition persistence prior (\k{appa}) and confidence-adaptive
boundaries (δ) to enforce macroeconomic persistence. Strictly non-anticipative
walk-forward backtesting across 65 out-of-sample months (August 2020 to December
2025) demonstrates that the architecture elevates CAGR from 10.65% (Plain NCO)
to 13.41% (+276 bps/year), delivers a 97.74% total return versus 72.99%,
suppresses maximum drawdown to 14.22% (versus 23.93% for the S&P 500), and
achieves a Calmar ratio of 0.94 (+38.2% over S&P 500; +84.3% over Baseline
Regime-NCO). Crucially, sticky dynamics cut regime whipsaws by 75.0% (from 16 to
4 switches) and reduce monthly turnover from 60.78% to 30.48%, addressing core
limitations of machine learning asset allocation.
