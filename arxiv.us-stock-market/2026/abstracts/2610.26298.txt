Title: Deep-$π^*$: A Neural Network Estimate of Trend Inflation
Authors: Carlos Montes-Galdón
ID: 2610.26298
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26298

Abstract:
Most empirical models of trend inflation treat it as a latent random walk driven
by unobserved shocks, so they cannot say which economic conditions move it.
Deep-$π^*$ makes the innovations to the trend a possibly nonlinear function of a
large number of macroeconomic indicators, estimated with neural networks, so
that Shapley values can be used to attribute every movement of the trend to the
indicators behind it. Two complementary loss functions define a current trend,
which fits inflation today, and a forward-looking trend, which best predicts
inflation over the next year. In US data, employment-related variables were the
largest contributors to the post-pandemic rise of the current trend. However,
the forward-looking trend rose earlier, with real activity, employment, and
money and credit as the main drivers, and peaked eight to twelve months before
the current trend. Both trends looked through most of the movements in energy
and commodity prices. The contributions of the indicators are nonlinear, with
sizeable interactions between them. Re-estimated every month during the surge,
the model forecast inflation more accurately than the standard UC-SV model and a
random walk, and in early 2021, when professional forecasters expected inflation
of 3% or less, the forward-looking trend was already signalling an increase in
persistent inflation.
