Title: Evaluating LLM Research Agents as Tests of Return Predictability
Authors: Chang Liu
ID: 2611.04070
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04070

Abstract:
Researchers ask large language model research agents whether a forecasting model
beats the historical-mean benchmark, a question that real data cannot settle. We
evaluate the agent's verdict as a statistical test in runs of three commercial
models and one open-weight model in simulated return series whose predictive
signal, if any, is known. The commercial agents detect weak signals more often
than an out-of-sample test, mainly because they accept weaker evidence: held to
an agent's false-positive rate, a conventional in-sample test does as well or
better. All four models claim predictability too often when no signal exists;
the commercial agents also state miscalibrated probabilities and change their
verdicts after a sentence about prior research. Magnitudes vary by model; excess
false claims persist without a resampling artifact. Agent verdicts are
informative but need a neutral brief, a decision rule fixed outside the agent
and a sealed holdout.
