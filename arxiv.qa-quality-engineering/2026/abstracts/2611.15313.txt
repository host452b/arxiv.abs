Title: Influence of Assortativity on the SICP Spreading Process in Hypergraphs
Authors: Jelmer Stolwijk, Renze Dijkhuizen, H. A., Peters, Huijuan Wang
ID: 2611.15313
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15313

Abstract:
Many real-world systems involve group interactions that cannot be captured by
pairwise graphs. They can be better represented by hypergraphs where each
hyperedge could connect an arbitrary number of nodes. The Susceptible-Infected
Contact Process (SICP) has been proposed to model the spreading of information
and epidemics on hypergraphs. However, how higher-order topological properties
of a hypergraph influence the SICP spreading process remains understudied. In
this paper, we examine the impact of hypergraph assortativity based on the
Neighborhood Coefficient (NC)-the average size of hyperedges incident to a node-
on the SICP spreading process. By introducing a low-complexity measure of
hypergraph assortativity grounded in analysis of variance, we apply hyperdegree-
preserving rewirings to real-world networks and synthetic hypergraph models to
tune their assortativity. We find and explain why lower NC assortativity yields
higher average epidemic prevalence and lower prevalence variance across
different seed selections. This phenomenon is evident when hyperdegree-
preserving rewirings are applied to real-world networks with heterogeneous
topological structures, but it is largely absent when comparing synthetic random
hypergraphs to their rewired counterparts.
