Title: Safety Analysis of CPSs Over-the-air Updates: Test Case Prioritization and Selection
Authors: Andrea Bombarda, Luca Carminati, Nunzio Marco Bisceglia, Mehrnoosh Askarpour, Sahar Kokaly, Ramesh S, Marsha Chechik, Claudio Menghi
ID: 2610.24475
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24475

Abstract:
Over-the-air (OTA) updates for Cyber-Physical Systems (CPS) allow engineers to
modify their software wirelessly. In the automotive domain, they enable
manufacturers to update software without physical access to the vehicle.
However, OTA updates can generate a large number of possible software
configurations. For example, end users may decide whether or not to install any
given OTA updates. The combinatorial explosion of these configurations hampers
SW testing, a standard practice for detecting errors in CPS. To overcome this
limitation, we propose CRISP (CRIticality-based Selection and Prioritization), a
novel framework supporting test case prioritization and selection in the context
of OTA updates. Our framework leverages test case robustness, i.e., how far they
are from violating the system requirements, to drive test case prioritization
and selection. It enables engineers to rank test case executions from most to
least critical, and to select only the critical test cases relevant to a given
OTA update. We evaluated our approach using a representative SW product line
consisting of seven different vehicles, 22 driving scenarios, and two software
components. We considered 224 tests, requiring 59 hours of execution time. We
assessed the effectiveness of CRISP across five OTA updates. We compared our
solution with (a) a random test case prioritization and selection algorithm, and
(b) AP-DISC, identified as the state-of-the-art solution in a recent empirical
study on the subject. Our results show that CRISP outperforms our baselines in
finding failure-revealing test cases, and that our selection procedure can be
configured to find reasonable and practical tradeoffs among accuracy (percentage
of failure-revealing test cases identified) and computational time (time
required to find them).
