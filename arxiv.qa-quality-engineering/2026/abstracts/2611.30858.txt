Title: The AMPEL Platform: A Concept for Open-Source Collaboration in Developing In-House Medical Device Software under EU-Regulation
Authors: Daniel Steinbach, Maria Schmidt, Alexander Twrdik, Clemens Malkawi, Toralf Kirsten, Martin Federbusch
ID: 2611.30858
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30858

Abstract:
Clinical decision support systems (CDSS) are expected to enhance medical
treatment through automated data analysis, yet a substantial evidence gap
persists as to whether they actually improve patient outcomes. It arises due to
the black-box nature of commercial products and the constrains of the European
medical device regulation (MDR) for such studies: patient outcomes may only be
collected through the use of medical device software (MDSW). In-house MDSW under
Article 5(5) MDR is a promising solution, allowing healthcare facilities to
manufacture and study such software for their own use under reduced
requirements. However, the overall limited experience with in-house MDSW
development in Europe currently restricts the widespread use of this regulatory
approach. While open-source software generally fosters evidence generation,
publishing an in-house MDSW as open source would risk a classification as
"placement on the market", which the MDR prohibits. The concept presented here
builds on experience from developing an in-house CDSS at the University of
Leipzig Medical Center, expert discussions, a literature review and a formal
legal opinion. A companion online wiki summarizes and regularly updates the
results. The current concept outlines a shared open-source codebase, which
doesn't constitute a medical device under the MDR. Also, "Data Input" modules
must be programmed separately at each site, while still allowing for multicenter
collaboration on the codebase. Atypically for open-source projects, this leaves
a considerable effort to deploy the software within each site, e.g. each
healthcare facility must independently meet the MDR requirements for an in-house
MDSW. The AMPEL concept describes a regulatory pathway for the open-source
publication and development of in-house MDSW. It underpins the open-source AMPEL
platform, which helps healthcare facilities build their own in-house CDSS.
