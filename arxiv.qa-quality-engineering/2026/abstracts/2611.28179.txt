Title: Quality-Control Policies Shape Model Behaviour in Computational Pathology
Authors: Mario Parreño Centeno, Yu Guan, Pedro Montero Pavón, Sanna Palosaari, Juha Väyrynen
ID: 2611.28179
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28179

Abstract:
Quality-control policies determine which image regions are used by computational
pathology models, but their effects are difficult to isolate when quality
estimation and input selection are coupled. We make these policies explicit,
configurable and testable. We introduce PathQC-3C, comprising 1,661 densely
annotated regions from 1,194 haematoxylin and eosin whole-slide images across 32
cancer/tissue categories, and TileNet, a framework that separates dense
tissue/background/artefact estimation from task-specific input-selection
policies. We evaluated the default predictor across three held-out cohorts and
against harmonised quality-control comparators, assessed policy decisions in a
blinded single-pathologist audit, and tested alternative policies in three
downstream settings. In retrospective CAMELYON16 multiple-instance learning,
background-aware selection improved the pooled out-of-fold area under the
receiver operating characteristic curve from 0.758 to 0.839 compared with random
sampling from the same exhaustive candidate pool under a fixed tile budget. In
weak-label lung classification, strict artefact exclusion reduced the pre-
matching candidate pool by approximately 43%, with small metric-dependent
differences after matching training-set size and patient contribution.
PathQCAug, which composites real artefact components during tumour-stroma model
adaptation, improved macro Dice on high-artefact tiles under severe held-out
composited artefacts across three training seeds and outperformed random-erasing
and clean-tissue controls. Separating quality estimation from policy execution
provides an auditable way to control which image content enters computational
pathology models; the appropriate policy is task specific, and clinical utility
remains to be established.
