Title: A Synthetic Multi-Modal Testbed for Security Stress Testing of Agentic Counter-UAS Systems
Authors: Ivan A. Fernandez, Jingdao Chen
ID: 2611.25249
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25249

Abstract:
Agentic cyber-physical systems rely on perception and state estimation to
support autonomous decisions. Degraded, inconsistent, or manipulated sensor
observations can therefore propagate through an autonomous pipeline and create
security-relevant failures. This work presents a synthetic Counter-Unmanned
Aircraft System (C-UAS) testbed developed in NVIDIA Isaac Sim for repeatable
stress testing of perception and tracking algorithms supporting agentic
decision-making. A simplified quadrotor follows scripted trajectories while co-
located virtual sensors generate synchronized RGB, RTX LiDAR, and illustrative
IR-like observations. The testbed varies target range, motion, clutter,
occlusion, and sensor availability, with future stress cases including sensor
dropout, temporal misalignment, cross-modal disagreement, and false
observations. As an initial exemplar, a nonlinear coordinated-turn Extended
Kalman Filter (EKF) is evaluated over a 20-s maneuvering trajectory using LiDAR
target-centroid measurements. Position error and uncertainty are compared as
LiDAR observations degrade and are lost. The framework provides a reproducible
environment for studying how sensing degradation affects downstream autonomous
estimation and resilience in agentic C-UAS systems.
