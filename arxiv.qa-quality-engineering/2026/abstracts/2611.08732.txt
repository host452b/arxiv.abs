Title: FishSense Lite: A Camera-Based, Single Laser Hardware Framework with Software System for In Situ Fish Length Measurement
Authors: Christopher L. Crutchfield, Kyle Hu, Nathan Hui, Omair Qazi, Jennifer Loch, Alli Candelmo, Brice X. Semmens, Curt Schurgers, Ryan Kastner
ID: 2611.08732
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08732

Abstract:
Fish stock assessments need fish lengths paired with species from far more
places than trained surveyors can reach. Recreational divers already visit many
of those places, but measuring a fish underwater has required trained observers
or specialist equipment. We present FishSense Lite, which recovers fish length
from a single photograph taken with a recreational dive camera, a dive laser
pointer, and a 3D-printed mount. The diver aims the laser at a fish and takes a
picture; afterward, the dot's position in the image gives the range to the fish,
and the range turns the fish's size in the image into a length. A citizen-
science program, trained by the system's builders, taught its own divers and
collected lengths paired with species for 73 wild fish across seven open-water
dives. On seven wild fish also measured by an underwater stereo-imaging system,
the two instruments agreed without detectable bias (mean difference +0.8%). On
rigid targets of known length, FishSense Lite recovered every target to within
1.3%, and 99% of 1,055 frames fell within a 15% error budget. From the error
sources we measure, we derive a field protocol: calibrate against a dive slate
on every dive, photograph fish within 15 degrees of perpendicular and beyond 1
m, and take at least ten frames of each.
