Title: Software-Defined Internet Integration of Satellite Networks (SDISN)
Authors: Michael Menth, Moritz Flüchter, Jens Haala
ID: 2611.25837
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25837

Abstract:
This paper proposes SDISN, a software-defined Internet integration of satellite
networks. It facilitates the transmission of IP traffic from user terminals
(UTs) or Internet nodes over a satellite network with inter-satellite links
(ISLs). Gateways (GWs) together with Points of Presence (PoPs) support the
transition between the satellite network and the Internet. UTs and GWs connect
to the satellite network via serving satellites (SrvSats). As the satellites
orbit the Earth at high speed, UTs and GWs frequently change their SrvSats. This
causes frequent changes to their reachability, which poses a major challenge for
integration. In the proposed solution, GWs and SrvSats encapsulate traffic with
forwarding information so that the traffic can be carried over the satellite
network towards appropriate SrvSats of UTs or GWs. The forwarding information on
the encapsulating nodes is continuously updated by a controller to reflect
changed SrvSats and possibly changed topology of the satellite network. Design
goals for the proposed architecture were low complexity on satellites, high
ability for traffic engineering, and keeping UTs out of the routing system for
security and compatibility reasons. UTs may have increased security requirements
(ISRs) such that their traffic should be carried within the constellation to
avoid non-trusted terrestrial infrastructure. Extensions are proposed to support
such ISRs. UTs may obtain either dynamic IP addresses from a nearby GW/PoP
wherever they roam or they may obtain static IP addresses from a fixed Home
GW/PoP. In the latter case, triangle routing via the Home GW occurs when the UT
roams away from the Home GW. As this adds latency to the communication, we
investigate architectural extensions for latency reduction. In addition, we show
how traffic engineering can be performed in such an architecture with the help
of alternate GWs and SrvSats.
