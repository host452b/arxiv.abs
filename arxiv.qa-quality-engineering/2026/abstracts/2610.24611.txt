Title: HSPR: Recovering Software-Agent Benchmark Scores via Hierarchical Posterior Inference
Authors: Wanyi Chen, Wanqin Shen, Yuanchen Shi, Zhensu Sun, Fang Kong
ID: 2610.24611
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24611

Abstract:
Evaluating software agents on a full benchmark is expensive. A small task subset
can preserve agent rankings while yielding inaccurate scores. Historical
responses provide useful structure, but tasks with identical source responses
can have different outcomes for a new target agent. We introduce Hierarchical
Score Posterior Recovery (HSPR), which combines source-fitted response factors
with uncertain group success rates. Observed target outcomes update the
posteriors over the target latent state and group rates; posterior-guided
acquisition selects subsequent tasks. Its score-based criterion equals the
expected one-step reduction in full-score posterior variance. Observed successes
and posterior-predictive counts for unqueried tasks yield a full-score
posterior. The final estimate uses its median with source-selected calibration.
At a 10% task budget on four task banks from the SWE-bench family, HSPR achieves
the lowest mean absolute error (MAE) on three banks and the lowest maximum
absolute error (MaxErr) on all four among the methods compared. Removing group
uncertainty or replacing adaptive queries increases macro MAE in the respective
controls. Macro MAE decreases across 5-20% budgets. Code and project resources
are publicly available.
