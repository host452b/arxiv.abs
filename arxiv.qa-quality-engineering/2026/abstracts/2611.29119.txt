Title: From Footprints to Service Regions: Modeling and Analysis of Dense LEO Satellite Networks Using an Orbit-Aware Cox Point Process
Authors: Chang-Sik Choi
ID: 2611.29119
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29119

Abstract:
In dense low Earth orbit (LEO) satellite networks, several accessible satellites
cover the same location. A footprint therefore identifies where communication is
feasible but does not determine which satellite serves each user. The service
region of a satellite, namely the set of locations it serves, governs satellite
load, resource sharing, and user throughput. Using an orbit-aware Cox point
process on a common orbital shell, we model each service region as the
intersection of a satellite's spherical-cap footprint and its spherical Voronoi
cell under nearest-satellite association. We then use Palm calculus to derive
exact expressions for the mean area and perimeter and a coverage--load identity.
After coverage saturates, mean area and load decrease inversely with population.
In contrast, adding satellites along existing orbits leaves a positive limiting
mean perimeter. We connect this geometry to an approximate noise-limited user
rate. Along the tested densification paths, the increase in user rate comes
mainly from the decrease in mean satellite load rather than from the improvement
of the serving link. We also compare the model with Walker constellations with
Starlink-type parameters, using Cox parameters matched to the local orbit and
satellite densities at each latitude. The model reproduces the mean area and
perimeter of these constellations over broad latitude ranges, with larger
differences near component maximum latitudes. These results show that service-
region geometry, and hence satellite load and user rate, depend not only on the
total satellite population but also on its allocation across orbital planes.
