Title: Noise-Awareness in Quantum Software Testing: An Empirical Landscape Analysis
Authors: Mir Mohammad Yousuf, Shabir Ahmad Sofi, Bisma Majid
ID: 2610.25233
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25233

Abstract:
Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum (NISQ) hardware has transformed quantum
computing into a practical experimental platform while introducing fundamental
challenges associated with hardware noise, decoherence, and probabilistic
execution. Although quantum software testing (QST) has rapidly emerged as an
active research area, it remains unclear to what extent existing testing
methodologies explicitly engage with noisy and hardware-constrained execution
environments. This paper presents a systematic empirical landscape analysis of
noise-awareness in QST research through a deterministic, taxonomy-guided
analytical framework applied to 61 papers published between 2018 and 2025 across
eight testing paradigms, contrasting how contributions are framed with how they
are evaluated. Among the analyzed papers, 47 (77.0%) are noise-oblivious and
only 14 (23.0%) explicitly engage with noise-related execution concerns, while
only 11 (18.0%) report evaluation on physical hardware or a noisy simulator.
Test generation, the largest paradigm, exhibits a noise-awareness rate of only
14.3%, and debugging and repair 18.2%; coverage-based, metamorphic, and
differential testing contain no noise-aware contributions. Noise-aware
methodologies emerged late and remain outnumbered in all analyzed years. Framing
and evaluation are strongly associated (odds ratio 40.5, p < 0.001) yet diverge
for individual papers: 5 of the 14 noise-aware papers evaluate only on ideal
simulators. Inferential statistical tests are reported in only 16.4% of papers.
The classification framework was validated against a blinded, human-coded
reference sample, achieving almost perfect inter-rater agreement on testing-
technique classification and substantial agreement on noise-awareness. The
framework provides a reusable empirical basis for longitudinal analysis of
hardware-conscious testing practice.
