Title: Agent Trajectories as Evidence for Software Quality Assurance
Authors: Jonathan Ullrich, Julien Siebert, Michael Kläs, Adam Trendowicz, Andreas Jedlitschka
ID: 2611.22323
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22323

Abstract:
Autonomous software agents increasingly plan, implement, and test software with
limited human oversight. Their decisions may introduce implicit assumptions that
final code and passing tests do not adequately expose. We propose analyzing
observable agent trajectories alongside software and agent specifications and AI
generated artifacts as evidence for targeted quality assurance. The aim is to
prioritize consequential decisions for scrutiny, investigate their possible
contribution to software problems, and inform improvements to specifications and
development practices. Industrial experience motivates the idea; available
execution records and preliminary analysis methods make it plausible. However,
trajectories alone do not establish that decisions are justified, outcomes
acceptable, or corrective changes effective. We ask how this evidence can
support review, diagnosis, and continuous quality improvement, and when simpler
QA approaches suffice. Perspectives from requirements engineering, verification
and validation, agent systems, causal inference, empirical software engineering,
and human factors could help develop and evaluate the idea.
