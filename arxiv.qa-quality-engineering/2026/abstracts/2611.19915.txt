Title: CHERI under Fault Injection: An Emulation-Based Security Evaluation of Capability Hardware Enhanced RISC Instructions
Authors: Eva Sophia Schreyer
ID: 2611.19915
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19915

Abstract:
Memory-safety vulnerabilities continue to dominate real-world exploit chains
despite decades of research into software mitigations. CHERI is a hardware
architecture that enforces spatial and temporal memory safety by augmenting
pointers with hardware-checked metadata, thus preventing software-based
exploitation, but its threat model explicitly excludes fault injection. This
thesis investigates whether targeted fault injection can undermine CHERI's
memory-safety guarantees in a pure-capability CHERI-RISC-V environment. Two
fault classes are considered: bit flips corrupting capability metadata, and
fault-induced check skipping on capability store instructions. We implement
fault injection primitives in a QEMU-based emulation environment, which enables
deterministic, repeatable experiments without specialised hardware. With them,
we demonstrate four attack scenarios: buffer overflows, control-flow hijacking,
data-only attacks, and use-after-free. Each succeeds with one or two targeted
faults (lower bounds under the emulation-based setting). Check skipping offers
an alternative route to control-flow hijacking and data-only attacks, with a
minimum of one fault. These results show that CHERI's guarantees depend on the
physical integrity of capability metadata, the uninterrupted execution of
metadata checks, and the integrity of the revocation bitmap: a single targeted
fault can circumvent spatial safety, temporal safety, or control-flow integrity.
CHERI still raises the cost of exploitation, since fault injection is a
prerequisite for every attack and the sentry mechanism forces an attacker to
supply a valid tagged capability or spend a second fault restoring the tag.
Based on these findings, we propose mitigations: ECC-protected capability
memory, capability field authentication codes, redundant execution of security-
critical checks, and revocation bitmap integrity verification.
