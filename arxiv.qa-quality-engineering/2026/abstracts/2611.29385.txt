Title: Exploiting Queue Information for Quality of Services Routing in SDN Networks
Authors: Hong-Li Lin, Yung-Chung Wang
ID: 2611.29385
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29385

Abstract:
Routing costs determine how deterministic networks allocate feasible queue
resources to successive flow requests. We propose a topology-aware cost that
combines normalized network-calculus post-admission delay and backlog bounds
with static path diversity and shortest-path load concentration. Their product
weights the backlog premium above a delay baseline within a shared queue-level
routing, LARAC, and admission-control framework. Across ten paired runs, the
proposed cost admits 657.7, 478.9, and 168.2 more flows than Max-Risk on Polska,
NSFNET, and COST266, respectively; the corresponding 95% confidence intervals
exclude zero. Germany50 shows a smaller gain of 67.6 flows with an interval
crossing zero. The proposed cost has the highest mean acceptance among five
costs on three of the four topologies. Gains are accompanied by comparable or
lower mean hop counts and cannot be explained simply by higher aggregate
utilization. On 100-1000-node networks, risk-aware costs remain comparatively
stable while delay-only acceptance declines. A centralized SDN realization
reduces distributed control and protocol-state overhead, while large-scale
results expose the associated routing-performance tradeoff.
