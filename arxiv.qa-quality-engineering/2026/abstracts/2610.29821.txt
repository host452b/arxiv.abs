Title: Controlling Variability in~Neuroimaging Analysis Pipelines with~Software Product Lines
Authors: Gauthier Le Bartz Lyan, Paul Temple, Boris Clenet, Mathieu Acher
ID: 2610.29821
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29821

Abstract:
Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) is an acquisition technique used for medical
diagnosis. It is notably used for structural and functional MRI (or fMRI) image
analysis. MRI data is known to be complex and noisy, so pipelines are created to
extract and enhance relevant information in retrieved images. The NARPS study
highlighted the significant variability that can emerge from different pipelines
set up by independent teams while trying to answer the same research question
about brain functioning. Having different implementations realizing the same
task is a typical playground for software product lines (SPLs), which may be out
of the sight of neuroimage practitioners. Thus, proposing an SPL out of these
different pipelines falls back on a typical reverse-engineering problem with its
usual difficulties. In this document, we advocate for the conceptual adoption of
SPL principles as a promising direction to improve reproducibility and
consistency in neuroimaging research.
