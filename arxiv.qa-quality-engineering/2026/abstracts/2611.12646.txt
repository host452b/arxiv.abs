Title: Surrendering and Reclaiming Judgment: How Software Engineering Students Experience AI Reliance
Authors: Nathan Cassee, Umit Akirmak, Luiz Guerra, Margaret-Anne Storey, Neil Ernst
ID: 2611.12646
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12646

Abstract:
Software architecture is one of the skills novice developers must master to
succeed in industry, and as AI disrupts software development, it is also
transforming how students learn architecture. Existing work on how students and
novices work with AI has mostly focused on small programming exercises or on
students' perceptions of AI, so little is known about how students experience AI
as they learn software architecture in larger team projects. We report on an
exploratory case study of 43 senior software engineering students who used AI
throughout a semester-long, team-based software architecture project. We
combined surveys with focus groups and analyzed the focus group transcripts and
the open survey responses through Braun and Clarke's reflexive thematic
analysis. We constructed two themes. The first, Surrendering Judgment to AI,
traces how participants' reliance on AI deepened as they used it to architect
and implement their systems, until they surrendered their judgment and became
stuck in a codebase they had created but could not understand, which made them
rely on AI even more. Participants described feeling ashamed, frustrated, and
emotionally disconnected from their work, a toll that, in our reading, left them
with even less reason to break their reliance. The second, Applying Human
Judgment, centers on participants who kept or reclaimed their judgment: the
knowledge and experience it rested on, and the strategies they used to apply it.
Participants' accounts suggest that relaxed deadlines and a lecture and lab on
working with AI gave them room to acquire knowledge and reclaim judgment. Given
the expectations placed on junior developers and the widespread adoption of AI,
we believe these first-hand accounts can help software engineering education and
industry adopt AI in ways that preserve novice developers' judgment, ownership,
and motivation.
