Title: Understanding Post-Quantum Cryptography Adoption: Practices, Challenges, and Evolution in Open-Source Software
Authors: Souhardya Saha Dip, Moshood A. Fakorede, Krishna Upadhyay, Umar Farooq
ID: 2610.38054
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38054

Abstract:
Post-quantum cryptography (PQC) standards establish algorithms for quantum-safe
systems, but empirical evidence about how software projects implement and
maintain PQC support remains limited. We investigate this implementation and
maintenance work by mining 114,361 commits and 5,713 issues across 179 open-
source repositories. We use human annotation and validated LLM classification to
identify PQC algorithms in commits, classify their adoption status and
deployment mode, and identify technical challenges in issue discussions. The
commit classification for PQC algorithms reveals that 90% of commits concern
maintenance of existing support. From the issue discussions, we construct an
11-category taxonomy of recurring technical challenges. Build, packaging, and
dependency failures, along with functional defects, account for approximately
half of categorized issues. To examine how PQC support evolves, we use time-
series analysis and first implementations of individual algorithms within
repositories around NIST standardization milestones. After the 2024
finalization, standardized algorithm names appeared in more than 80% of the PQC
development changes analyzed, although earlier names such as Kyber continued to
appear. Development activity increased over the observation period. Our
milestone analysis, however, did not find a significant increase in first
implementations. Migration plans should therefore account for sustained
maintenance, prioritize build reliability and integration testing, and track
both standardized and predecessor algorithm names. We make the dataset,
taxonomy, annotations, and analysis pipeline publicly available.
