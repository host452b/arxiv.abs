Title: Generative AI in Software Testing: Practitioners' Perspectives on the Benefits, Challenges, Opportunities, and Risks
Authors: Chamari Dasanayake, Rahul Mohanani, Vladimir Mandić, Burak Turhan
ID: 2611.23256
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23256

Abstract:
Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) increasingly supports software
testing, yet empirical evidence on practitioners' perceptions remains limited.
This study investigates perceived benefits, challenges, opportunities, and risks
of GenAI in software testing through a questionnaire survey of 64 practitioners.
Closed-ended responses were analyzed descriptively, while open-ended responses
were qualitatively coded to contextualize the findings. Among the respondents,
34 were users (those with regular or experimental use of GenAI in testing) and
30 were non-users (those without prior use of GenAI in testing). Results show
generally positive perceptions of GenAI for reducing effort, test generation,
and debugging, alongside concerns about prompt dependence, trust, and incorrect
outputs. Users were more positive about several concrete benefits while also
more frequently prioritizing reliability-related risks, whereas non-users showed
greater uncertainty about some technically specific challenges. Overall, the
findings position GenAI as an assistive technology requiring human validation
and highlight the importance of considering practitioners' experience with GenAI
when interpreting perceptions of GenAI-assisted testing.
