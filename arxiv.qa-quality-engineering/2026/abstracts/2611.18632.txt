Title: LLMs in DevSecOps: A Systematic Mapping of Models, Evidence, and Research Gaps Across the Software Lifecycle
Authors: Sindu Chitraju, Mir Mehedi Ahsan Pritom
ID: 2611.18632
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18632

Abstract:
Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly being integrated into software
security and DevSecOps workflows, yet their adoption, evaluation, and maturity
vary substantially across lifecycle stages. This study systematically maps
recent LLM-enabled security research published between 2024 and 2026 (until
June) across six DevSecOps stages: \textit{Plan}, \textit{Code}, \textit{Test},
\textit{Review}, \textit{Deploy}, and \textit{Monitor}. For each stage, we
analyze the security tasks addressed, models and adaptation strategies employed,
evaluation settings, reported effectiveness, and key limitations. Our findings
reveal an uneven research landscape, with \textit{Test} and \textit{Code}
receiving substantially greater attention than \textit{Deploy} and
\textit{Review}. Across stages, recent systems increasingly rely on structured
context, iterative reasoning, fine-tuning, retrieval augmentation, or agentic
workflows rather than direct single-shot prompting. However, reported
performance remains difficult to compare because studies use heterogeneous
tasks, datasets, metrics, and baselines. Furthermore, most approaches are
evaluated using benchmarks or open-source repositories, while industrial-scale
evidence remains limited. We also find that existing systems are predominantly
stage-specific, with little evidence of LLM-enabled security reasoning spanning
multiple DevSecOps stages. Based on these observations, we identify research
opportunities in standardized evaluation, production-scale validation, supply-
chain and Infrastructure-as-Code security, trustworthy autonomous remediation,
and cross-stage LLM integration. This study provides a recent lifecycle-level
view of where LLM-enabled DevSecOps research is concentrated, what
methodological patterns are emerging, and where significant gaps remain.
