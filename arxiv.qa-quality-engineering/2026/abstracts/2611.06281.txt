Title: Building a Method: Challenges and Lessons from Developing an Impact Evaluation Framework for Public Investment in Open Source Software
Authors: Laia Domenech Burin
ID: 2611.06281
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06281

Abstract:
Modern societies and institutions depend on an invisible layer of open digital
infrastructure: foundational, openly accessible digital technologies that
support a broad range of social and economic activities. Open source software is
a core component of this infrastructure, underpinning everyday technologies and
critical systems in various critical sectors. As open source software becomes
increasingly recognised, funded, and governed as open digital infrastructure,
policymakers face growing demands for accountability, transparency, and credible
evidence of impact. However, methods for evaluating public investment in open
source software remain underdeveloped, with no widely accepted methodological
standard. This paper describes the Sovereign Tech Agency's transition from
qualitative reporting and funding-recipient self-assessment towards a mixed-
methods approach that incorporates quantitative, goal-oriented evaluation to
inform funding decisions. Drawing on the development of this framework, it
examines its initial design, the practical and structural challenges
encountered, and the responses adopted. The work identifies three central
challenges: evaluation indicators must reflect heterogeneous project goals and
contexts; quantitative assessment relies on fragmented and fragile data
infrastructure; and observed changes before, during, and after investment do not
establish causal attribution without additional research designs. The paper
argues that evaluating investment in open source software is not only a
methodological task but also a governance practice, dependent on sustainable
open research infrastructure, community knowledge, and ongoing methodological
refinement.
