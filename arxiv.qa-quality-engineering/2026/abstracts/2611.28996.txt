Title: "What Should I Do?" Guidelines for Responsibly Integrating AI in Software Engineering
Authors: Rudrajit Choudhuri, Fatima A. Moussaoui, Carmen Badea, Christian Bird, Jenna L. Butler, Brian Houck, Margaret Burnett, Anita Sarma
ID: 2611.28996
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28996

Abstract:
Speculations abound about the "right" ways for developers to integrate AI into
their software engineering (SE) work, but the evidence that exists is sparse and
contradictory. Yet, developers are held responsible for the products they ship
with (or without) AI's help, so they need to integrate AI in responsible ways.
To address this gap, we conducted a two-phase, large-scale investigation with
1,118 developers at Microsoft, GitHub, and Pfizer. In Phase 1, we surveyed 860
developers at Microsoft on where they sought and resisted AI support in their
daily work, and from their accounts, synthesized 35 developer-facing guidelines:
29 across five categories of SE work and 6 cross-cutting ones. A year later, as
AI capabilities and developers' experiences co-evolved, we validated them in
Phase 2 with 258 developers in technology (Microsoft, GitHub) and
biopharmaceutical (Pfizer) domains. Developers in our study found the guidelines
clearly worded (92%) and relevant to their work (91%), and most wanted to adopt
them in their own work (89%). We also report how developers described
operationalizing these guidelines in practice: using AI to refine their craft
and delegate verifiable work, while keeping judgment and accountability for
themselves. For relationship-centric work, endorsement varied with developers'
domain and their SE and AI experience. Overall, we offer concrete advice from
in-the-trenches developers to other in-the-trenches developers, and a shared
vocabulary that engineering teams can adopt to integrate AI well in their work.
