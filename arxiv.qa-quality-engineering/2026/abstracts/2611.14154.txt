Title: Uneven Self-Reported AI Use: An Embedded Case Study Across Eleven Teams in a Software Organization
Authors: Daniele Veri'
ID: 2611.14154
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14154

Abstract:
This paper presents a descriptive, embedded single-case study examining self-
reported AI use within a software organization. The study draws on 98 structured
interview records collected across 11 teams, using ten standardized team-level
instruments (n = 65) and one instrument with distinct response anchors (n = 33).
Among respondents assessed using standardized instruments, 36 of 65 (55.4%)
reported AI-use frequency scores of 4 or 5 on a five-point scale. Team-level
proportions varied from 20.0% (1/5) to 71.4% (5/7). A record-level cross-
tabulation revealed limited awareness or understanding of organizational AI
policies across both frequency groups: 86.1% (31/36) of respondents scoring 4-5
and 96.6% (28/29) of those scoring 0-3 reported either no policy awareness or
uncertainty about policy details. In the group assessed using the distinct
instrument, 31 of 33 respondents (93.9%) scored 4 or 5. Although the pooled
proportion reached 68.4% (67/98), differences in response anchors limit direct
comparability, making the standardized-instrument results the primary analytical
reference. Interviewer-recorded observations provide qualitative context on
reported practices and constraints but do not support estimates of their
prevalence or causal explanations for differences in AI-use frequency. The
findings characterize AI-use patterns and policy awareness within the
interviewed sample, without supporting inferences about organization-wide
adoption, policy compliance, or the effectiveness of AI use. The study
contributes an empirically grounded organizational case and an explicit account
of the methodological boundaries of cross-team comparisons using heterogeneous
interview instruments.
