Title: Metamorphic Testing of Learned Spatial Surrogates for Transit Accessibility
Authors: Till Zacher, Markus Lienkamp
ID: 2611.32373
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32373

Abstract:
Transit-network design requires evaluating many candidates, but detailed
accessibility calculations are too slow to repeat inside an optimization loop.
We therefore train a neural surrogate to approximate them. Given a candidate bus
network and fixed information about rail service and nearby opportunities, it
predicts the calculated spatial change in accessibility. We compare its
predictions with routing-derived targets in geographically held-out regions and
test specified qualitative relations under controlled input changes. Reference
calculations on constructed networks assess the applicability of relations that
have a routing-level counterpart. Models with positive predictive skill can
still fail basic connectivity tests. One targeted intervention substantially
increases pass rates for two connectivity relations while predictive skill also
improves. Average prediction error is therefore insufficient when a learned
model becomes an optimization objective. Predictive agreement and controlled
behavioral tests should be reported separately.
