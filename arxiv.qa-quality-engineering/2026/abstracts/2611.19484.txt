Title: Leveraging Dual-Process Theory for Multimodal Multiparty Turn-Taking Prediction
Authors: Rutherford A. Patamia, Ming Liu, Wei Luo, Favour Ekong, Rehan Mohammed, Gang Liu, Akan Cosgun
ID: 2611.19484
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19484

Abstract:
In multiparty conversations, a pause does not always mean a speaker has
finished, and another voice does not necessarily indicate a change in who holds
the conversational floor. Reliable turn-taking therefore requires recognising
not only whether a speaker is ready to yield the floor, but also when a possible
transition deserves attention. We propose a dual-process-inspired framework that
separates continuous candidate discovery from selective verification. A fast,
recall-oriented stage identifies plausible transition points. In contrast, a
deliberative stage combines acoustic, linguistic, speaker-related, and predicted
future-activity evidence, together with multimodal cues where available, to
reject false candidates and refine transition timing. We evaluate the framework
through three distinct tasks: SHIFT/HOLD classification at supplied decision
points, verification of automatically generated candidates, and continuous
discovery and localisation of floor transfers. On VoxConverse, we derive action-
aware turn-taking events from speaker-diarization annotations to examine
unconstrained conversations with varying numbers of speakers. Multi-TPC provides
complementary evaluation of controlled triadic interactions using speech, gaze,
motion, and prosodic information. The findings reveal that identifying plausible
transitions is considerably easier than reliably confirming and localising
genuine floor transfers, while the contribution of individual evidence sources
varies across conversational settings. These results highlight the importance of
treating multiparty turn-taking as a continuous discovery-and-verification
problem rather than classification at predefined boundaries.
