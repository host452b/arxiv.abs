Title: Model Discovery for Virtual Chaos Engineering with Incomplete Tracing: An eBPF Study
Authors: Anatoly A. Krasnovsky
ID: 2611.23313
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23313

Abstract:
Model-based fault experiments can assess the consequences of component failures
before intervening in a running application. Recent work on model discovery and
graph simulation acquired operation models from application telemetry and used
them for availability prediction. Applying this approach to operational legacy
systems raises an acquisition problem: the required traces may be absent or
incomplete, while introducing them can require changes across heterogeneous
components. These systems continue to serve business operations during extended
maintenance and modernization. External observation offers a possible route to
model-based fault analysis before a tracing retrofit, but collecting telemetry
does not by itself establish that an executable operation model can be obtained.
We investigate this route using extended Berkeley Packet Filter (eBPF)
observations and the existing graph-based modelling approach. A 108-case study
varied application software development kit (SDK) tracing across absent, partial
and full conditions in three public applications. Discovered dependencies were
combined with declared operation requirements, deployment facts and scenario
assumptions; model forecasts preceded independent execution checks. With SDK
tracing disabled, the tested OpenTelemetry eBPF Instrumentation path supported
all seven operations in two applications across three repetitions. Forecasts
agreed exactly in 17 of 21 operation-repetition groups; the maximum state-
weighted discrepancy was 4.22 percentage points. Combining observed context
links supported nine additional groups under partial tracing. The results
establish an acquisition path for virtual fault experiments under the tested
observation constraints and identify limits imposed by protocols, evidence
association and operation semantics.
