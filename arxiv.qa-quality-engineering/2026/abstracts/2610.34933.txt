Title: What Will Replace Pull Requests? Rethinking Software Collaboration for AI Agents
Authors: Christoph Treude, Sebastian Baltes
ID: 2610.34933
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34933

Abstract:
The emergence of artificial intelligence (AI) coding agents has put pull
requests under pressure: agents open millions of pull requests per month, while
some open-source projects have begun to restrict or disable external
contributions. We investigate which alternatives to pull requests practitioners
are proposing or adopting and what these changes mean for empirical software
engineering research. We reviewed 50 gray-literature documents from 31
organizations or individuals published between October 2025 and October 2026,
coding the problems each named, the mechanisms it proposed or adopted, which of
five roles of pull requests (integration, review, rationale, accountability, and
external contribution) each mechanism takes over, and the evidence offered. Most
responses retained pull requests in some form, whether by adding tooling,
attaching a new artifact, or restricting access, and restriction was the
response most often reported as adopted. Several proposed alternatives moved
review and rationale into agent conversations and session records, but only Zed
reported using a replacement in practice. Only two documents outlined a
replacement for external contribution, and neither reported adoption. Pull
requests are changing in ways that pull-request datasets will not capture:
reviews conducted in agent conversations leave no pull-request comments, and
projects that disable pull requests disappear from samples of newly opened ones.
We outline a research agenda on the effectiveness of the emerging alternatives,
their consequences for open-source participation, and the adaptation of
empirical methods.
