Title: Software-Engineering-as-an-Artifact: From Engines to Systems That Build Themselves
Authors: Julian Frattini, Robert Feldt, Per Lenberg
ID: 2611.30247
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30247

Abstract:
Software engineering (SE) is evolving rapidly as generative AI and LLM-based
agents grow more capable. Yet, as a community, we risk using these new tools to
solve problems that a changing SE process may no longer pose. We consider how AI
may transform, and in part already is transforming, both the SE process and the
systems it produces. Drawing on scenario planning methods from futures studies,
we extrapolate from observed trends to a deliberately extreme scenario in which
the SE process fully materializes in artifacts (like context documentation and
executable skills). Such a process would culminate in SE engines that
autonomously produce and update systems directly from requirements and user
requests. The work of human engineers would then shift from the product to the
process: they would maintain the artifacts of these engines, while the engines
produce and evolve the software. We probe this scenario in scenario-validation
interviews with four SE industry practitioners. The interviewees found the
scenario largely plausible and desirable, and two reported that parts of
industry are already moving this way; one questioned whether tacit engineering
knowledge can ever be fully captured in artifacts. Even if this end state is
reached only in part, we argue that reasoning about it can inform the
intermediate stages. SE research should therefore take such futures into account
when choosing its research directions.
