Title: MoE Pretraining:Parallelism Layout Changes the Model, Not Quality
Authors: Esther Xin
ID: 2611.15271
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15271

Abstract:
Large Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models are trained across many machines, and the
way the work is split, the parallelism layout, often changes between runs: a job
resumes on a different GPU count, a cluster is resized, or an ablation runs on a
new setup. In exact arithmetic the layout should not matter, but in real
arithmetic it changes the order in which numbers are added, and some MoE rules
such as load balancing and expert capacity are often computed per machine. We
ask whether the layout changes the trained MoE model. We pretrain the same small
MoE language models (25M to 117M parameters, 20M FineWeb-Edu tokens) with the
same seed, data and hyperparameters under 9 data-parallel and expert-parallel
layouts, in fp32 on CPUs and fp16 on a GPU, and compare each run with the effect
of a new random seed. Across 80 runs we find: (1) The layout does not change
model quality, but it does change the model: final predictions differ about 80%
as much as with a new seed, and over 90% of routing decisions differ. Any
layout, a one-bit change to one weight, or a different CPU thread count has the
same effect. (2) The cause is the router: decisions that flip are almost exactly
those where two experts were nearly tied (AUC 0.97 to 1.00 in the first 16
steps). (3) More, smaller experts create more near-ties (2.7% with 8 experts,
23% with 64) and less reproducible routing (68% vs. 100% of decisions differ),
but outputs do not drift further. (4) A dense model of the same active size is
far more stable (0.12x the seed level in fp32, 0.58x in fp16), while the MoE
reaches about 0.8x at both precisions and twice as fast. (5) When pretraining
resumes on a new layout, timing matters: switching at 84% of pretraining barely
changes the final model, while switching at 10%, just after warm-up, changes it
substantially. We release code, run logs and data.
