Title: Properties with Holes: Meta-Property-Based Testing of Data-Intensive Computing Frameworks
Authors: Yaoxuan Wu, Ingrid Lee, Ahmad Humayun, Muhammad Ali Gulzar, Miryung Kim
ID: 2606.11132
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11132

Abstract:
Data-intensive computing frameworks such as Apache Spark and Flink keep
expanding their APIs with new data types, expressions, and operators. Their most
damaging bugs return silently wrong results rather than crashing, which tools
that detect only crashes or exceptions cannot see. Property-based testing can
catch such bugs, but only if someone writes a property with a strong oracle, and
writing properties one API at a time cannot keep pace with this growth. We
observe that many such properties are instances of a few textbook principles:
congruence of equivalent expressions, relational-algebra equivalences, and
aggregate decomposition. Each principle holds not only for an isolated operator
but also for any workload built around it. We present Holey, which introduces
meta-properties: properties with holes. A meta-property fixes how a workload is
transformed and which relation its outputs must satisfy, but leaves open the
expressions or operators being related. A developer fills the holes with a short
binding. Holey then turns the resulting concrete property into many executable
tests by generating schema-valid multi-stage workloads and input data around it.
Each meta-property has a typing rule over its holes. A binding is checked once
against this rule, and we prove that every test generated from a type-correct
binding is schema-valid. Seven meta-properties yield 207 concrete properties,
with bindings averaging under 20 lines. Holey found 17 previously unknown wrong-
result bugs in the latest releases of Spark and Flink, 9 of which need relations
that existing SQL testing oracles such as TLP and NoREC do not state, and its
meta-properties express 55 of 65 testable historical Spark correctness issues.
Its generated workloads cover 1.9x the branches covered by CometFuzz.
