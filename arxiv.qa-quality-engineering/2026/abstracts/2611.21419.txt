Title: Quality and Similarity Indicators for Video-Based Heart Rate and Respiratory Rate Fusion: A Controlled Evaluation
Authors: Nhi Nguyen, Le Nguyen, Manuel Lage Cañellas, Miguel Bordallo López, Constantino Álvarez Casado
ID: 2611.21419
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21419

Abstract:
Camera-based heart rate (HR) and respiratory rate (RR) estimates vary in
reliability from window to window, and a common remedy is to fuse several
extraction methods with weights from reference-free signal quality indicators.
However, few studies test whether these indicators identify the most accurate
method in each window or outperform simpler fusion. We evaluate 11 quality
indicators and 8 inter-method similarity measures for fusing 7 HR and 10 RR
extractors on four public datasets (75 test subjects). Filters, indicator
directions, weighting parameters and a learned ridge baseline are selected on a
separate five-subject set before testing. Fusion is compared with equal
averaging, a constant typical-rate predictor, the best single method selected in
advance and in hindsight, and a per-window oracle with a permutation-based null,
using subject-level bootstrap confidence intervals. For HR, quality-weighted
fusion reduced mean absolute error by 10.9% [5.2, 18.4] relative to averaging
and by 15.6% [8.1, 23.1] relative to the method selected in advance, and was
statistically indistinguishable from the best method selected in hindsight.
Weighting methods by their agreement did not improve on averaging. For RR, the
indicators could not identify the more accurate estimates: sharpened quality
weighting increased error (-2.6% [-4.8, -0.8]), fused estimates were
uncorrelated with the reference (r <= 0.22), and averaging was less accurate
than the typical-rate predictor (-3.1% [-6.1, -0.4]). With a second respiratory
reference, apparent RR gains reflected estimates moving toward its typical rate.
Quality-guided fusion is worthwhile for video HR; for RR, 30-s windows improved
estimates but not fusion.
