Title: In the Driver's Seat: A Multi-Company Study on the Reality of Autonomous Driving System Testing
Authors: Qunying Song, Yuan Gao, Johannes Betz, Dietmar Pfahl, Mohammad Reza Mousavi, Federica Sarro
ID: 2607.15820
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15820

Abstract:
Autonomous driving systems (ADS) are increasingly deployed in complex real-world
environments, creating strong demands for rigorous testing to establish their
functionality and safety. However, ADS testing remains difficult because
practitioners must determine not only what and how to test, but also whether the
resulting evidence is sufficient to support progression and release decisions.
To understand how this is handled in practice, we conducted semi-structured
interviews with experts involved in ADS development and testing across nine
companies in six countries. Through thematic analysis, we examined how
practitioners organize ADS testing to produce and assess evidence, and what
limits confidence in that evidence. Our findings show that industrial ADS
testing can be understood as an evidence-building process involving three
recurring activities: planning what evidence is needed, producing and evaluating
evidence across complementary testing environments, and deciding when testing is
sufficient. We further identified recurring limitations concerning scenario
adequacy, simulation and deployment fidelity, test execution and observability,
and assurance and decision sufficiency. Practitioners reported several
approaches for addressing these limitations, although important gaps remain.
Based on these findings, we propose an evidence-centered closed-loop testing
framework that connects testing intent, scenario selection, evidence production,
acceptance decisions, and operational feedback. The study provides an industry-
grounded account of how ADS testing is organized, where confidence in current
testing breaks down, and how testing processes can be structured more
systematically.
