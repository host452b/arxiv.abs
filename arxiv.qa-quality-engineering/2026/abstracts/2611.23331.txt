Title: What Should an LLM Know? Knowledge-Guided Warm Starts for Software Optimization
Authors: Srinath Srinivasan, Tim Menzies
ID: 2611.23331
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23331

Abstract:
Large language models (LLMs) process gigabytes in minutes; human experts work
far more slowly. Prior studies that explored combinations of human/AI optimizers
ignore this gap. Those studies asked systems or humans to label too many
examples (e.g., SynthCore needs over four dozen per trial). Here, we test human
and AI knowledge under a budget where evaluation is practical (a few dozen
labels or fewer). We compare five baselines (random search, RRP, EZR, few-shot
prompting, and a budget-matched SynthCore variant) against three ways to give an
LLM domain knowledge before it proposes its first configurations: (1) AMP lists
the domain's rules, then checks each candidate against them; (2) HKMA adds
scouted data or documentation to the prompt; and (3) H-DKP has human experts
correct the LLM's rules. We find that domain knowledge improves the "start-up"
phase of LLM optimization, but only when that knowledge is checked, by the LLM
itself or by a human expert. Across 49 datasets, unchecked knowledge did no
better than few-shot prompting or EZR. On the other hand, AMP's self-checking
variant (AMP-4) reached the best statistical rank in 44 of 49 datasets (budget-
matched SynthCore: 23 of 49), and H-DKP did so in 7 of 8 expert studies. These
checks cost little: half a cent of LLM calls per trial, or 6 to 10 expert
contacts. Hence we say LLMs may not need more data, but rather their knowledge
needs checking (and checking is cheap).
