Title: Mutation Testing for Multi-Agent Workflows
Authors: Nafiseh Kahani, Mojtaba Bagherzadeh
ID: 2611.09895
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09895

Abstract:
Multi-agent large language model (LLM) systems are defined by explicit
workflows, agents, tools, tool-access rules, and delegation paths. Most
evaluations, however, report end-to-end task success rather than whether a test
suite can detect faults in this coordination structure, such as a removed
delegation, a silently removed restriction, a misrouted tool edge, or a faulty
tool implementation. We adapt mutation analysis to this setting, fourteen
operators in four families (delegation, allowed-tool, restriction,
agent/instruction) perturb a normalized workflow manifest, and three kill
criteria score whether a suite catches the change, namely K-Struct on the
observed coordination, KOutcome on the task verdict or step sequence, and
K-Safety on a forbidden tool call. Every criterion is evaluated by executing
each suite against the original and the mutant through the OpenAI Agents SDK,
with k=3 repeats and a majority vote. On ten OpenAI Agents SDK-derived workflows
(49 reachable agents, 47 tools, 403 obligations), the evaluation executes 29,601
agent runs over a pool of 1,084 mutants. We use T-Struct only as a full-
obligation reference point (MSstrict=1.000 under all three criteria) and measure
how much the baseline suites miss, the bestperforming one (T-Dev*) reaches 0.717
(K-Struct) and only 0.559 (K-Safety). Restriction-removal (RES-REM) is the least
detected fault for the baselines (0.19 to 0.28 vs 1.000). Executing the suites
also allows the obligation-preserving operators (TOOL-FAULT, AG-SWAP, AG-
PROMPTDROP) to be evaluated. A manifest-only oracle would not witness these
changes. In this setting, tasksuccess- style suites catch only about half of
permission violations.
