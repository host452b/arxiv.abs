Title: Same Evidence, Different Verdicts: Delivery of CT Images and DICOM Metadata Changes VLM Quality-Control Decisions
Authors: Nicolas Avelar
ID: 2611.18790
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18790

Abstract:
Vision-language models (VLMs) are being proposed as automated checks on clinical
imaging inputs, where a scan and its DICOM metadata can disagree; a truncated CT
series wrongly admitted to volumetric follow-up, such as lung-nodule growth
assessment in screening, biases the measurement. A natural safeguard is to let
the model judge the image before it sees the metadata. We find this backfires.
On 60 NLST chest CT pairs with coverage defects whose ground truth is computed
from scan geometry, a false header that Claude Haiku 5.5 resisted when shown
with the image (2% of its detections overruled) overturned 91% of them when it
arrived after the model's answer (Nemotron-3.5-VL 43% to 100%). With the order
reversed, the image overturned 98-100% of the header's errors (Claude models):
whichever source came second won. This is not generic sycophancy: a neutral
"please confirm" reversed 0 of 1,072 correct refusals, and the effect persisted
when the header arrived as a tool result, as in agentic pipelines. One warning
sentence cut sequential reversal to at most 9%, and the pattern held on 24 real
mismatched pairs. Contributions: (i) evidence that VLM input-check decisions on
identical evidence depend on delivery; (ii) a testbed with exact geometric
ground truth; (iii) a validation protocol for clinical VLM input checks. Code,
prompts and all call logs: https://github.com/nav92061/ct-vlm-delivery-order
