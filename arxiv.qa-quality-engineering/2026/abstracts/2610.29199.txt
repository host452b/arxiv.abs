Title: Motivation and Contribution in Open Source Software: A Meta-Analytic Investigation
Authors: Parisa Omidmand, Saeed Nosratabadi
ID: 2610.29199
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29199

Abstract:
Research on why people contribute to open source software (OSS) and related open
collaboration platforms spans more than two decades, but its findings conflict,
and studies often treat the work people contribute and their intention to keep
contributing as interchangeable. Drawing on self-determination theory and
motivation crowding theory, we meta-analyze 41 studies. The main analysis pools
178 correlations of eight antecedents with contribution effort and continuance
intention from 29 independent samples (N = 9,632). Seven antecedents relate
positively to effort, all eight relate positively to intention, and each relates
at least as strongly to intention as to effort. Contrary to a common assumption,
intrinsic motivation is not more strongly related to effort than extrinsic
motivation, but its gap between intention and effort is larger. Reputation
relates to effort mainly on platforms with a reputation system, and we find no
sign that monetary rewards crowd out enjoyment. The findings inform how
platforms can turn contributors' intentions into work.
