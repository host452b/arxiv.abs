Title: Fault Injection Attacks on Q-Learning: Analysis, Vulnerabilities, and Countermeasures
Authors: Tom Slooff, Anthony Etim, Jiaqi Yu, Francesco Regazzoni, Jakub Szefer
ID: 2611.16151
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16151

Abstract:
Hardware fault injection attacks threaten machine learning systems, yet their
impact on reinforcement learning (RL) has received little attention despite the
use of RL in safety-critical applications. This paper presents a security
analysis of Q-Learning implementations under voltage and clock glitching attacks
on a ChipWhisperer platform, evaluating five variants: Standard, Robust,
Resilient, Linear, and Deep Q-Learning. In standard Q-Table implementations,
faults corrupt reward computation, action selection, and Q-value updates during
learning, and change the selected action during inference. Robust Q-Learning
does not prevent these attacks: NaN and infinite values injected during learning
persist in the Q-table, and its greedy policy uses the same code as standard
Q-Learning. This paper also evaluates a Resilient Q-Learning variant that
applies triple modular redundancy with majority voting. It corrects corruption
of a single Q-table copy, but inputs shared by the copies, such as the reward,
and the voting code itself remain outside its protection. The analysis extends
to Linear and Deep Q-Learning, where faults corrupt stored weights during
learning and change actions during inference. Moving beyond benchmark
environments, this work evaluates an online traffic light controller in a
microscopic traffic simulator, where a single injected infinite value degrades
accumulated vehicle waiting time by 148x while corrupting under 4% of the
Q-table, and where triple modular redundancy corrected the faults injected in
our tests, although glitching could still halt the device. Altogether, this work
provides a basis for understanding and securing RL deployments against fault
injection attacks.
