Title: EAST: Embodied AI Safety Testing via Systematic Fault Injection in Digital Twins
Authors: Yuhao Tian, Tao Li, Ling Zhou, Dave Towey, Zhaoxin Fan, Wenjun Wu, Rubing Huang, Tsong Yueh Chen
ID: 2610.34358
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34358

Abstract:
Task success is not a sufficient measure of safety for embodied artificial
intelligence (AI), because robots may complete objectives while violating safety
requirements. Existing evaluations often emphasize performance and robustness,
but lack a systematic route from documented safety hazards to executable,
verifiable tests. We present EAST (Embodied AI Safety Testing), a digital-twin
framework that converts literature-grounded safety evidence into fault-injection
tests across perception, instructions, environments, actions, and timing. EAST
combines an expert-reviewed safety-fault taxonomy and eight executable operators
with applicability and validity constraints. Its three-stage workflow generates
test specifications, verifies fault realization during simulated execution, and
independently assesses safety challenges, violations, task outcomes, and
behavioral signatures using simulator-state evidence. The framework preserves
execution traces and replayable records, supporting attribution and
reproducibility across experiments. We evaluate EAST on six RoboTwin
manipulation tasks and two policy architectures, ACT and RDT, against
alternative testing strategies under matched budgets. Across five comparative
campaigns, EAST targets all 74 admissible task-policy-operator combinations and
obtains valid evidence for 63, while achieving safety-challenge exposure rates
close to Random-Legal and Rule-Based. Moreover, 82 valid EAST executions
complete their tasks despite safety violations, revealing risks concealed by
success-oriented evaluation. These findings support systematic, validity-aware,
reproducible safety testing beyond task-completion metrics.
