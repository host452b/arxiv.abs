Title: Exteroceptive Sensor Faults in Autonomous Vehicles: A Cross-Modal Taxonomy and Fault Injection Toolkit
Authors: Davide Possenti, Daniele Cianca, Stefano Arrigoni, Francesco Braghini
ID: 2611.31667
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31667

Abstract:
Autonomous vehicles rely on cameras, LiDAR, and radar to perceive their
surroundings, yet these exteroceptive sensors can be degraded by hardware aging,
environmental interference, miscalibration, embedded software errors, and
combinations of these causes, with direct consequences for perception quality
and safety. Existing surveys and benchmarks mostly address individual sensors,
isolated conditions, or vehicle-level consequences, and no common vocabulary
relates fault origins across the three modalities. This paper reviews the
literature on exteroceptive sensor faults and proposes a cross-modal, origin-
driven taxonomy that organizes them into five categories, namely hardware,
environmental, calibration, software, and synergistic faults, mapping each entry
to its physical or logical mechanism, observable manifestation, and affected
subsystem, and relating the categories to the safety analyses of ISO 26262 and
ISO 21448. To make the taxonomy operational, we release an open-source fault
injection toolkit with 94 symptom-level routines (41 camera, 27 LiDAR, and 26
radar), four graded severity levels, reproducible burst scheduling, and per-
frame labels recording the fault category, affected subsystem, and severity.
Examples on the Boreas dataset illustrate how each category alters camera
images, LiDAR point clouds, and radar scans. Together, the taxonomy and the
toolkit provide a shared reference and a controlled testbed for studying sensor
reliability, developing and benchmarking fault detection and diagnosis methods,
and building fault-annotated datasets for the safety validation of perception
systems. The toolkit is publicly available at
https://github.com/davidepossenti/AV-SensorFault-Toolkit.
