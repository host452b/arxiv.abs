Title: Toward Evidence-Based Agentic Software Engineering
Authors: Sebastian Baltes, Christoph Treude
ID: 2610.35348
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35348

Abstract:
Task success rates and execution costs of agentic coding tools depend on both
model and harness. The harness manages context, provides tools, and runs the
agent loop. In empirical software engineering (SE) studies, comparisons that
change model and harness together cannot isolate their effects. We qualitatively
annotated 325 experiments reported in 146 peer-reviewed full papers published
between 2025 and 2026. We examined the reported configurations and whether
comparisons isolated harness effects, including harness configuration and
version, model, tasks, and user interaction. The exact version of every harness
used was reported for only 15 experiments. For five, the paper reported when the
experiments were run, and for 31, authors shared execution traces for every
session. Of the 226 experiments comparing harnesses, 35 never varied the harness
in isolation. Based on this analysis, we derive requirements for a reference
harness that the SE community can use for experiments. We call on the community
to report all degrees of freedom of experiments with agentic coding tools and on
SE venues to pilot dedicated tracks or special issues around a specified
reference harness release. In these tracks, papers would hold the harness
version fixed and vary features through separately versioned extensions to
isolate feature effects and test their interactions.
