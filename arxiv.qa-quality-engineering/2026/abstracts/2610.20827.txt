Title: Performance Characterization of Pulsar's Software-defined Watermark Verifier
Authors: Jason Anderson, Kaz Gunning
ID: 2610.20827
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20827

Abstract:
Pulsar provides the world's most secure PNT service by incorporating a
Combinatorial Watermark into its ranging service. Under conservative signal
conditions, Pulsar's Combinatorial Watermark, where 21 ranging code chips are
inverted among the 1023, provides 32-bit authentication security each second. In
this work, we present our software-defined radio (SDR) implementation of the
Pulsar watermark processor. We discuss various threat models, multiple receiver
architectures, implications to the receiver, and results from using Pulsar's SDR
processing watermarked signals daily from the Pulsar-0 mission. Our results
demonstrate the feasible implementation of Pulsar's watermark verification
process in commercial GNSS receivers. This is an important milestone in making
Xona's Pulsar service available to customers later this year after deployment of
our first four production satellites.
