Title: Controlled Radio Fault Injection and Telemetry Evaluation for Open 5G RANs
Authors: Babak Azkaei, Maxim Bîtiu, Georgios Exarchakos, Kishor Chandra Joshi
ID: 2610.31598
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31598

Abstract:
Radio access network (RAN) telemetry reveals radio conditions, protocol
behavior, and their consequences for applications. Evaluating its usefulness
requires controlled impairments and observations of the same execution. We
present C and Python channel brokers and a fault injection workflow for software
5G testbeds connecting an OCUDU base station and a software UE through ZeroMQ.
The brokers apply scheduled, directional transformations to I/Q samples and
restore the link, while the workflow jointly records applied faults, native and
programmable (codelet) telemetry, and application delay. We demonstrate this
capability through 45 comparative trials of brief uplink interruptions and
stepped additive noise. These use cases distinguish recognizing a radio change
from measuring its service consequence: native missing-feedback events
retrospectively identify interruptions, whereas noise changes decoding-error
measurements without producing those events. Median on-time round-trip times
remain near 30 ms, but the prespecified comparisons show mean increases in on-
time 95th-percentile delay of 354 and 604 ms for one and five interruptions, and
113 ms for noise. Adding receive-side radio link control (RLC) codelet summaries
did not improve interruption classification on held-out trials. The framework
provides a reusable way to evaluate measurement choices under known radio
faults, before using them in monitoring or learning systems.
