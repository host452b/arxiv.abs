Title: Disposable Implementations: What Is Software Engineering For?
Authors: Christoph Treude
ID: 2611.04383
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04383

Abstract:
Existing implementations are usually maintained for most of their life and
replaced only as an exception. Coding agents may change that. If agents can
produce new implementations from requirements, tests, architectural constraints,
and operational records that survive replacement, changing a system may no
longer require modifying its existing codebase. We call this process
regeneration. An implementation is disposable when regeneration becomes a
routine alternative to maintenance. Regenerating and verifying a clean
implementation from current requirements and tests may be cheaper than having
agents navigate (and potentially compound) existing technical debt, and could
eliminate much of that debt. Yet existing code may contain knowledge absent from
the preserved artifacts, including safeguards introduced after failures and
assumptions that were never documented. A regenerated implementation may pass
the available tests and still lose behavior that matters in production. This
leaves open how to establish continuity across replacements and whether
regeneration reduces maintenance effort or shifts it to the artifacts and
processes that support it. We examine these questions through an exploratory
review of practitioner literature. Published examples show that agents can
replace existing implementations, but do not establish that repeated
regeneration is safe or economical. If implementations become disposable, what
should software engineering preserve?
