Title: Breaking the Myth of Four-Tier Stacked Transistors for Logic: an Investigation from Process to Device and Block-Level PPA
Authors: Jianxiang Jin, Haoran Lu, Yihan Luo, Yanbang Chu, Yibo Zhang, Jingru Jiang, Yuji Cheng, Wanyue Peng, Xun Jiang, Feiyu Teng et al.
ID: 2611.23806
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23806

Abstract:
For the first time, this work evaluated whether 4-tier stacked transistors can
translate the straightforward density gains into realistic chip-level scaling
benefits. A practical dual-sided 4-tier transistor-stacking CFFET (Flipped CFET-
to-CFET) process was proposed, on which a DTCO framework spanning standard-cell
design, local interconnects, RO analysis, and physical implementation was built
under 2A node design rules. The CFFET was also benchmarked with its 2-tier
transistor-stacking counterparts FFET. Among CFFET configurations, the best-
performing Symmetric Power still lags FFET by 11.2% in RO frequency due to the
complex MOL parasitics. At the block level, CFFET reduces the Ibex RISC-V core
area by only 12.8% (far below the 40% nominal cell-height reduction), while
running 10.9% slower at iso-power. We further introduced a novel Tetris-like
cell stitching to minimize the area overhead, which allows non-rectangular cell
design and recovers 7.4% area in a 1-bit ALU design. An optimized NanoStack
CFFET was finally proposed, inspired by previous work. This work indicates that
straightforward vertical 4-tier stacking proves insufficient to guarantee enough
PPA benefits, though the resulting design insights may guide future advanced
architectures.
