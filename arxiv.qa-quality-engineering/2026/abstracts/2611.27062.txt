Title: Distribution-Robust Halfspace Testing
Authors: Xi Chen, Renato Ferreira Pinto, Nathaniel Harms, Rocco A. Servedio
ID: 2611.27062
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27062

Abstract:
Standard property testing algorithms are typically designed either for a
specific input distribution (distribution-specific algorithms) or for an
arbitrary unknown distribution (distribution-free algorithms). Halfspaces are an
important example where distribution-specific testers can be extremely
efficient, while distribution-free testers are expensive and essentially require
learning. We introduce an intermediate type of algorithm, a distribution-robust
halfspace tester, which must provide the same guarantees as a distribution-
specific tester when the input distribution is the one specified, but must also
provide a trustworthy answer under arbitrary distributions. This is achieved by
allowing the tester to report that its input distribution is not what it
expected (inspired by the recent testable learning framework of Rubinfeld and
Vasilyan). We prove that distribution-robust testing of halfspaces under the
standard Gaussian requires only $O(n^{2/3})$ queries, compared to the known
$\widetilde Ω(n)$ required for distribution-free testing. We also prove that
sample-based distribution-robust testing of halfspaces is harder than sample-
based testing under the standard Gaussian, requiring at least $Ω(n^{2/3})$
samples compared to the known $\widetilde Θ(\sqrt n)$ bound for the latter. In
fact, we show that the latter $\widetilde Θ(\sqrt n)$ bound becomes $Ω(n^{2/3})$
when the covariance matrix is only mildly perturbed.
