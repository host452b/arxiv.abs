Title: The Customer Is the Builder: How AI Coding Agents Are Rewriting the Economics of Software
Authors: Ripon Chandra Malo, Zimeng Yu, Tong Qiu
ID: 2610.30307
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30307

Abstract:
AI coding agents could change whether organizations buy software or build the
functionality they need. We focus on organizations and professional buyers whose
domain knowledge can help them specify and evaluate tools for their work. We
define the software substitution frontier as the functions customers could build
and maintain at an acceptable standard for less than buying them. Using public
data, we compare growing capacity to build with indicators of market change.
Since 2023, the length of benchmark tasks that leading agents can complete at 50
percent success, measured in human working time, has doubled about every 129
days. A broad search identified 575 potential open-source alternatives to
selected commercial products, although some functional matches still require
independent validation. The Craft suite produced 12 application repositories
over 12.0 days of public development, but these remain early versions with
unverified completeness and reliability. Market evidence is mixed: purchased
software gained US investment share and tracked commercial installations grew.
Yet 32 percent of respondents to an organizational survey reported forgoing at
least one software purchase because their organization could build the
functionality with coding agents. These self-reports do not establish the
spending displaced or the reliability of the resulting tools. The gap motivates
a warning: customers' capacity to build could affect individual buying decisions
before a broad purchasing shift becomes visible. We examine this possibility
using benchmark projections and observable market tests. The projections are an
extrapolation, not a forecast of customer switching.
