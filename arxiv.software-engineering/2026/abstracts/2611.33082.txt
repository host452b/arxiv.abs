Title: Who decides the norms in TCS? Analysing the composition of the STOC 2027 committees and of the Simons Institute "AI+TCS" working group
Authors: Guillaume Munch-Maccagnoni
ID: 2611.33082
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33082

Abstract:
A working group organised by the Simons Institute released a report last
September containing recommendations aiming to increase the usage of LLMs in
theoretical computer science (TCS), including introducing them for communication
and evaluation. Most of these recommendations were enacted by the STOC 2027
programme committee and the ACM SIGACT executive committee (as the steering
committee of STOC). The composition of the Simons Institute working group is
analysed along two axes: country of affiliation, and ties with industry (i.e.
affiliated with or funded by). The results show an overwhelming representation
of researchers from US institutions and private companies, and a large over-
representation of researchers with ties to AI/Big Tech companies (in the present
or the recent past). The composition of the STOC 2027 and 2026 programme
committees is analysed in terms of country of affiliation. The results show a
large over-representation in 2027 of researchers from US institutions/companies,
and large under-representation of researchers from EU and Asian institutions, as
compared to 2026. The composition of the ACM SIGACT extended executive committee
is analysed along the same two axes as the Simons Institute working group:
country and industrial ties. The results show the same overwhelming
representation of researchers from US institutions/companies, and a large
representation of researchers with ties to AI/Big Tech companies.
