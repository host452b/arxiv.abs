Title: The Productivity-Reliability Paradox: Specification-Driven Governance for AI-Augmented Software Development
Authors: Sabry E. Farrag
ID: 2605.01160
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01160

Abstract:
Since 2022, AI-powered coding assistants have produced contradictory evidence:
controlled studies report 20-56% productivity gains on well-scoped tasks, while
the most rigorous RCT documents a 19% slowdown for experienced developers, and
telemetry across 10,000+ developers shows 98% more pull requests but 91% longer
review times with flat delivery metrics. This paper argues these findings
constitute the Productivity-Reliability Paradox (PRP): a systematic phenomenon
emerging from non-deterministic code generators and insufficient specification
discipline. Through a multivocal literature review of 67 sources (2022-2026),
this paper: (1) formally defines the PRP with three moderating variables (task
abstraction, codebase maturity, developer experience) and two amplifying
mechanisms (code review bottleneck, context window constraint); (2) proposes the
AI-Augmented Methodology Taxonomy (AAMT), classifying six methodologies under
three AI integration tiers; (3) introduces the Specification Governance Model
(SGM), grounded in Transaction Cost Economics, with a practical governance
decision guide; and (4) evaluates Spec Kit and TDAD as SGM instantiations via a
four-month pilot study. Specification discipline, not model capability, is the
binding constraint on AI-assisted software dependability.
