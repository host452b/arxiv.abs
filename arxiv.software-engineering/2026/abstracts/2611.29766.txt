Title: CivicBench: Robust Detection of AI-Generated Political Video
Authors: Mert Onur Cakiroglu, Elham Buxton, Mehmet Dalkilic, Hasan Kurban
ID: 2611.29766
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29766

Abstract:
AI-generated video of politicians can pass for real footage, and once such fakes
are common, real footage can be dismissed as fake. Platforms address both
problems with detectors, but whoever uploads a clip controls its pixels.
Existing datasets of political video are small, show real politicians, and test
no attacks. We introduce CivicBench, with 10,800 generated clips of invented
politicians from eight open generators and 2,632 public-domain or openly
licensed real clips. Each clip can be attacked, to hide a fake or to frame real
footage. Published detectors trained on a general benchmark reach at most .781
AUC on CivicBench. Adversarial changes of 1 gray level per pixel hide nearly
every generated clip from the detectors not built to resist attacks. An attack's
reach is how far it can move a clip's score. A clip keeps its label when its
margin from the threshold exceeds this reach, and we find that the reach grows
almost linearly with the budget. On this basis, we propose Tau-Anchor, which
fine-tunes an encoder pretrained on attacked images. It attacks real and
generated clips during training and adds a margin term at its deployed
threshold. It reaches .941 AUC on CivicBench. It also keeps .668 of generated
clips flagged at 2 gray levels, against at most .289 for published detectors.
When real clips are framed at the same 2 gray levels, Tau-Anchor's false alarms
rise only from 8% to 14%, whereas four published detectors flag nearly every
framed real clip at just 1 gray level. External political footage and two
general benchmarks show the same pattern under attack. Code and data are
available at https://github.com/KurbanIntelligenceLab/CivicBench.
