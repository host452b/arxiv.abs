Title: AI-Assisted Programming and Labor Demand
Authors: Lindsey Raymond, Eleanor Wiske Dillon
ID: 2611.31083
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31083

Abstract:
Generative AI has demonstrated human-level performance on technical work tasks,
yet evidence on how these task-level capabilities are changing labor demand
remains scarce. We study the firm-level adoption of GitHub Copilot, an AI coding
assistant, across 5,414 multinational firms accounting for 38% of the U.S.
programming workforce. To identify causal effects, we exploit a natural
experiment generated by firms' predetermined software procurement cycles. Early
AI-adopting firms hired more workers before the launch of GitHub Copilot and had
larger, more technical, and older workforces. After one year, GitHub Copilot
adoption reduces quarterly technical hiring by 29% among review-induced
adopters, with no effect on separations, overall technical headcount, or vacancy
duration. The hiring slowdown is concentrated in the subset of junior technical
roles that involve the highest share of coding work. Overall, AI coding
assistance causes firms to shift demand towards more experienced workers and
roles that pair some coding with broader design responsibilities.
