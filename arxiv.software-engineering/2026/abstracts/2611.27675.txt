Title: From 6G Capabilities to Software-Business Value: A Multivocal Review of Characteristics, Opportunities, Challenges
Authors: Muhammad Zohaib, Azeem Akbar, Sami Hyrynsalmi, Muhammad Hamza
ID: 2611.27675
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27675

Abstract:
Sixth-generation (6G) technology is expected to enable intelligent, autonomous,
and software-defined ecosystems, creating new ways for software firms to
generate value. However, evidence on the characteristics, opportunities, and
challenges of 6G software businesses remains fragmented across academic and
practitioner literature. We conducted a multivocal literature review of 133
sources (102 peer-reviewed studies and 31 grey literature sources) to examine
this emerging landscape from the perspective of software-producing
organizations. Thematic analysis identified 18 characteristics grouped into five
themes, 32 business opportunities across eight themes, and 19 challenges and
constraints across six themes. Three independent raters assessed the reliability
of factor-to-theme classification, with Fleiss' kappa indicating substantial to
almost perfect agreement (0.744 to 0.832). Findings suggest that value creation
depends not only on technological capabilities but also on translating them into
viable services and business models while addressing integration complexity,
organizational and economic barriers, regulatory uncertainty, and security and
sustainability concerns. The synthesis reveals that characteristics,
opportunities, and challenges are interdependent dimensions of 6G software
businesses. By integrating academic and practitioner evidence, this study
provides a traceable foundation for future empirical research and strategic
decision-making as 6G technologies, standards, and markets evolve.
