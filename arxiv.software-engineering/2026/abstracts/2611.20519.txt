Title: AI Tool Adoption and Loyalty among Product, Industrial, and UX/UI Designers across the Double Diamond
Authors: Sepideh Tajarmakan, Khashayar Hojjati Emami
ID: 2611.20519
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20519

Abstract:
Designers share many AI tools, yet their roles produce different work. This
survey compared 337 Product, Industrial and UX/UI Designers from 40 countries
and regions across the four phases of the Double Diamond. It measured AI
adoption, breadth and frequency, tool function and the number of tools named per
phase. A tool was loyal when a role picked it in all four phases. Industrial
Designers adopted AI less often (73.9% against 88.2% and 87.9%), a gap that went
with physical projects once project type and country were held constant. Of 60
AI tools, 25 were common to all three roles and carried at least 90% of each
role's picks. Tool function differed by role, most of all in Develop. A few
loyal tools carried about half of the picks, led by ChatGPT. Tool counts fell
across the phases in every role. A hurdle model found a Phase x Role interaction
for using AI in a phase (p = 0.041), which was not shown for the number of tools
(p = 0.080). Predicted adoption among UX/UI Designers fell from 87.2% in Define
to 43.9% in Develop. Support for designers needs one shared core, role-specific
tools and most attention in Develop.
