Title: Blind Watermarking of Software Repositories via Module Placement
Authors: Khadija Mohamed, Khaled Fathy, Mamdouh Farouk, Mohamed M. Darwish
ID: 2610.21031
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21031

Abstract:
Source-code watermarking supports provenance tracking by embedding a recoverable
identifier in a software artifact. Many existing approaches encode this
identifier in tokens or program syntax, exposing the watermark to
transformations that modify the source text. We present PlaceMark, a blind
watermarking system that encodes a payload in the placement of modules within a
repository. Each eligible module is identified by its filename and top-level
definition names. A secret key determines its ordered candidate destinations and
its position in the reading order; the module's location within its candidate
list determines the encoded symbol. Embedding relocates selected modules and
updates their import references. Extraction uses the distributed repository, the
key, and agreed decoding parameters, without an original copy or placement
ledger. Capacity measurements on ten Python repositories yield nominal
capacities of 249 to 2,166 bits in nine repositories, while one provides no
placement channel. In 216 trials of the evaluated text transformations, the
payload is recovered exactly. Relocation degrades recovery gradually on Django,
Airflow and Salt, while on Pylint a path-based exclusion in our implementation
lets relocation change carrier membership, and we measure it for an untargeted
adversary and for one that concentrates its budget on the public carrier set.
Changes to filenames or definition names alter the identity input and are
measured separately. Exact recovery also depends on placement feasibility. The
results characterize module placement as a carrier that can complement
watermarks embedded in source text.
