Title: Exploring Students' Insights on Creating Videos to Learn Software Design and Engineering
Authors: Pedro G. Feijóo-García, John D. Centner, Lucas G. Zangari, Chase G. Wrenn, Neha Rani, Juan S. Sánchez-Gómez, Olufisayo Omojokun
ID: 2611.23075
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23075

Abstract:
This research-to-practice study examines the use of student-created
instructional videos as an active learning strategy in a large undergraduate
Software Design and Engineering (SWE) course. Supporting engagement and
understanding in large-scale computer science (CS) courses remains a challenge,
as instruction often relies on automated activities and summative assessments
that prioritize grading over meaningful learning. This can make the learning
experience feel impersonal, leading to frustration, anxiety, and confusion,
especially when students face complex topics or new programming tools. To
address these issues, we implemented a learner-centered strategy grounded in the
principle of ``learning by teaching," where students created instructional
videos to explain course concepts in their own words. This approach allowed
students to take ownership of their learning, adapting the material to fit their
individual perspectives and reinforcing their understanding through explanation.
We report on students' reflections from a large introductory SWE course (over
700 students), analyzing their open-ended feedback on the benefits and drawbacks
of the strategy. Students highlighted increased engagement and learning, and
many appreciated the flexibility of the video-based format compared to
traditional multiple-choice exams, which they found more restrictive. However,
students also noted that the video creation process was time-consuming. This
study contributes to the growing conversation on active learning in computing
and engineering education by showcasing the potential of student-created
instructional videos to enhance student agency, motivation, and conceptual
understanding in large-scale learning environments.
