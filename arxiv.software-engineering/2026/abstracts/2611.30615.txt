Title: Bug Bounty Programs in the AI Era: How Does Platform Anti-Spam Friction Affect Vendor Profitability?
Authors: Haruki Oyama, Masato Kamba, Koshi Ota
ID: 2611.30615
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30615

Abstract:
Since 2023, bug bounty platforms have introduced participation rules, such as
proof-of-concept mandates, against a flood of AI-generated low-quality
submissions. The platforms set these rules, but vendors running programs on them
bear the cost when a vulnerability goes unreported and gets exploited. We ask
whether the rules benefit the vendors they are meant to protect. Adding to Gal-
Or, Hydari, and Telang's 2024 model a vendor triage cost per report reviewed and
a participation cost paid in effort by the researchers reporting
vulnerabilities, we locate a threshold in an effective triage-burden parameter,
which captures the vendor's processing burden per unit of aggregate search
effort, with the rules lowering profit below it and raising it above. Because
attackers never use the platform, the participation cost falls on researchers
alone and fewer take part. Bug-finding coverage falls and breach damage rises,
which costs the vendor, while its triage bill falls, which helps.
