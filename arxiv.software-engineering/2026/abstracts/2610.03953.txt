Title: Characterizing Open-Source Video Games from a Software Engineering Perspective
Authors: Henrique Mandelli Canella, Yohan Duarte, Cristiano Politowski, Vinicius Durelli, Andre Takeshi Endo
ID: 2610.03953
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03953

Abstract:
Video games hold a prominent role in the entertainment industry, which
constitutes a multibillion-dollar global market. Within this ecosystem,
independent studios, small teams, and solo developers operate with constrained
budgets while making their games available through platforms such as itch.io,
where many projects are also available as open-source. Where even if the project
is fully released, other developers can still have access to the development
process through the source code, helping other developers to develop their own
games. These open-source games also constitute a valuable corpus for empirical
software engineering research, offering transparent access to development
artifacts that are typically inaccessible in commercial projects. This study
characterizes open-source video games from a Software Engineering standpoint,
focusing on their genres, popularity, repository activity, implementation
characteristics, and testing practices. To this end, we extracted metadata from
491 video games from itch.io, published as open-source projects in GitHub. The
results indicate that open-source games predominantly fall within limited genres
(Puzzle, Action, and Platformer), that player ratings, when available, tend to
be high, bringing a perception that the community receives well those kind of
games, that repositories are largely dormant or minimally maintained, that the
majority of these projects adopt either Godot or Unity as their game engine, and
that documentation of testing-related practices is seldom included. The results
offer insights into the development of open-source video games and may
contribute to advancing software engineering practices and tools designed for
small teams and independent game developers.
