Title: Isolation Is Not Confidentiality: Security Boundaries of Geometric Access Control in RAG
Authors: Steve Nouyep, Maxime Puys, Sébastien Salva
ID: 2611.14061
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14061

Abstract:
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems increasingly rely on vector
databases containing documents governed by different access-control policies.
Recent search-preserving geometric approaches enforce such access control by
structuring the embedding space so that transformed embeddings, which we call
protected representations, remain retrievable without exposing information
across policy boundaries. We provide a unified security analysis of this
paradigm, covering policy isolation, representation confidentiality, query
forgery resistance, and policy-membership hiding. We consider both a curious
cloud service provider observing the protected representations stored in the
vector database and a curious user holding aligned pairs of original embeddings
and protected representations. Evaluating the available implementations of the
works proposing these geometric transformation mechanisms across three encoders,
we find that geometric transformations preserve retrieval utility and provide
strong policy isolation, but do not provide the other security properties. A
curious user can recover the original embeddings from a few hundred pairs and
forge policy-compatible queries at a higher cost that grows with encoder
dimension for secret per-policy transformations. Moreover, policy-dependent
geometry can leak policy membership even when unauthorized retrieval is
prevented. These results show that policy isolation, confidentiality, forgery
resistance, and membership hiding are distinct security objectives.
