Title: 3D-MoE: Complementarity-Guided Hardware-Software Co-Design for MoE Decode on 3D-Stacked NMP
Authors: Yudong Pan, Lian Liu, Shixin Zhao, Yintao He, Kaiyan Chang, Tianhua Han, Chenyang Ai, Haonan Zhu, Mengdi Wang, Yinhe Han et al.
ID: 2610.26991
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26991

Abstract:
Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) have become a mainstream way to scale Large Language
Models (LLMs), because each token activates only a small subset of experts,
reducing inference cost. However, MoE decode remains highly memory-bound and
exhibits strongly skewed, time-varying expert loads. 3D-stacked DRAM near-memory
processing (NMP) provides the bandwidth that decode demands, yet its tiled
organization makes load balancing expensive: expert parallelism (EP) preserves
locality but suffers from hotspot-induced imbalance, while more distributed
strategies weaken locality and increase NoC traffic. To address this locality-
balance conflict, we observe that domain-specialized token routing creates
offsetting expert-load fluctuations across decode steps, a property we call
expert complementarity. Using this insight, we propose 3D-MoE, a hardware--
software co-designed system for MoE decode on 3D-stacked NMP. 3D-MoE uses
offline, one-time complementarity-guided grouping and locality-aware mapping to
establish coarse-grained inter-group balance, handles residual intra-group
hotspots through a lightweight Collaborative Memory-Network (CMN) that streams
each hot expert's weight tiles once from its host DRAM channel to helper cores
over a reconfigured local broadcast path, and uses cost-aware online remapping
to remain robust to workload drift. Across MoE models, 3D-MoE achieves an
average 4.63$\times$ MoE-layer speedup over EP and improves end-to-end
throughput by 1.58$\times$--1.86$\times$ over the strongest NMP baselines, with
2.11\% additional logic-die area.
