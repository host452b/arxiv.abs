Title: Making Protocol Effects Explicit: LLM-to-Packet Simulation for Superpod Interconnect Exploration
Authors: Yu Zhu, Xinhao Yang, Zhaoshi Li, Baodong Wu, Boxun Li, Wenxue Li, Guohao Dai, Hongyang Jia, Shuwen Deng, Huazhong Yang et al.
ID: 2611.30191
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30191

Abstract:
Large language models (LLMs) have become the foundation of modern AI services.
The rapid growth of LLM sizes and context lengths has exceeded the memory
capacity of a single GPU or NPU, requiring model partitioning across devices.
However, partitioning models across devices introduces costly communication
overhead, driving the need for superpods with large-scale, high-bandwidth, low-
latency scale-up fabrics. Exploring superpod designs requires accurate
interconnect simulation, as communication latency is jointly determined by link
technologies, topologies, and scale-up protocol-stack choices. Latency-bandwidth
models estimate communication latency using a startup term and a transfer term
derived from message size and effective bandwidth. But they fail to capture
contention and protocol effects. Packet-level simulators like ns-3 model
routing, queueing, and packet timing, but require protocol-specific LLM-to-
packet implementations. This paper presents ProtoBridge, an ns-3-based LLM-to-
packet simulator for superpod interconnect exploration. To make protocol effects
explicit, ProtoBridge introduces two abstraction layers over ns-3: (1) an
operation-to-packet (OTP) layer that captures protocol-specific packet exchanges
and dependencies, and (2) a packet-execution (PEX) layer that applies shared
packet-level mechanisms to the ns-3 event system. ProtoBridge achieves average
latency error of 17.8\% on H200 NVLink4 and 10.3\% on C500 MetaXLink. To enable
faster superpod evaluation for further interconnect exploration, we derive a
surrogate model that combines OTP parameters with analytical models of PEX
mechanisms. The surrogate reduces evaluation time from 2.38$\sim$75.70s to
20.5us per operation. We use ProtoBridge to analyze two emerging superpod
designs, scale-up memory pool and co-packaged optics (CPO) interconnects,
revealing design insights for future LLM superpod architectures.
