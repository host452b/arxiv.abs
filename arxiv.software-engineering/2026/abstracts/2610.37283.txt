Title: BenchMeUp: Building Software Engineering Benchmarks from Natural-Language Specifications
Authors: Islem Bouzenia, Michael Pradel
ID: 2610.37283
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37283

Abstract:
Software-engineering research relies on benchmarks that evaluate new techniques
on a set of problem instances. Unfortunately, manually constructing benchmarks
is labor-intensive, as researchers must find suitable instances, prepare
execution environments, establish expected outcomes, and validate correctness.
This paper presents BenchMeUp, an approach for automatically creating software-
engineering benchmarks from natural-language specifications. Given a
specification defining the desired instances, data sources, and evaluation
procedures, BenchMeUp generates search rules and a construction plan, invokes
parallel LLM agents that identify candidate instances and construct executable
instances, validates the results in fresh execution environments, and packages
the instances behind a unified command-line interface. We evaluate the approach
on four tasks: program repair, issue reproduction, compiler autotuning, and
fixing static-analysis warnings. Targeting 50 instances per task (200 in total),
BenchMeUp constructs 198 instances, of which 196 (99\%) are successfully
packaged. Search and construction take 36 minutes and \$0.79 per instance, on
average. Evaluating eight existing techniques on the generated benchmarks, we
find that 189 of 196 packaged instances (96\%) support end-to-end evaluation,
allowing users to study the effectiveness of the techniques. Checking every
constructed instance against its source, the specification, and its expected
outcome, we find that 180 of 198 (91\%) meet all criteria, compared with 10 of
200 instances delivered by Codex (general-purpose coding agent). Overall, we
show that LLM-based agents can automate benchmark construction across diverse
software-engineering tasks and produce executable instances that existing
techniques can use, while validity beyond execution still needs to be checked.
