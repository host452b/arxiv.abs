Title: AdapBench: Adaptive Early-Stopping and Prioritization for Efficient Microbenchmarking in Cloud-based CI/CD
Authors: Sebastian Koch, Jonas Heisterberg, Nils Japke, Trever Schirmer, David Bermbach
ID: 2611.01255
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01255

Abstract:
Cloud-based software systems frequently release new versions, a fact further
accelerated through agentic coding. Each new release, however, brings the risk
of performance regressions which can irritate end users, increase infrastructure
costs, and increase carbon footprints. Therefore, developers strive to catch
such regressions in testing stages, usually through so-called continuous
benchmarking. The standard way of doing this is using microbenchmark suites
which, however, take notoriously long to execute. Optimizing the execution of
microbenchmark suites is, hence, an active field of research. In this paper, we
propose AdapBench, an online scheduling system for reducing the execution
duration of microbenchmark suites. AdapBench is the first approach which
dynamically makes at-runtime decisions for prioritizing the most informative
benchmarks and early-stopping each one once enough evidence has accumulated to
accept or reject a performance change hypothesis. We evaluate our prototype on
eight popular Java projects, commonly used by microbenchmark research, and four
additional Go projects. At matched 90% change detections and false-positive rate
compared to an optimistic uniform run, AdapBench saves 42% of the aggregate
execution duration on the Java projects, with negligible scheduling overhead
(below 0.001% of the total runtime). On the Go projects, AdapBench finds every
performance change with zero false positives while saving 85% of the full run.
