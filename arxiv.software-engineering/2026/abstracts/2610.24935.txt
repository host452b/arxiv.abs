Title: Theory of Developer Momentum: The Developer's Experience of Progress and the Craft of Maintaining Motion
Authors: Arty Starr, Keith Mann, Margaret-Anne Storey
ID: 2610.24935
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24935

Abstract:
This paper introduces a Theory of Developer Momentum that helps us see the
activity of developing software in motion and the skilled craft through which
progress unfolds. We define Developer Momentum as the developer's experience of
progress and forward motion toward the goal of a task. Our theory disentangles
the related constructs of flow state, momentum, and motivation, so we can more
clearly see the relationships between them and how they interweave to support
progress. In order to make progress, developers build up working context in
memory, become immersed in a cognitive puzzle, and gain clarity through motion
in uncertain terrain. The theory provides researchers with a rich experiential
lens to investigate how developers' cognitive experiences are shifting in
emerging AI workflows. Building on prior grounded theory work, we conducted in-
depth interviews with a theoretically sampled subset of 12 professional
developers, and eight feedback interviews to evaluate resonance and investigate
how the theory translates to agentic AI workflows. We followed a Constructivist
Grounded Theory approach to construct a theory grounded in empirical data. Our
theory contributes a novel paradigm for seeing the motion of developing software
at multiple scales and resolutions, seeing the living capacities that support
this motion, and expanding what becomes available for improvement, skilled
judgment, and craft.
