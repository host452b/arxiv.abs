Title: Early Prediction of Cross-Service Implementation Scope: Evidence and Limits from Multiple Microservice Projects
Authors: Zhoumaocuo, Xiaozhou Li
ID: 2610.39640
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39640

Abstract:
Microservice architectures aim to localize change, yet feature requests may
require implementation across service boundaries. We investigate how well such
downstream scope can be anticipated before implementation begins. Using issue--
pull-request--commit traceability and implementation evidence corrected through
targeted human adjudication, we reconstruct 866 repository-traceable feature
requests from 13 open-source microservice projects and map their implementations
to validated service boundaries. Cross-service changes occur in 101 requests
(11.7%) and exhibit broader implementation footprints, particularly higher code
churn, but no clear difference in lead time. Pre-implementation evidence
provides limited cross-project predictive signal: the strongest leave-one-
system-out model reaches a project-macro Macro-F1 of 0.484, but its advantage
over the majority baseline is not significant after Holm correction and is
sensitive to small evaluation folds. A local LLM offers no reliable improvement
over classical models. Downstream workload is substantially harder to estimate,
as learned models do not consistently outperform training-fold median baselines.
A simple service-mention heuristic also performs comparably to learned scope
triage. These findings show that early feature-request evidence is more useful
for lightweight cross-service scope triage than for automated scope
determination or precise workload estimation.
