Title: Agents on the Patch Path: Mining AI-Associated Security Remediation in GitHub Security Advisories
Authors: Claudio Segal, Paulo Segal, Hudson Silva Borges, Paulo Silveira, Daniel S. Menasché, Eduardo Santana de Almeida, Felipe de Sant'Anna Paixão, Joanna C. S. Santos, Anton Kocheturov, Gaurav Srivastava et al.
ID: 2611.09808
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09808

Abstract:
Autonomous coding agents are increasingly involved in software development, but
their participation in real-world security remediation remains poorly
understood. We investigate observable evidence of coding-agent involvement in
commits referenced by reviewed GitHub Security Advisories (GHSAs), characterize
the associated vulnerabilities, and examine review timing in relation to
advisory disclosure routes. We develop a local-Git mining pipeline that
identifies agent evidence through known agent accounts, explicit commit-message
attribution, repository instruction files, and agent configuration artifacts.
Linking these findings to GHSA-referenced commits yields 469 valid candidate
advisories across 189 repositories, including 448 with direct attribution and 21
supported only by repository-level evidence. Claude is the most frequently
observed tool family, followed by Copilot and Cursor. We further compare review
timing against other advisories and historical baselines using an exact route--
timing decomposition. Relative to advisories published in the same months,
candidate advisories exhibit a modestly longer mean review lag, primarily
associated with within-route timing rather than disclosure-route composition.
Compared with a historical baseline, they exhibit substantially shorter mean
review lags, with both route composition and within-route timing contributing to
the difference. These findings demonstrate that coding-agent involvement is
already observable across hundreds of security advisories and repositories,
including high- and critical-severity vulnerabilities. By distinguishing direct
attribution from repository-level adoption evidence and separating route-
composition effects from within-route timing differences, we provide an
empirical characterization of agent-associated remediation in the GHSA review
pipeline.
