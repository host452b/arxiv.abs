Title: FORGETMENOT: Auditing Memory Driven Privacy Risks in Commercial AI Agents
Authors: Ismat Jarin, Tu Le, Athina Markopoulou
ID: 2611.29804
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29804

Abstract:
Commercial AI agents increasingly incorporate long-term memory (both persistent
and chat-history) to enable more accurate and personalized responses, raises
significant privacy and policy concerns. Yet little is known about user privacy
risks associated with memory behavior, in particular, about memory storage,
disclosure, transparency and deletion control. To bridge this gap, we propose
FORGETMENOT, an auditing framework for analyzing the privacy risks of long term
memory in commercial AI agents. Using our framework, we collect around 3800
multi-turn sessions comprising around 6M tokens across five popular AI agents.
Our results show that agents store a wide range of personal attributes,
including demographics, health, and sensitive details. Furthermore, user visible
memory reveals substantially less (around 20% on average) attributes than the
agents store in practice, exposing a significant transparency gap. Our stealth
memory injection (SMI) technique further increases the amount of personal and
sensitive attribute stored in memory, raising it by 25% and 15% for Copilot and
Claude, respectively. We further observed memory deletion control is unreliable,
as all five agents exhibit non-zero post deletion leakage, including health and
self-harm related attributes. Finally, we demonstrate the feasibility of memory
disclosure to a 3rd-party email recipient as a proof-of-concept, as Gemini shows
31.9% leakage rate. Together, our findings reveal notable privacy risks in
commercial agents memory. Finally, we discuss both design implications and
regulatory recommendations to protect users memory privacy in commercial agents.
