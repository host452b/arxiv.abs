Title: Secure Deterministic Identification with Feedback and Sensing over State-Dependent Wiretap Channels
Authors: Vida Gholamian, Yaning Zhao, Christian Deppe, Holger Boche, Rafael F. Schaefer
ID: 2610.41646
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41646

Abstract:
Identification differs from classical transmission in that the receiver
determines whether a particular identity was sent rather than recovering a
message. We study secure deterministic identification over state-dependent
discrete memoryless wiretap channels with private noiseless feedback and a
sensing distortion constraint. The encoder has no local randomness and uses only
the identity and past feedback. A symbol-wise input restriction guarantees the
sensing constraint. We show that the secure deterministic identification
capacity equals its non-secure feedback counterpart when the admissible main
channel has positive ordinary transmission capacity and H(Y|Z,X=x)>0 for some
fixed admissible input x. This equality does not require positive classical
wiretap secrecy capacity. For achievability, noiseless feedback generates common
randomness for identification and enables secret-key extraction by privacy
amplification. The extracted key one-time-pad encrypts a short identification
tag, whose reliable transmission requires only a sublinear number of additional
channel uses. We also present an alternative construction in which feedback-
generated randomness replaces the local randomization of a stochastic wiretap
encoder. We further prove that secure identification capacity vanishes whenever
non-secure identification capacity is zero. We also identify a class of channels
with input-independent wiretapper observations that achieves full non-secure
identification capacity even when the private conditional feedback entropy is
zero, illustrated by a binary state-dependent wiretap channel. Thus, feedback
can support both identification and secrecy without local encoder randomization
while satisfying sensing constraints.
