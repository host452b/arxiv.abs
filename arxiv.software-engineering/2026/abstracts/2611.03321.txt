Title: uTECH Turbo-MOVES: The Intersection of Software Architectural Innovation and Sustainable Mobility Analysis
Authors: Cafer Avcı, Alireza Yazdiani, Roopak Srinivasan, Megh Khaire, Mohammad Tayarani, H. Oliver Gao
ID: 2611.03321
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03321

Abstract:
Transportation is a major source of energy consumption, greenhouse gas
emissions, and criteria air pollutants, making efficient emissions estimation
essential for environmental planning and policy analyses. The U.S. Environmental
Protection Agency's Motor Vehicle Emission Simulator (MOVES) supports detailed
emissions modeling, but its computational demands constrain large-scale scenario
analyses. This study introduces uTECH Turbo-MOVES, a software architecture that
expedites MOVES computation without modifying its Calculator modules or
input/output structure. By combining RunSpec partitioning, container isolation,
in-memory intermediate storage, and concurrent workload orchestration, it
improves execution efficiency. We evaluate six benchmark cases spanning County,
Default, and Project scales and both Emission Inventory and Emission Rates
modes. Results demonstrate a measured 5.5x and an estimated 15.3x per-run
speedup for Default-scale workloads, and an 84.8x campaign speedup for an eight-
county Emission Rates analysis. Although gains vary by workload and hardware
configuration, uTECH Turbo-MOVES enables more extensive environmental
assessments and transportation policy evaluation.
