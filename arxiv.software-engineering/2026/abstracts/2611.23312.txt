Title: AppSec Core: An Ontology for Security-by-Design Software Engineering
Authors: Pedro Farinha
ID: 2611.23312
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23312

Abstract:
When engineers or coding agents change an application, a dependency, a
configuration or a build pipeline, they need to know three things: which
security rules apply, how to meet them, and what evidence to leave. Today the
answers are spread over many standards, frameworks, laws and lists of
weaknesses, and each one uses its own words and level of detail. This paper
presents AppSec Core, a model of the security work that engineers do. It says
what must be true, which practices get there, which mechanisms do the job and
which artefacts show that it was done. It has 259 elements in ten domains. We
built it from a security-by-design manual written from practice, and then
checked it against 31 outside sources, ten of them about AI systems and agents.
Along the way we made three changes, which added five objectives and made one
domain wider. We did not need a new domain. Most objectives are backed by
several sources: the typical objective is used by six of the twenty technical
sources, and 194 of the 202 objectives, practices and mechanisms are linked to
items in the sources. For the domain of a change, the model gives back a closed
set of typed elements, and for a source, it gives back the objectives that the
source points to. We keep the model apart from the manual, the selection rules
and the server that delivers the result, and we follow one change, adding a
third-party package, through all four. When a code generator received the
requirements, the share of security tests passed on a public benchmark went from
77.4% to 93.0%. On a second benchmark, the result was close to that of the
benchmark's own "oracle" prompt, which is written with the tests in mind.
