Title: Application-Oriented Evaluation of CPU/GPU Quantum Simulators: From Foundational Circuits to Quantum Chemistry
Authors: Anurag K. S. V., Ashish Kumar Patra, Afra Navas, Ruchika Bhat, Sai Shankar P., Rahul Maitra, Manas Mukherjee, Jaiganesh G
ID: 2611.18676
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18676

Abstract:
Quantum computing simulators underpin the development and prototyping of quantum
algorithms, yet the practical choice between competing software stacks and their
CPU and GPU backends is rarely guided by application-oriented evidence. We
present a systematic, quantum hardware-agnostic evaluation of two widely used
stacks, CUDA-Q and Qiskit, selected as the simulators closest to production
execution paths, across CPU and GPU backends, and across single- and double-
precision arithmetic. The evaluation spans foundational utility circuits (GHZ-
state preparation and QFT/QFT$^\dagger$ mirror circuits) through applied quantum
chemistry (Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE) energy estimation with standard
UCCSD and a depth-reduced LCNot-UCCSD ansatz, and direct sampling on hydrogen
chains up to 32 qubits). The results yield concrete selection guidance: GPU-
accelerated double precision delivers consistent speedups, typically $2\times$
to $10\times$ for tractable application workloads and substantially larger at
the widest circuits up to $100\times$ or more, and governs the maximum simulable
system size under fixed compute memory, while CPU statevector backends and
Qiskit Aer remain competitive, and at times preferable, at low qubit counts and
wide but shallow circuits. We demonstrate the practical payoff of this guidance
by extending an in-house developed QSCI(LCNot-UCCSD)-RBM electronic-structure
workflow, previously constrained to a 16-qubit active space on a CPU, to a
32-qubit (10 electrons, 16 orbitals) active space with GPU acceleration,
completing a 20-point potential-energy-surface scan of N$_{2}$ in the cc-pVDZ
basis. This end-to-end evaluation establishes that the principled simulator
selection translates directly into expanded scientific reach and faster
algorithmic prototyping.
