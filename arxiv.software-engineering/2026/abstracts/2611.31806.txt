Title: Where Did My Tokens Go? Verifiable Token Billing for LLM Services
Authors: Jiayuan Du, Yebo Feng, Lin Zhang, Konglin Zhu
ID: 2611.31806
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31806

Abstract:
Users of large language model (LLM) services receive token bills with limited
evidence of which calls incurred their charges. In broker-mediated agent
workflows, calls arise after authorization and billing evidence spans service
domains. A bill can count one genuine receipt twice and omit another. Valid
metering evidence can also be attributed to the wrong execution. We present
\textsc{TokenProv}, which uses authenticated provider acceptances to establish
settlement coverage and verify token charges. An authenticated causal history
links runtime attempts to the signed policy, and jointly signed handoffs bind
intent, dispatch, and acceptance to the same call. Handoffs submitted
independently of the bill can reveal omitted calls. The auditor requires a one-
to-one match between observable acceptances and settled receipts. For each
acceptance, a common provider relation binds metered usage to the cache-
dependent execution of the accepted input using the registered model and serving
rules, and checks the charge under the pricing rule. To limit serving-path
overhead, providers can use attested state transitions in trusted execution
environments or defer proof generation until audit by committing to witnesses in
receipts before settlement. The auditor recomputes the bill from the verified
charges. We prove audit soundness relative to authenticated evidence. Across
five models, \textsc{TokenProv} with signed provider receipts achieves 94.4--
107.4\% of baseline throughput with 54--66\,ms of online non-LLM processing per
request. In a separate eight-call replay, deferring scoped proofs reduces
foreground protocol time from 1.20\,s to 8.65\,ms (99.28\%). The auditor rejects
all 1,600 validly signed attack queries across 100 adaptive episodes.
