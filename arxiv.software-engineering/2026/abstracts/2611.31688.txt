Title: Harvesting Apple's Orchard: A Security Analysis of On-Device CoreML Models Across the Apple Ecosystem
Authors: Justice Owusu Agyemang, Winifred Harriet Asante, Kwame Opuni-Boachie Obour Agyekum, Kwame Agyeman-Prempeh Agyekum, Francisca Adoma Acheampong, Jerry John Kponyo
ID: 2611.31688
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31688

Abstract:
Apple ships hundreds of task-specific machine-learning models inside iOS,
iPadOS, macOS, and tvOS. We present, to our knowledge, the first systematic
security analysis of this on-device surface. We harvested 1,137 CoreML artifacts
from six platform images and two device harvests, and a conversion pipeline
recovered 155 previously opaque bundles as loadable mlpackage archives. Eight
findings. (1) 368 of 1,137 artifacts expose plaintext learned parameters (281
blobs, 369.61 MiB) and 166 of 166 MIL bundles leak internal model-database
tokens. (2) Specification versions 1-9 coexist and locale variants diverge
architecturally. (3) Across 82,092 adversarial evaluations (221 model paths),
worst-case flip rates reach 100%; zero-width spaces flip 65% of the Indian-
English and 70% of the US-English SMS filter. (4) A 16-byte weight edit flips 7
of 8 probe inputs of a system model, with no runtime integrity check observed.
(5) Loading Apple's own hand-gesture model crashes libBNNS through the public
CoreML API (4/4 on production M2, also macOS 27.0); Apple ruled it a self-crash
outside the security boundary. (6) Eleven bundles ship isUpdatable=1 hooks. (7)
SMS text-classifier latency is 13.1-52.0 ms, 92.4% in the CPU-side NLP core, not
the ANE. (8) The classifiers label 8-9 of 10 phishing messages Transactional. We
filed three reports with Apple Product; all were declined as not crossing a
security boundary, and we report the findings as robustness results.
