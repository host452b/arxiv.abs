Title: Normative and neural network models of multisensory integration
Authors: Marcus Ghosh, John S. Butler
ID: 2611.32829
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32829

Abstract:
Animals continuously detect scents, sights and sounds, which their brains
combine into diverse multisensory streams. Multisensory integration accelerates
learning, increases reaction speeds and helps to resolve sensory ambiguities. In
studying this process, the field has developed a continuum of models; from
concise mathematical equations, with a handful of variables, to artificial
neural networks, with billions of parameters. In this review, we divide this
spectrum into two classes: normative models start from a hypothesis about how
multisensory signals should be integrated, while neural network models aim to
emulate how circuits could integrate them. Together, these models demonstrate
that observers can integrate cues in a statistically optimal fashion, and help
to explain experimental findings, including the diversity of multisensory neuron
response profiles and network architectures. In this review, we provide a
didactic overview of multisensory models, complete with accessible equations and
Python code, and compare their assumptions and explanatory power. We detail what
has been learned from these models. Finally, we discuss how the ideas and
insights from classical theoretical models can be reconciled with those from
artificial neural networks, and how this convergence should shape future
research.
