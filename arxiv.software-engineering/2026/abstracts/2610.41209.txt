Title: Falcons: Pushing the Speed-Ratio Limits of Lossy Compression for Parallel Scientific Applications
Authors: Bing Lu, Jinyang Liu, Xinyang Ma, Quantong Fu, Chenwei Hou, Zedong Liu, Wenjing Huang, Yida Gu, Xingchen Liu, Zheng Wei et al.
ID: 2610.41209
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41209

Abstract:
The extreme volume of scientific data has become a critical challenge for modern
exa-scale scientific computing applications, and error-bounded lossy compression
remains indispensable for scientific data management due to its high data
reduction rate and controllable data fidelity. However, to ensure sufficient
data reduction, existing compressors incur prohibitive computational overheads
in real-world parallel scientific computing applications. To overcome the
performance bottlenecks of error-bounded lossy compression, we present
\textbf{Falcons}, a broadly deployable error-bounded lossy compressor that
simultaneously achieves \textbf{high compression ratios with ultra-fast
throughput, minimizing the overhead in parallel scientific applications}.
Falcons integrates three key designs: memory-regularized interpolation with SIMD
(Single Instruction Multiple Data) vectorization, distribution-adaptive lossless
coding, and application-oriented optimizations for multiple parallelization
schemes. Across our evaluations on cutting-edge supercomputers, Falcons clearly
outperforms state-of-the-art compressors, simultaneously achieving up to 29\%
compression ratio improvement and 255\% throughput improvement. In case studies
of real-world applications, Falcons accelerates large-scale parallel data I/O
and remote transfer by up to 44.6\% and 51.5\%, and provides 1.55$\times$ end-
to-end speedup for the full WRF (Weather Research \& Forecasting) application
workflow.
