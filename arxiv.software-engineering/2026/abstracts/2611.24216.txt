Title: The Comprehension Gap in LLM-Assisted Software Development: An Experiment
Authors: Julian Oertel, Jil Klünder, Regina Hebig
ID: 2611.24216
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24216

Abstract:
When LLM tools are pushed beyond their capabilities for software development
tasks, developers must be able to step in. However, the comprehension that
developers acquire during development might be negatively impacted due to
excessive code generation. This could create a cognitive debt that needs to be
resolved before tasks can be solved efficiently again. We explore how well
developers understand code they generated by prompting in comparison to self-
written and unseen code and how they reflect on their comprehension. We conduct
a crossover experiment and interviews with 35 participants consisting of
students, academic personnel, and software developers. After completing two
programming tasks with or without LLM chat usage, respectively, participants are
tasked with finding introduced mutations in the produced and additional unseen
code. We use insights from interviews and screen recordings to supplement our
results. Regarding the experiment, 36% of participants report a better
understanding of self-written code compared to their generated code. On average,
participants solved the comprehension task 20% faster for self-written code than
for generated code and found 11% more mutations. Participants needed
significantly less time to solve programming tasks when working with the LLM.
However, participants with high reliance on the LLM chat and low task engagement
showed significantly decreased comprehension. Our qualitative results show great
variability in individual perspectives on comprehension differences and
strategies to mitigate issues. Strategies for dealing with comprehension
differences range from accepting comprehension differences to adapting their LLM
interactions or avoiding the tools altogether. Our results warrant further
investigation into cognitive debt in larger systems and developer practices to
mitigate issues.
