Title: Batch-Dynamic Coreness and Applications with Logarithmic Work via Balanced Pressures
Authors: Quinten De Man, Laxman Dhulipala, Kishen N Gowda, Zhongqi Wang
ID: 2611.24905
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24905

Abstract:
We give a parallel algorithm that maintains a one-sided
$(2+\varepsilon)$-approximation of all core numbers under batches of edge
insertions and deletions. For fixed $\varepsilon>0$, the expected amortized work
is $O(\log n)$ per edge update, and each batch has deterministic polylogarithmic
depth. This improves on the previously known $(4+\varepsilon)$-approximation and
$O(\log^2 n)$ amortized work bounds. This approximation factor approaches the
factor-two limit for polylogarithmic update and query time under the OMv
conjecture. Our algorithm is based on assigning each vertex a "pressure" and
splits each edge's unit load smoothly between its endpoints according to their
pressures. After each batch of updates, our dynamic algorithm adjusts the
pressures of vertices in parallel to restore balance between the pressures and
loads. We show that by maintaining balanced pressures we can obtain good
approximations of core numbers. Our construction also gives a
$(1+\varepsilon)$-approximation of the densest-subgraph value with logarithmic
expected amortized work and supports batch-dynamic maximal matching, clique
counting, forest decomposition, and coloring in low-arboricity graphs with
improved or matching work bounds. We give a practical implementation of our
batch-dynamic coreness algorithm and find that it is able to achieve smaller
empirical error than the state-of-the-art parallel batch-dynamic implementation,
while running faster. On six real-world graphs, it runs $32\times$ faster than
that implementation in geometric mean and $4.1\times$ faster than a state-of-
the-art heuristic baseline. On three graphs with up to 1.8 billion edges, it is
$9.2\times$ faster than the heuristic baseline.
