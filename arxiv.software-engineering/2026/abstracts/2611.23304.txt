Title: LLMs for Software Vulnerability Prediction and Prioritization: A Review
Authors: Faheem Ullah, Muhammad Moiz Khalid, Shayan Ayaz Khan, Hussain Ahmad
ID: 2611.23304
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23304

Abstract:
Large language models have moved beyond vulnerability detection into prediction,
assessment, exploitability estimation, risk assessment and prioritization, yet
the evidence for this shift has not been synthesised separately from the
detection literature. In this systematic literature review, we examine how LLMs
are used across those five task classes, how well they perform against
established baselines, and what prevents their operational adoption. Following a
prespecified protocol, we identified 197 records, removed 53 duplicates, and
excluded 103 records at title and abstract stage. We assessed 41 full texts and
finally retained 23 primary studies published between 2024 and 2026 after a six-
question quality assessment. The review contributes a five-class taxonomy
ordered by decision outcome, a method taxonomy covering prompting, tuning and
retrieval or agentic architectures, an evaluation-practice audit, a bounded
performance synthesis, and a deployment-barrier analysis. The main findings are
that (i) assessment is the largest class (9 of 23 studies), ahead of risk
assessment and prioritization (7 each); (ii) reported accuracy is highest where
the label space is tightest and is not stable across evaluation periods; (iii)
explanation usability is an audited evidence gap rather than a demonstrated
capability; and (iv) reproducibility is the weakest reported dimension. We
conclude that present systems should support analyst judgement rather than
replace it, and we specify three validation studies that would settle the open
questions.
