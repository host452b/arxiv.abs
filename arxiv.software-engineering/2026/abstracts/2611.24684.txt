Title: Hi Gemini, Who Should I Vote For? The Benefits (and Problems) of Using LLMs As Voting Advice Applications
Authors: William John Atkinson, Paul Saproza
ID: 2611.24684
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24684

Abstract:
Large language models (LLMs) can provide voters information about political
issues, party positions, and even advise on which party to vote for. However, no
previous study explores whether off-the-shelf LLMs could be used as voting
advice applications (VAAs) to improve voter-party congruence. In this article,
we address this gap by examining how Gemini 3 Flash advises a sample of 6,022
German survey respondents on which party to vote for, based on their policy
preferences and priorities alone. In brief, we find that for most respondents,
following the LLM's advice would benefit voter-party policy alignment (i.e.
congruence), with a significantly stronger remedial effect among voters with low
levels of political interest and formal education. However, our analysis also
reveals concerning biases in the LLM's recommendations. For instance, the LLM
suggested voting for the AfD at approximately 1.5 times the frequency of a
standard salience-weighted proximity model. In addition, the LLM hardly ever
advised voting for either Die Linke or the BSW. We conclude that while LLMs can
approximate the behaviour of VAAs, users should be aware that the LLM's
recommendations are still biased in ways that are extremely difficult to trace.
