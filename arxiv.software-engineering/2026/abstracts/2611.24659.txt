Title: ChainIT Transaction Truth: Stablecoins, Web3 Wallets, and Multi-Rail Commerce. A Zero-Trust Control Plane for Verified Identity, Organizational Authority, Runtime Compliance, Secure Wallet Execution, and Immutable Settlement Evidence
Authors: Eric Tacl, Matthew Koepp, Tim Hines, Russell Lessard, Jason Patrick, Jeremy Blackburn
ID: 2611.24659
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24659

Abstract:
Stablecoins can move value continuously across programmable networks, and Web3
wallets can authorize those transfers with cryptographic signatures, but neither
capability, standing alone, establishes a complete commercial transaction. A
blockchain may prove that a transaction satisfied protocol rules, and a wallet
may prove that sufficient signing authority existed; neither proves who
initiated the transaction, for which legal entity, under what current authority,
against which current compliance evidence, on what exact instruction, or with
what settlement and accounting outcome. We describe the ChainIT Transaction
Truth Architecture, a zero-trust control, execution, and evidence architecture
that places verified individual and organizational identity, the organizational
authority record and authority policy of the ChainIT Authority Protocol,
transaction-specific runtime compliance decisions, exact-digest approval, atomic
capacity reservation, and a single-use Execution Authorization Credential in
front of wallet execution, and that records settlement, reconciliation, and
lifecycle evidence after the transaction. The architecture separates five
boundaries: control of a key is not identity, identity is not organizational
authority, authority is not compliance, compliance is not execution authority,
and network confirmation is not complete commercial settlement. We describe
wallet and custody profiles, including a multi-party computation (MPC) profile
in which custody security is kept independent of business authority, stablecoin
and Web3 evidence domains, layered finality, the U.S. stablecoin regulatory
context as of September 2026, and a phased deployment in which pilot success
requires that the control invariants hold under attack and failure.
