Title: Trustworthy Distributed Machine Learning in Cloud-Edge-IoT Systems: A Heterogeneity-Aware Survey of Security, Privacy, and Robustness
Authors: Long Dang, Ali Alfatemi, Kaiqi Xiong, Mohammed Aledhari, Abdellah Chehri, Shawqi Al-Maliki, Mohamed Rahouti
ID: 2610.38793
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38793

Abstract:
Distributed machine learning (DML), including conventional distributed training
and federated learning (FL), enables collaborative model development across
cloud-edge-IoT systems without fully centralizing raw data. However,
distributing data, computation, updates, and orchestration increases the attack
surface and introduces coupled challenges across privacy, robustness,
reliability, fairness, and governance. This survey presents a security-centered,
heterogeneity-aware roadmap for trustworthy DML, with FL treated as a prominent
high-risk case. We first characterize the assets, adversaries, trust
assumptions, and coordination patterns that define DML/FL security boundaries.
We then analyze how statistical, system, and trust heterogeneity, including non-
IID data, resource asymmetry, intermittent participation, and cross-silo/cross-
device trust differences, affect convergence, fairness, attack detectability,
and defense reliability. Next, we synthesize attack-defense interactions across
poisoning, backdoors, Byzantine manipulation, privacy leakage, model extraction,
network/orchestration attacks, Sybil behavior, and strategic participation. We
further compare layered defenses, including robust aggregation, drift-aware
optimization, differential privacy, secure aggregation, homomorphic encryption,
secure multiparty computation, trusted execution environments, watermarking,
provenance, authenticated communication, and monitoring. Finally, we discuss
deployment, evaluation, governance, and open research guidelines, emphasizing
reproducible, non-independent and identically distributed (non-IID) partitions,
attack-aware benchmarks, privacy budgets, attack success rate, client-level
disparity, latency, communication, memory, energy/carbon overhead, and auditable
lifecycle artifacts.
