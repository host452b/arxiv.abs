Title: Understanding Users' Security, Privacy, and Trust Concerns in Healthcare AI Systems
Authors: Tushar Nayan, Mohammed Al Duniawi, Song Fang, Aokun Chen, Arjun Arunasalam, Ruimin Sun
ID: 2611.20223
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20223

Abstract:
Artificial intelligence is increasingly integrated into healthcare systems, from
clinical information retrieval to workflow automation and decision support,
which introduces new security, privacy, and trust risks with direct implications
for patient care. While prior work has examined these risks from technical and
regulatory perspectives, less is known about how these risks are naturally
discussed in online communities. To address this gap, we conduct a large-scale
analysis of 14 medical communities on Reddit and extract 6,514 relevant posts.
Through systematic qualitative coding and LLM-assisted classification, we
investigate concerns expressed in healthcare AI discussions, their prevalence,
and how they evolve over time. Our analysis reveals that users perceive
healthcare AI as expanding traditional healthcare trust boundaries and attack
surfaces through integrations with electronic health records, third-party
services, cloud platforms, and automated clinical workflows. Posts express
concerns about over-privileged access to sensitive records, opaque data flows,
unauthorized secondary use, and unsafe automation can amplify the consequences
of errors and misuse. We identify four attitudes toward these risks: exploring
questions of accountability and harm, cautiously accepting AI with stronger
safeguards, skepticism about system trustworthiness, and resignation to growing
data exposure and automation. Longitudinal analysis shows that SP&T discussions
have increased significantly, with shifts coinciding with major healthcare AI
deployments, privacy incidents, and regulatory developments. These findings
illuminate changing trust relationships and data-governance challenges,
informing the design and governance of safer, more transparent, and accountable
healthcare AI systems.
