Title: Optimization Techniques for Solving the Minimum Red--Blue Separating Set Problem with an Application to Human--AI Text Separation
Authors: Marko Djukanović, Staša Vujičić Stanković, Žiga Zebec, Christian Blum
ID: 2610.41081
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41081

Abstract:
We study the so-called Minimum Red''Blue Separating Set Problem (MRBSSP) which
arises from computational geometry. In particular, we propose a compact Set-
Cover ILP formulation, safe preprocessing rules, a Greedy Randomized Adaptive
Search Procedure (GRASP), and two exact approaches: one is based on Logic-Based
Benders Decomposition (LBBD) approach, and the second represents a novel Branch-
and-Benders-Cut (BBC) approach. These two exact approaches are compared to ILP--
alongside GRASP and the Variable Neighborhood Search (VNS) from literature.
Experiments on 250 random graphs with up to 500 vertices reveal complementary
strengths: ILP yields the best feasible solutions, LBBD provides the strongest
lower bounds and smallest mean optimality gap, and BBC certifies optimality most
frequently. We further introduce an NLP application distinguishing human- and
AI-generated texts through vocabulary-based graph features. On 42 instances, BBC
achieves the smallest mean optimality gap, certifying 16 optima, while GRASP
outperforms VNS. Structural analysis of optimal vocabulary separators identifies
113 mandatory and 151 replaceable features across 272 tests, highlighting
substantial variability among optimal solutions. Overall, our results
demonstrate the effectiveness of complementary optimization strategies and the
interpretability of optimal vocabulary-based discrimination.
