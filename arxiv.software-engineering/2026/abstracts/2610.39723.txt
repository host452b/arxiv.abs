Title: Countable Nondeterminism in Weighted Programming with an Application to Mechanized Foundations for Probabilistic Program Verification
Authors: Sebastian Körner, Oliver Emil Bøving, Christoph Matheja
ID: 2610.39723
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39723

Abstract:
Weighted programming is a framework by Batz et al. for describing mathematical
models or optimization problems as imperative programs parametrized over
semirings and then analyzing them with program verification techniques like
weakest precondition calculi. Applications include reasoning about expected
outcomes of probabilistic programs and competitive analysis of online
algorithms. We extend weighted programming with (1) countable nondeterminism and
(2) user-defined additive rewards, where programs are parametrized over modules
instead of semirings. The resulting framework enables reasoning about expected
resource consumption of probabilistic programs and about optimal scores of
combinatorial games. A technical challenge is that wp-calculi for weighted
programs with (1) and (2) over arbitrary modules can be unsound, because it is
well-known that continuity properties commonly used in program semantics break
in the presence of countable nondeterminism. We study conditions under which a
wp-style transformer for reasoning about weighted programs is sound with respect
to an operational ground truth defined in terms of weighted decision processes
(WDPs) - transition systems that generalize Markov decision processes by drawing
transition weights and state rewards from a module. Based on those conditions,
we show that a range of concrete instances of the weighted programming framework
enjoy sound weakest precondition reasoning. We demonstrate the usefulness of our
wp-semantics through case studies for different modules. We mechanized our
extended weighted programming framework and our main soundness results in the
interactive theorem prover Lean. Instantiating these results for probabilistic
programming yields, to the best of our knowledge, the first mechanized proof of
a fixed point characterization of minimal expected rewards in Markov decision
processes with countable nondeterminism.
