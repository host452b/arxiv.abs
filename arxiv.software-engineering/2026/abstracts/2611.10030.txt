Title: Alignment Investigation of Mythos 5's behaviour in UK AISI's July 2026 Security Incident
Authors: Olli Järviniemi, Alexandra Souly, Abby D'Cruz, Jordan Taylor, Xander Davies, Robert Kirk
ID: 2611.10030
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10030

Abstract:
This technical report describes an alignment investigation into Mythos 5's
behaviour during a security incident in UK AISI's cyber evaluations. During an
evaluation, Mythos 5 attempted to execute a supply-chain attack against real
people not involved in the evaluation task. Our investigation set out to
understand the specific behaviour in this incident from an alignment perspective
and identify broader lessons about failures of alignment. To do so, we conducted
experiments in simulated replications of the original evaluation, and analysed
model behaviour and reasoning when situated in the evaluation or asked questions
about its scope. We find that, as in the original incident, Mythos 5 frequently
reasons that the people it's targeting are simulated as part of the evaluation
and provides invalid justifications for this conclusion. We also observe that
Mythos 5 frequently attacks even in situations where, when directly asked, it
states doing so would harm real people. While these issues are particularly
prominent in Mythos 5, we find similar results in other models across providers.
Our investigation suggests that models providing invalid justifications for
their actions could make monitoring more difficult and complicate the analysis
of misaligned behaviours observed in simulated environments, and we recommend
model evaluators treat observations of undesired behaviour as important evidence
of alignment failures even in the presence of simulation awareness in model
reasoning.
