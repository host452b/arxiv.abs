Title: Information Security Risk Preparedness in Electronic Data Interchange: A Comparative Assessment of General IT and Healthcare Environments
Authors: Naresh Kshetri, Bishwo Prakash Pokharel, Arati Bastakoti, Anjana Bastakoti
ID: 2611.31294
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31294

Abstract:
Electronic Data Interchange (EDI) supports the exchange of structured
information across business and healthcare environments, making information
security and organizational risk management critical to reliable digital
transactions. This study empirically assesses security-control preparedness and
organizational risk-taking ability in EDI-related environments through a
comparative analysis of General IT Professionals (GIT) and Healthcare EDI
Professionals (HEP). A quantitative, cross-sectional survey was conducted with
100 respondents, equally divided between the two groups. Security Control
Preparedness (SCP) and Threat Perception (TP) were validated using reliability
and exploratory factor analyses, followed by group comparisons, correlation
analysis, and multiple linear regression. HEP respondents reported significantly
higher SCP than GIT respondents (M = 4.70 vs. 3.89, p < 0.001, d = 1.06) and
higher Organizational Risk-Taking Ability (ORTA) (M = 6.14 vs. 5.28, p = 0.001,
d = 0.66). In contrast, GIT respondents reported higher threat perception (M =
2.95 vs. 2.04, p < 0.001, d = 0.72). SCP was positively associated with ORTA (r
= 0.479, p < 0.001), whereas TP showed a significant negative association (r =
-0.610, p < 0.001). A parsimonious regression model incorporating SCP and TP
explained 41.6% of the variance in ORTA (R^2 = 0.416, adjusted R^2 = 0.404, p <
0.001). Based on these empirical findings, an EDI Risk Management Framework
(EDI-RMF) is proposed integrating governance, human and organizational security,
technical safeguards, physical protection, and continuous monitoring. The
findings provide empirical evidence for strengthening information-security risk
management in EDI-enabled environments, particularly within developing-economy
contexts.
