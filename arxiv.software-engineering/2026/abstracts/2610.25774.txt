Title: What Is Under the Hood? A Cross-Ecosystem Study of Vulnerable Dependencies in Construction Software
Authors: Mahzabin Tamanna, S M Jamil Uddin
ID: 2610.25774
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25774

Abstract:
The construction industry increasingly relies on software built from third-party
components, yet the dependency composition and known-vulnerability exposure of
construction-related software remain poorly analyzed. In this study, we conduct
an empirical, cross-ecosystem investigation of 904 construction-related GitHub
repositories spanning six construction functions and a multifunction category.
Using commit-pinned repository snapshots and OSV-Scanner, we examine dependency
assessability, known-vulnerability prevalence, severity, weakness
classifications, and shared upstream exposure. Our results reveal that only 494
repositories (54.6%) completed vulnerability assessment, revealing substantial
gaps in dependency visibility. Among successfully scanned repositories, 395
(80.0%) contained at least one dependency version associated with a published
vulnerability advisory, and 97.2% of affected repositories contained a High or
Critical severity risk. Additionally, Common Weakness Enumeration (CWE) mapping
identified recurring resource-consumption, path-traversal, information-exposure,
and code-injection weaknesses. Vulnerability burden was uneven, with a median of
50 distinct advisory clusters per affected repository, while just two affected
upstream package families collectively reached ~70% of affected repositories.
These findings establish an empirical baseline for dependency assessability and
known-vulnerability exposure in construction-related open-source software. Our
findings underscore the importance of documenting assessment coverage,
interpreting advisory matches in their deployment context, and examining shared
upstream dependencies when evaluating construction software supply-chain
security.
