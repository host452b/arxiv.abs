Title: Experience: ENTRAP - An IaC Enterprise Cyber Range for Reproducible Security Assessment and Dataset Generation
Authors: Rathan Appana, Aditya Kulkarni, Bheemarjuna Reddy Tamma
ID: 2611.20594
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20594

Abstract:
An enterprise breach causes most of its damage after the perimeter is crossed.
Once an attacker gains a foothold, they can move laterally to other hosts, take
over the trusted services of the network, and encrypt the backups needed for
recovery. Defenders, on the other hand, need to study this post-compromise
stage, but they cannot rehearse it on a production enterprise network. Building
such an enterprise network manually is time-consuming, and resetting it to its
clean state between runs is still harder. As a result, security experiments are
difficult to perform, compare and reproduce. We develop Enterprise Network
Testbed for Reproducible Attack and Defense Practices (ENTRAP), an
Infrastructure-as-Code (IaC) cyber range that defines a fully segmented
enterprise as version-controlled automation. ENTRAP uses Ansible, KVM/Libvirt,
and Cloud-init to provision the environment and reset it to a clean state
whenever necessary. The enterprise network includes several entities such as a
gateway, an internal Demilitarized Zone (DMZ), DNS, DHCP, a backup server, a web
server, and clients. On this network, a user can learn to assemble the entities,
launch attacks, and write rules or defensive measures that harden its security.
We launch three attacks: a Ransomware-as-a-Service (RaaS)-backed ransomware
campaign, a Layer-2 DHCP starvation and rogue DHCP attack, and perimeter web
exploitation. Each attack runs from a clean state, and ENTRAP acts as a
reproducible generator of labeled attack datasets. Using the collected dataset,
we train a Random Forest classifier for ransomware detection and present its
evaluation results. We publicly release the codebase, collected logs, and
datasets to facilitate further research.
