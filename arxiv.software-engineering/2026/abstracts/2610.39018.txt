Title: Tunable Spatial Task Allocation with Application to Maritime Swarms
Authors: Vishnudatta Thota, Tyler Paine, Anastasia Bizyaeva
ID: 2610.39018
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39018

Abstract:
In this chapter, we explore how a team of autonomous agents can flexibly
reconfigure the distribution of its members across many spatially embedded tasks
using a task allocation strategy grounded in a generalization of the recently
proposed Nonlinear Opinion Dynamics model. Motivated by an adaptive maritime
survey application, we couple opinion dynamics directly to spatial movement and
examine how the emergent macroscopic allocation is shaped by the interplay
between environmental geometry, nature of social interactions, and task demands.
We illustrate in simulation how social coupling in the model tunes group
cohesion, producing macroscopic allocation patterns ranging from maximally
dispersed states, to intermediate semi-dispersed states with a smaller number of
distinct subgroups, to maximally cohesive consensus states, in distinct and
interpretable parameter regimes. Furthermore, we demonstrate with illustrative
examples how a team can reconfigure itself in real time under changing task
demands. For allocation scenarios with distance-based task preference and
matched number of agents and tasks, we relate numerically the final allocation
in the group to the Hungarian optimum, illustrating close-to-optimal
performance. We illustrate performance of this method under realistic
communication constraints in the MOOS-IvP-Multi simulation environment with
simulated autonomous underwater vehicles (AUVs). Together, these examples show
how a common set of local interaction rules can support a large range of
interpretable macroscopic allocation states and flexible transitions between
them.
