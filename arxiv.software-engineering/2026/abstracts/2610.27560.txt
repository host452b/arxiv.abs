Title: Restraint-Induced Loss in Software-Intensive Enterprises: Large A Verified Technical Economic Impact Model of Operational Downtime and Delayed Commercialization, with Tail-Risk Security Sizing and Three Enterprise Case Studies
Authors: Mahdi Eslamimehr
ID: 2610.27560
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27560

Abstract:
When an enterprise must stop using a software component (for example, because of
an interim court restraint in a technology patent dispute), the economic loss
passes through a chain of technical mechanisms. Withdrawing the component
exposes dependent capabilities. Fallback paths degrade throughput, correctness
or feature coverage. Incident rates and recovery backlogs rise. Customers
respond. Remediation programmes compete against the restraint clock, and planned
launches are pushed back. This paper develops and verifies a \emph{technical--
economic impact model} (TEIM) that quantifies this chain end to end as a
probability distribution of present-value loss rather than as a point estimate.
The technical layer combines (i)~a lattice-valued, fixed-point semantics of
service dependencies with degraded fallbacks, (ii)~continuous-time Markov
reliability models evaluated by uniformization and specified in continuous
stochastic logic, and (iii)~a fluid queueing model of post-outage backlog. These
feed (iv)~convex acute-outage loss functions with regulatory and contractual
thresholds, (v)~compound-Poisson excess-incident processes, (vi)~survival-
weighted functional, attrition and funding channels that provably avoid double
counting, (vii)~a real-options valuation of forcibly delayed launches with an
explicit closed form, and (viii)~a COCOMO~II--driven stochastic activity network
for design-around and substitution strategies. We prove seven theorems and eight
propositions.
