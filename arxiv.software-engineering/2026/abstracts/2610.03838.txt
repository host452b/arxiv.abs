Title: Smells and Refactorings for Software Environmental Sustainability: A Systematic Literature Review
Authors: Ornela Danushi, Flavio Sampirisi, Jacopo Soldani, Stefano Forti, Antonio Brogi
ID: 2610.03838
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03838

Abstract:
Context: The environmental impact of ICT is constantly growing. Enhancing its
environmental sustainability requires dealing with energy-hungry software
systems, by possibly reducing their footprint. Objective: To better frame the
problem and its persistent nature, software can be viewed as carrying structural
inefficiencies regardless of how advanced its functionality may be. Identifying
these inefficiencies as "green smells" provides a basis for refactoring code to
mitigate its ecological impact. We aim at identifying well-known green smells
impacting environmental sustainable software and map them to corresponding
refactoring aimed at reducing the impact therein. Method: In this article, we
conduct a systematic literature review on the articles that discuss green smells
and their possible refactorings. We analyse and classify 66 primary studies
published up to March 2026. Results: We identify 20 smells impacting
environmental sustainability of software. Each smell is associated with one or
more refactoring techniques known to mitigate their negative effects on the
environmental sustainability of software. Conclusions: The literature witnesses
the interest in resolving the green smells affecting software systems. The
catalogued green smells and the corresponding refactorings can provide pragmatic
value for practitioners in their daily development workflows, while also
offering researchers a starting point to establish new research directions in
sustainable software engineering.
