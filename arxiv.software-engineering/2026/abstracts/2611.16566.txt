Title: Towards Automated Recommendation of Steady-State Detection Algorithms for Software Performance Benchmarks
Authors: Martin Beseda, Vittorio Cortellessa, Federico Di Menna, Daniele Di Pompeo, Luca Traini
ID: 2611.16566
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16566

Abstract:
Steady-state detection is a central step in software performance benchmarking,
yet different detection methods rely on different assumptions and may perform
best on different execution profiles. This paper formulates steady-state
detection as an algorithm-recommendation problem: given a benchmark time series,
predict which of two complementary detectors is more appropriate. The first, CP-
SSD, identifies steady state through changepoint-based segmentation of the
execution trace, whereas the second, KB-KSSD, uses a kernel-based approach to
identify stable behaviour. We propose a data-driven recommender that
characterises benchmark time series using automatically extracted features and
combines supervised prediction with information derived from groups of time
series exhibiting similar behaviour. The approach is evaluated on 407 labelled
benchmark time series for which one of the two detectors can be identified as
preferable with respect to a reference steady-state index. The proposed
recommender selects the better detector correctly for 72.97% of the series,
compared with 62.65% when always selecting the detector that performs best most
often, corresponding to an absolute improvement of 10.32 percentage points.
Paired statistical testing and bootstrap confidence intervals support this
improvement. A comparison with a recommender using time-series features alone
shows that the cluster-derived information mainly improves interpretability
rather than predictive accuracy. Recommendations also remain highly stable when
the unsupervised representation is learned from either the labelled subset or
the full collection of 5,860 time series. These results demonstrate the
feasibility of per-series steady-state detector recommendation while
highlighting the need for broader labelled data, particularly for execution
profiles favouring the changepoint-based detector.
