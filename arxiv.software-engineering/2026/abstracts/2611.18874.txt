Title: Stargate: Security Analysis and Mission-Aware Firewall for the CubeSat Space Protocol
Authors: Owen Ramsey, Carlos Gonzalez-Cortes, Efren Lopez-Morales
ID: 2611.18874
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18874

Abstract:
The CubeSat Space Protocol (CSP) is widely used for communication in CubeSat
missions, yet its security model and attack surface remain largely unexplored.
In this work, we present a systematic security analysis of CSP and identify
fundamental limitations in its trust assumptions, coarse-grained network
abstractions, privileged management services, application command semantics, and
lack of mission-context awareness. Based on these findings, we develop a
taxonomy of CSP attacks spanning four classes: network identity abuse,
management service abuse, application semantic abuse, and mission context abuse.
We then introduce Stargate, a modular, mission-aware firewall that enforces CSP-
specific policies derived from this taxonomy, including node and service
authorization, command-level filtering, and mission-context constraints. We
implement Stargate and evaluate it on a virtual testbed based on a real-world
CubeSat mission and flight software. Stargate successfully blocks all 11
representative attacks across the four attack classes while introducing only
1.42 ms of additional median round-trip latency. Our results demonstrate that
protocol- and mission-aware policy enforcement provides a practical approach for
securing CSP-based spacecraft networks.
