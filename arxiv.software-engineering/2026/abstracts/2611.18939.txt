Title: Level Up! AI-Driven Challenges and Adaptations to Code Review Practices
Authors: Kaia Newman, Christian Bird, Jenna Butler, Emerson Murphy-Hill
ID: 2611.18939
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18939

Abstract:
AI can write code faster than humans can review, causing a bottleneck and
necessitating changes to code review practices. This bottleneck is hotly
debated, giving rise to two extreme and orthogonal imagined futures: one where
humans continue to be firmly entrenched in the review process, and one where AI
agents merge pull requests entirely without human involvement. Yet, these
futures remain rooted in folk wisdom. Through a mixed-methods study of
interviews ($n = 24$) and a survey ($n = 2{,}590$) at a large, multinational
software company with widespread access to AI, we identify AI-driven code review
challenges and strategies reported as effective to meet them. We found that AI
enables, necessitates, and complicates a shift in the human reviewer's role
toward high-level concerns (e.g., design and intent). We found that AI-assisted
authoring results in increased burden for reviewers due to challenges such as a
lack of author stewardship and PR volume and size, potentially leading to
downstream debt. Our survey showed AI review usage was not correlated with less
difficulty keeping up with PR volume, indicating using AI for review alone is
not sufficient for solving AI-driven review challenges. Developers found the
most useful strategies to be organizations supporting and resourcing review,
pushing review burden upstream, and prioritizing reviewer attention on the
concerns that matter most.
