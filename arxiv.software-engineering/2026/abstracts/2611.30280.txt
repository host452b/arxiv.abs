Title: Evaluating Persona Consistency in LLM User Simulators Without External Reference Annotations
Authors: Anping Zhang, Yang Tan, Yipeng Liu, Clive Bai, Blaze Chen, Yang Li
ID: 2611.30280
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30280

Abstract:
Standard evaluations assess LLM-simulated personas against an external ground
truth, such as community trait annotations, but that reference is unavailable
when the persona is inferred only from a short, task-oriented chat. We ask how
persona consistency can be evaluated without such a reference, and propose a
self-anchored internal-consistency diagnostic: the persona describes itself once
and acts twice. A Likert self-report supplies a soft internal anchor; two
behavioral profiles come from constrained scenario choices scored without a
judge and from open-ended responses judged by an LLM. Consistency is the
correlation between the self-report and each behavioral profile, in a shared
six-facet BFI-2 space. The framework compares persona-construction strategies by
assessing how consistently their elicited responses reflect their self-reported
traits. Across ordinary-user chats (WildChat and LMSYS) and well-documented
personas with community annotations (the Personality Database, PDB), profile-
enriched representations agree with the anchor more closely than a raw-dialogue
baseline. On PDB, this self-anchored ordering reproduces the community-
annotation ordering. The pattern is stable across answering and judge models.
