Title: Impact of Edge Software Evolution on Audio Tagging Models: An Empirical Study on NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano
Authors: Guillem Fustier-Castillo, Ruben Ribes-Serrano, Jordi Grau-Haro, Marta Garcia-Ballesteros, Javier Naranjo-Alcazar
ID: 2611.26831
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26831

Abstract:
Audio tagging is a fundamental task in machine listening, enabling the automatic
identification of acoustic events in applications such as intelligent
monitoring, industrial inspection, environmental sensing, and smart
environments. As these applications increasingly migrate to edge platforms,
deployment-oriented metrics such as latency, energy consumption, and resource
utilization become critical alongside predictive performance. However, most
existing studies evaluate audio tagging models under a single software
environment, overlooking the potential impact of software stack evolution on
deployment efficiency. This paper investigates how software ecosystem updates
affect the execution characteristics of state-of-the-art audio tagging
architectures on edge devices. We evaluate representative convolutional and
Transformer-based models, including PANNs (CNN and ResNet variants), AST, and
PaSST, on an NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano platform using two generations of NVIDIA's
software ecosystem: JetPack 6.2 and JetPack 7.2. Under identical hardware
conditions, we analyze inference latency, energy consumption, CPU/GPU
utilization, and memory footprint across multiple inference backends and power
modes. The results show that software stack evolution can substantially affect
deployment performance. JetPack 7.2 reduces latency and energy consumption by
more than 50% for several convolutional architectures when combined with
TensorRT, while significantly increasing GPU utilization. In contrast, AST
exhibits only marginal gains, whereas PaSST shows intermediate behavior. These
findings demonstrate that software evolution is a critical factor in Edge AI
evaluation and can alter the relative deployment efficiency of competing
architectures. Consequently, software stack versions should be explicitly
considered when benchmarking and selecting audio tagging models for embedded
deployment.
