Title: Embodied Neuro-symbolic AI: Making Robot Behavior Auditable, Safe, and Scalable
Authors: Ruwan Wickramarachchi, Jonathan Francis, Alessandro Oltramari
ID: 2611.31488
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31488

Abstract:
Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) end-to-end systems have driven major
progress in robot learning, yet they still face persistent barriers to
dependable real-world deployment. In practice, these models often exhibit
brittle generalization under distribution shift (i.e., novel objects, layouts,
lighting, or contact conditions), limited physical and causal reasoning, and
inconsistent fine-grained motor control. Critically, many VLA pipelines offer
limited ways to express and audit what a robot must not do, making it difficult
to provide assurance in safety-critical settings. These limitations constrain
scalability, maintainability, and trust when robots move beyond controlled
demonstrations. We propose Embodied Neuro-Symbolic (NeSy) AI: a deployment-
oriented paradigm that integrates learned perception, planning and control with
explicit, machine-readable background knowledge and formal constraints. By
combining knowledge-guided perception, constraint-aware symbolic planning, and
hybrid learning, Embodied NeSy AI enables robots to reason about their
environment, adapt to new tasks, and provide auditable, machine-checkable
compliance with safety and operational requirements, thus supporting scalable,
trustworthy robot deployment.
