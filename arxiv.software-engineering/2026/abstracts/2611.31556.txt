Title: Real-Time AI Acceleration on a 2D Spatial Dataflow Architecture
Authors: Shriram Raja, Anton Njavro, Richard West
ID: 2611.31556
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31556

Abstract:
Machine Learning (ML) is increasingly being integrated into real-time systems,
which makes the timing analysis of ML accelerators necessary. However, the most
popular commercial accelerator, the Graphics Processing Unit (GPU), remains
difficult to analyze as several aspects of its behavior, including scheduling of
threads involved in single-instruction multiple-thread computations and memory
access latency, are not fully documented. Spatial dataflow accelerators present
an interesting alternative to GPUs, as they inherently support compute and
memory partitioning. Data movement and computation locality are explicitly
controlled by the programmer. In this work, we consider the Tenstorrent
Blackhole, which is based on a commodity RISC-V architecture. It consists of a
2D grid of cores connected by Networks-on-Chip (NoCs), supporting a fully open
software runtime. We profile the Blackhole platform and develop a worst-case
execution time task model as a Homogeneous Synchronous Dataflow Graph. We extend
this model to a preliminary analysis of multi-core workloads by considering NoC-
related delays. Finally, we evaluate our model across 12,567 task configurations
and workloads. Results show that the estimate derived from our model lies above
the observed worst case in every configuration. For single-core workloads, the
overhead is less than 100% in most cases, with a maximum of less than 200%, and
for multi-core workloads, it is less than 500% using a pessimistic NoC
contention analysis. This paper lays the foundations for real-time computing on
a promising open accelerator design.
