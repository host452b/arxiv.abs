Title: A Defense-Aware Explainable AI Framework for Feature-Level Vulnerability Analysis in Optimal Power Flow
Authors: S. M. Monzurul Hoque Chowdhury, Qinghua Li, Jianhui Wang
ID: 2610.38945
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38945

Abstract:
Optimal power flow (OPF) is a foundational analysis in power system operation,
and inaccuracies in OPF decision-making can result in significant operational
and security consequences. With the increasing adoption of machine-learning-
based OPF surrogate models for real-time and large-scale applications, power
system operation has become more susceptible to cyber threats, including data
manipulation and false data injection attacks. Explainable artificial
intelligence (XAI), particularly Shapley value-based approaches, has been
employed to quantify the influence of input features on OPF outputs. Features
exhibiting high influence are commonly interpreted as vulnerable. However, this
interpretation neglects the role of attack detection mechanisms in the system,
which typically monitor input-to-input relationships to detect anomalous
behavior. Consequently, highly influential features may be readily detectable
and may not represent the most vulnerable attack surfaces. To address this
limitation, we propose a defense-aware explainable AI framework that explicitly
incorporates attack detectability alongside operational impact. The framework
integrates an OPF surrogate model representing input-to-output dependencies with
a vanilla autoencoder representing input-to-input dependencies. Shapley-based
explainability is applied to both models to construct a feature-level
vulnerability metric. Empirical evaluation on IEEE test systems demonstrates
that the proposed framework more accurately identifies security-relevant
vulnerabilities than conventional OPF-surrogate-model-only explainability
approaches.
