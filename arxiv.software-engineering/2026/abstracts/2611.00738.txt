Title: Cross-Evidence Authorization State Learning for Runtime Detection of Broken Object-Level Authorization in Black-Box Web Applications
Authors: Siqi Lin, Qianheng Cheng, Yiping Mao
ID: 2611.00738
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00738

Abstract:
Broken object-level authorization (BOLA) remains difficult to detect in black-
box Web applications because external runtime monitors generally lack access to
source code, authorization policies, and object-ownership records. This paper
proposes a cross-evidence authorization-state learning method that reconstructs
subject--resource--object--action relations from request--response transactions.
The method represents authorization relations with confidence-aware states,
aggregates positive and negative evidence while accounting for provenance
independence, and applies contamination-gated promotion, evidence quarantine and
rollback, expiration, and revocation. A Python prototype is evaluated using a
deterministic synthetic benchmark comprising 64,400 requests across five
independent random seeds. In this benchmark, the proposed method achieves
100.00% recall, an F1-score of 56.63%, and a false-positive rate of 33.65%,
while the measured unauthorized-relation pollution rate is 0.00%. Ablation
experiments show that removing provenance independence or evidence quarantine
increases the pollution rate to 100.00%. The experiments provide an initial
evaluation under controlled synthetic conditions.
