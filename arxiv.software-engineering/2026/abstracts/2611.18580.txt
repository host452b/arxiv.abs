Title: Model-Driven Approaches for DevOps: A Systematic Literature Review
Authors: Hugo da Gião, Rui Pereira, Jácome Cunha
ID: 2611.18580
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18580

Abstract:
DevOps integrates tools and methodologies aimed at optimizing software
development, building, deployment, and monitoring by automating and reducing the
development lifecycle and improving software quality. However, despite its many
advantages, it presents notable challenges, particularly in terms of usability
and migration. On the other hand, model-driven engineering (MDE) offers a way to
abstract from specific technologies and implementation details, which can be
leveraged to improve these aspects, as happens in software development. In this
paper, we present the first systematic literature review aiming to understand
the current MDE approaches for supporting DevOps development. We focus on
categorizing the works based on computing domains, the phases of the DevOps
process to which they contribute, and the reengineering steps they propose. Our
findings highlight a dominant focus on cloud computing, while some studies
propose domain-agnostic approaches. We also observe that the deployment and
integration phases are the most explored. This reveals research opportunities
regarding both the domains and DevOps phases addressed. We also found limited
use of text-to-model transformations, while nearly half of the papers employed
model-to-model transformations. Text-to-model transformations were more
prevalent overall. Most notably, we discovered that no study proposed a complete
reengineering process, which represents an exciting avenue for future research.
