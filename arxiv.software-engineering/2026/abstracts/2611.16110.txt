Title: Harnessing Environmental Scatterers for Endogenous Target Covertness in 6G ISAC: A New Paradigm for Sensing Security
Authors: Yifan Zhang, Boxuan Xie, Mingyang Yang, Ying Liu, Riku Jäntti, Zan Li
ID: 2611.16110
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16110

Abstract:
Integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) is a cornerstone of 6G networks,
enabling simultaneous data transmission and environmental perception. However,
the ubiquitous radiation of sensing waveforms introduces a critical security
vulnerability where the physical existence and characteristics of targets are
continuously exposed to unauthorized wardens. Meanwhile, existing sensing-secure
methods primarily focus on degrading a target warden's parameter-estimation
accuracy, yet they fail to prevent wardens from detecting the target's physical
presence. In this article, we first analyze sensing security threats in ISAC
systems and survey existing sensing-secure methods. To address the critical gap,
we propose a novel security paradigm termed endogenous target covertness. By
intentionally harnessing environmental scatterers, such as known objects,
ambient backscatter devices, and reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (RIS), as
ghost targets, the proposed scheme ensures a low probability that the warden
detects the true target for the true target at the warden. This deliberately
renders the true target's existence indistinguishable to the warden, while the
legitimate base station can extract the true target's response using prior
knowledge of the scatterers. We demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed
scheme through a case study, proving that absolute target covertness can be
achieved without consuming dedicated jamming power. Finally, we outline key open
challenges and promising research directions to realize truly secure, ubiquitous
sensing in future ISAC ecosystems.
