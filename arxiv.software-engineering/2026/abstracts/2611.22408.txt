Title: From Attack Tree to Model Checker: Binding CAPEC Patterns to Verify Robotic System Security
Authors: Juel Hussain, Louise A. Dennis, Clare Dixon, Marie Farrell
ID: 2611.22408
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22408

Abstract:
Security-minded verification of autonomous and robotic systems presupposes an
attacker, yet the path from a threat catalogue to a checkable attacker model is
still traversed by hand. This research preview presents the theory and
construction of a pipeline that mechanises it. Each leaf of an attack tree is
mapped to a CAPEC attack pattern and written in first-order logic as a rule: the
attacker's prerequisites imply the attack's effect. Casting the pattern as such
a rule is what makes it operational - its variables can be bound to a specific
system's parts, and its prerequisites can then be tested against that system
under a chosen configuration of security controls. Binding is our means of
verifying whether a system is vulnerable to a given attack: from a bound pattern
we derive an attacker model, turn its effect into a temporal-logic property, and
check that property against the system with and without a countermeasure,
reporting the control that defeats the attack. We describe the signature,
schemas, profiles for approach, and explore how CAPEC can support reasoning
about robotics security.
