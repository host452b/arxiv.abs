Title: Can SOLARBURST go silicon? A systematic analysis on software supply chain attacks for VLSI design flows
Authors: Fabiha Hashmat, Zeren Li, Santiago Torres-Arias
ID: 2610.22580
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22580

Abstract:
An attacker targeting the software supply chain (SWSC) of VLSI tools can fully
subvert the VLSI design pipeline through purely software level compromises.
However, cur- rent literature on both SWSC and VLSI hardware security has not
explored how software can be used to subvert the security of the VLSI design
flow. In order to bridge this gap, this paper presents a systematic analysis of
the vulnerable surface of VLSI from a SWSC standpoint. We carry this anal- ysis
through three major activities. First, we did an empirical analysis of VLSI
software suites, focusing on their software composition, security features, and
tool interactions within the VLSI design flow. Second, we develop a VLSI-SWSC
attack taxonomy model that links software supply chain at- tacks and their
mechanisms to the VLSI attacks they enable, the design-flow stages they affect,
and their resulting security outcomes, allowing us to identify 717 possible new
attacks, including 60 high feasibility attacks supported by evidence collected
in our empirical analysis. Third, we identify what possible security outcomes
can be achieved by an attacker from these attacks and produce a comprehensive
VLSI SWSC threat model. We evaluate the feasibility of the attacks identi- fied
by our taxonomy across VLSI design stages and assess existing defenses to
determine which attacks are mitigated and which remain unaddressed. In addition,
we present case studies using a reference VLSI design flow consisting of open-
source VLSI tools and highlight attacks identified through our taxonomy on the
VLSI SWSC uniquely from a software perspective.
