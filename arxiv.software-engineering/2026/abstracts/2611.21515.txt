Title: Beyond Leaderboard Ranks: Selecting Software Engineering Agents Under Budget Constraints
Authors: Srinath Srinivasan, Tim Menzies
ID: 2611.21515
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21515

Abstract:
Teams that adopt LLM agents for issue resolution must decide which agent to pay
for, and how much to spend finding out. Leaderboard ranks give weak evidence for
this decision. Accuracy differences may be statistically unresolved, reported
costs may be inconsistent, and evaluating candidate agents can itself be
expensive. We investigate budget-constrained agent selection using 58,197 run--
task records extracted from the Holistic Agent Leaderboard (HAL), focusing on
SWE-bench and six additional benchmarks. Across seven benchmarks, 27 of 35 steps
along the accuracy--cost Pareto frontiers are statistically unresolved. On SWE-
bench, a run costing $34.16 trails the $170.78 leader by 14.6 percentage points,
but the available tasks cannot statistically distinguish their accuracy.
Reported costs also differ from standardized token-based estimates by up to
11.7x, while selective task evaluation can bias estimates of full-benchmark
execution cost. We therefore compare evaluation strategies under explicit
evaluation budgets and deployment-cost caps. Under advance knowledge of task
costs, successive halving reaches our uncertainty-aware selection target on SWE-
bench using 6.6% of exhaustive evaluation spend, achieving the lowest cost on
six of seven benchmarks and tying on the seventh. Its advantage narrows when
task costs are unknown before execution or exact-best selection is required. Our
findings motivate decision-oriented agent evaluation that accounts for accuracy
uncertainty, validates execution costs, and compares complex selection methods
against simple adaptive allocation.
