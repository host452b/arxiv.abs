Title: NonSense: On the (In)security of FPGA Anti-tamper Sensors
Authors: Mehmet Ali Cetin, Fatemeh Khojasteh Dana, Saleh Khalaj Monfared, Nima Seyed Aghazadeh, Maryam Saadat Safa, Shahin Tajik
ID: 2611.17065
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17065

Abstract:
FPGAs are widely used in mission-critical systems, where a successful physical
attack can have serious consequences. This makes resilience to such attacks a
critical security requirement. To defend against active physical attacks, such
as glitching attacks, modern FPGAs rely on on-chip sensors that detect these
disturbances and trigger responses, such as key zeroization, before a device is
compromised. These sensors are deployed as both hard-IP blocks and soft-IP
cores, and their trustworthiness is often taken for granted. In this work, we
challenge that assumption and argue that the sensors themselves are a fragile
and underexplored attack surface. Drawing on recent results, we show that both
the detection and response mechanisms of state-of-the-art sensors can be
bypassed without invasive tampering. We further argue that sensor reliability is
reduced by factors rarely captured in threat models, including the spatial and
frequency-dependent behavior of the system's power delivery network, which can
steer a fault to a target while sparing nearby sensors; environmental conditions
such as temperature, which shift detection thresholds; and long-term aging,
which drifts sensor calibration and may open silent detection gaps. Finally, we
propose a set of countermeasures that harden detection and response mechanisms
against these effects.
