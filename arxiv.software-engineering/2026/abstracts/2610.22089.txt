Title: Are We Testing the Wrong Quantum Bugs? A Study of Fault Representation Gaps in Quantum Software Testing
Authors: Asif Mohammed Saad, Ahsanul Ameen Sabit, Jonah Brandenburgh, Jiyuan Wang, Qian Zhang, Soneya Binta Hossain
ID: 2610.22089
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22089

Abstract:
Quantum software relies on compilers, intermediate representations, and
execution frameworks that transform programs before execution. Faults in these
components can change the behavior of otherwise correct quantum programs. Yet
quantum testing techniques are often evaluated using mutation-generated faults
and curated bug benchmarks. We investigate how well the faults represented by
these artifacts reflect those encountered in modern quantum software. We audit
four quantum mutation-testing tools and three curated bug benchmarks, and
collect 410 verified bugs from ten quantum software repositories over two years.
We classify faults from all three sources into a common taxonomy of 56 bug
families and compare their representation across mutation operators, curated
benchmarks, and recent real-world bugs. The mutation tools support 20 bug
families, and curated benchmarks add 15 more. Yet only 56 of the 410 real-world
bugs (13.7%) belong to families represented by either source; the remaining 354
(86.3%) belong to families absent from both. The largest unrepresented families
concern compiler transformations (66 bugs), type handling (53), and input
validation (47). Unlike local gate or statement changes, these faults often
depend on intermediate representations, compiler pipelines, input types, or
dependency versions. Existing mutation operators primarily modify circuits and
program constructs, whereas many real-world faults arise in the compilers,
representations, and framework interfaces that process them. Addressing this gap
requires looking beyond circuit-level faults toward testing semantic
preservation across compiler transformations, correctness at representation
boundaries, and interactions between software components. Our study provides
empirical evidence to guide fault models and benchmarks that capture these
mechanisms and preserve the contexts needed to reproduce them.
