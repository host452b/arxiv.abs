Title: Testing CCZ-equivalence to permutations via projections of Walsh zero spaces, with applications to APN functions
Authors: Emanuele Bellini
ID: 2611.10269
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10269

Abstract:
Whether almost perfect nonlinear (APN) permutations exist in even dimension
$n\ge 8$ is a long-standing open question, the big APN problem. The only example
known in even dimension, on six bits, was found from a quadratic APN function
that is not itself a permutation, by Carlet--Charpin--Zinoviev (CCZ)
equivalence. It is therefore natural to test APN functions for CCZ-equivalence
to a permutation. This amounts to finding two complementary $n$-dimensional
subspaces among the zeros of the Walsh transform, which lie in
$\mathbb{F}_2^{2n}$. A known necessary condition only uses their projections to
$\mathbb{F}_2^n$ and cannot decide every case. We give an exact test for
functions whose Walsh supports are affine, including all quadratic functions. It
reconstructs the subspaces from their projections, through searches for
subspaces of $\mathbb{F}_2^n$ and for constrained bilinear maps. On the
benchmarked functions in dimension twelve, it lists them $39$ to $55$ times
faster than the generic search of the commonly used sboxU library. We then study
the functions $x^3+a^{-1}\mathrm{Tr}(a^3x^9)$, which Göloğlu and Pavlů proved
not to be CCZ-equivalent to permutations when $4$ divides $n$. With their
result, our computations show that this holds for every even $n\le 24$. In
dimension $18$ the necessary condition is satisfied, and the new test decides
the case. We conjecture this for every even dimension. We also compile $25$
classes of quadratic APN functions in dimension ten from published instances,
with equivalence certificates. No function in these classes or in their
switching closure is CCZ-equivalent to a permutation. All code and data are
public.
