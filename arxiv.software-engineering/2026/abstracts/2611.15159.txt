Title: An Empirical Study of Maintainer-Identified AI Slop Pull Requests in Free and Open Source Software Projects
Authors: Hugo Raskin, Xavier Devroey, Benoît Vanderose
ID: 2611.15159
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15159

Abstract:
The FOSS ecosystem has always relied on outside contributions, notably through
issues or Pull Requests (PRs), to sustain itself. With the advance of GenAI, the
barrier to contributing to FOSS projects has never been lower, leading to the
emergence of AI slop PRs: low-quality, low-value generated PRs. Past studies
have examined AI slop PRs through theoretical, platform-level, or practitioner-
account frames, but not at the project level. To this end, this study
investigates the incidence of maintainer-identified AI slop PRs in FOSS projects
by focusing on PRs flagged as slop by maintainers. Using a mixed-methods
approach, we trace the origins of maintainer-identified AI slop PRs, quantify
their volume in FOSS projects, describe their characteristics, and highlight
maintainers' reasons for flagging or closing them. Our results show that the
volume of maintainer-identified AI slop PRs increased in mid-2025, peaking in
May 2026. These PRs are mostly additive, rarely merged, and typically closed
within 3 days. The authors of these PRs disclosed the use of AI in 14% of cases,
with 1% disclosing after a maintainer's request. Most PRs are closed or marked
as slop without any reason provided by the maintainer. Based on our open coding,
reasons range from concrete elements, like build failures, to ideological or
intuitive (i.e., "looks AI to me"). This exploratory study captures a phenomenon
in its early stage and highlights GenAI's potential impact on FOSS, emphasizing
the pressure it puts on maintainers and the need to shift current open-source
development practices.
