Title: When Does UML Software Modeling Pay Off? A Contingency Model of Industrial Modeling Value
Authors: Kleinner Silva Farias de Oliveira
ID: 2610.24884
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24884

Abstract:
With the emergence of specification-driven development (SDD), software modeling
has regained strategic importance as a way to translate human intentions,
requirements, and design decisions into precise specifications that can guide
both developers and AI-assisted development tools. Despite the renewed relevance
of modeling in SDD, empirical evidence remains limited on when its benefits
outweigh the socio-technical effort required to create, communicate,
synchronize, and maintain software models in industrial practice. This study
investigates when software modeling generates value in industrial practice. We
analyzed 405 survey responses collected from software practitioners. The
questionnaire combined closed items concerning UML use, benefits, barriers, and
improvement mechanisms with open-ended questions about current use,
difficulties, and worthwhile contexts. Most respondents associated modeling with
integration discussions (90 percent) and shared understanding (82 percent), but
also reported time pressure (85 percent) and the effort required to create and
maintain models (80 percent). Selective modeling, model-code synchronization,
and collaborative support emerged as conditions that improve its viability,
whereas only 24 percent reported daily UML use. Software modeling pays off when
its expected contextual value exceeds the socio-technical friction required to
create and sustain it. Its value is therefore contingent, not universal.
