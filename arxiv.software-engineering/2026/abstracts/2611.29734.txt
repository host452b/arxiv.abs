Title: Lightweight Spectral Subspace Filtering for Trojan-Resilient AI Inference Engines
Authors: Soumita Chatterjee, Sudip Ghosh, Tamal Ghosh, Bhargab B. Bhattacharya, Hafizur Rahaman
ID: 2611.29734
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29734

Abstract:
Backdoor attacks pose a significant security threat to deep neural networks
(DNNs) deployed on AI inference engines and hardware accelerators. By embedding
hidden triggers during training, these attacks induce malicious behavior while
maintaining high accuracy on benign inputs. Multi-trigger backdoor attacks are
particularly challenging to detect and mitigate because of the distributed and
stealthy nature of their effects. To address this challenge, this paper proposes
a training-free Spectral Subspace Filtering framework that detects and
suppresses backdoor-induced activations by analyzing latent feature
representations. Exploiting the geometric separation between benign and
malicious feature distributions, the framework employs Singular Value
Decomposition (SVD) to identify the dominant malicious subspace and performs
inference-time spectral scrubbing through activation projection. This approach
suppresses backdoor effects without model retraining, requiring only a small set
of triggered samples collected offline. Experimental evaluation across CNN and
transformer architectures, including AlexNet, ResNet18, VGG16, MobileNetV2, and
Vision Transformer (ViT), demonstrates that the proposed method reduces the
attack success rate (ASR) from 94.2-99.8% to an average of 2.07%, while
preserving clean accuracy of up to 98.54% with low inference overhead. The
framework also demonstrates robustness across varying poisoning rates and
multiple concurrent triggers, highlighting its potential as a lightweight
runtime defense for enhancing the security and reliability of AI inference
engines against backdoor attacks.
