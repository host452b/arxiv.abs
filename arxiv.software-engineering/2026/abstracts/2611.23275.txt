Title: AI Code Review Requires Revision-Aware Evidence of Concern Resolution
Authors: Mamdouh Alenezi
ID: 2611.23275
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23275

Abstract:
AI-assisted code review increasingly judges whether reported concerns have been
resolved. Yet closing a thread or modifying a commented line does not establish
repair, and later revisions can invalidate earlier fixes. This position paper
argues that resolution requires inspectable, revision-specific evidence that is
revalidated when its dependencies change. We model review concerns as persistent
verification obligations, derive a sufficient condition under which dependency-
gated revalidation matches exhaustive reassessment, and introduce REVAL, a
lightweight procedure adapting principles from regression-test selection and
build systems. We evaluate REVAL against oracle ground truth in synthetic
revision histories covering 441 concerns, 1,284 assessments, and 12 defect
families. A sticky outdated-line proxy falsely marked 72.9% of unresolved states
as resolved, while stopping checks after initial success missed every
recurrence. With three oracle-sampled witnesses, REVAL achieved a false-
resolution rate of 0.11 (95% CI: 0.06-0.16) and detected 80.4% of recurrences.
With witnesses derived solely from review comments and assessed using
independent held-out checks, the corresponding results were 0.13 and 88.3%.
Across 6,420 paired comparisons, REVAL matched exhaustive reassessment while
reducing post-comment test executions by 34%. Maintenance overhead increased
runtime for microsecond-scale tests; the estimated break-even test cost was 0.42
ms. These findings support revision-aware evidence maintenance in a controlled
setting, while distinguishing revalidation correctness from witness adequacy. A
prespecified evaluation protocol outlines how to assess generalisability in real
review histories.
