Title: Taking Time Out of TOCTOU: From Race Reproduction to Security Discovery
Authors: Hasnain Naeem, Ryan Henry
ID: 2611.21214
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21214

Abstract:
Time-of-check-to-time-of-use (TOCTOU) races are hard to reproduce and lost races
can make vulnerable programs look fixed. We present Chronos, a framework that
separates intervention timing from verdict evidence and builds reusable one-
trial security regression oracles from declared races for deterministic
reproduction, commit bisection and CI. Race Specifications compose reusable hold
engines, actions, observers and outcome rules. When the declared hold is reached
and the attack completes while held, Chronos orders it before the use under
blocking and interference assumptions. Chronos uses seccomp notifications to
enforce declared hold-attack-release ordering at syscall boundaries on stock
Linux; ptrace and a KVM guest gate provide holds at userspace and kernel
instruction sites. One decision procedure labels every mechanism's runs: fixed
requires positive, case-specific evidence of preservation, justified refusal,
omission under checked coverage, or a declared rule; missing or contradictory
required evidence remains indeterminate. On known-vulnerable builds, effect-only
checkers made over 800 false-fixed classifications in failed-intervention
trials; Chronos made none. All 17 syscall-held cases and four of six
instruction-held cases had vulnerable and fixed labels. Two kernel cases remain
one-sided. All 27 historical bisections recovered the expected boundaries. For
runc's masked-path bisection with one victim per launch, fixed retry and Git
Bayesect took medians of 475 and 398 natural launches; Chronos used seven held
trials including endpoint checks. Since a label means the same however a fix is
written, we also ran the specifications and attack variants on releases,
distribution packages and other runtimes. There, Chronos helped us uncover three
issues: incomplete fixes in chrony and rpm, and a Debian rpm package its tracker
listed as fixed.
