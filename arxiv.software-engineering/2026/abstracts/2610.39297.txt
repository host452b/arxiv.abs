Title: Spreading Dynamics on Higher-Order Networks: A Structure-Mechanism-Behavior Perspective with Applications
Authors: Ming Xie, Shibo He, Qihao Huang, Yuzhen Fan, Jiming Chen
ID: 2610.39297
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39297

Abstract:
Spreading in real-world networks, including modern communication and networked
systems, often unfolds through group interactions rather than isolated pairwise
contacts. Higher-order network representations such as simplicial complexes,
hypergraphs, and their temporal, multilayer, and directed extensions provide a
principled way to encode group interactions. Unfortunately, existing studies on
spreading over these substrates remain fragmented and largely model-centric,
offering limited insight into how higher-order representations and contagion
mechanisms jointly shape emergent dynamics. This review addresses this gap by
introducing a structure-mechanism-behavior three-level perspective that provides
a systematic mapping from higher-order network structures and spreading
mechanisms to collective behaviors. Within this framework, we compare how
different higher-order network representations encode group interactions,
introduce a taxonomy of contagion mechanisms, and analyze their structural
carrying capacity, defined as the ability of a given representation to support
specific contagion mechanisms and the associated dynamical behaviors. We further
map specific structure-mechanism combinations to three hallmark emergent
behaviors, thereby offering a unified cross-level perspective beyond existing
model-centric surveys. These insights are particularly relevant for
communication systems involving group interactions, such as social information
spreading, cooperative protocols, and distributed networked processes. Lastly,
leveraging this three-level perspective, we offer a roadmap for guiding and
controlling dynamics on higher-order networks, while discussing future
directions in representation choice, mechanism design, and data-driven
inference.
