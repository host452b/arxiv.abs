Title: Estimating GPU Roofline Arithmetic Intensity with Off-the-Shelf LLMs and Compiler Artifacts
Authors: Gregory Bolet, Giorgis Georgakoudis, Konstantinos Parasyris, Harshitha Menon, Niranjan Hasabnis, Kirk W. Cameron, Gal Oren
ID: 2611.32975
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32975

Abstract:
GPU developers use profiling to understand computation and data movement, but
access to target hardware can delay optimization. We investigate whether off-
the-shelf large language models (LLMs) can estimate Roofline arithmetic
intensity (the ratio of floating-point operations (FLOPs) to device-memory
bytes) without executing the target GPU workload. We evaluate three models
(deepseek-v4-flash, gemini-3.7-flash, and gpt-5.6-sol) across 179 combinations
of five kernel families, four NVIDIA and AMD GPUs, four parallel runtimes (CUDA,
HIP, OpenMP, and OpenCL), and modified code variants, comparing source-only
queries, queries with compiler artifacts, and sandboxed agentic analysis.
Compiler artifacts raise the share of arithmetic-intensity estimates within a
factor of 1.20 of profiled values from 8-14% to 26-32%, while the resulting
bandwidth-bound or compute-bound classifications are correct in 78-92% of runs.
With compiler artifacts, FLOP estimates meet the 1.20x tolerance in 59-88% of
runs, compared with 36-40% for device-memory traffic. Agentic analysis increases
the share of arithmetic-intensity estimates within the 1.20x tolerance by 6.5
percentage points for gpt-5.6-sol, at 3.6x the median cost and 2.5x the median
latency of compiler-assisted queries; the other models incur higher costs
without a statistically clear improvement. These findings suggest LLM estimates
could help prioritize optimization when GPU access is constrained, while
identifying cache-dependent device-memory traffic as the main obstacle to
precise arithmetic-intensity prediction.
