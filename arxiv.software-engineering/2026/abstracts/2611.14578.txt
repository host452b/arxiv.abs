Title: Designing Collaborative Virtual Environments for Software Engineering Education
Authors: Filipe Fernandes, Cláudia Werner
ID: 2611.14578
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14578

Abstract:
Collaborative virtual environments offer opportunities to make software models
available for shared inspection, but educational design and usability need to be
examined separately from claims of learning effectiveness. This article presents
MetaSEE, an approach connecting requirements guidance, a Web XR feature model, a
reusable UML component, and a browser-based multiuser platform. We synthesize
documented formative evaluations of the framework (16 participations), feature
model (15), UML component (9), and platform (14). These counts represent
evaluation participations; the same 12 developers took part in the first two
evaluations. The principal platform study involved six teachers working
individually and eight students working in four pairs through desktop or
notebook computers, keyboard, and mouse. The documentary synthesis traces
educational intentions to implemented features and reported difficulties.
Participants identified potential value in shared modeling and simulation,
alongside navigation, interaction, visualization, and distraction concerns. A
numerical audit reconstructs a student System Usability Scale mean of 67.50 from
published item frequencies, conflicting with the reported 79.38; an incomplete
teacher item distribution prevents recovery of that group's overall mean. The
contribution is a traceable account of educational design and formative
limitations, with practical implications for onboarding, model readability,
collaboration, and evaluation reporting. The evidence supports bounded
feasibility and perceived usefulness, without establishing learning gains,
sustained engagement, or adoption.
