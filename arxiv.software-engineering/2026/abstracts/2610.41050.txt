Title: Closing the Applied Cybersecurity Gap in Critical Infrastructure: A Cyber-to-Physical Security Assessment Framework for Water Systems
Authors: Halil Dursunoglu, Kaan Sulkalar
ID: 2610.41050
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41050

Abstract:
The integration of information technology and operational technology in water
infrastructure creates pathways through which cyber compromise may affect
physical service delivery. This study revisits the Applied Cybersecurity Gap
(ACG) framework by evaluating an experimental Applied Cybersecurity Gap Index
(ACGI) alongside the Cyber-to-Physical Propagation Rate (CPPR), defined as the
proportion of initially compromised trials that produce a specified physical-
impact outcome. The expanded evaluation comprises 2,160 synthetic trials across
three water-network benchmarks, including EPANET Example Network 3 (Net3), and
couples declared cyber-attack progression rules with hydraulic simulations. A
source-trained machine-learning model evaluated on 247 compromised Net3 trials
achieved ROC-AUC 0.772, PR-AUC 0.775, and F1 0.733, compared with 0.661, 0.642,
and 0.704 for a scenario-only baseline. The configuration-cluster bootstrap
interval for the ROC-AUC difference was 0.038--0.183. A separate 720-run Net3
hydraulic operating-point sensitivity analysis achieved the study's exploratory
baseline pressure criterion in all reruns, preserved 717 of 720 physical-impact
labels, and produced ROC-AUC 0.791 with the frozen source-trained model.
Additional analyses examine the sensitivity of CPPR to the physical-impact
threshold and assumed control-blocking probabilities, and demonstrate that the
ACGI--CPPR association is partly induced by the simulation design rather than
independently validated. The findings establish a reproducible simulation-based
assessment workflow, but do not constitute field calibration of cybersecurity-
control effectiveness or prediction of real-world utility incidents.
