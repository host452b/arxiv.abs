Title: Security Authentication as Protective Friction: Understanding the Dual Effects of Authentication Measures in e-Government Platform
Authors: Jianshan Pan, Zongyi Chen, Qiyou Lin, Xiaofang Cai
ID: 2611.19435
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19435

Abstract:
E-Government platforms, such as government credit platforms, handle highly
sensitive personal data, requiring stringent security authentication (e.g.,
facial recognition, multi-factor verification) that protects users but impedes
their experience. Drawing on Tomalin's (2023) e-friction typology and the
emerging literature on beneficial design friction, we reconceptualize these
authentication measures as protective friction, a theoretically distinct form of
non-elective, overt, impeding e-friction that serves a security-enhancing
function. We propose a dual-pathway model in which protective friction
simultaneously reduces privacy-security risk perception (protection pathway) and
increases effort cost (cost pathway), with self-efficacy moderating only the
risk pathway. This differential pattern suggests that what matters is not users'
ability to overcome friction, but their comprehension of its protective purpose,
a construct we term protective mechanism comprehension. We outline a mixed-
methods research design combining a survey of platform users and semi-structured
interviews with platform staff at a Chinese municipal credit center to test this
model. This research-in-progress contributes to IS theory by extending
e-friction research from conceptual typology to empirical investigation,
identifying when security friction becomes beneficial rather than burdensome.
