Title: From Code Review Comments to Reuse in Production: An Industrial Study of Extracted Rules
Authors: Mutsuha Asada, Yuhei Nakasaka, Keito Tabuchi, Shinpei Hayashi
ID: 2611.25918
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25918

Abstract:
Much organization-specific code review knowledge remains embedded in comments on
individual changes, and recent systems reuse it by turning those comments into
rules a language model applies to later changes. Teams must then decide which
rules to keep, and the evidence at hand is how often the system's later comments
name each rule. Tracing the rules of one such system, Kleene, through 65 days of
production use, we found that such a count does not show what a rule
contributed. A rule attribution indicates neither whether the source content was
preserved nor whether the comment requested a change. Of 56 clauses in 12 rules
that later comments named as their reason, 2 were explicit in a source review
thread, 38 added conditions to a corresponding passage, and 16 had no passage we
could locate; 45 of 79 further comments naming those rules requested no change,
27 of them while stating that the rule applied. On 20 constructed code changes,
a reviewer model given only the unedited source thread met the detection
criteria on 8 of the 10 that require a report, against 10 of 10 with the
extracted rule, a difference whose confidence interval includes zero. Stage
counts locate where rules drop out but not why: of 464 rules deployed while the
rule-based reviewer subagent ran in their repository, 259 (55.8%) were named,
195 (42.0%) produced a comment that was not withheld, and 94 (20.3%) one with a
recorded code change; the largest reduction occurred before any rule was named.
Nor did the deployment-time properties we tested settle the choice in advance:
retaining the top-ranked half preserved 53.8% of the rules that later produced a
non-withheld comment, against 49.2% at random, a difference whose confidence
interval includes zero. Judging extracted rules therefore calls for recording
each stage separately and comparing each rule against the threads it was
extracted from.
