Title: Exploiting Aggregate Feedback for Decentralized Equilibrium Learning in Markov Games: A Smart Grid Application
Authors: Siying Huang, Yifen Mu, Ge Chen, Qingshan Jia
ID: 2611.22762
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22762

Abstract:
In many multi-agent systems, an agent's payoff depends on its own action and an
aggregate of other agents' actions, rather than their individual decisions. This
paper investigates decentralized equilibrium learning in Markov games (MGs) with
such aggregative reward structures. However, existing decentralized learning
algorithms for general MGs typically rely on bandit feedback, without exploiting
the additional information available from aggregate observations. To exploit
this information, we develop an aggregation-predictive stage-based V-learning
algorithm with hybrid-feedback Exp3-IX. Using known local reward functions and
observed aggregate quantities, each agent evaluates the immediate rewards of all
candidate actions and predicts their immediate losses from historical aggregate
feedback, while continuation values are learned from selected-action state
transitions. The algorithm requires neither opponents' individual information
nor knowledge of the transition kernel. We establish a high-probability regret
bound that explicitly depends on aggregate prediction errors and retains the
standard worst-case order. Under a regularity condition on adaptive stage
lengths, the algorithm learns an $ε$-approximate coarse correlated equilibrium
(CCE) within $\widetilde O(SA_{\max}T^5/ε^2)$ episodes and extends to correlated
equilibrium (CE) learning. We further apply the algorithm to a renewable-rich
microgrid with strategic prosumers and shared energy storage. Numerical results
show smaller deviation gaps and lower gap-AUC than three learning baselines.
Compared with representative demand-response rules, the learned policies exhibit
weaker incentives for unilateral deviations while maintaining favorable user
rewards, high renewable utilization, and relatively stable electricity prices.
