Title: Pooled Testing under Congestion: Operational Scaling Laws for Pool-Size Selection
Authors: Ioannis Dimitriou, Johan van Leeuwaarden
ID: 2610.37729
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37729

Abstract:
Classical pooled-testing rules choose pool sizes to reduce the number of tests.
In practice, however, samples arrive over time, pools take time to form, testing
capacity is finite, and positive pools create bursts of follow-up work. These
operational features can make the test-efficient pool size far from the delay-
efficient one. We formulate pooled testing with stochastic arrivals and finite
testing capacity as a queueing system, and derive exact performance
representations and asymptotic scaling laws for fixed-batch Dorfman testing and
a dynamic two-threshold policy. The analysis reveals three distinct design
regimes. When test usage is the main concern, the classical Dorfman rule is
recovered and the optimal pool size scales with the inverse square root of
prevalence. When delay accumulated while the laboratory is busy testing another
batch matters, positive pools create service-time variability that grows more
rapidly with pool size than the mean retesting burden, and the optimal scale
shifts to the inverse cube root of prevalence. When waiting to assemble a pool
is itself costly, the optimal fixed pool size remains bounded even as prevalence
becomes very small. Under severe capacity scarcity, congestion remains relevant
at the classical Dorfman scale: the square-root law survives, but with a
capacity-dependent constant. Dynamic pooling separates two operational decisions
that fixed pool sizes conflate. A lower threshold determines when routine
testing should begin, while an upper threshold limits pooling when backlog is
high. The lower threshold follows the cube-root scale, whereas the upper
threshold primarily acts as congestion control. The results show how prevalence,
capacity, and delay jointly determine pooling decisions and explain why
operationally optimal pools can be much smaller than those suggested by test-
efficiency calculations alone.
