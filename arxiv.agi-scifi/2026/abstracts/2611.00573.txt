Title: Scaling Laws for Parameter Setting in Digitized Counterdiabatic QAOA
Authors: Yaocheng Chen, Jiapei Zhuang
ID: 2611.00573
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00573

Abstract:
Optimizing parameters separately for each problem instance limits the reuse of
variational quantum algorithms. We develop empirical scaling laws for parameter
setting in the digitized counterdiabatic quantum approximate optimization
algorithm (DC-QAOA) for weighted MaxCut. The prescription combines exact
covariance under uniform edge-weight rescaling with power laws in mean degree
fitted separately to offline-optimized cost and counterdiabatic angles. After
the weight and degree scales are removed, the optimized angles of different
training graphs cluster around common amplitudes, so a small coefficient table
suffices. Frozen coefficients assign angles from a target graph's mean degree
and root-mean-square edge weight without target-instance variational
optimization. In ideal-circuit simulations across 1050 test graphs spanning ten
graph families and five positive edge-weight distributions, mean optimal-
solution probability is higher than that of the fixed-angle quantum approximate
optimization algorithm (QAOA) at each depth from one to four layers, reaching
19.36% versus 13.11% at four layers. A six-layer extension on 600 graphs also
shows a mean probability advantage. Hardware execution on the IBM Quantum
processor ibm_fez for 21 unit-weight 3-regular graphs shows a mean probability
advantage at one layer, while the ordering reverses at two layers. In separate
energy-based experiments, direct assignment gives a higher mean approximation
ratio than random-start optimization reaches within 500 evaluations, and
refinement started from the formula angles improves it further. These results
show how a scaling-based representation of optimized angles can guide parameter
reuse across graph instances, enabling direct parameter assignment without
target-instance variational optimization and providing initial angles for local
refinement.
