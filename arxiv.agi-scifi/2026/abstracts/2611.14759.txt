Title: U.S. Data Center Growth Through 2030: A Critical Review of Grid Adequacy and Transmission Expansion Planning
Authors: Babak Porkar, Mona Ghassemi
ID: 2611.14759
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14759

Abstract:
The growth of large data centers raises questions about both supply adequacy and
the suitability of load representations used in connection studies. This paper
critically reviews the U.S. demand, generation, and transmission outlook through
2030 and examines the implications for transmission expansion planning (TEP).
Comparison of public forecasts highlights distinctions among annual average
demand, peak demand, and grid connection capacity. National generating-capacity
totals cannot establish a dependable surplus because resource availability,
regional adequacy, transmission constraints, and project timing determine supply
capability. Recent grid events and published dynamic studies demonstrate that
faults can trigger substantial data-center transfers to backup supply, followed
by delayed or coordinated demand recovery. These behaviors require appropriate
representation within the conventional planning framework, alongside assessment
of the requested maximum grid import. The selected literature reveals limited
integration of validated data-center dynamic models into the technical screening
and economic comparison of expansion alternatives. The review identifies the
need to establish whether fault response and recovery change feasible connection
capacity, required reinforcement, or the preferred transmission investment.
