Title: Human-AI Co-Ideation Across Socio-Technical Configurations: An Interactional Analysis of Planar and Immersive Workspaces
Authors: Orit Shaer, Mary Calabro, Zoe Mitchell, Angelora Cooper, Raffaella Sadun, Andrew L. Kun, Osnat Mokryn
ID: 2611.21125
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21125

Abstract:
Remote collaborative platforms increasingly integrate generative AI to support
group ideation. These platforms are sociotechnical configurations in which
spatial mediation, interaction modality, AI output format, and the mechanisms
that make contributions visible to a group co-vary as properties of a deployed
system. This paper examines how such configurations shape participants'
collaboration and their engagement with AI during creative work. We report a
comparative study of two commercially available platforms that instantiate
contrasting configurations: a planar shared canvas with text-based AI
integration, and an immersive virtual reality workspace with concise AI output
embedded in the shared space. Fourteen all-women triads completed a structured
ideation task consisting of human-only idea generation, AI-supported idea
expansion, and group convergence. We analyzed collaboration patterns, selection
of AI-generated ideas, perceived idea quality, workload, presence, and their
lexical characteristics. The immersive configuration yielded greater spatial
presence and introduced a participation paradox, in which spatial freedom
enabled exploration and fragmented group coherence; it was also associated with
more balanced role distribution during convergence, at the cost of higher
physical demand. In both configurations, AI multiplied the idea pool, and ideas
groups authored with AI available were rated above participants' own. AI output
format: about 21 words per idea on the planar canvas and 3 in the immersive
workspace. Tracked how participants evaluated AI-generated ideas and how often
they selected them unaltered as finalists. Neither configuration made AI
contributions visible or attributable to the group: participants invoked AI
individually and asynchronously in both. We derive design implications for AI
output format, group-visible AI invocation, and spatial support for convergence
