Title: Labor Market Returns to Education and Intra-family Educational Inequality: Theory and Experimental Evidence
Authors: Esther Gehrke, Michelle González Amador
ID: 2611.02959
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02959

Abstract:
How do labor market conditions shape educational inequality within- and between
families? A growing body of evidence documents convex wage returns to education
in labor markets across the world. We develop a theoretical model that
demonstrates that earnings convexity can increase or decrease between-sibling
differences in investments and outcomes depending on the parameter of parental
inequality aversion. We recover the distribution of this structural parameter
from a highly-incentivized lab-in-the-field experiment with 246 parents in San
Luis Potosí, Mexico, in which token allocations determine children's access to
an AI course. Parents make allocation decisions across six scenarios that vary
convexity in the payoff function and endowment-investment substitutability. We
identify three types of parents: Rawlsian parents display perfect inequality
aversion (12.6%), interior parents that are moderately inequality tolerant
(76.8%), and Utilitarian parents who maximize total payoffs (10.6%). While these
three groups respond to earnings convexity in heterogeneous ways, on average,
greater convexity increases the share allocated to the high-endowed child,
thereby widening within-family inequality.
