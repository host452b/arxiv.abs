Title: Invariance before structure: two modes of emergent compression in representation learning
Authors: Hardik Rajpal, Dan Goodman
ID: 2611.07952
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07952

Abstract:
Learned representations show striking invariance across biological and
artificial neural networks, with different substrates and variations driven by
drift, heterogeneity and initialisation. To understand this invariance, we
propose Emergent Adaptive Representation Learning (EARL), a framework that
distinguishes two modes of emergence in learned representations: state-space
compression, where a learned representation gains predictive power over its own
future beyond what the network's units can predict individually, and parameter-
space compression, where networks with different micro-level parameters converge
to the same representation. We formalise both as Fisher sensitivities and
introduce ensemble-level information-theoretic measures for each. In a recurrent
reservoir with a trained bottleneck, parameter-space compression rises within
the first few epochs of training and partly independently of the task, whereas
state-space compression rises about 30 epochs later and only when the network
begins to learn the temporal structure of the task. These results suggest that
invariance to the substrate and task-specific emergent structure are acquired in
distinct phases of learning.
