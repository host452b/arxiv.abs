Title: What the annual energy balance hides: the coordination value of digital platforms for positive energy districts
Authors: Arega Getaneh Abate, Per Sieverts Nielsen, Xiufeng Liu, Brian O'Regan, Gerfried Cebrat, Cigdem Karadag, Valentina Zaccaria, Yang Liu, Madalin Silion, Ami Vadiee et al.
ID: 2611.29485
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29485

Abstract:
Positive energy districts (PEDs) are usually assessed by annual net energy,
whereas distribution networks operate hour by hour. An annually positive
district still imports on low-generation hours and exports or curtails on high-
generation hours. How large this hourly imbalance is behind an annual balance,
and how much of it coordination among prosumers can close without new physical
capacity, is not established. This paper models a multi-carrier prosumer
community coupling electricity and heat behind a shared grid connection with a
net-export hosting limit. The model is solved as a centralized community problem
and as a price-taking peer-to-peer (P2P) market. Under convexity, the
equilibrium implements the regularized community optimum and the P2P and
congestion prices are the duals of the pool-clearing and hosting constraints.
The model is calibrated to six European district archetypes with constructed
hourly profiles. Hourly self-sufficiency is only 48-50% in the four deeply
studied districts and ranges from 48-55% across all six, leaving a no-platform
coordination gap of 2.8-5.9% of no-platform economic cost. The shared-forecast
step recovers only 6-18% of the gap, while grid-aware pricing reduces
curtailment by 45% in the primary district. Coordination mainly reduces surplus-
side inefficiency, whereas closing the deficit side requires storage. The
results are scenario-based projections for calibrated archetypes, and the
operating benefit is small and contingent on binding hosting limits and high
feed-in remuneration. For regulators and distribution system operators this
supports hourly, bidirectional indicators alongside the annual positive-energy
target, and a congestion charge active only in binding hours with a
predetermined refund, while the business case for a platform at district scale
rests on grid-side value and aggregation across districts.
