Title: Towards a Unified Taxonomy of Agricultural Harvesting Technologies: Linking Physical Interaction, Automation, and Selectivity
Authors: Renato Cristiano Torres, Fabiano Mariath D Oliveira, Claudio Gottschalg Duque
ID: 2610.13688
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13688

Abstract:
Harvesting technologies have evolved from conventional mechanized systems based
on bulk mechanical interaction toward increasingly automated and robotic systems
capable of perception, decision-making, and localized actuation. This evolution
has also created a classification problem, as harvesting systems are commonly
described according to crop type, mechanical removal principle, sensing
technology, robotic architecture, or degree of automation, making direct
comparison difficult. This paper proposes a unified taxonomy for agricultural
harvesting technologies based on two primary classification axes: the physical
principle governing plant-machine interaction and the level of automation
incorporated into the harvesting process. Selectivity is treated as an emergent
functional attribute that expresses the biological and spatial scale at which
perception and decision-making influence physical actuation. Conventional
mechanized methods, including cutting, pulling, vibration, impact, brushing,
pneumatic action, and combined systems, are analyzed together with semi-
automated and robotic harvesting approaches. The analysis shows that increasing
automation does not necessarily result in greater selectivity. A qualitative
transition occurs when perception and decision-making directly influence
product-level actuation, shifting harvesting from distributed or bulk
interaction toward localized and selective manipulation. The proposed framework
provides a common conceptual basis for positioning and comparing mechanized,
automated, and robotic harvesting technologies. By separating the physical
mechanism of product removal from the automation level and resulting
selectivity, the taxonomy clarifies technological boundaries, identifies
intermediate configurations, and supports the analysis and development of future
harvesting systems.
