Title: How Latent Transition Geometry Matters for Planning in World Models
Authors: Xuanya Zhu, Da Li, Xiatian Zhu
ID: 2611.30064
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30064

Abstract:
World models support planning by predicting how the environment evolves under
candidate actions. However, one-step predictive accuracy alone does not
characterize multi-step planning utility, which depends on distinguishing
candidate futures. This raises a natural question: How does latent transition
geometry shape planning? We investigate this through an action-conditioned
transition-field view, using curvature regularization as a controlled
intervention. Across four continuous-control tasks, appropriately tuned
regularization improves planning, while excessive regularization can be
detrimental. By decomposing the transition field into affine and non-affine
components, we find that regularization reduces action sensitivity mainly
through the non-affine component. To assess its role in planning, we directly
manipulate the non-affine action response in frozen models and show that it
affects candidate selection and closed-loop success. Further analysis shows that
curvature generally brings predicted futures closer together, while preserving
more of the differences along the goal direction. This helps explain why the
planning effects vary across tasks. We observe broadly consistent planning
trends across five sampling- and gradient-based solvers on LeWM and similar
rollout-geometry trends on DINO-WM. Qualitative attention visualizations reveal
accompanying shifts toward task-relevant visual structure. Together, these
findings clarify how latent transition geometry influences multi-step planning.
