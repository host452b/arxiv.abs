Title: Designing sustainable high-entropy alloys directly from mixed oxides: A thermodynamic perspective
Authors: Albert Linda, Shiv Shankar, Dierk Raabe
ID: 2610.30870
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30870

Abstract:
Hydrogen-based direct reduction (HyDR) of mixed oxide precursors offers an
alternative route to produce sustainable alloys, bypassing the sequential
extraction, refining, and remelting steps that dominate the carbon footprint of
conventional metallurgy and individual metal production. However, the
thermodynamic limits and most effective H$_2$/H$_2$O ratio needed for full
metallization of multicomponent oxides are not yet established. This is because
the formation of metallic and oxide solid solutions alters the chemical activity
of both the reduced metallic phases and the unreduced oxides, coupling their
equilibria in ways that cannot be deduced from the stability of the pure
individual oxides. Herein, we develop a CALPHAD-based thermodynamic framework to
establish the compoistion-temperature-H$_2$/H$_2$O window for complete
metallization of CoCrFeMnNi-based oxide mixtures. Mn and Cr generally increase
oxide stability, while Co, Fe, and Ni decrease it, allowing reduction at lower
H$_2$/H$_2$O ratios. The effect of each element is not fixed; it depends on the
starting mixed oxide composition and can reverse when the last oxide to reduce
changes. Furthermore, we show that the reduction pathway complexity does not
correlate with the difficulty of removing the final oxygen: a system with few
intermediate transformation steps can still terminate as a highly stable
residual oxide, whereas extensive partial reduction may leave the stability of
the final residual oxide essentially unchanged. By linking residual-oxide
stability to the H$_2$/H$_2$O ratio, the framework identifies the thermodynamic
limits and provides guidelines for designing sustainable multicomponent alloys
directly from mixed oxide precursors.
