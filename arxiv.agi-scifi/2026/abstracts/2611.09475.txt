Title: Validation of Habitable Zone Limits with a New Radiative-Convective Equilibrium Climate Model
Authors: Jacob Haqq-Misra, Eric T. Wolf, Ravi K. Kopparapu
ID: 2611.09475
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09475

Abstract:
The habitable zone (HZ) boundaries calculated with the Clima one-dimensional
radiative-convective equilibrium (RCE) model have served as a standard reference
for exoplanet target selection for three decades, but they have never been
independently validated with a comparable model. We present ExoColumn, a new
one-dimensional RCE model that couples an independently developed atmospheric
column physics package to the ExoRT correlated-k radiative transfer core that is
also used by the three-dimensional ExoCAM model. We calibrate ExoColumn to
present-day conditions, which matches other RCE models to within a few kelvin
through the troposphere. Line-by-line benchmarks show that the ExoRT and Clima
radiation cores each reproduce the outgoing longwave flux to within a few
percent, with the differences due to the spectral line databases used by each
model. We follow the same inverse climate modeling method as previous studies
have done to find inner edge moist- and runaway-greenhouse limits of 0.970 au
and 0.966 au, and an outer-edge maximum-greenhouse limit of 1.611 au at a CO$_2$
partial pressure of 8.7 bar. We extend these calculations to 12 host stars
spanning 2000-7200 K and to planets of 0.1, 1, and 5 Earth mass, and we provide
updated HZ boundary coefficients for community use. The ExoColumn limits
systematically fall closer to the host star than those of Clima but agree to
within a few percent in orbital distance, which validates the robustness of the
published Clima HZ boundaries.
