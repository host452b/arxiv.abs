Title: Quantum Gravity from Fractal Entanglement Geometry
Authors: Jaume Gine
ID: 2607.26035
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26035

Abstract:
In this work, we explore the possibility that spacetime geometry may emerge from
the entanglement structure of an underlying quantum-information network. We
develop an exploratory framework, termed Fractal Entanglement Quantum Gravity
(FEQG), in which information-theoretic correlations provide a candidate
pregeometric structure from which aspects of geometry, quantum dynamics, and
gravity may arise. An information-based distance on the correlation graph is
considered as a possible means of relating quantum correlations to geometric
separation. The framework also investigates whether scale-dependent and fractal
properties of the underlying structure could lead to dimensional flow,
potentially approaching an effective dimension of two near the Planck scale.
Within a scale-relativistic perspective, nondifferentiable trajectories and
stochastic descriptions are examined as possible ingredients in an effective
quantum dynamics; however, the emergence of the Schrödinger equation from the
network structure remains to be established. Similarly, the relation between
evolving correlations and gravitational dynamics is explored through candidate
generalized field equations, while recovering Einstein gravity in an appropriate
macroscopic limit remains an open problem. Potential phenomenological
consequences include dimensional reduction, modifications to gravitational
potentials, and possible deviations from standard quantum mechanics at
ultrashort scales. These possibilities are not presented as established
predictions, but as directions for further investigation. FEQG thus provides a
research program for examining whether quantum-information structure, fractal
geometry, and gravitational phenomena can be connected within a common
framework.
