Title: Quantifying the hosting capacity value of workload flexibility for AI data centers: A two-stage electricity-computation co-optimization framework
Authors: Jiakai Wu, Wei Sun, Pengyu Ren, Yifan Wang
ID: 2611.24506
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24506

Abstract:
Grid connection constraints increasingly limit the expansion of AI data centers,
yet the additional hosting capacity enabled by computational workload
flexibility remains insufficiently quantified. This paper develops a two-stage
electricity--computation co-optimization framework to quantify the individual
and coordinated hosting capacity values of spatial and temporal workload
flexibility. Data center capacities are determined endogenously, with
computational task arrivals scaling with admitted capacity. Spatial routing of
eligible interactive requests and temporal deferral of batch workloads are
linked to server operation and facility power demand, subject to workload
completion requirements and transmission constraints. The first stage maximizes
aggregate hosting capacity; the second fixes the resulting site capacities and
optimizes annual workload scheduling and generation dispatch to minimize
generation and carbon costs. An 8760-hour case study with three data centers on
a modified IEEE 118-bus system shows that temporal and spatial flexibility
individually increase hosting capacity by 21.26\% and 5.37\%, respectively.
Their coordinated use raises hosting capacity from 4635.1~MW to 6231.5~MW, a
34.44\% increase without network reinforcement. The coordinated gain exceeds the
sum of the individual gains by 361.9~MW, demonstrating complementarity between
spatial migration and temporal deferral. By redistributing computational demand
across locations and operating hours, these mechanisms enable fuller utilization
of existing transmission capacity. The findings establish workload flexibility
as a quantifiable planning resource for accommodating additional data center
capacity within existing transmission networks.
