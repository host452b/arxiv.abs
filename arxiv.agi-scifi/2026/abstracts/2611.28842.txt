Title: Learning How a Body Moves: Few-Shot Body Inference for Visual World Models of Animated Characters
Authors: Amarnath R
ID: 2611.28842
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28842

Abstract:
Two visually identical animated characters can respond differently to the same
action command because their body dynamics differ. For example, a stiff blob and
its bouncy twin reach different heights when both receive the jump command.
Given only the first frame and the action command, a visual world model has no
information to distinguish these twins, because their initial appearances are
identical. We therefore propose an in-context world model that encodes clips of
a character's other actions into a body representation, using a contrastive loss
to separate look-alike characters. We introduce ToonTwins, a benchmark of 144 2D
cartoon characters that run, jump, fall, turn and crouch. Its characters form
look-alike groups of four that differ only in their dynamics profile, and we
also evaluate the model on 32 animated animals from DeformingThings4D. Even with
a body code from these clips, the latent pixel world model we tested recovered
at most 25 percent of the twins' jump height difference. On new shapes, our
model identified the correct twin in 85.9 percent of cases, compared with 76.7
percent for a transformer baseline that reads the same clips without a body
summary, and in a controlled ablation the contrastive loss gave more than half
of this gain. On the animated animals, it reduced the mean path prediction error
by 8.5 percent against a model without body information.
