Title: Digital Twins for Multi-Agent Systems: When to Trust, What to Change
Authors: Truc Nguyen Thi Le, Phuoc Nguyen T. H
ID: 2611.28517
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28517

Abstract:
Digital twins of agent networks need to flag unreliable forecasts and help users
choose interventions. Both decisions depend on what happens over several steps:
an error can spread through the network, and an edit whose trajectory is easy to
predict may still yield a poor collective outcome. We ask when information about
error propagation improves failure detection and whether a learned twin can
select effective network edits despite imperfect forecasts. We propose
Propagation-Aware Validation for Networked Digital Twins (PAV-Twin). Its monitor
combines Jacobian-based propagation features with recent errors and ensemble
disagreement to estimate the risk that a forecast exceeds an error threshold.
Its planner simulates each candidate edit and ranks the edits by their predicted
collective outcomes. In simulations, the monitor gained 0.13 in area under the
precision-recall curve on larger oscillator networks with familiar topologies
and 0.09 on larger networks with held-out topologies, relative to a graph
baseline without propagation features. Gains were small in contractive
consensus. Giving the graph baseline the same features narrowed the gap. In a
separate planning study, ensemble simulation recovered about 99% of the possible
improvement over random intervention selection in oscillators and nearly all of
it in consensus. With a simplified oscillator twin, selecting by predicted
outcome also gave lower regret than selecting by trajectory fidelity. These
results show when propagation features help flag unreliable forecasts and why
the quality of intervention choices should be measured directly.
