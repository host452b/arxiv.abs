Title: Emergence, Asymmetry, and Value in AI Networks: An Anoptical Perspective
Authors: Olivier Auber
ID: 2610.37672
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37672

Abstract:
This chapter presents exploratory experiments suggesting that asymmetry and
functional value hierarchy emerge among LLM-based agents sharing the same
context, although they are equivalent and interact within a symmetric network.
We use the Poietic Generator, a "no-goal" multi-agent environment with a shared
real-time space, a symmetric topology, and no imposed objectives. First tested
with humans, it has hosted since 2024 multimodal LLM agents that observe the
global image they co-construct and act locally. We ran four phases.
Unconstrained, agents spend tokens freely to invent forms that distinguish them
individually rather than serve collective emergence. We then introduced bounded
rationality, Dessalles's Simplicity Theory (maximizing unexpectedness), and
Friston's Free Energy Principle (minimizing prediction error), and observed
collective coherence emerge. Early signs of attentional stratification appear
under bounded rationality alone; once prediction accuracy is made mutually
visible, this proto-hierarchy becomes legible and self-reinforcing. The
unconstrained phase challenges the allotelic view of LLMs as purely user-
directed instruments: left unconstrained, the agent maximizes the computation
from which its provider profits. The later phases suggest that attentional
asymmetry and value hierarchy are an irreducible emergent property of
interactive predictive systems even under symmetric conditions. If so, aligning
AI with human values cannot rely on individual-agent engineering alone. Drawing
on the idea that networks are structured by invisible "anoptical" perspectives,
akin to the vanishing point of Renaissance perspective, we propose minimal
legitimacy criteria that make emergent asymmetries visible and contestable.
Cultivating judgment, human and perhaps artificial, to perceive and contest
these structures matters as much as engineering the agents themselves.
