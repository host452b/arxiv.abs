Title: Controlling the latency in temporal coded spiking models for neuromorphic datasets
Authors: Alban Gattepaille, Alexandre Muzy
ID: 2610.19732
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19732

Abstract:
Spiking neural networks (SNNs) promise energy-efficient, event-driven
computation on neuromorphic hardware, where response time, or latency, matters
as much as energy for real-time applications. Time-to-first-spike (TTFS) coding
with learnable synaptic delays is a natural way to exploit the timing of events.
However, in state-of-the-art spiking neuron models, spike times emerge from
nonlinear membrane dynamics and thresholds: no closed-form expression links
network parameters to spike times, so latency can only be measured empirically,
not specified or guaranteed at design time. Moreover, TTFS models are mostly
evaluated on static datasets, which hides their latency on truly temporal data.
Our hypothesis is that, if spike times are computed directly as closed-form
functions of presynaptic spike times and synaptic delays, bypassing membrane
potential and thresholding, latency becomes an explicit function of the
learnable parameters that can be bounded, and therefore controlled,
analytically. We build on this hypothesis with the Delay Neural Network (DeNN)
in real time, which turns event streams into successive single-spike images,
computes each spike time from standardised inter-spike intervals and synaptic
delays, and links successive images through a long-term memory. We prove lower
and upper bounds on the latency of every neuron of a layer, and show that
bounding the synaptic parameters after training with a single hyperparameter
places the latency anywhere between these bounds, without retraining. On a ten-
class subset of the Google Speech Commands dataset, this post-training control
exposes the accuracy--latency trade-off: a three-hidden-layer network reaches
94.25\% accuracy while deciding, on average, 477~ms after the end of the one-
second input, and about 250~ms with confidence-based early decisions. Learning
is also shown to concentrate the long-term memory on a few neurons. Latency thus
becomes a design parameter of spiking models rather than a quantity measured
after the fact, a step towards neuromorphic systems with real-time guarantees.
