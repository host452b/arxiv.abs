Title: Foundations of the Scaling Law
Authors: Tianyu Zhao, Zhixiang Ren
ID: 2610.27789
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27789

Abstract:
Empirical scaling laws relate loss to parameters $N$, training tokens $D$ and
compute $C$. This technical note explains three mechanisms behind their
exponents. Local approximation bounds function error at rate $N_e^{-γs/d}$, with
effective parameters $N_e$, smoothness $s$, reuse exponent $γ$ and dimension
$d$; the loss function determines the risk bound. Independent feature coverage
gives $α_D=(β-1)/ζ$ from predictive-weight decay $β$ and occurrence rarity $ζ$.
Separable-loss optimization determines training allocations and deployment
crossovers. Nonlinear scoring can produce apparent emergent abilities, while
grokking concerns delayed generalization and verification constrains test-time
scaling. For regular molecular configurations, the $\mathrm{SE}(3)$ quotient
gives $d_{\mathrm{int}}=d-6$ and relative approximation-exponent gain
$6/d_{\mathrm{int}}$. Optimized dense-to-equivariant excess-loss ratios scale as
$C^{p_{\mathrm{eq}}-p_{\mathrm{dense}}}$, where $p_j$ are compute-loss exponents
and the prefactor depends on loss amplitudes and FLOP costs. Domain examples
also cover visual resolution, conformational coverage and correlated-sample
precision.
