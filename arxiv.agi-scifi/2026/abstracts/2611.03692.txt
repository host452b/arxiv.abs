Title: Matter-Only Late-Time Acceleration via Barrow Entropy in Kaniadakis Statistics
Authors: Navaneeth Preman P K, Sarath Nelleri, Sreerag Radhakrishnan, Pranav Prasanthan, Nandhida Krishnan P, Emmanuel Tom
ID: 2611.03692
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03692

Abstract:
The Barrow correction to the horizon entropy changes the total number of
microstates associated with the horizon, whereas the Kaniadakis correction
provides an alternate statistical description of these microstates different
from Boltzmann-Gibbs statistics. In this paper, we consider the Kaniadakis
description of the Barrow-corrected horizon entropy analogous to its description
of the standard horizon entropy. The modified Friedmann equations were derived
for an $(n+1)$-dimensional non-flat universe through two different approaches
--- the law of emergence and the first law of thermodynamics. The generalised
second law of thermodynamics was investigated, and the density contrast
evolution equation was also derived. We take the universe to be composed of only
matter and constrain the model parameters through an MCMC analysis using
observational Hubble data, Pantheon+ Type Ia supernovae data, baryon acoustic
oscillation distances from the DESI DR2 data release, and $fσ_{8}$ measurements
from redshift-space distortions. The Kaniadakis parameter $K$ was constrained
away from zero, showing a preference for the $K$-deformed statistical approach.
Motivated by recent studies, we considered a wider parameter space for the
Barrow index, $Δ\in (-1,1]$. In agreement with observational data, we obtained
$Δ= -0.335\pm0.031$, motivating a horizon structure different from Barrow's
sphereflake configuration. The present value of the Hubble parameter was
constrained as $H_{0}=(66.98 \pm 0.71)$ km/s/Mpc, which leans towards the early-
universe Planck estimates; however, late-time data alone are insufficient to
study the Hubble tension problem. Analysing the evolution of various
cosmological parameters, we conclude that the modified Friedmann dynamics of the
model produces a viable expansion history and late-time acceleration without
invoking a dark energy component.
