Title: Understanding the Effects of Multi-Agent Deliberation for Human-AI Decision-Making
Authors: Harry Yizhou Tian, Ming Yin
ID: 2611.14901
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14901

Abstract:
Multi-agent deliberation (MAD), where multiple LLMs engage in structured
discussion, improves AI performance on many benchmarks, but whether it helps
humans make better decisions remains untested. We conduct two user studies on
LLM-response-verification and fact-checking. We first compare MAD against common
AI decision-support methods that isolate its two defining dimensions ---
multiple AIs and multi-round interaction. We find MAD outperformed single-round
AI assistance on both tasks; multi-round interaction was the main driver of this
benefit, while a second AI added value when the single AI had room to improve.
Then, we study two design choices in MAD --- AI persona and human engagement
level --- finding a devil's advocate persona to lower accuracy and user
experience, while humans' active participation in MAD improved accuracy in one
task at the cost of frustration. We conclude by discussing implications for
when, why, and how multi-agent deliberation can support human decision-making.
