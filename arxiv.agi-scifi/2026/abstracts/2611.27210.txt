Title: The Rise of Agents and the Labor Market
Authors: Nicola Borri, Anton Cheremukhin, Yukun Liu, Aleh Tsyvinski
ID: 2611.27210
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27210

Abstract:
We provide the first economy-wide and cross-AI model evidence on the effects of
agentic AI on labor demand. We combine proprietary OpenRouter data covering
about two quadrillion tokens of realized AI consumption (2 percent of global AI
token consumption) across more than 400 AI models, including 1.1 quadrillion
agentic tokens, with the full text of 34.8 million U.S. job postings for public
firms from Lightcast. We compute our measure of firm-level exposure to aggregate
agentic AI consumption (agentic beta) using the comovement of U.S. public firms'
stock returns with realized weekly aggregate agentic AI token consumption. We
find that a one-standard-deviation increase in a firm's exposure to agentic AI
predicts 2.11 percent lower growth in job postings over the next six months and
3.20 percent over nine months. We then conduct an event study based on the
release of Claude Code 2.0, which significantly expanded the capabilities of AI
agents, and show that a one-standard-deviation higher pre-release exposure to
agentic AI predicts 4.73 percent lower posting growth over six months post the
release. Furthermore, we show that the decrease in job postings is concentrated
among non-senior jobs and is statistically insignificant for senior jobs. In
contrast, we find no statistically significant relation between broad AI
realized consumption exposure and subsequent job posting growth once agentic
exposure is controlled for. Finally, we validate our market-implied agentic
exposure measure using independent evidence from firms' mentions of AI
deployment in securities filings and earnings calls.
