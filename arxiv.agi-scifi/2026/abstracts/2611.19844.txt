Title: A Speech-LLM Framework for Enrollment-Free Wake-Word-Guided Target-Speaker Recognition in Complex Noisy Scenarios for Seamless Human-Machine Interaction
Authors: Yuxiang Mei, Dongxing Xu, Yanhua Long
ID: 2611.19844
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19844

Abstract:
Target-speaker automatic speech recognition (TS-ASR) is essential for reliable
human-machine interaction in complex acoustic environments, where overlapping
speech and background noise make it difficult to identify and recognize the
intended speaker. Existing approaches typically rely on speaker enrollment or
cascaded processing pipelines, limiting their flexibility in practical
applications. To address these limitations, we propose an enrollment-free
Speech-LLM framework that leverages wake-word text as a lexical cue to guide
target-speaker recognition. Specifically, a text-cue encoder extracts lexical
representations from the wake word, which are integrated into a pretrained
speech encoder through cross-attention to establish an implicit association
between the wake-word occurrence and the target speaker. This enables the model
to selectively recognize subsequent commands from the target speaker without
requiring enrollment speech or explicit wake-word boundaries. Furthermore, a
large language model (LLM) unifies command transcription and non-target speaker
rejection within a single autoregressive framework. To improve robustness
against complex acoustic interference, we develop a large-scale speech synthesis
and simulation pipeline with a progressive four-stage training strategy.
Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed framework achieves a command
character error rate of 16.15% and a non-target speaker rejection rate of
91.56%, yielding a unified score of 87.70%, compared with 71.70% for the
strongest enrollment-free baseline. These results demonstrate the effectiveness
of wake-word-guided speaker selection and highlight the potential of integrating
target-speaker recognition and rejection within a unified Speech-LLM framework
for robust human-machine interaction.
