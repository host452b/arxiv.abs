Title: Entropy-Based Wireless Technology Recognition
Authors: Mohammad Alhellani, Eli De Poorter, Mamoun Guenach, Adnan Shahid
ID: 2610.23158
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23158

Abstract:
Wireless Technology Recognition (WTR) enables identifying wireless technologies
from received radio signals and is important for spectrum monitoring and
management systems. Existing approaches either rely on handcrafted signal
features with limited robustness or computationally intensive Deep Learning (DL)
models operating on high-dimensional In-phase and Quadrature (IQ) samples or
spectrograms. In this paper, we investigate a different hypothesis: wireless
technologies can be distinguished through the temporal evolution of their local
time-frequency complexity. To capture this behavior, we propose a lightweight
WTR framework based on two-dimensional dispersion entropy. Entropy values are
extracted from successive spectrogram regions to characterize the evolution of
local spectrogram complexity throughout a transmission and summarized into a
compact feature representation for classification using a Multilayer Perceptron
(MLP). The framework is evaluated on seven wireless technologies, namely IEEE
802.11ah, IEEE 802.15.4 SUN-FSK, IEEE 802.15.4 SUN-OFDM, LoRa, IEEE 802.11n,
LTE, and 5G NR, at Signal-to-Noise Ratios (SNRs) ranging from -20 dB to 40 dB.
Experimental results show that the proposed approach consistently outperforms
IQ-based and Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT)-based Convolutional Neural
Network (CNN) baselines, particularly at low and moderate SNRs. Averaged across
all evaluated SNR levels, it achieves 82.73% accuracy, corresponding to
improvements of 23.8% and 14.6% over the IQ-based and STFT-based CNN approaches,
respectively. Furthermore, the approach maintains its recognition advantage with
data-augmented training while requiring substantially shorter training times.
The results demonstrate that temporal evolution of local spectrogram complexity
provides a robust technology-specific fingerprint and an effective,
computationally efficient representation for WTR.
