Title: Benchmarking Classical and Quantum Machine Learning for Intrusion Detection Across Multiple Datasets
Authors: Taha M. Mahmoud, Naima Kaabouch
ID: 2610.33119
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33119

Abstract:
Intrusion detection is examined through a comparative benchmark of classical and
quantum machine learning models on three datasets: NSL-KDD, UNSW-NB15, and
MQTTEEB-D2025. The analysis focuses on how preprocessing decisions, feature
selection strategies, and quantum encoding schemes shape model performance on
data with differing levels of noise and complexity. A single unified pipeline is
applied, covering normalization, class-imbalance handling, dimensionality
reduction, and two feature selection methods: Random Forest importance and a
quantum-aware approach that combines Quantum Kernel Alignment with Mutual
Information (QKA-MI). Four classifiers are compared: Support Vector Machine,
Random Forest, Quantum Support Vector Machine, and Pegasos Quantum SVM. The
classical models perform consistently across all three datasets, whereas the
quantum models depend more strongly on feature representation and kernel
alignment. Quantum-aware feature selection yields marked gains for the quantum
models, especially on cleaner datasets, while heterogeneous traffic remains
difficult. Pegasos Quantum SVM offers a good trade-off between accuracy and
computational cost, underscoring the role of preprocessing alignment in
practical quantum intrusion detection.
