Title: Scaling Laws for SAE Training Data
Authors: Nikita Koriagin, Nikita Balagansky, Daniil Gavrilov
ID: 2611.07756
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07756

Abstract:
Activation storage is often a bottleneck in sparse autoencoder (SAE) training.
We study how many unique activations an SAE needs when its total training budget
is fixed. Quality improves rapidly as the buffer grows, then reaches a plateau
where replaying the buffer is nearly as effective as using fresh activations
throughout training. Beyond this point, additional unique activations bring
little benefit to reconstruction or interpretability, so the buffer need not
grow with the training budget. We describe this tradeoff between diversity and
repetition with a scaling law for limited data. We validate it across dictionary
sizes and token budgets, early, middle, and late layers of Llama-3.1, Qwen3
model sizes, and BatchTopK, TopK, and JumpReLU SAEs. Sweeps using mathematical
text and Python code test the effect of changing the activation source.
Evaluation covers explained variance (EV), reconstruction MSE, feature
absorption, automated interpretability, and sparse probing. These results give a
practical training rule: choose the smallest buffer that reaches the quality
plateau and replay it for the remaining budget. In our experiments, this reduces
activation storage by $8\text{--}64\times$ with negligible quality loss.
