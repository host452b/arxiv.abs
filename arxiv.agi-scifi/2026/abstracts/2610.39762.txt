Title: Neuromorphic design of a rhythmic network
Authors: Robin Wroblowski, Rodolphe Sepulchre
ID: 2610.39762
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39762

Abstract:
Central Pattern Generators (CPGs) have long inspired the design of rhythms that
combine endogenous generation and exogenous interaction. In neurodynamics and
engineering studies, CPGs have been primarily analysed and designed as networks
of coupled oscillators. In contrast, the present article regards CPGs as
networks that propagate events through excitable rather than oscillatory nodes.
The proposed event-based perspective reduces the design of network-level
rhythmic properties to the tuning of local nodal input-output characteristics.
This reduction enables a distributed and robust design methodology: nodes can be
tuned independently, they can be heterogeneous, and the global rhythm results
from local event propagation rather than from a specific global tuning of the
network parameters. The present article builds on previously introduced concepts
that parallel existing characterizations of oscillators: the event describing
function (eDF), which characterises the propagation delay of a node, and the
event phase response curve (ePRC), which characterises the phase sensitivity of
a node. Event-based CPGs made of excitable rather than oscillatory nodes emerge
as a principled design framework for robust rhythm generation.
