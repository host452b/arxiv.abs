Title: From Anoxic to Oxic Earth: Detectability of Atmospheric Biosignatures across Stellar Hosts with ELT/ANDES
Authors: Dwaipayan Dubey, Spandan Dash, Liton Majumdar
ID: 2610.41621
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41621

Abstract:
Earth's atmosphere evolved from an anoxic, methane-bearing Archean state through
Proterozoic oxygenation to the O$_2$-rich modern atmosphere. However, most
biosignature detectability studies focus on modern Earth, leaving earlier
atmospheric states comparatively unexplored. We investigate how atmospheric
evolution and stellar environments influence the detectability of O$_2$, CH$_4$,
H$_2$O, and CO$_2$, and their potential to distinguish anoxic from oxygenated
atmospheres. We construct two complementary photochemical grids: representative
Archean, Proterozoic, and modern-Earth scenarios around the Sun, and four
geological epochs (3.9, 2.0, 0.8 Ga, and modern Earth) across FGKM hosts.
Combining one-dimensional photochemical modeling, high-resolution transmission
spectral simulations, and instrument-noise modeling, we assess molecular
detectability with ELT/ANDES under optimistic photon-noise-limited conditions.
For solar-host Archean and Proterozoic models, CH$_4$ requires approximately 33
and 42 transits, respectively, to reach the adopted 5$σ$ criterion, whereas
O$_2$ remains below 3$σ$ within the observing limits. Modern-Earth O$_2$ reaches
3$σ$ after approximately 38 transits. Across the FGKM grid, O$_2$ does not reach
5$σ$ for the 3.9 Ga models. However, For the M3V, K2V, and M8V proxies, modern-
Earth O$_2$ reaches 5$σ$ in 9, 15, and 20 transits, respectively, whereas CH$_4$
requires 11, 12, and 28 transits at 3.9 Ga and only 2--4 transits across the
oxygenated epochs. These results reveal a strong O$_2$ detectability contrast
around M and K-dwarfs, but not Sun-like hosts in the YJH bands. Robust
biosignature interpretation requires complementary molecular constraints,
including informative non-detections, within the context of atmospheric
evolution and stellar environment.
