Title: Parallel Design of a GPU-Resident Semi-Lagrangian Drift-Kinetic Solver on Unstructured Extruded Tokamak Meshes
Authors: Mark F. Adams
ID: 2610.12585
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12585

Abstract:
Semi-Lagrangian (SL) schemes advect a distribution function along
characteristics and remap it to a fixed grid. They are unconditionally stable
without a global solve, and with discontinuous Galerkin (DG) integration the
transfer along a magnetic field line conserves mass exactly. This paper presents
the parallel and GPU design of a 3X+2V SLDG drift-kinetic tokamak solver on
unstructured, flux-surface-aligned poloidal planes of shaped cross section,
extruded toroidally. Transport follows magnetic field lines across the planes,
and each velocity channel advances a fixed distance per step, proportional to
its parallel speed, so the halo depth a channel needs grows with its speed.
Velocity-dependent ragged halos exploit this, sending each channel only its own
upwind depth. The 4D decomposition includes the toroidal angle, the 2D poloidal
plane and the magnetic moment, and leaves the parallel velocity whole: no stage
transports across the magnetic moment, while a parallel-velocity partition would
put a halo inside the acceleration stage. On NVIDIA A100s the design strong-
scales over the magnetic-moment axis from one to 12 four-GPU nodes, and a weak
series of three points refining all five phase-space dimensions together, 16x in
total per series point, holds a weak-scaling efficiency of 75% on 16 GPUs and
57% on 256, from a one-GPU base case. The pointwise interpolations within the
poloidal plane do not conserve mass, and the full step's mass drift stays below
2.1x10^-5 at every series point. The design was implemented with an AI code
assistant under a machine-checked regression discipline, in which every change
is held to one of four exactness classes, from bit-identical to a physics rate
within a stated tolerance.
