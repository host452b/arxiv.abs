Title: Infrastructure and its Discontents: Contestations of Power in Canada's AI Governance Scaffold
Authors: Christelle Tessono, Nusrat Jahan Mim, Shion Guha
ID: 2610.38077
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38077

Abstract:
This paper develops a critical theoretical framework for understanding national
Artificial Intelligence (AI) strategies through the lens of "Governance as
Infrastructure." Integrating insights from Infrastructure Studies, Science and
Technology Studies (STS), this paper employs Black Feminist Technoscience (BFTS)
to reveal how AI governance infrastructures are assembled, maintained, and
contested, and whose forms of knowledge, labour, and experience are rendered
visible or invisible in these processes. We analyze the Canadian AI governance
landscape through in-depth interviews with bureaucrats, civil society advocacy
groups, academic researchers, and industry actors. Our inductive thematic
analysis reveals how AI governance infrastructure enacts specific configurations
of power, particularly through the politics of expertise; how actors navigate
profound ambiguities related to AI's opacity, often involving strategic
ignorance; and how mechanisms for justice and accountability struggle to address
AI-related harms. We contend that a BFTS perspective highlights the limitations
of current governance approaches and points toward alternative infrastructural
designs prioritizing care, maintenance, participatory democracy, and adaptive
temporalities to foster more equitable and accountable technology governance.
