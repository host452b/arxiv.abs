Title: AMLBench: A Real-Incident Benchmark for Agentic Blockchain Anti-Money Laundering
Authors: Qishuang Fu, Hang Zheng, Yuchen Lei, Yuexin Xiang, Qin Wang, Yuanzhe Zhang, Shiping Chen, Andreas Deppeler, Joseph Liu, Ziyao Liu et al.
ID: 2611.02530
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02530

Abstract:
Tracing illicit funds on public blockchains remains largely manual. As funds
move across accounts and undergo asset conversions, they become mixed with other
inflows, requiring repeated queries and expert judgment to trace. Large language
model (LLM) agents offer a way to automate this process, but their ability to
reconstruct case-related transfer networks from limited clues remains unclear.
To bridge this gap, we present AMLBench, an anti-money laundering (AML)
benchmark built from 24 real-world cases and 5.05 million Ethereum transfer
records. For each case, agents start from limited clues and explore these
transfer records through composable transaction-query and fund-flow analysis
tools, deciding which accounts and branches to pursue. The final tracing results
are evaluated against case annotations, while tool interaction logs reveal how
agents discover and select relevant transfers and at what resource cost. We also
develop AMLAgent, which integrates fund-tracing guidance and query feedback to
dynamically guide investigative decisions. With GPT-6 Astra, AMLAgent achieves
macro averages of 80.82% transfer-level precision and 84.73% query coverage
across 24 real-world cases. Further analysis of the large-scale Bybit case
reveals substantial differences in transfer retention and token usage across
models, even with similar submission precision. AMLBench establishes a
reproducible benchmark for evaluating both tracing outcomes and investigative
processes, laying the groundwork for more reliable and efficient autonomous AML
investigations.
