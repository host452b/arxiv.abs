Title: Nudging Decision Models: Do Digital Nudges Steer Fast, Generation-Free AI Agents?
Authors: Sascha Kaltenpoth, Haya Halimeh, Kevin Bösch, Oliver Müller
ID: 2611.09264
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09264

Abstract:
LLM-based agents increasingly automate business and consumer tasks but are slow
and expensive. Decision models, a new class that chooses among options without
generating text, are far faster and less costly. Developers call them "System
One models," referencing dual process theory. We ask whether such models can be
steered by digital nudges online and whether, as the theory predicts, automatic
nudges affect them more than reflective ones. We adapted an online shopping
experiment, previously run with humans and LLM agents, to three decision models
(Jev, Decision-1, d1). Across 600 runs per model, they chose a preselected
default in 97.5-99.9% and a product with social influence information in 75-96%
of choices, versus 47-49% in the control. All responded more strongly to the
automatic nudge, the opposite of what has been reported for reasoning-capable
LLM agents. We provide first evidence that non-generative decision models are
susceptible to digital nudges in a System 1 pattern, implying agent-type-
specific nudge effects.
