Title: Scaling Laws For Data-Constrained Dense Retrieval
Authors: Bowen Fu, Jingtao Zhan
ID: 2611.04979
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04979

Abstract:
Dense retrieval models are typically obtained by fine-tuning pretrained language
models on supervised retrieval data, where high-quality annotations or
interaction logs are often limited and reused across many epochs. This data-
constrained regime differs from the abundant-data setting assumed by standard
scaling laws, making it unclear how the best attainable retrieval performance
changes with model size and unique fine-tuning data. We study this question
through the minimum achievable evaluation loss (MAEL), defined as the lowest
evaluation loss reached during training under fixed model-size and data-budget
constraints. We first show that learning-rate choices strongly affect
optimization trajectories but have little effect on the final attainable MAEL:
after rescaling the optimization-step axis, learning curves collapse across
learning rates, and CKA analysis shows that checkpoints reaching MAEL learn
highly similar representations. We then fine-tune 35 BERT-family dense
retrievers on MS MARCO subsets and find that MAEL is accurately described by a
simple multiplicative power law over non-embedding parameters and unique
samples, outperforming commonly used additive scaling formulations. We further
derive a scaling law for the training step at which MAEL is reached and show
that optimal weight decay follows an approximately invariant EMA timescale,
yielding lower MAEL and stronger retrieval performance on both in-domain and
out-of-domain benchmarks. Finally, combining loss and step scaling suggests
that, in our observed regime, increasing pretrained model size is approximately
1.46 times as fine-tuning-compute-efficient as increasing unique in-domain data
for reducing MAEL.
