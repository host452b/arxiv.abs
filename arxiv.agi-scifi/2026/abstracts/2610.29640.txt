Title: Entangled Futures: Quantum Computing's Potential and Challenges in Computer Science
Authors: Devki Nandan Jha, Jonte R. Hance, Tomasz Szydlo, Nick Chancellor, Omer F. Rana, Rajiv Ranjan
ID: 2610.29640
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29640

Abstract:
Quantum computing represents a fundamental shift in computation, promising to
solve problems currently intractable for classical computers. However,
misconceptions persist; quantum computers are not universally faster, nor do
they replace classical systems. Current Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum (NISQ)
devices face significant challenges, including decoherence, high error rates,
and limited scalability. Additionally, leveraging quantum systems requires
rethinking traditional computing principles, adopting different programming
models, and addressing the complexities of integrating quantum and classical
workflows, making their practical use in computer science both demanding and
non-trivial. This article provides a system-level perspective on quantum
computing for computer scientists, software engineers, and IT professionals,
examining its current capabilities, key architectural and software challenges,
and potential paths toward quantum advantage in real-world applications within
hybrid quantum-classical environments.
