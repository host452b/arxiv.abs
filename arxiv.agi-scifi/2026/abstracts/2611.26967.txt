Title: Data-Driven Detection of Volatility Regimes and Sector-Level Asymmetry During Sovereign Default
Authors: M. J. S. Ahamed, M. R. Hatheem, M. A. H. Ahamed, U. I. Jayasekara, H. Y. Ahamed, Madara Mendis, Nevidu Jayatilleke, Nisansa de Silva, Sandareka Wickramanayake
ID: 2611.26967
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26967

Abstract:
We present a data-driven framework for detecting changes in stock-market
volatility and characterising their effects across industry sectors. The
framework identifies volatility regimes directly from historical returns using
dynamic programming, validated with a sup-F test and a Markov-switching model.
We apply it to 1,559 trading days of the Sri Lankan All Share Price Index (ASPI)
and 13 sector indices, covering pre-COVID, COVID-19, sovereign-default, and
recovery periods. Within each regime, Student-$t$ EGARCH models quantify
volatility response, persistence, and asymmetry between positive and negative
shocks. The results reveal a distinct regime-specific pattern: significant
negative-shock asymmetry occurs in 9 of 14 series during the sovereign default,
compared with none during COVID-19 despite 2.6 times higher volatility than pre-
COVID. For the ASPI, volatility persistence decreases from 0.936 during COVID-19
to 0.647 during the default. Cross-sector correlations increase during the
default but largely disappear after adjusting for increased variance, providing
little evidence of stronger shock propagation. Macroeconomic variables explain
less than 4% of ASPI forecast-error variance at a 30-day horizon. These findings
demonstrate how combining automated regime detection with interpretable time-
series models can uncover structural changes hidden by aggregate analysis.
