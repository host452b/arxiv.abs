Title: Connecting Coronal Loop Scaling Laws and Kink Wave Heating: An Analytical and Numerical Study
Authors: Luka Banović, Tom Van Doorsselaere, Max McMurdo, Haruka Washinoue, Anna Krupka
ID: 2611.26202
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26202

Abstract:
Turbulent dissipation of kink waves has been proposed as one of the mechanisms
responsible for the observed high coronal temperatures. We test this hypothesis
by examining the effect of this heating mechanism on coronal loops. The coronal
loop static and dynamic behaviour is investigated under the assumption that the
heating in the loop is primarily provided by kink wave dissipation. The heating
term is borrowed from the recently developed UAWSoM model. Loop modelling is
done in 1D using three different methods: one analytical following the
principles first set in the RTV scaling laws, and two numerical: one based on
the setup used to derive the RTVS scaling laws, and UAWSoM. We provide scaling
laws and estimate density, temperature, and pressure, as well as kink wave
heating rate and kink wave energy density needed to counteract energy loss by
thermal conduction and radiative cooling. Estimates of kink wave energy density
needed to heat the loop to 1 MK correspond to maximum velocity amplitudes of
around 35 km s$^{-1}$ near the loop apex. Loop radius, density contrast and
filling factor are found to affect the predictions significantly. The two
numerical methods agree to a high degree. The analytical method predicts that
$W_\textrm{k} \propto PL^{-2/3}$, while the two numerical methods show a wider
range of scaling exponents which depend on the location in the loop. UAWSoM
simulations suggest that loops may exhibit dynamic behaviour for a wide range of
loop conditions, which may include thermal non-equilibrium cycles and periods of
emerging and dissipating slow waves. Overall, estimates of kink wave energy
density and scaling laws provide observational constraints on the importance of
the proposed kink wave heating mechanism, with the current preliminary results
suggesting that the uniturbulent kink wave heating may be a significant source
of energy and heat in coronal loops.
