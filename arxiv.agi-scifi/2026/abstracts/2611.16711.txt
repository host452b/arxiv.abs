Title: Directionally Supervised Neuromorphic Protection for Distribution Systems Using Local Measurements
Authors: Saurabh Prabhakar, Yubo Song, Pascal Schirmer, Bijaya Ketan Panigrahi, Subham Sahoo
ID: 2611.16711
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16711

Abstract:
Reliable protection under varying power system operating conditions requires
rapid fault detection while preserving selectivity against external faults and
non-fault disturbances. This requirement is particularly challenging in
communication-free schemes, where relays must determine trip conditions solely
from local electrical measurements. To address this challenge, this paper
proposes a directionally supervised neuromorphic protection scheme that
integrates disturbance-responsive leaky integrate-and-fire dynamics with a
direction-dependent firing threshold to distinguish internal faults from
reverse-direction events. The unified relay architecture combines local
disturbance detection, directional discrimination, and spike-based trip
generation without requiring inter-relay communication. Its performance is
evaluated under grid-connected and islanded conditions using a ring microgrid
and the IEEE 13-node distribution feeder. Across 240 evaluated fault scenarios,
the framework achieves median operating times of 5.4--7.6 ms across the two
networks, with 78% of cases operating within 10 ms and 95% within 20 ms. It also
maintains protection security during load disturbances, while fault-resistance
analysis confirms its response under high-resistance faults. Controller-
hardware-in-the-loop (CHIL) experiments using an RTDS simulator and an external
dSPACE controller demonstrate trip-command assertion in approximately 10 ms and
5 ms for the tested single-line-to-ground and three-phase-to-ground faults,
respectively. The results establish real-time feasibility within the evaluated
conditions and characterize operating-time variability across fault types and
resistance levels for different network configurations.
