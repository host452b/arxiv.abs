Title: Quantum computing for high energy nuclear physics
Authors: João Barata
ID: 2611.05491
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05491

Abstract:
Quantum information science (QIS) offers a new set of conceptual and
computational tools ideally suited to exploring the many-body, out-of-
equilibrium, and real-time properties of strongly interacting quantum systems.
These themes find a natural counterpart in high energy nuclear physics, whose
central goal is to describe the properties of Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD), the
theory of the strong interaction, under extreme conditions. Relevant examples
range from the formation and internal structure of hadrons, to the collective
and thermalization dynamics of strongly interacting QCD matter, and the
properties of complex many-body scattering processes. Although tremendous
progress has been made over the past decades in our understanding of QCD in the
nuclear physics context, through perturbative methods, lattice QCD, and
effective theory descriptions, phenomena that are intrinsically connected to
real-time dynamics, highly entangled many-body evolution, or finite density
regimes remain only partially accessible with existing approaches. In this
article, we argue that QIS can help realize a new paradigm for addressing these
open questions. With the aim of making the discussion accessible to both high
energy nuclear physics and QIS audiences, we provide an overview of selected
open problems in high energy nuclear physics, with emphasis on aspects motivated
by collider phenomenology, and discuss how they can be approached using QIS
tools. This connection is illustrated through representative examples drawn from
the recent literature, making the case that QIS can become a driving force for
discovery in nuclear physics in the coming years.
