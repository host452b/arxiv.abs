Title: World Models and the Law
Authors: Claudio Novelli
ID: 2611.29899
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29899

Abstract:
Current AI may have a model of how we use human language, but this does not
translate into a reliable representation of physical reality. World models
promise to fill this gap by learning how an environment changes over time and
predicting the consequences of an action, and they are already used to steer
robots and generate explorable worlds. This article asks how AI regulation in
three major legal contexts, the European Union, California, and China, copes
with the distinctive challenges they raise. I propose a definition and a
taxonomy of world models based on who or what acts on their predictions, and
identify the risks of misplaced reliance on them. I argue that existing rules
tend to cover world models but leave three questions open, i.e., action
provenance, trajectory validity, and the weight of simulated evidence, and
recommend ways to fill these gaps.
