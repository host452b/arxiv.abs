Title: Self-governed access control in open robot swarms using a blockchain-based DAO
Authors: Raina Zakir, Alexandre Pacheco, Andreagiovanni Reina, Marco Dorigo, Volker Strobel
ID: 2611.03975
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03975

Abstract:
Robot swarms harness simple, local interactions among many individually limited
robots to produce complex, coordinated collective behavior, making them valuable
across domains from agriculture to disaster response. We envision the next
frontier for such swarms as open swarms, in which robots join or leave the swarm
dynamically at runtime. The ability to dynamically adjust swarm size has been
shown to be beneficial, for instance by reducing inconsistencies and network
partitioning, especially under sparse connectivity or in complex environments,
ultimately improving task performance. For practical deployment, however, open
swarms requires governance, particularly protection against Byzantine behavior
(i.e., faulty or malicious robots), since allowing robots to join freely also
makes the swarm easier to infiltrate. Blockchain technology has recently been
incorporated into robot swarms, offering secure, decentralized coordination and
improved resilience, though largely in closed settings with a fixed,
predetermined membership. In this work, we extend these benefits to open swarms
by introducing robot swarms governed by a blockchain-based decentralized
autonomous organization (DAO), in which the swarm regulates itself using
autonomous rules encoded in smart contracts. We outline several DAO-based
governance mechanisms for robot swarms, depending on the needs of the
application, and present a proof-of-concept implementation of a simple DAO that
regulates swarm membership and prevents Byzantine infiltration. Our simulation
results and real-robot validations show that DAO-governed access control and
robot removal can maintain proper swarm functioning while keeping the number of
Byzantine robots within acceptable limits.
