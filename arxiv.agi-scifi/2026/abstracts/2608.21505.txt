Title: Earth as a Closed Life-Support System: Finite Buffers, Dynamical Thresholds, and Planetary Sustainability
Authors: Jean-Pierre Gazeau
ID: 2608.21505
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21505

Abstract:
We formulate planetary sustainability as a minimal nonequilibrium problem for
coupled finite stocks and fluxes, using the operational language of closed life-
support systems: inventories, flux balances, recycling efficiencies, processing
capacities, reserve times and failure thresholds. Rather than asserting that
waste is universally more limiting than resources, we propose a reserve-time
principle: the binding constraint is whichever buffer becomes critical first. A
nonlinear model coupling a regenerative resource stock, an accumulated waste
stock and an activity variable exhibits a soft threshold, where waste suppresses
growth while processing remains formally capable, and a hard threshold, where
waste production exceeds maximal processing capacity. With immediate waste
feedback, activity settles globally at the soft threshold; the hard threshold is
crossed only transiently or under exogenous forcing. A resource-side threshold
completes the picture. We extend the model to recycling and to multiple resource
and waste classes, give a numerical example, situate the four-function
description of life against the origin of life, viruses and aging, contrast the
biosphere's two long-term failure modes, and relate the approach to Spaceship
Earth, planetary boundaries, Limits to Growth, System Dynamics and stock-
pollution economics.
