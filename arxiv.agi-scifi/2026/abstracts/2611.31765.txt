Title: Dreamcatcher: Remembering What Surprises a World Model in Video Streams
Authors: Jinxi He, Ce Zhang, Wenhe Liu, Jianshu Zhang, Katia P. Sycara, Yaqi Xie
ID: 2611.31765
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31765

Abstract:
As live video streams grow longer, repeatedly encoding and processing their full
histories becomes increasingly costly. Answering questions as they arrive
therefore calls for visual memory built in one pass before future questions are
known. Existing methods mainly keep tokens that differ from earlier ones or
match surrogate queries fixed in advance. Without modeling world dynamics, these
criteria struggle to tell subtle surprising details from expected change. We
introduce Dreamcatcher, a training-free framework and, to our knowledge, the
first to use predictive signals from an independently pretrained, frozen video
world model for online memory formation in streaming video question answering.
Conditioned on recent history, the world model predicts incoming features using
learned world priors. Its normalized prediction errors yield temporal and
spatial surprisal signals that guide online segmentation and the retention of a
frozen Video-LLM's native visual tokens, balancing surprising details with broad
visual coverage under budgets that decay with segment age. At query time, an
evidence gate admits a segment from a similarity-based shortlist only when the
Video-LLM judges that it adds evidence for the question beyond the current view.
We evaluate Dreamcatcher on three streaming and three offline long-video
benchmarks. On OVO-Bench, Dreamcatcher achieves the highest overall accuracy
among the compared training-free methods for each of four frozen Video-LLMs,
reaching 78.9% with Qwen3.8-27B. With the three Qwen Video-LLMs, it also reduces
average input tokens for answer generation by more than 80% relative to their
base models at 1 fps.
