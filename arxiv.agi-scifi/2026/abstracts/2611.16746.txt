Title: Topological Entropy of Group Cellular Automata is Effectively Computable
Authors: Niccolò Castronuovo, Alberto Dennunzio, Jarkko Kari, Luciano Margara
ID: 2611.16746
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16746

Abstract:
Topological entropy is a fundamental invariant of dynamical systems, quantifying
their complexity. For cellular automata, however, determining topological
entropy is generally algorithmically intractable. We study group cellular
automata, namely cellular automata over a finite group alphabet whose global map
is a group endomorphism. In dimension one, we decompose topological entropy into
two one-sided components describing information propagating from the left and
from the right in space--time. We introduce finite families of left and right
traces and prove that the entropy is the sum of their growth rates. Hence, the
entropy is completely determined by the growth rate of a fixed-width family of
space--time patterns. This reduction from arbitrarily large windows to uniformly
bounded width yields an effective procedure for computing the entropy. In
particular, the entropy of every one-dimensional group cellular automaton is the
logarithm of a positive integer. Using Pontryagin duality and the Bridge Theorem
between topological and algebraic entropy, we also derive an explicit formula
for linear cellular automata over $(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^n$ in terms of their
characteristic polynomial. Moreover, in every dimension $D\geq 2$, a group
cellular automaton is either equicontinuous with zero entropy or sensitive to
initial conditions with infinite entropy. Combined with decidability of
equicontinuity, this implies that topological entropy is effectively computable
from the local rule in every dimension. Thus, group cellular automata form a
natural nontrivial class in which algebraic structure restores computability of
topological entropy.
