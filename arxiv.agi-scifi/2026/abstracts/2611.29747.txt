Title: Sharpe Ratio Inference after Machine Learning
Authors: Sander Barendse, Ming Cheng
ID: 2611.29747
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29747

Abstract:
The economic value of return signals is often assessed using the Sharpe ratios
of portfolios based on those signals. In modern asset pricing, return signals
often depend on many features, including state variables and firm-specific
characteristics, and machine-learning methods are increasingly used to estimate
them. But how much confidence can we place in the estimated Sharpe ratios? We
find that signal-estimation error can remain consequential for inference even in
large asset panels. Moreover, pooling observations across assets does not
necessarily make this uncertainty negligible when predictors contain common
features or exhibit substantial cross-sectional dependence. As a remedy, we
develop a debiased Sharpe-ratio estimator that accounts for signal-estimation
error and provides valid confidence intervals and tests against benchmarks. Our
target is the population Sharpe ratio of a specified portfolio rule evaluated at
the true conditional expected returns given the features. The framework covers
market-timing, long-short, and other signal-based portfolios, allowing the
number of assets to grow with and greatly exceed the time-series sample size.
The method combines orthogonal portfolio moments with time-series cross-fitting
and remains valid under suitable estimation-error bounds for machine-learning
forecasts. Empirically, we revisit the setting of Gu et al. (2020) using
machine-learning estimators of the return signals. The strongest long-short
Sharpe-ratio gains relative to market benchmarks remain significant after
debiasing, providing statistical evidence of the economic value of their feature
set. Conventional and debiased Sharpe-ratio estimates are generally similar,
while the associated confidence intervals and tests can differ substantially.
These differences vary across signal estimators and portfolio rules and do not
uniformly weaken the evidence of portfolio performance.
