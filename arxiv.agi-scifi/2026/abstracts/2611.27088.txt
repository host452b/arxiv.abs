Title: Railroads as Cyber-Physical Systems: Cybersecurity Implications of Increasing Automation -- Threats, Consequences, and Operational Resilience
Authors: Syed Owais Athar, Mohsin Kamal, Aemal Khattak, Muhammad Naveed Aman
ID: 2611.27088
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27088

Abstract:
Objectives: Rail automation improves train protection, traffic management,
inspection, and operational efficiency but increases dependence on software,
communications, remote access, and external providers. This paper examines its
impact on railroad cyber-physical system security and operational resilience.
Methods: A narrative review synthesizes railroad automation literature, OT
security guidance, FRA and TSA materials, and documented rail cyber incidents.
Automation functions are mapped to exposed assets, attack vectors, and
operational consequences, focusing on signalling, communications, onboard
systems, remote access, and human oversight. Findings: Automation shifts rail
risks from isolated mechanical failures toward interconnected cyber-physical
dependencies. Key vulnerabilities involve train detection, positioning,
signalling, grade crossings, remote diagnostics, vendor access, and AI-enabled
monitoring. Cyber incidents can cause operational disruption, supply-chain
impacts, and national-security risks, even when fail-safe mechanisms prevent
catastrophic outcomes. Novelty: The paper introduces a rail-specific taxonomy
connecting automation functions, cyberattack pathways, operational consequences,
and resilience requirements, framing cybersecurity as an operational rather than
purely IT concern. Practical Applications: The framework supports security-by-
design procurement, IT/OT segmentation, secure remote access and software
updates, degraded-mode planning, trusted recovery, and human oversight.
Operators and regulators can use it to assess automation deployments and
mitigate cyber risks.
