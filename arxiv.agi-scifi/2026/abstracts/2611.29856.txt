Title: World Models Are the Right Foundation for Simulating Mars Terraforming
Authors: Sameera S Kashyap, Akshitha Nagaraj, Razvan Marinescu
ID: 2611.29856
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29856

Abstract:
Terraforming Mars is a long-horizon, action-conditioned control problem that
requires predicting atmospheric responses to interventions over centuries to
identify effective policies. This position paper argues that physics-regularized
recurrent state-space world models are the most promising foundation for this
setting. Data-driven climate emulators lack action-conditioning, and physics-
based general circulation models (GCMs) are too expensive for policy search.
Among learned world models, recurrent state-space architectures with physics
constraints are better suited than transformer-based alternatives: they provide
native uncertainty quantification through their stochastic-deterministic
decomposition, remain stable under sparse non-terrestrial training data, and
enforce physical consistency at each rollout step. To make this position
concrete, we describe a potential architecture combining JEPA-inspired latent
encoding, universal differential equation constraints, and a Dreamer-lineage
planning head. We also introduce MarsWorld, an open benchmark proposal for
intervention-conditioned planetary world models, and discuss broader
implications and objections.
