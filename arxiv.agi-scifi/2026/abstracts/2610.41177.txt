Title: TraceSpec: Understanding and Improving Speculative Decoding for LLM Agents
Authors: Jihao Zhao, Shuaishuai Zu, Pengfei Li, Biao Qin, Guanghuan Fang, Ben Feng, Baochuan Yang
ID: 2610.41177
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41177

Abstract:
LLM agents execute tasks through repeated model calls over evolving histories of
reasoning, actions, and tool feedback. Aggregate speculative-decoding metrics
merge these distinct decisions, providing limited guidance on where draft
supervision is needed. We present TraceSpec, a framework connecting trajectory
evaluation with adaptive draft training. It combines dynamic execution with
fixed-prefix replay and introduces TraceSpec-Traj, a corpus of 10,000 training
states and 2,500 test states spanning browsing and software engineering.
Analysis of five speculative baselines reveals that aggregate acceptance can
rise with context length while declining within matched late browsing actions.
Workload and phase further distinguish these trends, motivating supervision
guided by trajectory conditions. TraceSpec trains on intermediate assistant
turns and allocates a fixed prediction budget using state-conditioned estimates
of prefix-aware acceptance gaps. Across three target sizes and two temperatures,
it improves replay throughput by 12.5% to 22.6% over its DSpark base. Qwen3-4B
ablations show that adaptive allocation increases accepted length by 3.3% to
10.0% over fixed full-turn supervision, with additional comparisons
substantiating the roles of state conditioning and consecutive-token agreement.
The resulting evaluation resources, trajectory findings, and training strategy
connect the analysis of agent execution to practical draft improvement.
