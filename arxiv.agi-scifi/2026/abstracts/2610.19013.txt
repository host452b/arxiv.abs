Title: Data Density Scaling Laws for Image Self-Distillation
Authors: Alvard Barseghyan, Ani Vanyan, Hakob Tamazyan, Hrant Khachatrian
ID: 2610.19013
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19013

Abstract:
Current scaling law formulations suggest that increasing the number of unique
samples in a dataset consistently improves model performance, while repeated
exposure to the same samples provides diminishing value. In this work, we
challenge this assumption for image self-distillation models. We train iBOT
models on random subsets of seven datasets spanning satellite imagery, driving
scenes, egocentric video, and natural images, and evaluate their frozen
representations on downstream tasks. We propose a density-interaction scaling
law that combines unique-image count $U$ and repetition $R$, fitting it on runs
with at most 50 million image presentations and testing it at larger budgets
$C$. On Maxar dataset, a random subset containing approximately 1.5% of the
training pool improves average nearest-neighbor classification accuracy by 3.11
percentage points over training on the full pool at the same budget of 200
million image presentations. Smaller subsets also outperform the full pool on
SATLAS and Ego4D, whereas ImageNet favors larger pools at higher budgets. At a
fixed subset size, further training can improve performance, lead to a plateau,
or reduce performance. Our law captures smaller-subset advantages that the
compared baselines cannot represent and improves prediction at higher budgets on
Maxar, SATLAS, and Ego4D under nearest-neighbor evaluation. These findings show
that both the number of unique images and how often they are repeated matter
when allocating a training budget.
