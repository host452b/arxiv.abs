Title: Learning a Food World Model That Predicts the Next State from Production Data
Authors: Inkyu Sa, Gaurav Kuppa, Steve Van der Hoeven, Konstantin Stulov, Rajat Bhageria, Luis Rayas
ID: 2611.28319
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28319

Abstract:
Food assembly robots pick ingredients from a pan, and every pick must deliver a
target weight. We learn a world model of food picking from 61,773 production
picks, labeled automatically by a camera and a scale (self-supervised learning).
From the pre-pick heightmap of the pan on a 2.5 mm grid, the commanded pick
action, the food and the utensil, the model predicts the post-pick heightmap and
the picked mass with uncertainty. We evaluate the mass prediction on 12,294
held-out picks of nine foods. The production controller keeps each pick near its
commanded target. The model's root-mean-square error (RMSE) of 10.93 g is 9%
lower than that of the commanded target or of a per-food mean. This RMSE is 24%
lower than that of the controller's internal mass estimate. Gradient-boosted
trees on 20 hand-crafted features have a 0.2 g lower RMSE, but they predict only
the mass, not the next state that planning and policy learning need. Using the
predicted uncertainty, we decline the most uncertain picks and answer 89.4% of
picks with an RMSE of 10.28 g. The prediction barely responds to the commanded
volume and depth, because they vary little in closed-loop production. The model
also fails without warning at target weights not seen in training. Future
directions include action-primitive conditioning and a more general model across
foods and utensils. We give recommendations for learning world models from
production data.
