Title: BioMem: Agentic Memory for Biomedical LLM Agents
Authors: Mingchen Li, Yongkang Xiao, Rumeng Li, Rui Zhang, Hong Yu
ID: 2611.13426
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13426

Abstract:
Large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated considerable potential in
biomedical applications, while agentic memory systems have emerged as a
promising approach to preserving and utilizing information across tasks and
interactions. However, a fundamental question remains: how effectively and
reliably do these systems retain, retrieve, update, and utilize biomedical
information? In this study, we systematically investigate agentic memory in the
biomedical domain, focusing on task effectiveness, retrieval quality,
information fidelity, and long-term robustness. We evaluate five memory
approaches across four LLMs in three representative settings: biomedical
knowledge reasoning, clinical prediction, and long-term conversational memory.
Our analyses reveal how memory construction, evidence relevance, and
conversational history shape downstream biomedical performance, highlighting the
distinct challenges of retrieving relevant information, preserving its fidelity,
and effectively utilizing it for reasoning. Building on these findings, we
introduce Hybrid Memory Retrieval and Utilization (HMRU), a training-free
framework that jointly enhances evidence accessibility and task-aware
utilization to improve biomedical reasoning and long-term conversational memory.
HMRU achieves an average relative improvement of 8.6% across biomedical
question-answering and conversational memory benchmarks, with additional gains
in selected clinical prediction tasks. It further reduces conversational answer
errors by 24.9% and closes 48.0% of the performance gap to full-context
inference. Our findings provide a systematic characterization of biomedical
agentic memory and practical insights into developing more effective and
reliable memory-augmented LLM systems.
