Title: Tabular Foundation Models Are Surprisingly Effective In-Context Robot Learners
Authors: Zhen Xu, Hao-Run Cai, Heng-Zhuang Li, Si-Yang Liu, Jun-Peng Jiang, Han-Jia Ye
ID: 2611.30889
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30889

Abstract:
Can foundation models pretrained for generic numerical prediction serve as in-
context robot learners? We investigate this question through tabular foundation
models (TFMs), which are pretrained to infer input-output relationships from
labeled examples in context. We introduce TabAct, which casts visual imitation
as numerical prediction: frozen visual features and robot proprioception form
the inputs, future action chunks form the targets, and each demonstration time
step provides one labeled example that conditions a frozen TFM for closed-loop
control. The predictor receives no robot-specific training or target-task fine-
tuning. With one target-task demonstration available to each method, TabAct with
TabICLv2 achieves the highest average success among all evaluated methods on
both LIBERO and Meta-World, exceeding the strongest baseline by 20.9 and 5.8
percentage points, respectively. Additional demonstrations improve control
without updating model weights: with five demonstrations, success reaches 94.2%
on LIBERO-Object. These results demonstrate that generic numerical pretraining
can provide an effective foundation for in-context robot learning.
