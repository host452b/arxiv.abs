Title: A van der Waals Floating-Gate Platform for Broadband Optoelectronic Neuromorphic Computing
Authors: Thi Phuong Anh Bach, Sangeun Cho
ID: 2610.34738
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34738

Abstract:
Artificial synapses that integrate nonvolatile memory with broadband
optoelectronic learning are essential for next-generation neuromorphic
computing. However, their realization remains challenging because conventional
floating-gate memories exhibit limited optical functionality and poor
scalability. Here, we report a van der Waals ReS2/hBN/InSe floating-gate
synaptic transistor that combines efficient charge storage with broadband
optoelectronic learning in a single device architecture. The heterostructure
incorporates InSe as a floating-gate layer for stable charge storage, hexagonal
boron nitride (hBN) as a tunneling dielectric, and ReS2 as a photoresponsive
channel, enabling carrier tunneling under electrical and optical stimulation.
Through the combined operation of these layers, the device achieves a large
memory window, an on/off current ratio exceeding 10^6, stable multilevel
conductance states, and reliable retention over 10^4 s. Broadband visible-to-
near-infrared optical excitation enables controllable synaptic weight modulation
and the emulation of essential synaptic functions, including short- and long-
term plasticity, paired-pulse facilitation, and pulse-amplitude- and pulse-
duration-dependent plasticity. Optical potentiation and electrical depression
further enable bidirectional modulation of synaptic weights. Beyond these
synaptic functions, the device demonstrates higher-order learning through
associative and discrimination conditioning inspired by the Pavlovian learning
paradigm, as well as reconfigurable Boolean logic driven by electro-optical
inputs. These results establish a multifunctional van der Waals floating-gate
platform that integrates nonvolatile memory, broadband optoelectronic learning,
and adaptive information processing within a single transistor, providing a
promising strategy for compact, energy-efficient neuromorphic hardware.
