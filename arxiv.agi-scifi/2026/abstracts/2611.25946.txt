Title: Point Dynamics as Proxy for Robotic World Models
Authors: Dong An, Tianyu Liu, Haoran Ding, Wen Yang, Anqing Duan, Zhengzhong Liu, Eric P. Xing, Guangyi Liu
ID: 2611.25946
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25946

Abstract:
Robotic world models predict future video frames from the current observation
and a sequence of robot actions. While recent models follow the given actions
closely, manipulated objects often collapse or move inconsistently with the
action. We attribute this to the conditioning: an action, or a rendering of the
robot, specifies where the robot goes but not how the scene responds, leaving a
single video model to infer the interaction dynamics implicitly while also
rendering appearance. We therefore separate dynamics prediction from visual
rendering, with 3D point dynamics as the proxy between them. Specifically, we
lift the first frame into 3D points, simulate how they respond to the robot's
motion with a learned point dynamics model, and render the simulated points back
into pixel-aligned videos that any video model can read. In controlled
experiments on the DROID dataset with two video renderers of different
architectures, this point dynamics proxy reduces object motion error by 8.6% and
6.2% without degrading image quality, and by 8.1% on a real bimanual robot.
Moreover, the video follows the simulation: more accurate dynamics yield more
accurate object motion, and editing the points steers the video. This suggests
that advances in dynamics modeling can translate into more faithful robotic
world models.
