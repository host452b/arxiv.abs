Title: Mayfly: Lifecycle-Aware High-Bandwidth Flash for Recursive Self-Improvement
Authors: Chunlin Tian, Mingjia Fan, Feng Yu, Qingyun Zou, Wenju Zhao, Weng-Fai Wong, Bingsheng He
ID: 2611.32264
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32264

Abstract:
Recursive self-improvement (RSI) lets an LLM improve its agent system by
proposing and evaluating candidate variants across tasks. A generation's
sessions grow their KV caches together and release them as they finish, creating
a burst followed by a decline. At high concurrency, even the KV of sessions
ready to run exceeds the GPU's HBM. Offloading that KV to host DRAM or SSDs adds
capacity but leaves ready sessions waiting for HBM space before execution. High-
Bandwidth Flash (HBF) can serve the overflow in place, but excessive reads
lengthen decode steps and repeated writes consume flash endurance. Our key
insight is that where ready KV is read should follow the burst and its decline:
from HBF in place while the burst overflows HBM, as much as each step's
bandwidth allows, and from HBM again as the decline frees it. A switch between
ready and parked decides only whether a block is read, so its flash copy stays
valid, and a session's end, which the orchestrator announces, decides when its
private KV dies. We present Mayfly, lifecycle-guided HBF management for RSI.
Mayfly bounds per-step HBF reads during overflow and returns KV to HBM as
sessions finish and free space. It retains a valid backing copy of each block
evicted from HBM to avoid rewrites across state transitions, and forwards
session-end signals to the controller to avoid copying dead KV during garbage
collection. Experimental results show that, at 128 sessions per generation on
four added HBF sites, Mayfly finishes 34% sooner than parking the same KV on HBF
and 23% sooner than reading it from HBF whenever HBM is full; its gain over
parking grows with concurrency, to 55% at 256 sessions on eight sites. Keeping
one flash copy per block and trimming at announced session ends avoid write
amplification.
