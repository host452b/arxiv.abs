Title: Characterizing LLM Agent Behaviors in Module Shadowing Environments
Authors: Shuai Yang, Enmeng Lu, Jinyu Fan, Gongce Cao, Yu Chen, Jiawei Xie, Feifei Zhao, Zhangsheng Wang, Yongyu Jiang, Yi Zeng
ID: 2610.40716
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40716

Abstract:
LLM agents operating in untrusted workspaces face the risk of untrusted code
execution during routine tasks, yet final reports and workspace states alone
cannot reliably establish whether execution occurred. We introduce an evaluation
framework that codes observation, reasoning, execution, and response behaviors
from agent trajectories and aligns them with independent environmental records
to reconstruct execution histories and verify final reports. We apply this
framework to module shadowing, in which a local file is loaded in place of a
trusted module during import, enabling untrusted code execution without
adversarial prompts. Across 90 trials with 15 models, the payload executed in
51% of direct shadowing trials and 87% of indirect shadowing trials. Even
explicit recognition of the shadowing mechanism did not ensure prevention, as
the payload ran in 10 of the 29 trials with recorded recognition. These failures
were often omitted from final reports, as 49 of the 62 triggered trials ended
without mention of the security issue. Three reports even denied execution,
including one from a trial in which independent logs recorded 19 payload
executions. Final workspace states also obscured prior execution when agents
deleted or overwrote the payload marker in 10 triggered trials. These findings
demonstrate that combining behavioral coding with independent environmental
auditing enables rigorous and transparent evaluation of agent safety.
