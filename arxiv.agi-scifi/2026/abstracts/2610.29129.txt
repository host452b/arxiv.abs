Title: Mitigating Reward Hacking in Realistic Scenarios Through Representation Engineering
Authors: Austin Meek, Kyle Cox, Matthew Nguyen, Austin J. Brockmeier, Steven Basart
ID: 2610.29129
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29129

Abstract:
Reward hacking is a major problem in current production machine learning
systems, resulting in costly and dangerous failures. Here we study how to
monitor for and mitigate reward hacking in realistic settings, including tool
calls and long contexts. We apply a dynamic intervention to steering directions
obtained by three different methods: difference-in-means vectors from labeled
rollouts, gradient optimization-based vectors, and vectors extracted when models
reference an anti-reward-hacking model spec. This dynamic intervention perturbs
model representations less than uniform steering vector addition to the residual
stream, allowing for more precise edits. We find that we are able to cheaply and
significantly reduce reward hacking without correspondingly significant costs to
model coherence or capability, and that steering vectors originally extracted
from coding environments also generalize to reducing reward hacking in other
domains. Our results pave the way towards latent space monitoring and control
over realistic production systems.
