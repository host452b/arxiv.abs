Title: RiskLens: Human-Informed Incident Transfer for AI Risk Evaluation
Authors: Yiliu Tang, Hangyue Zhang, Yun Huang
ID: 2611.16287
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16287

Abstract:
As AI capabilities advance, early-stage teams need better ways to judge whether
historical incidents transfer to new products, demanding support beyond static
risk checklists or one-off ideation tools. We present RiskLens, a human-AI
workflow with an interactive system built around a live crawl, refreshable
ranker, and extensible, human-informed schema. RiskLens analyzes 2,034 AI
incidents and 10,443 AI products to rank transferable incidents using a feature
schema spanning technical, operational, and user-facing attributes and supports
an interface that presents ranked incident evidence, generated scenarios, risk
metrics, and mitigation suggestions. In a study with 21 AI-familiar
participants, users surfaced evaluation criteria that we added as 13 human-
informed dimensions, including harm categories, failure mechanisms, and
vulnerable groups. The feature-based model outperformed a generic baseline with
an 86.2% win rate, and remained stable on a twenty-month future holdout; after
human-informed schema expansion, the updated model outperformed the same
baseline on 91.4% of pairs.
