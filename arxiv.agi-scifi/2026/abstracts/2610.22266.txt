Title: Millimetre/Sub-Millimetre SETI: Expanding the Radio Technosignature Parameter Space with ALMA2040
Authors: Louisa A. Mason, Michael A. Garrett, Sofia Z. Sheikh, Kelvin Wandia, Anita M. S. Richards, Vishal Gajjar, Chenoa D. Tremblay, Andrew P. V. Siemion
ID: 2610.22266
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22266

Abstract:
The search for extraterrestrial intelligence (SETI) has been largely
concentrated at centimetre wavelengths, and in particular in the 'water hole'
(1.42-1.66 GHz) range. The millimetre and sub-millimetre sky is, by contrast,
almost entirely unsearched. It also offers some natural advantages: above 35
GHz, the interstellar and interplanetary media are effectively transparent and
non-dispersive, terrestrial interference is sparse, and interferometry
suppresses what interference remains. Both antenna gain and usable fractional
bandwidth increase with frequency, so a civilisation optimising directed, high-
data-rate links has advantageous reasons to operate in this regime, as
illustrated by the ongoing migration of our own telecommunications systems to
much higher frequencies. Building on the first ALMA technosignature survey
(Mason et al. 2025), we identify four searches ALMA2040 is uniquely placed to
make: (i) narrowband searches using a phased-up, beamformed array, extending the
commensal model pioneered for the VLA and MeerKAT into a regime where drift
rates reach ~ 103 Hz s^-1; (ii) broadband aggregate leakage (BRaTs) from
Kardashev Type I civilisations, for which 128-512 GHz of instantaneous bandwidth
places a ~ 60 pc horizon within an integration time of one hour on source; (iii)
Dysonian searches for the cold waste heat of computation - Slysh haloes - whose
arcsecond and subarcsecond angular scales around M dwarfs are well matched to
ALMA2040's array configuration; and (iv) geometric discontinuities imprinted by
occulting megastructures, detectable as ringing in the visibility function with
a phased ALMA operating in a mm/sub-mm VLBI array. We set out the corresponding
requirements: bandwidth, beamforming, commensal access, spectral resolution and
sub-second time resolution.
