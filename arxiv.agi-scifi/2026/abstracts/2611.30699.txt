Title: Stress2Twin: Profile-Driven Network Digital Twins for Guarded Edge Computing Resource Management
Authors: Julian Jimenez-Agudelo, Paola Soto, Ayat Zaki-Hindi, Sébastien Faye, Nina Slamnik-Kriještorac, Johann Marquez-Barja, Miguel Camelo-Botero
ID: 2611.30699
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30699

Abstract:
Heterogeneous edge systems require resource managers to determine how many
application replicas to deploy, where to place them, and how to distribute
incoming requests while satisfying service-level performance constraints.
Acquiring the performance knowledge required for these decisions through
exhaustive multi-node characterization is measurement-intensive and becomes
increasingly difficult as the number of workers and configurations grows.
Stress2Twin is a profile-driven Network Digital Twin (NDT) framework that
profiles edge nodes independently and composes their measured behavior into a
system-level resource-management model. Machine-level oracles characterize
latency, throughput, loss, queueing, and power as functions of assigned request
rate and replica count. A guarded optimizer derives operating regions and tests
neighboring request-rate states before retaining a replica count, reserving
capacity before measured nonlinear degradation. Independent profiling of our
three-worker Kubernetes testbed requires 271 measured states, corresponding to
approximately 4.5 hours, compared with an estimated 123 hours for exhaustive
characterization of the considered cluster configuration space. In a single-run
ramp evaluation, the selected policy records no latency or loss-threshold
violations within the derived 1,650 requests per second (RPS) supported range
and reduces estimated power by 27.84% relative to RPS100 Round Robin (RR)
placement. An additional single-run evaluation under a non-monotonic workload
records no latency violation and 19.67% lower estimated energy than a stabilized
RPS50 RR policy. These results show that independently acquired machine profiles
can support capacity-aware resource consolidation within a measured operating
range. Power savings use an idealized active-worker accounting model.
