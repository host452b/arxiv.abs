Title: Space AI Infrastructure: Where the Edge Meets the Orbital Cloud
Authors: Zhanwei Wang, Jianhao Huang, Min Sheng, Kaibin Huang
ID: 2610.16395
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16395

Abstract:
The fusion of artificial intelligence (AI) and space technologies is extending
spaceborne intelligence beyond onboard payloads toward a global infrastructure,
here termed Space AI infrastructure (SpaceAI-Infra). Its promise rests on two
distinctive advantages of space systems: abundant solar energy in orbit and the
global reach of satellite constellations. Realizing this promise hinges on a
fundamental systems question: How should satellite constellations be designed
and orchestrated to deliver data-center-scale AI services competitive with their
terrestrial counterparts? This article develops a roadmap-oriented answer. We
first chart the evolution of SpaceAI-Infra through three progressive stages:
onboard AI, Space Edge AI, and Space Cloud AI. By revisiting the space-
constrained fundamentals for end-to-end token services, we derive enabling
design principles and translate them into an integrated space edge--cloud
architecture. The communication-centric space edge comprises free-flying LEO
constellations that provide user-proximate computing and form the access and
backhaul fabric for cloud AI services. Complementing the edge, the computing-
centric space cloud comprises formation-flying satellite constellations
organized as space data centers in dawn--dusk sun-synchronous orbits. A case
study demonstrates that the proposed architecture can support data-center-scale
foundation-model inference for millions of concurrent users worldwide.
