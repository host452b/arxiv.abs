Title: A Wideband Technosignature Search of 210,191 Nearby Main-sequence Stars with the VLA Sky Survey
Authors: Steve Croft
ID: 2611.31943
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31943

Abstract:
Searches for radio technosignatures have concentrated on narrowband signals. We
present a complementary search for excess broadband 2-4 GHz emission from
210,191 Gaia DR3 main-sequence stars within 100 pc in all epochs of the VLA Sky
Survey (VLASS; 2017-2026). Cross-matching with the Epoch-1 catalog yields 51
high-reliability positional associations (versus 1.5 expected by chance). All
but one are known natural emitters that, where measurable, move with their
stars; the remaining source, near LSPM J1943+5337, is not at the star. Because
stellar flares can mimic transmitters in continuum flux, a detection counts as a
possible transmitter at any luminosity only at the 32,985 apparently single AFGK
stars outside known radio-emitter classes, and at M dwarfs and AFGK multiples
only above $10^{10}$ W Hz$^{-1}$. Forced photometry of 208,232 stars in all
epochs finds 1 steady-test detection and 3 stars detected in at least one epoch
among the eligible stars, against 1.3 and 1.9 expected by chance. The median
per-star limit on steady emission is $L_ν<4.0\times10^{8}$ W Hz$^{-1}$. Without
background subtraction, fewer than $1.5\times10^{-4}$ of eligible AFGK stars
host a steady transmitter above $10^9$ W Hz$^{-1}$, and fewer than
$2.3\times10^{-5}$ of all stars outside the known-emitter classes host one above
$\sim1.1\times10^{10}$ W Hz$^{-1}$; counting every M-dwarf detection as a
possible transmitter, fewer than $5.5\times10^{-4}$ of M dwarfs host one above
$10^9$ W Hz$^{-1}$. Stacks detect population-averaged emission from M dwarfs,
concentrated in those with activity evidence. Continuum surveys thus constrain
broadband, modulated, or rapidly drifting signals that narrowband pipelines do
not search for.
