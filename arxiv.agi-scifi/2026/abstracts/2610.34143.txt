Title: Feasibility of Distributed Fault-Tolerant Quantum Computing: From Quantum Links to Algorithms
Authors: Takumi Kobayashi, Hodaka Kurokawa, Yasunari Suzuki, Hideo Kosaka
ID: 2610.34143
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34143

Abstract:
Practical fault-tolerant quantum computation requires a large number of physical
qubits, posing a major challenge for scaling up individual quantum processors.
Distributed architectures offer a promising solution by interconnecting multiple
processors through quantum networks. However, remote Bell-pair generation
remains technically demanding, making it essential to assess computational
performance under limited communication resources. In this work, we present a
comprehensive resource analysis of distributed fault-tolerant quantum
computation using superconducting processors connected by microwave or optical
links. We show that even a single physical communication channel per node can
substantially reduce the physical-qubit requirements per node under
experimentally motivated, forward-looking microwave-link assumptions of a 1 MHz
physical Bell-pair generation rate and 90% fidelity, although at the cost of a
roughly tenfold increase in execution time. Conversely, reducing the execution-
time overhead requires substantially greater communication resources, including
higher Bell-pair fidelities and extensive multiplexing. These results
demonstrate the potential feasibility of distributed fault-tolerant quantum
computation with limited communication resources and provide a quantitative
basis for balancing quantum-link performance, processor size, and execution
time.
