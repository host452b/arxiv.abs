Title: State of Mind: Learning and Evaluating World Models of Naturalistic Brain Dynamics
Authors: Yuxiang Wei, Zundong Wu, Weiyu Sun, Qizhen Lan, Yanteng Zhang, Vince D. Calhoun
ID: 2611.28508
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28508

Abstract:
Understanding naturalistic brain activity requires going beyond the stimulus
alone, as external inputs continuously interact with an individual's evolving
brain state. A world model captures such dynamics in a latent state by inferring
it from neural observations, predicting stimulus-driven evolution, and updating
with new observations. However, existing brain world models fail to maintain a
persistent latent state over time, and standard metrics of neural prediction
accuracy cannot tell whether a model tracks an individual's brain state or
relies solely on the stimulus. To directly test state inference, prediction, and
updating, we introduce BrainStateBench, which evaluates a model's latent state
through a frozen linear probe on whole-cortex, movie-driven fMRI. Its State task
tests whether a model captures trial-to-trial variability in an individual's
responses to repeated stimuli, while Forecast and Updating evaluate future
activity predictions without and with new observations, respectively. To track
individual brain state across time, we propose Spectator, a linear latent world
model trained with a joint-embedding predictive objective. Spectator encodes
short- and long-horizon observation histories into a two-timescale latent state
and predicts state transitions linearly from the current state and stimulus.
Stimulus-only encoders reach 0.320 in fMRI prediction correlation without
observing the brain (0% on State), while prior deep brain world models reach
0.239 yet score at most 0.4% on State. In contrast, Spectator achieves 0.349
correlation alongside 5.5% on State, 16.0% on Forecast, and 20.3% on Updating,
outperforming all baselines while forecasting over 20x faster than deep
forecasting models.
