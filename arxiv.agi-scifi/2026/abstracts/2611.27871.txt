Title: World Models for Learning Quantum Many-Body Dynamics
Authors: Yuan Wang, Oleksandr Kyriienko
ID: 2611.27871
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27871

Abstract:
We propose using world models for learning Hamiltonians from measured dynamics
of a quantum computer or simulator. Implicitly, the parameters of a Hamiltonian
are encoded in how the measured few-body observables evolve. By using a joint-
embedding predictive architecture (JEPA) to perform prediction in latent space,
we show that the device Hamiltonian can be efficiently inferred from noisy
measurements. We train a spatiotemporal encoder and predictor with approximately
$2.7$ million trainable weights using a single objective, to predict the
representation of the next window of measurements from the previous one. The
network is thereby driven to retain the reproducible dynamics and discard shot
noise. We recover the field and coupling maps of a two-dimensional Ising lattice
in real and imaginary time, and the disorder map of a one-dimensional Heisenberg
chain of $100$ sites. In every settings the encoder achieves lower error than a
masked autoencoder and than the same encoder left untrained. It also outperforms
the same regression head applied directly to time-averaged observables, by
factors of $4.4$ to $30$. We establish latent prediction as a practical self-
supervised objective for learning from measurements of quantum dynamics.
