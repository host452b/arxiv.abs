Title: Prospects for Astrobiology and Technosignature Searches with the Vera C. Rubin Observatory Legacy Survey of Space and Time
Authors: Andjelka B Kovacevic, Nigel J. Mason, Aleksandra Ciprijanovic, Becky Long, Dominika Korczakowska, Maia Moore, Juulia Vuolukka
ID: 2606.00574
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00574

Abstract:
he Vera C. Rubin Observatory Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST) will
repeatedly measure the brightness of billions of astrophysical sources using six
colour filters. We test a simple idea: real physical signals tend to appear as
coordinated patterns across several measurements, whereas noise does not; we
call this behaviour ``coherence''. We illustrate the approach with three LSST-
like simulations: (i) five Kuiper Belt Object (KBO) surface/activity states,
(ii) 648 exoplanet spectra with an injected vegetation-red-edge-like spectral
feature, and (iii) 500 multiband light curves. In idealised simulations, a
weakly active KBO is strongly separated from its inactive counterpart when all
five colours are combined. The injected red-edge-like feature becomes detectable
at effective visible-disc coverage of about 13\%, and stacking about 150
detections could reach 5$σ$. Coherent, natural, and noise-like variability
occupy distinct regions. A small Gaia DR3 stellar sample illustrates how colour
and stability may help prioritise Galactic search regions.
