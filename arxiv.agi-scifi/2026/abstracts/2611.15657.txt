Title: Global Seasonality Factor Investing
Authors: Andrew Butler, Adam Butler, Maciej Zawadzki
ID: 2611.15657
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15657

Abstract:
We study seasonality as a factor for predicting risk-adjusted returns across
global futures markets. We document five seasonality anomalies that, in
aggregate, are pervasive across bond, commodity and equity markets. We
demonstrate that each seasonality factor produces excess returns that are not
reasonably explained by established global time series carry, momentum and
skewness factors. We apply recent innovations in statistical learning and fit
nonlinear seasonality models at the individual market level, thereby allowing
each feature-market pair to express its unique seasonal prediction pattern. We
discuss candidate economic rationales for the existence and persistence of each
seasonality anomaly. Walk-forward out-of-sample testing demonstrates that fast-
moving seasonal patterns, such as day-of-week and day-of-month, produce strong
gross performance but with extremely high turnover, resulting in negative
performance net of transaction costs. In contrast, slower-moving seasonal
patterns, such as day-of-quarter, day-of-half-year and day-of-year, produce
positive gross performance and, with the exception of day-of-quarter, positive
net performance. An equal-weight ensemble of seasonality models produces an out-
of-sample Sharpe ratio that is competitive with, and uncorrelated to,
established global time series factor portfolios. Empirical results demonstrate
that adding seasonality to a global factor portfolio increases the out-of-sample
Sharpe ratio by approximately $30\%$ over the full evaluation period.
