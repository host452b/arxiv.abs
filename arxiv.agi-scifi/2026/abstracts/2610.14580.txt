Title: Entanglement-Capability-Aware Finite-Bond Circuit Cutting for Distributed Quantum Computing
Authors: Weidong Liu, Qingqing Jiang, Yufu Liu, Ruiqing He, Jiandong Shang, Hengliang Guo, Qiang Chen
ID: 2610.14580
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14580

Abstract:
Distributed quantum computing distributes workloads that exceed a single
processor's capacity across resource-constrained components. Circuit cutting
enables this decomposition, but its effectiveness depends on partition choice.
We present SACE, a Simulation-Aware Circuit Partitioning framework with
Entanglement-Capability-Aware Screening. EP-Cost screens partitions using a
parameter-dependent entanglement-capability proxy and native implementation
cost. Under reconstruction constraints, finite-bond refinement selects the
candidate that minimizes the worst-subcircuit discrepancy between normalized MPS
simulations at bond dimensions $χ$ and $2χ$. Across 14 exhaustively enumerated
small-scale candidate spaces, EP-Cost-guided genetic algorithm selections attain
co-optimal native quasiprobability decomposition (QPD) overhead, with a median
within-circuit Spearman correlation of 0.9965 between EP-Cost and QPD overhead.
At matched cut counts, EP-Cost reduces QPD overhead by $25.20\times$ and
$16.89\times$ for 8- and 9-qubit QFT circuits, respectively, and improves
sampled reconstruction. On 12-qubit approximate QFT, deterministic
reconstruction reduces Pauli-observable mean absolute error in all six low-bond
conditions and total-variation distance in five. With five cuts and 7,776 QPD
terms, SACE is best on all four reconstruction metrics in four of six
conditions. At 24 qubits, SACE improves exact-local QFT accuracy in all 30
instance-bond-dimension conditions against each EP-Cost-only, Kernighan-Lin, and
spectral baseline. The 50-qubit QFT comparisons remain favorable as reference
convergence improves. Weighted QAOA circuits provide complementary, more bond-
sensitive evidence. Refinement benefits persist across three search methods;
larger candidate panels improve the optimized proxy without guaranteeing lower
exact-local error.
