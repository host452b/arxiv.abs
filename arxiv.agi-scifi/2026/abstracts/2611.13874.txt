Title: Towards Design of Efficient Neuromorphic Controllers
Authors: Koen Scheres, Erik Steur, Maurice Heemels
ID: 2611.13874
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13874

Abstract:
One of the major sources of inspiration for neuromorphic engineering is the
central nervous system, which is orders of magnitude more efficient than
classical digital clock-based computers. Neuromorphic control aims at recreating
this efficiency in control systems, combined with (formal) stability and
performance guarantees. In our previous work [1], we have established formal
guarantees for the emulation of static output feedback controllers implemented
using integrate-and-fire neurons. In this paper, we investigate the
``efficiency'' of the resulting controllers in terms of sparse spiking activity.
By changing the network structure of our original implementation, foregoing the
unit gain assumption made in our previous work, we will establish that the
chosen architecture of the spiking controller significantly affects its sparsity
and discuss the implications of these observations for the efficient
implementation of spiking controllers.
