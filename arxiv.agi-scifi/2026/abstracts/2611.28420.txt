Title: Diversity over Scale: Recursive Self-Improvement with Ensembles of Small Open-Weight Models
Authors: Navapat Nananukul, Mayank Kejriwal
ID: 2611.28420
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28420

Abstract:
The advancement of large language models (LLMs) has significantly enhanced the
capabilities of agents across various tasks. Recursive self-improvement (RSI)
loops, in which a language model repeatedly rewrites a program and builds on its
previous version, now perform well in machine-learning tasks, but they rely on
frontier models and hundreds of calls per run. Small open-weight models are far
cheaper, yet when used alone, their programs often crash and their improvements
stall. We propose DiVE (diverse models evolve), an ensemble method for RSI that
aims to improve creativity and diversity of generated solutions. DiVE uses three
components: a shared memory, a failure log, and best-idea-wins rounds. This
allows models to write candidate solutions from the same context and retain the
best idea at each iteration. On 22 MLE-bench Lite competitions, an ensemble of
five 8-12B open-weight models has 25.4% less test error than its average member
and 16.7% less than Llama 3.3 70B, and comes within 7.1% of Qwen 3.5 122B-A10B
at 75% of its cost. Diversity among small models can thus stand in for part of
the scale that RSI relies on.
