Title: Reusing Battle-tested Code: A Systematic Literature Review of Automated Software Transplantation
Authors: Thanatad Songpetchmongkol, Earl T. Barr, Mark Harman, Federica Sarro, Justyna Petke
ID: 2611.21630
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21630

Abstract:
Software reuse remains a fundamental challenge in software engineering, often
caught between the security risks of external libraries and the reliability
concerns of generative AI. Automated software transplantation offers a
compelling alternative by enabling the direct extraction and integration, into
new host systems, of battle-tested logic (code that has already survived the
rigors of production, possibly over many years of extensive in-production use).
This paper presents a systematic literature review to evaluate the current
state-of-the-art and identify the remaining barriers preventing this technology
from becoming a mainstream engineering practice. We identified 27 distinct
automated software transplantation approaches. Our analysis reveals that while
the adaptation of code is highly automated, significant bottlenecks remain in
the manual prerequisite steps of organ location and test suite augmentation. We
conclude with a research roadmap that suggests how these manual obstacles might
be overcome, including the strategic use of Large Language Models to assist in
identifying and verifying high-value, production-hardened logic for
transplantation.
