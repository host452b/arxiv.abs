Title: Can Your AI Twin Meet People for You? It Can Hold a Conversation. But What Does It Need to Judge Like You?
Authors: Wanghao Ye, Sihan Chen, Bowei Tian, Yiting Wang, Shwai He, Guoheng Sun, Ziyi Wang, Ziyao Wang, Yexiao He, Zheyu Shen et al.
ID: 2611.28692
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28692

Abstract:
Should your AI twin go meet people on your behalf? We built LLM twins of 710
real people from their other conversations in the CANDOR corpus, sent them to
meet each other in 5,680 simulated first conversations, and checked every
rehearsal against what the real people said after meeting for real (355 held-
out, participant-disjoint pairs, against baselines that include each person's
rating history). The rehearsals work: a twin's conversation predicts real liking
($ρ$=0.277), carries information that questionnaires and rating history do not
have (partial $ρ$=0.16 [0.05, 0.26]), and recovers most of what the same reader
gets from the real conversation (0.321). Swap controls show that this signal
travels through the conversation, not through the persona card. What, then, does
a twin need to judge like you? Not your voice: making twins talk more like their
owners, with person-specific activation steering and a LoRA trained on their own
speech, did not help in a pre-specified test and lowered accuracy in an
exploratory second round (-0.183 [-0.294, -0.066]). Nor simply your stated
ratings: a persona-prompted twin that holds the conversation does not yet carry
its owner's judgment, and when told its owner's past ratings it anchors on them
instead of weighing them. Learning helps: a small appraisal head trained on
other real conversations, which combines the twin's post-call answers with its
owner's rating history, lifts rehearsal-based prediction above the history
baseline out of fold (+0.059 [0.001, 0.115], against +0.022 without the twin's
answers; pre-specified primary contrast +0.056 [-0.001, 0.111]). We propose an
attribution protocol for twin-based social simulation and identify learned
appraisal as the component twins need next.
