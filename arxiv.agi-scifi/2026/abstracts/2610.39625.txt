Title: Non-Volatile Memory Devices for Neuromorphic Computing
Authors: Debasis Das
ID: 2610.39625
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39625

Abstract:
This chapter explores the critical role of emerging non-volatile memory (eNVM)
devices in advancing neuromorphic computing. Inspired by the human brain's
remarkable ability to perform complex tasks like pattern recognition and
adaptation, neuromorphic computing offers immense potential for implementing
complex neural networks (NNs) in real hardware. Despite ongoing research into
the brain's complexities, its fundamental operation relies on neurons
(processing units communicating via electrochemical signals) and synapses
(junctions between neurons). The modifiable strength of the synapse is key to
learning and memory functionality, allowing the brain to adapt and rewire
itself. For dense and low-power circuits in neuromorphic computing, these eNVM
devices are ideal due to their synaptic-like behavior and non-volatile
information storage. Properties such as conductance modulation of these devices
emulate synaptic plasticity, which is crucial for learning, and their
scalability makes them promising for large-scale neuromorphic architectures.
This chapter will delve into the various types of eNVM devices such as resistive
RAM (ReRAM), phase-change memory (PCM), spintronics-based memory devices, and
their applications in neuromorphic computing, discussing their challenges and
opportunities, and highlighting recent advances in their integration within
neuromorphic systems.
