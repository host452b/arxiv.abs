Title: PWLNet: A Framework for Linearized Neural Surrogates in Nonlinear Process Optimization
Authors: Sunoy Sanyal, Canberk Demir, Metin Türkay, Erdal Aydın
ID: 2611.25969
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25969

Abstract:
Neural networks represent nonlinear process systems accurately, but embedding
them in optimization is difficult because smooth activations introduce
nonlinear, nonconvex constraints. PWLNet is a Python framework for building
optimization-compatible neural surrogates through exact and piecewise-linear
reformulations. It combines baseline and physics-informed training,
optimization-aware pruning, and Pyomo embeddings based on ReLU mixed-integer
linear models, exact tanh nonlinear models, PWL-tanh formulations, and binary-
free SOSX branch-and-LP. PWL-native training uses the same activation in
training and embedding, avoiding extra linearization error. It is evaluated on
Boston housing, the Williams-Otto process, the Van de Vusse reactor, and a
hydroformylation semi-batch reactor. On Boston housing, SOSX training gave the
best common-architecture model (test MAE 1.984, R2 = 0.925). On Williams-Otto,
pruning 15% of hidden neurons reduced MILP solution time 6.5-fold while
preserving the selected operating point. For the Van de Vusse reactor, a
physics-informed ReLU RNN pruned from 32 to 12 hidden units enabled tractable
exact MILP embedding and a 4% true-plant economic gain over the best ramp-and-
hold policy, while terminal-aware shrinking-horizon prediction reached 99.7% of
the direct-shooting reference. For the hydroformylation reactor, one-step exact-
tanh IPOPT reached 99.2% of the certified reference product with peak pressure
20.002 bar (limit 20 bar) and 14.36 s total solve time over 280 moves; ReLU and
PWL-native controllers achieved comparable recovery, with ReLU fastest. Longer
horizons and physics-informed training did not consistently improve closed-loop
results. Overall, compact linearized neural surrogates enable accurate,
tractable optimization in steady-state and dynamic applications, with constraint
fidelity identified as a key direction for improvement.
