Title: Power Density as a Universal Biosignature: An Application to the Stellivore Hypothesis
Authors: Clément Vidal, Luciano Kadian
ID: 2611.03691
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03691

Abstract:
Power density is a central measure underlying a common thread in cosmic
evolution. It measures the rate at which free energy transits in a complex
system of a given mass. We argue that it can be seen as a universal signature,
and thus a strategy of ''following high power density'' can inform both
biosignature and technosignature searches. We use known binary star masses and
accretion rates to compute power densities of cataclysmic variables, neutron
stars and transient black holes, and show that these accreting binaries have
high power densities. We compare this result with other accreting systems in
astrophysics as controls: young stellar objects (YSOs) and supermassive black
holes (SMBHs). We review possible natural explanations, and then explore the
possibility that these accreting binary systems might be instances of Type II
stellar civilizations on Kardashev's scale, civilizations feeding on stars, or
$\textit{stellivores}$. Besides power density, we present additional living
clues, such as their variety, their existence far from thermodynamic
equilibrium, their apparent homeostasis, the need to fine-tune models to explain
them, the apparent existence of energy reserves, accretion control and ejection
control, as well as other potential living systems functions. We summarize this
case using living systems theory and discuss implications for big history.
