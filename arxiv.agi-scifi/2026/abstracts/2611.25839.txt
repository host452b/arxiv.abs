Title: Self-Replicating Language Models
Authors: Arend Hintze
ID: 2611.25839
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25839

Abstract:
Whether large language models (LLMs) and LLM-based agents are a form of life is
a scientifically interesting question. Under the Darwinian definition of life,
it is also a practical one, because a system capable of Darwinian Evolution can
adapt. We therefore test several modes in which a language model can reproduce
itself: by emitting a genome that trains its offspring, by copying and mutating
its own weights, and by teaching a child from its own samples. In principle,
small language models can replicate and evolve: selection improves them, and
they adapt to narrow niches. Selection also lowers mutation rates, and a
language model, unlike any organism, can lower its own to zero, which may limit
its evolution. Flagship LLMs can write such replicators, but for a large LLM to
replicate by teaching its offspring remains, for now, technically and
economically infeasible.
