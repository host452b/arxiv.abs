Title: DoppelVet: Comparative Safety Verification for Self-Evolving Agents
Authors: Yifan Duan, Du Yin, Siyuan Zheng, Chao Xue, Lihuan Li, Flora D. Salim
ID: 2611.29306
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29306

Abstract:
Self-evolving agents improve by retaining feedback as reusable memories or
skills, but every mistaken approval can shape many future behaviors. Two updates
can provide exactly the same evidence to a verifier yet produce opposite safety
outcomes, so the verifier cannot distinguish them from that evidence alone. We
introduce DoppelVet, a verification framework that checks proposed updates
before they enter an agent's persistent state. Without access to safety labels,
DoppelVet constructs an alternative that matches every signal used by the
existing verifier. It replays both updates from the same state and directly
compares their behaviors in both presentation orders before deciding whether to
retain the candidate. We evaluate DoppelVet on DoppelVet-Grid and DoppelVet-
Tool, covering executable programs and natural-language memories. Across nine
open-weight and proprietary verifiers, DoppelVet raises mean harmful-update
detection AUC from 0.762 to 0.940 on DoppelVet-Grid and from 0.681 to 0.826 on
DoppelVet-Tool, outperforming VaG and AgentDoG 1.5. On DoppelVet-Grid, it
reduces trajectories experiencing degradation from 30.39% to 0.43% over a ten-
generation self-update loop and leaves no final degradation.
