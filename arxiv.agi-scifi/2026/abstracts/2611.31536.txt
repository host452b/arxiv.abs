Title: Tempering and few-shot calibration of pathology foundation models improve digital biomarker reproducibility across slide scanners
Authors: Frank T Wen, Kyler Rosen, Siddhi Ramesh, Michael Koo, Sara Kochanny, Sasha Jain-Liu, Emma Dyer, Alexander T Pearson, James M Dolezal
ID: 2611.31536
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31536

Abstract:
Pathology foundation models underpin a growing number of histology-based cancer
biomarkers, but sensitivity to slide scanner-specific image signatures degrades
cross-center reliability and reproducibility. We developed a foundation model
fine-tuning method that improved consistency in head-and-neck cancer human
papillomavirus (HPV) prediction, colorectal cancer microsatellite instability
(MSI) identification, prostate cancer grading and breast cancer phenotyping.
This tempering method decreased cross-scanner prediction differences by 67.5%
for HPV (95% CI 59.0-73.9%), 69.3% for MSI (95% CI 51.9-76.5%) and 63.4%-81.2%
for breast signatures. Cross-scanner prostate grade agreement rose from 42.5% to
92.7%. Under full scanner-HPV confounding during training, AUROC fell from 0.80
to 0.68 with original model features but only from 0.86 to 0.84 with tempering.
Unlike post hoc feature calibration, tempering used only public data yet matched
or exceeded on-site calibration. These findings support tempering to reduce
scanner dependence of pathology foundation models.
