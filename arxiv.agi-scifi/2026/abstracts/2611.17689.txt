Title: Orbit-AWARE: From Onboard AI Deployment to Sustainable In-Orbit Intelligence
Authors: Yunxin Geng, Kaige Zhu, Tongtong Shu, Zhan Gao, Yanxiao Li, Yan Liu, Weiwei Jiang, Zhiqing Wei, Zhiyong Feng
ID: 2611.17689
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17689

Abstract:
The growing role of space computing in time-critical satellite services makes
the long-term reliability of onboard artificial intelligence (AI) a mission-
level requirement. Model drift can undermine this reliability through critical-
observation suppression or noncritical priority flooding, displacing both
mission information and evidence for model correction. To address this drift-
induced evidence displacement, this article presents Orbit-AWARE, a safeguard-
first lifecycle management (LCM) architecture. A comparison of existing
approaches motivates cooperative Data, Cognitive, and Trust Planes for
continuous monitoring, event-triggered agentic orchestration, and deterministic
governance. The architecture retains a model-independent sample stream before
degradation is confirmed and coordinates evidence selection and exchange for
diagnosis and ground-assisted adaptation. Candidate updates are verified before
control over data disposition is restored. In an xBD-based emergency Earth
observation (EO) case study, Orbit-AWARE delivers 14.3\% more critical
observations and achieves 8.3\% higher sustainable mission utility than the
Rule-Based Drift Loop under a common 10\% downlink budget. Finally, we discuss
future research directions for extending LCM through task-aware reliability
assessment, memory- and tool-enabled onboard agents, and evidence-centric space
networking. This work makes evidence availability and recovery authorization
explicit design objectives for dependable onboard AI services.
