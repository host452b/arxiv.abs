Title: In-Situ TEM Observation of Solid-State Phase Evolution in High-Entropy Alloy Nanoparticles
Authors: Nirmal Kumar Katiyar, Noorbasha Bhavani Sai, Guilherme Gurian Dariani, Douglas S. Galvao, Pulickel M. Ajayan, Pedro Alves da Silva Autreto, Sriram Vijayan, Chandra Sekhar Tiwary
ID: 2610.14147
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14147

Abstract:
The emergence of new materials has gone beyond conventional design principles.
Among them are high entropy alloys (HEAs), in which multiple metallic elements
coexist within a single unit cell. In this case, each atomic position in a solid
material has a different chemical environment, leading to configurational
entropy as high as that in the fully random or melted state, which has been
frozen to a solid solution. However, the behaviour of such alloy systems at pre-
melting temperatures is largely unknown, where atoms gain sufficient mobility
and time to rearrange, leading to an enthalpic rather than an entropic
interplay. This study demonstrated the phase transformation of HEA nanoclusters
(CoCrFeMnNi) under high-resolution transmission electron microscopy (HRTEM) at
different temperatures, including room temperature (RT), 550 °C, and 850 °C. The
clustered nanoparticles coarsen into larger clusters, accompanied by
compositional heterogeneity due to surface and lattice diffusion. This
experimental finding of atomic mobility was further corroborated using molecular
dynamics, which provides a detailed mechanistic description.
