Title: Information-theoretic entropy imaging of quantum matter
Authors: Chen Wu, Leonard W. Cao, Lingyuan Lyu, Liam Cohen, Jiahui Nie, Can Uzundal, Yujia Hu, Quanjin Wang, Ziying Yan, Sneh Pancholi et al.
ID: 2610.35790
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35790

Abstract:
Phase transitions can generate intricate spatial organization invisible to
spatially averaged probes, yet quantitative measures of this organization remain
limited. Here we develop "entropy imaging," an information-theoretic technique
that harnesses lossless compression to quantify spatial complexity,
predictability, and order in quantum materials. Using this approach, emergent
spatial textures can be imaged and converted into an experimentally-accessible
observable: the computable information density (CID), a compression-based
measure of information complexity that approaches the Shannon entropy rate in
the large-system limit. Applied to millikelvin microwave impedance microscopy of
graphene, the method reveals a pronounced maximum in the CID near quantum Hall
transitions, a quantitative measure of the information-rich spatial organization
of the critical landscape. A disordered Hofstadter model reproduces this
behavior and shows that the CID can identify the quantum critical point from a
sequence of images, without ensemble averaging over disorder realizations. The
same framework tracks fractional quantum Hall transitions and resolves isolated
fractional puddles that coexist with a disordered background. By contrast, the
trivial band-edge crossover in a MoSe2/WS2 moiré heterobilayer exhibits
qualitatively different behavior, suggesting that the pronounced CID enhancement
observed at the quantum Hall critical point is not simply a generic feature of a
conductive-insulating crossover. We further show that the geometric information
encoded in CID is not reducible to a correlation length, and it can even
distinguish spatial textures with identical multifractal statistics. Together,
these results establish lossless compression as a framework for quantifying
emergent spatial organization in quantum materials, with portability across
imaging modalities and physical systems.
