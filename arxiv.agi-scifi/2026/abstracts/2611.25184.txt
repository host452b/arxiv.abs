Title: An AI-Powered Multiverse Analysis of Empirical Finance Research: Evidence from Reused Natural Experiments
Authors: Ruijiang Gao, Steven Chong Xiao
ID: 2611.25184
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25184

Abstract:
Empirical finance research often reuses natural experiments to study other
outcomes, raising concerns about multiple testing and publication bias. We use
AI agents to run a multiverse analysis of 324 papers that reuse 13 natural
experiments. The agents rebuild the data, reproduce each paper's main estimate
and re-estimate it across grids totaling 62,409 estimated defensible
specifications. Based on the reproductions and the specification grids, we
develop measures of empirical robustness. We reproduce 42% of the main estimates
of claims with a signed effect under the papers' own specifications. Across all
defensible specifications, on average 25% keep the published sign and have $p <
0.10$. Across experiments, claims based on regression discontinuity are more
robust than difference-in-differences claims. Within experiments, claims about
first-order effects, claims with larger published $t$-statistics, claims whose
authors' data or code we find, claims in top journals, and claims with fewer
earlier papers on the same experiment are more robust. Claims on experiments
that more papers reuse are not less robust on average. Our findings quantify how
much published claims depend on analysis choices, thereby showing that large-
scale multiverse analysis becomes feasible with agentic coding.
