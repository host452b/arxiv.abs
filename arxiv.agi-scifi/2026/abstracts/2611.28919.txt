Title: When Do Better Visual World Models Make Better Plans? Decision-Aligned Selection for Latent Planning
Authors: Chan Aristella Lu, Arya Fayyazi, Junil Yeo, Jin Lu, Kyu Hyung Lee, Mehdi Kamal, Massoud Pedram
ID: 2611.28919
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28919

Abstract:
Visual world models enable goal-conditioned planning by predicting the outcomes
of candidate actions in latent space. However, planning depends on how well a
model ranks candidates, whereas training optimizes how accurately it predicts
each one. We identify the Decision-Relevant Residual (DRR), the part of the cost
error that can reorder candidates, and show that the more training reduces the
DRR, the more it improves selection (Spearman $ρ= 0.82$, vs. $0.61$ for
prediction error). Building on this insight, we introduce Decision-Aligned
Selection (DAS), a plug-in selection rule that scores candidates by the
standardized consensus of several independently trained checkpoints, which
averages out their checkpoint-specific residuals. Across two model families
(LeWorldModel and DINO-WM) and five tasks, DAS selects better actions than
single checkpoints in 12 of 18 configurations and outperforms training one model
three times longer in 14 of 18. In closed loop, DAS improves success over single
checkpoints in 6 of 9 pre-registered LeWM comparisons.
