Title: Information-Theoretic Scaling Laws for Generative Diffusion Training
Authors: Druv Pai, Dejan Stancevic, Yi Ma, Luca Ambrogioni
ID: 2610.33337
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33337

Abstract:
Diffusion models trained on increasingly large datasets sampled from the same
data distribution will converge to the same learned distribution. However, in
practice, a model trained on finite data depends on the particular training
dataset; this dependence is reflected in properties of the model's outputs, such
as memorization and sample quality. To understand this dependence through an
information-theoretic lens, we study \textit{model information}, a
computationally tractable measure of how much samples from the trained model
depend on the particular training dataset. We explore its scaling behavior as
model size and training dataset size vary. Specifically, we uncover two
qualitatively distinct scaling regimes: (1) a nonlinear model information regime
marking the transition from pure memorization to imperfect generalization, and
(2) a generalization regime characterized by linear scaling of the model
information and progressively improved generalization and sample quality. We
then posit an information-theoretically principled scaling law to quantify these
two regimes, and show that this law yields induced scaling laws for memorization
and sample quality. We finally demonstrate that these induced scaling laws
extrapolate well outside the fit's domain. Our results demonstrate information-
theoretic principles for memorization and sample quality scaling laws in
diffusion models. Our code is publicly available on
\href{https://github.com/DruvPai/diffusion_info_theory_scaling_law}{GitHub}.
