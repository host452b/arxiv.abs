Title: Path-Aware Fusion of Infrastructure RF Sensing and Robot Perception in a Live Robotic Digital Twin
Authors: Alberto Martínez Méndez, Antonio de la Oliva, Carlos J. Bernardos
ID: 2611.27497
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27497

Abstract:
Mobile robots in industrial workspaces must react to people that their onboard
sensors cannot yet observe. Communication infrastructure can fill part of this
gap by acting as a sensor, but its observations differ from camera and LiDAR
evidence in meaning, spatial support, and rate. We present a live robotic
Digital Twin (DT) that integrates heterogeneous sensing branches, a commodity
60-GHz link, joint Wi-Fi/5G sensing, a fixed camera, and robot-borne LiDAR, as
spatially grounded observations with explicit freshness, and that uses the
robot's live pose and heading to decide whether evidence lies on the path the
robot is about to sweep. We show that a 60-GHz link carrying near-gigabit
traffic detects human presence through the phase signature of its antenna array
(balanced accuracy 0.98 on unseen sessions and 0.999 from one day to the next in
a first site), but that a second site with a different link geometry requires a
site-specific model (0.98 on unseen sessions after retraining, 0.73 without). On
a Unitree Go2 patrolling under the DT, every person entering the camera field or
the 60-GHz link stopped the robot (20/20); for the camera, the median time from
physical entry to standstill was 1.83 s, of which detection, DT update, and
networking take about 0.15 s and the rest is the 0.5-s confirmation and the
robot's braking. Path-aware gating removed all stops caused by off-path presence
(5 stops versus 0 for the same scenario), stale branches stopped the robot in
fail-safe mode (6/6) and were ignored otherwise (0/6), and five unattended laps
produced no false stop.
