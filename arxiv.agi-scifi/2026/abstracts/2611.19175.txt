Title: Scaling of the Radial Motion and Size of Filamentary Structures in the Scrape-Off Layer Inferred from 100 kHz Visible Imaging on 294 MAST Discharges
Authors: Jessie Dong, Ryan Wu
ID: 2611.19175
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19175

Abstract:
Filaments are important carriers of cross-field transport in the scrape-off
layer of a tokamak. Analytic models predict that their radial velocity varies
with their size. We measured the radial velocity and size of a large population
of filaments on MAST, using data recorded from a 100 kHz, 48 x 256 pixel camera
in the outboard midplane in the FAIR-MAST archive. Computer vision techniques
were used to automate the identification of filaments and tracking of their
trajectories, which yielded 86,042 filament tracks over 294 discharges in 4
campaigns. The archive does not contain spatial calibration, so we have obtained
a pixel scale by regression of the plasma limb column in the image on the EFIT
outboard LCFS radius, with bootstrap intervals. The scale is 2.7-5.2 millimetres
per pixel. We find a median velocity of 424 m/s, and median radial width of 3.6
cm. The dependence of velocity on size is mild, with a log-log slope of +0.05
(95% interval +0.03 to +0.07) when the effects of exposure blurring are
corrected for. This is because filaments are not in either velocity regime; they
span the range $\hat{a}$ = 0.13-1.97 in two-region units, which straddles the
maximum velocity predicted by the two-region model, but the normalized velocity
$\hat{v}$ is much smaller than the scale of the model, at $\hat{v} \approx
0.06$.
