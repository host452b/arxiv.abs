Title: Estimations for strong and weak hot Jupiter magnetism: A Python package with interpolators and scaling laws
Authors: Albert Elias-López, Aline Vidotto, Daniele Viganò, J. R. Fuentes, Simon Müller, Emily Sandford
ID: 2611.09128
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09128

Abstract:
Hot Jupiters (HJs) are commonly thought to host the strongest dynamo-generated
magnetic fields among exoplanets, exceeding Jupiter's by up to an order of
magnitude, which can potentially induce observable star-planet interaction (SPI)
and planetary radio emission. However, while several indirect, model-dependent
estimates of HJ magnetic fields have been reported from SPIs and recent
transmission spectroscopy diagnostics, no unambiguous direct (radio) detection
has yet been achieved. We aim to provide a range of magnetic field estimates for
HJs, considering the mechanisms responsible for inflation and the convective
flux entering the magnetic field scaling law. Given that their dynamos operate
in the fast-rotating regime (Rossby number <~ 0.1), we combine one-dimensional
evolutionary interior models with magnetic field scaling laws under two limiting
heating scenarios: deep and shallow energy depositions. The deep-heating
scenario maximizes convective heat flux in the dynamo and predicts strong
magnetic fields of up to ~100 G, whereas shallow heating, which is arguably more
realistic for most proposed physical mechanisms, weakens convection and yields
much weaker fields of O(1) G. The strong field scenario is compatible with SPI
indirect detections, and the weak field scenario is in agreement with recent
atmospheric constraints. We derive scaling law relations based on observables
(planetary mass, radius, and/or incident stellar flux): Bstrong = 19 M_P^0.6
R_P^1.7 G; Bweak = 0.8 M_P^1.5 R_P^-1.2 G. These predictions provide a framework
for interpreting future atmospheric wind measurements, SPI signatures, and radio
observations, which may ultimately discriminate between competing interior-
heating scenarios.
