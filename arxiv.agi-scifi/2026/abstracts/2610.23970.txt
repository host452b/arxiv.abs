Title: Spatio-Temporal Detection of Photovoltaic Systems on Industrial Building Rooftops Using Satellite Imagery and Machine Learning
Authors: Frederick Bruch, Christian Knoth, Florian Detsch, Edzer Pebesma, Hanna Meyer
ID: 2610.23970
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23970

Abstract:
The global transition toward carbon-neutral energy systems is heavily dependent
on the rapid deployment of solar photovoltaic (PV) infrastructure, yet the speed
of this expansion has outpaced the ability of administrative registries to
maintain accurate records. While high-resolution commercial imagery is often
used for monitoring, its high cost and infrequent updates can create an
information gap for grid operators, energy infrastructure developers and
policymakers. This study addresses the lack of scalable monitoring for
industrial-scale rooftop PV systems by developing a machine learning framework
that utilizes open-access, multispectral Sentinel-2 satellite imagery. By
integrating building geometries from OpenStreetMap with Sentinel-2 reflectance
data, a Random Forest model was trained to identify the specific spectral
signatures of PV modules. Moving beyond theoretical modeling, this work
emphasizes practical implementation through the development of an interactive
web-based prototype for on-demand site monitoring. The application enables users
to retrieve up-to-date satellite observations and assess the presence of PV
systems on industrial building rooftops. The tool incorporates quality metrics
that inform users whether a prediction is supported by sufficient training data
density or requires manual verification. This end-to-end framework demonstrates
that open-source medium-resolution data can effectively bridge this monitoring
gap.
