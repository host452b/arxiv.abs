Title: The generic behavior of permutation entropy for continuous maps
Authors: Balázs Maga, Sára Szepessy
ID: 2611.12789
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12789

Abstract:
Permutation entropy, introduced by Bandt and Pompe in 2002, measures the
complexity of a dynamical system through the ordinal patterns of its
trajectories. Bandt, Keller and Pompe established that for measure-preserving,
piecewise monotone interval maps with finitely many monotonicity intervals, the
normalized order-$n$ permutation entropy $H_n^{\mathrm{perm}}(f)/n$ converges to
the Kolmogorov--Sinai entropy. Although this result has subsequently been
extended to broader classes of maps, whether normalized permutation entropy
always converges has remained unknown. We show that convergence can fail, and
indeed that nonconvergence is generic. More precisely, for a generic continuous
Lebesgue measure-preserving map $f:[0,1]\to[0,1]$, we prove that \[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{H_n^{\mathrm{perm}}(f)}{n}=0, \qquad
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{H_n^{\mathrm{perm}}(f)}{n\log n}=1. \] Thus, despite
the generic vanishing of Kolmogorov--Sinai entropy, permutation entropy exhibits
oscillations between vanishing linear growth and the maximal possible asymptotic
growth. We also construct an explicit Lebesgue measure-preserving interval map
with countably many monotonicity intervals for which the normalized permutation
entropy has lower limit zero and upper limit infinity. This demonstrates that
nonconvergence can occur even within the countably piecewise-monotone setting,
beyond the scope of the convergence theorem of Gutjahr and Keller, relying on
conditions concerning the lengths of the pieces.
