Title: Out of sync: Accommodation, evaluation, and emergence in five weeks with an agentic AI teammate
Authors: Nia Nixon, Jaeyoon Choi, Pedro Martins de Bastos, Mohammad Amin Samadi
ID: 2610.41057
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41057

Abstract:
As agentic AI in education shifts from reactive tools toward partners that take
initiative and persist over time, a central question is how learners relate to,
evaluate, and benefit from artificial agency. We conducted a longitudinal
experiment in an undergraduate learning analytics course, in which 17 student
teams (N = 48) collaborated with a persistent LLM-based teammate across weekly
sessions. The agent decided when to intervene through a trigger-based
architecture, and its social role was manipulated between teams: a Cognitive
Agent that challenged quick consensus or a Supportive Agent that invited quieter
members to contribute. Combining chat discourse, post-session surveys, and
spoken reflections, we identified distinct patterns unfolding across three
timescales. Students accommodated their language to the agent's role
immediately, using more social language toward the Supportive Agent from the
first session. Their helpfulness evaluations were recalibrated from session to
session in relation to the agent's observed behavior, rather than remaining
anchored to students' pre-existing attitudes toward AI. A slower crossover
emerged in perceived collaboration process and positive-affect word use in
reflections: Supportive-Agent teams began below Cognitive-Agent teams and ended
above them, although students rated the Supportive Agent less helpful
throughout. Because these responses follow different trajectories, single-
session evaluations of agentic AI may miss changes that emerge only through
repeated interaction.
