Title: A Covariance-Aware Framework for Spatially Resolved Exoplanet Biosignature Inference with the Solar Gravitational Lens
Authors: Slava G. Turyshev
ID: 2606.29138
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29138

Abstract:
Assessing possible life on an exoplanet requires spatial, spectral, temporal,
and environmental context. We develop a covariance-aware Solar Gravitational
Lens (SGL) framework for regional biosignature inference from reconstructed
Einstein-ring measurements. A controlled 0.45-2.40 um Stokes-$I$ experiment uses
ten illuminated cells, 128 channels, and R=70 to compare resolved and exactly
projected disk spectra with common parameters and priors. Across 96 synthetic
scenes, the joint gas, vegetation, and mineral block gains (1.52+/-0.03) nats
after marginalizing cloud and calibration/response parameters; adding
independently trained surrogate-discrepancy covariance gives (1.49+/-0.03) nats.
Held-out mismatch nevertheless produces linearized shifts up to 6.08 posterior
standard deviations; preserved precision does not establish unbiased recovery or
evidence for life. For fixed Gaussian hypotheses, the expected log-likelihood-
ratio loss under disk integration equals half the squared whitened contrast
discarded by the projection. A separate 0.40-20 um continuum calculation
provides absolute photon rates and observing-time estimates for an Earth-radius
planet at 30 pc, observed from 650 AU. It includes source-averaged SGL
amplification, finite-aperture extraction, coronal and host-star photons,
detector noise, and a declared calibration floor. A reconstruction-covariance
bracket reduces an 8 x 8 coadd gain from 7.77 to 3.00, costing 6.7 times the
exposure in the statistical-noise limit. The framework connects regional
spectroscopy and co-location tests to the physical and instrumental validation
needed to assess exoplanet biospheres and possible biological activity.
