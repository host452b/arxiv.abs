Title: BypassGym: A Benchmark for Evaluating AI Agents' Endpoint Defense Evasion Capabilities
Authors: Weifeng Yuan, Zifan Chen, Yucheng Zhang, Feng Dong, Peng Gao, Haoyu Wang
ID: 2611.13684
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13684

Abstract:
Artificial intelligence agents increasingly automate cybersecurity tasks,
including offensive operations. Frequent attacks across the web make endpoint
detection and response (EDR) a critical defense, but whether AI agents can evade
it remains unclear. Benchmarks evaluate the offensive capabilities of large
language model (LLM) agents in areas such as penetration testing and
vulnerability discovery. One important capability that these benchmarks do not
address is defense evasion, specifically whether an agent can develop a program
that executes a payload without being blocked or terminated by a deployed EDR.
We introduce BypassGym, to our knowledge the first benchmark to systematically
evaluate this capability on real endpoints. Given command-and-control (C2)
shellcode, an agent must generate and compile a loader that executes the payload
without being blocked or terminated. A supplementary evasion knowledge skill
provides domain knowledge and supports comparisons with and without this input.
BypassGym is built on Proxmox VE(PVE), so the evaluation environment runs real
Windows systems and EDR products inside virtual machines and returns structured
detection feedback to the agent. We evaluate 5 lightweight model backends
against 6 EDR products and 3 payloads, and find that even these simple models
generate successful submissions, with DeepSeek-V4.1 Flash reaching 17/18
successful runs and Bitdefender allowing only 5 of 15 runs to pass. These
results provide a systematic measurement of current AI agents' endpoint defense
evasion capabilities and identify areas for defenders to investigate.
