Title: Optimizing Locally Equivalent Graph States in Quantum Computing Using Greedy Best-First Search
Authors: Sora Harada, Ryo Kuroiwa, Shin Nishio, Takeaki Uno
ID: 2611.30738
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30738

Abstract:
In measurement-based quantum computation and quantum repeater networks, graph-
structured quantum states called graph states play a central role as an
essential resource. In a graph state, each vertex corresponds to a qubit, and
each edge is prepared by applying one controlled-Z (CZ) gate. Local
complementation, which toggles adjacencies among the neighbors of a selected
vertex, produces operationally equivalent graph states up to an inexpensive
operation called local Clifford gates. Therefore, finding equivalent graphs up
to local complementation with fewer edges and lower maximum degree can reduce
the preparation cost, such as the number of CZ gates and the circuit depth. In
this paper, we formulate the problem of finding a desirable equivalent graph via
local complementations as a state space search problem. We apply a heuristic
search algorithm, greedy best-first search (GBFS), to the formulated problem.
Our experimental evaluation shows that despite its simplicity, GBFS outperforms
existing approaches based on integer linear programming or local search across
multiple graph classes, including random graphs, bounded-degree graphs, and most
of the graphs constructed from quantum LDPC codes. In addition, we empirically
analyze GBFS's search behavior, confirming that we need search rather than
stopping at the first local minimum to find the best solution.
