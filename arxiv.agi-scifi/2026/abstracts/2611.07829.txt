Title: Multiplicative and Additive Extractors for Low Entropy Samplable Sources Under Reduced Assumptions
Authors: Marshall Ball, Eshan Chattopadhyay, Mohit Gurumukhani, Ronen Shaltiel, Yunya Zhao
ID: 2611.07829
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07829

Abstract:
Trevisan and Vadhan [TV, FOCS'00] constructed (seedless) extractors for
distributions samplable by poly-size circuits that have very high min-entropy,
under the very strong complexity theoretic hardness assumption that
$\mathsf{E}=\mathsf{DTIME}(2^{O(n)})$ requires size $2^{Ω(n)}$ circuits that
have oracle access to $Σ_6^{\text{P}}$. In this paper we give three new explicit
constructions of extractors for samplable distributions. First, we construct an
extractor that extracts one bit, with constant error, for min-entropy $k=O(\log
n)$, which matches the non-constructive probabilistic argument. This result uses
hardness for nondeterministic circuits, and achieves the same parameters as the
recent extractor of Oh and Shaltiel [OS, STOC'26] under an incomparable hardness
assumption. In contrast to the extractor of [OS26], our extractor (and,
similarly, the constructions below) also works for the more general class of
distributions samplable with postselection (a model introduced by [BGDM,
FOCS'23] that generalizes samplable and recognizable sources). Second, we
construct an extractor that extracts almost all the entropy, with polynomially
small error, for min-entropy $k=n^α$, where $α>0$ is an arbitrary constant. This
result also uses hardness for nondeterministic circuits, and significantly
improves upon the recent extractor of [Shaltiel, FOCS'25] which required a much
stronger assumption. This extractor is also \textit{multiplicative}: a stronger
notion suggested in [AASY, Comp. Complexity'16; Shaltiel, CCC'25] to make
extractors for samplable distributions suitable for selecting keys for
cryptographic protocols. Third, we construct an extractor that extracts almost
all the entropy, with polynomially small error, for min-entropy
$k=\mathrm{polylog}(n)$. This result uses hardness for circuits that have oracle
access to $Σ_2^{\text{P}}$.
