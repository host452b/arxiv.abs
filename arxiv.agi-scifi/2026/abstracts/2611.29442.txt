Title: Human-AI Collaboration in Accounting Judgment: A Proposed Cognitive Coding Scheme and a Rule-Based Adaptive Collaboration Protocol
Authors: Yulu Huang, Lijun Xiao, Gang Wang
ID: 2611.29442
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29442

Abstract:
Large language models create an accounting information systems design problem:
how should evidence production, professional evaluation, and responsibility be
allocated between a model and an accountant? We propose a Cognitive
Decomposition for Accounting Judgment (CDAJ) coding scheme crossing four process
stages with information-intensive and evaluative demands. An Adaptive
Collaboration Protocol (ACP) connects allocation rules to an independent human
estimate, source-linked model output, structured verification, and a documented
human conclusion. Two separately returned coding forms cover 20 constructed
cases. A disclosed primary-only analysis retains unresolved auxiliary response
formats. Raw agreement is 19/20 for unitization, 20/20 for stage, and 18/20 for
demand. On 14 jointly single, codable cases, nominal Cohen's kappa is 1.000 for
stage and 0.752 for I/E/M demand profiles; only 12/20 cases are jointly resolved
to I or E. A public-disclosure diagnostic searches 1,406 pages for 18 numeric
reference cards from three banks' annual reports. Adding a value/period/unit co-
occurrence gate to BM25 with identical query information does not improve
designated-origin recovery at ranks one or three: both methods recover 13/18
origins at rank one and 18/18 within three ranks. A post-hoc audit distinguishes
page recovery from requested table-cell verification. Synthetic checks examine
event ordering and record integrity; Gaussian planning experiments examine a
future effectiveness evaluation. A separate source-response audit records
adverse or unclear statuses in 16/36 returned records. These bounded findings
concern coding repeatability and implementation properties. They do not
establish an optimal allocation, a validated taxonomy, or improved professional
judgment.
