Title: Toward Solving the Galactic Foreground Problem in Gravitational-Wave Astronomy with Machine Learning
Authors: Bo Liang, Jinwen Deng, Minghui Du, Hanlin Song, Xilong Fan, Peng Xu, Ziren Luo, Lijing Shao
ID: 2611.32800
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32800

Abstract:
Space-borne gravitational-wave detectors will see tens of millions of Galactic
binaries. Resolved, they would form a census of the Galaxy's ultracompact
binaries unhindered by dust; unresolved, they are the dominant noise up to a few
millihertz for massive black-hole binaries, extreme-mass-ratio inspirals and
backgrounds from the early Universe. Separating the two requires the number of
binaries, which is ill-defined because binaries exist at every amplitude. We
make it an observable by defining a source as a binary above a signal-to-noise
threshold. Neural flows count the binaries to within one in 99% of 1 microhertz
intervals over 0.5-10 mHz, and nested sampling resolves them, even pairs closer
than one frequency bin. The data themselves set a limit: a source is resolvable
only if its signal pays for its own description, about 20 nats, which biases
even ideal trans-dimensional samplers. Like the diffraction limit in imaging,
this Bayesian confusion limit is set by the data rather than by the method, and
it governs any superposition of weak sources, from radio surveys and pulsar
timing arrays to super-resolution microscopy. For future space-borne detectors,
the method turns the Galactic foreground into a definite, calibrated count of
binaries with an analytic selection function, the basis for global fits and for
tests of population models.
