Title: Displacement Is Endogenous: AI Workers in a Search-and-Matching Labor Market
Authors: Ibrahim Denis Fofanah
ID: 2610.14850
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14850

Abstract:
We build a labor market in which AI systems enter as workers: they apply for
jobs, pass screenings, get hired, and get dismissed, alongside humans. Firms
post vacancies, workers search with friction, and product demand slopes
downward. Across 30 seeded runs per configuration, AI entry at 70% of the human
wage raises unemployment by 4.1 percentage points (95% CI 3.5 to 4.6) and leaves
12.2 fewer humans employed (CI 10.5 to 13.9). Layoffs dominate when AI is much
cheaper than humans: the layoff margin accounts for 7.2 of the lost jobs and
reduced hiring for 5.0, though most dismissed workers find work again. A wider
price gap reduces displacement, because cheap AI makes firms expand: at price
parity, with zero dismissals, the unemployment effect is largest at 9.2 points
(CI 8.6 to 9.9), all of it through hiring. That parity result survives cutting
AI applications from five per period to one. Demand elasticity decides how many
workers AI displaces. Screening changes who gets hired and leaves the
displacement total unchanged.
