Title: Input-dependent Neuromodulation Enhances Mixed-Signal Neuromorphic Hardware
Authors: Aashman Mehta, AbdelQader AlKilany, Dan F. M. Goodman, Giacomo Indiveri, Melika Payvand
ID: 2610.41265
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41265

Abstract:
Gating mechanisms allow a fixed neural network to adapt its computation to the
current input by dynamically modulating its effective parameters. In the brain,
neuromodulation uses this principle to move neural circuits between dynamical
regimes, and deep learning exploits input-dependent gating, e.g., in gated
recurrent networks and self-attention. Input-dependent modulation has also
recently been shown to improve spiking neural networks, with particularly strong
benefits in small, parameter-limited ones. This makes neuromodulation especially
promising for neuromorphic processors at the edge, where area, memory and energy
budgets constrain network size, while circuit physics limits parameter
precision. Here we demonstrate input-dependent, within-trial neuromodulation of
a spiking recurrent neural network on a mixed-signal neuromorphic processor. An
off-chip modulator reads the on-chip network's input and activity and adjusts
its population-level gains while the chip's analog circuits compute with the
physics of the silicon. Neuromodulation raises binary spoken-digit
classification accuracy by $+17.3 \pm 10.4$ percentage points, tested across
five hardware network instantiations.
