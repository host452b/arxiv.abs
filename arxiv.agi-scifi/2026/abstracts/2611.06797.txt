Title: Resource-efficient formulations of the Knapsack problem for quantum computing
Authors: Pim van den Heuvel, Vrinda Mehta, Dennis Willsch, J. A. Montanez-Barrera, Kristel Michielsen
ID: 2611.06797
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06797

Abstract:
Quantum computing has emerged as a new tool for solving combinatorial
optimization problems. Quantum optimization approaches usually operate on the
subclass of Quadratic Unconstrained Binary Optimization (QUBO) problems. Many
optimization problems can be reformulated in this form, though there are often
multiple ways to do so. The choice of formulation can considerably affect the
performance of QUBO solvers in practice. In this work, we study QUBO
formulations of the Knapsack problem, a canonical example of a problem with
inequality constraints. In the standard formulation these constraints are
converted into penalty terms, which requires additional slack variables. Here,
we investigate three more resource-efficient approaches for formulating the
Knapsack as a QUBO. The first two modify the standard formulation, either by
deriving a smaller penalty constant or by reducing the number of slack
variables. The third approach is a wholly different method, which avoids the use
of slack variables entirely. All three approaches increase the probability of
finding the optimal solution, both in simulations of the linear ramp Quantum
Approximate Optimization Algorithm (LR-QAOA) and on a D-Wave quantum annealer.
The slackless method performs best, increasing success probabilities by several
orders of magnitude even for relatively small problem sizes.
