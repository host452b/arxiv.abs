Title: Intent-Aware Protection for AI Agents
Authors: Fady Copty, Neta Haiby, Max Braitmaiere, Julia Korsunsky
ID: 2611.16122
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16122

Abstract:
AI agents increasingly access enterprise data, invoke powerful tools, and act
with limited human oversight. Existing security defenses primarily detect prompt
injection, harmful outputs, or suspicious behavior, but do not determine whether
an agent remains faithful to the purpose for which it was deployed. An action
may contain no attack pattern and stay within granted permissions while still
violating the agent's role, organizational constraints, or purpose. We introduce
Intent- Aware Security, a framework that treats trusted agent intent as a first-
class security object. We define Intent Preservation and Intent Drift, and
operationalize them through an Intent Profile: an agent-specific representation
extracted from organizational, role, and developer intent. We further introduce
an Intent-Aware benchmark of 887 validated samples across 35 agents, built by a
generation procedure that fixes ground-truth labels before LLM realization, and
four Intent Profile-based protections: pre-execution detection, post-execution
detection, input alignment, and system-prompt hardening. Across all evaluations,
Intent Profile-based protection outperforms all profile-free baselines on the
primary metric. On our benchmark, it raises four-class acro-F1 from 45% to 79%
(Out-of-Scope F1 from 8% to 76%). On more than 10,000 executed AgentDojo
trajectories, where ground truth comes from environment state, profile-based
post-execution detection reaches 0.949 AUROC versus AlignmentCheck 0.909, 0.897
with raw agent configuration and 0.843 without context. Profile-based input
alignment improves protection success by 7 points over DataFilter with a
comparable benign utility. These results suggest that an agent's intended
purpose can serve as a useful security reference for detecting and mitigating
deviations from its deployed role and operational boundaries.
