Title: OFDM-Based Neuromorphic Wake-Up Signal
Authors: Gaspard Goupy, Elias Raninen, Dani Korpi
ID: 2610.36914
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36914

Abstract:
Wake-up radios (WURs) reduce energy consumption by continuously monitoring the
channel for a wake-up signal (WUS) while the main receiver remains asleep.
Neuromorphic computing offers a promising approach to ultra-low-power WUS
detection through sparse, event-driven processing. However, realizing these
benefits requires spike-like inputs, which conventional orthogonal frequency-
division multiplexing (OFDM) waveforms do not naturally provide. To enable
neuromorphic WUS detection in OFDM-based systems, we introduce a neuromorphic
wake-up signal (nWUS) that can be generated by a standard OFDM transmitter. The
proposed design superposes frequency-domain vectors with linear phase
progressions across the OFDM subcarriers, producing a time-domain waveform with
sparse amplitude peaks at predefined positions. These peaks encode the wake-up
signature in a spike-like representation that facilitates neuromorphic
processing at the receiver. Building on this structure, we then propose a
noncoherent neuromorphic detector that encodes received peaks into spikes and
performs sparse correlation only upon spike events. Simulations over a 3GPP
urban macro channel show that the proposed approach outperforms on-off keying
(OOK) baselines in detection probability while achieving up to 43.5x lower
computational cost. During idle periods, event-driven processing further reduces
correlation operations by 86% relative to nWUS reception.
