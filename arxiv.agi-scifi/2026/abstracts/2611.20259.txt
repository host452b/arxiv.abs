Title: Scaling large language model-assisted line-of-therapy adjudication with human-in-the-loop evidence-calibrated selection
Authors: Olatunji Oni, Sanhita Sengupta, Marc De Benedetti, Nathanial Eddy
ID: 2611.20259
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20259

Abstract:
Large language models (LLMs) have enabled the ability to scale clinical
adjudication tasks traditionally dependent on exhaustive manual review. Line-of-
therapy (LoT) adjudication, a major bottleneck in oncology cohort construction,
is a prime example. Line boundaries must be inferred from longitudinal treatment
histories according to nuanced, study-specific definitions, at every regimen
transition for every patient. LLMs can apply this adjudication at scale, but
their reliability varies across patients. Treatment histories differ in
complexity, and ambiguous or atypical patterns depend on expert clinical
judgment. Reliable automation thus requires verified support for model
assignments and the ability to identify cases that warrant manual review. Here,
we present evidence-calibrated selection (ECS), a human-in-the-loop framework
for scaling LLM-assisted LoT adjudication. ECS derives confidence from observed
clinician adjudications, prioritizes patients with the least-supported
assignments for follow-up review, and returns completed adjudications to the
model as precedent. As the evidence base grows, ECS continuously calibrates
confidence to estimate residual error, thus enabling more efficient use of
clinician time. In simulations on 136 clinician adjudicated patients with
multiple myeloma, ECS achieved a residual error of 5% after review of 44.6% of
patients, compared to 62.2% when review was directed by LLM self-reported
confidence and 81.9% under random sampling. These results support a scalable
approach to study-specific LoT adjudication, using clinician evidence to inform
automated predictions, minimize error, and reduce review burden.
