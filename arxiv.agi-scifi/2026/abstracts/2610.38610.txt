Title: Repairs for Whom? Evaluator-Executor Mismatch in Selecting Repairs for Failed LLM Agents
Authors: Yijun Liao, Fanwei Liang
ID: 2610.38610
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38610

Abstract:
When an LLM agent fails, repair methods choose which action to replace and what
to put in its place. They often score candidate repairs with greedy replay, a
stronger model, an environment oracle, or a judge that reads the failed log. We
ask whether these evaluators pick repairs that help the agent that will actually
execute them. Holding failed trajectories and candidate single-step repairs
fixed, we label every candidate with independent rollouts of the deployed policy
and measure how much deployment value each evaluator's choice loses. On failures
not screened for repair value, pre-specified tests show that greedy replay loses
0.08-0.13 in expected net success gain in ALFWorld and 0.06-0.13 in net reward
in WebShop, relative to selection with the deployed agent's own rollouts. At an
equal rollout budget, selecting repairs for Qwen2.5-7B with Qwen2.5-14B rollouts
loses 0.25 in ALFWorld and 0.07 in WebShop, twenty 14B rollouts per candidate
are worth no more than two of the 7B agent's own, and fresh rollouts confirm
that the two models prefer opposite repairs on selected disagreement cases. The
agent's own fine-tuned successor is a worse evaluator still: after one round of
training on verified repairs, its rollouts lose 0.55-0.60 when they select
repairs for the agent it was trained from, as a pre-specified test confirms.
Relative to what selection is worth, substitute models forgo 11-53%; on
$τ^2$-bench, where selecting is worth only 0.10, they lose 0.03. Repairs should
be verified with the executor that will run them, and repair labels should name
their executor, including its training version.
