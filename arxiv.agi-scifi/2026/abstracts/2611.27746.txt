Title: Shared Understanding in Human-AI Teams: Coordination Continuity in Evolving Work
Authors: Siân Lindley, Britta Burlin, Christian Poelitz, Lev Tankelevitch, Richard Banks
ID: 2611.27746
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27746

Abstract:
People are increasingly working with AI agents as part of extended, asynchronous
workflows. In these settings, shared access to information does not guarantee a
shared basis for action: goals, plans, and roles may persist while their current
significance changes. We introduce coordination continuity, the extent to which
collaborators sustain a workable basis for interdependent action as work
progresses. Drawing on research on collaborative work, along with two
qualitative team studies and a reflexive case from our own repository-based
environment, we present a framework of three elements: durable representations
of collaborative work; relative coordination properties that support
interpretation of representations in the moment; and interaction mechanisms
through which the status of representations can be understood and altered. We
use a build-act-drift-rebuild heuristic and the notion of consequential moments
to characterise when rebuilding may be needed. We draw eight design implications
for collaborative AI infrastructure.
