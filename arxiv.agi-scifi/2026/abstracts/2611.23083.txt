Title: Performance of Fusion-Based Quantum Computing with Imperfect Photon Indistinguishability
Authors: Katia Hakem, Grégoire de Gliniasty, Nathan Shettell, Paul Hilaire, Stephen C. Wein
ID: 2611.23083
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23083

Abstract:
Fusion-based quantum computation (FBQC) is a promising route to fault-tolerant
photonic quantum computing, connecting small entangled resource states through
linear-optical fusion measurements. While logical encoding can substantially
improve resilience to photon loss, whether these benefits extend to other noise
remain an open question. In this work, we show that this is not generally the
case when accounting for partial photon distinguishability during resource state
generation and fusion measurement. We develop hardware-motivated noise models to
capture these effects and investigate their impact on fault tolerance in the
6-ring fusion network. We compare unencoded and Shor(2,2)-encoded resource
states prepared using all-photonic and emitter-assisted repeat-until-success
(RUS) schemes. For the all-photonic construction, encoding reduces the
distinguishability threshold from 0.136% to 0.08%. In contrast, the RUS
construction tolerates more distinguishability, with comparable unencoded and
encoded thresholds (0.256% and 0.271%, respectively). These results expose a
fundamental limitation of evaluating logical encodings under idealized resource-
state assumptions and demonstrate that resource-state generation can critically
determine the fault tolerance of photonic architectures.
