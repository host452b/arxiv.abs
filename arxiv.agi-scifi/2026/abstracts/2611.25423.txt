Title: Model-Based Low-Order Wavefront Sensing and Control with a Fully Physical Digital Twin on MagAO-X
Authors: M. Mars, S. Y. Haffert, J. R. Males, L. M. Close, K. Van Gorkom, O. Guyon, A. D. Hedglen, P. T. Johnson, M. Y. Kautz, J. K. Kueny et al.
ID: 2611.25423
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25423

Abstract:
Context. Residual low-order aberrations and post-AO tip-tilt jitter are major
drivers of stellar leakage in small-inner-working-angle coronagraphs and limit
high-contrast imaging performance. Aims. The aim of this work is to develop a
fully physical and differentiable digital twin for the focal plane low-order
wavefront sensor on MagAO-X. This digital twin can be used to provide accurate
non-linear and fast linearised wavefront sensing and control to deal with low-
order aberrations. Methods. We build a physics-based optical model of the focal
plane low order wavefront sensor (FLOWFS) system and calibrate it using
laboratory and on-sky measurements. The calibrated model is used for non-linear
phase retrieval, deriving a model-based linear reconstructor, and generating the
corresponding reference image for real-time control. Results. The calibrated
digital twin accurately reproduces the sensor response and recovers the dominant
low-order aberrations. In the laboratory, non-linear reconstruction achieves
residual floors of about 6 to 7 nm over the pupil, compared with about 11 nm for
the linear reconstructor, and the same digital-twin framework transfers across
different coronagraph and wavelength configurations. On-sky, the model can be
recalibrated with limited changes, achieves reconstruction performance close to
the residual atmospheric floor, and provides a linear reconstructor that enables
stable closed-loop low-order control at kHz speed. Conclusions. A fully physical
digital twin can unify accurate low-order wavefront reconstruction, fast
linearised control, and transfer across observing configurations and from lab to
on-sky operation within a single interpretable framework.
