Title: Large language models put existential risk from artificial intelligence at 10%
Authors: Julian Kauk
ID: 2611.18219
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18219

Abstract:
How likely is it that advanced artificial intelligence (AI) will cause human
extinction or a similarly permanent disempowerment of humanity? Humans,
including AI experts, frequently disagree on this question. Yet one important
perspective is missing from this debate: that of AI systems themselves. We put
the question to 241 large language models (LLMs) at every available reasoning
setting. The models converged on a median risk of 10.0%, with the middle 90% of
their estimates between 3.5% and 15.8%, far narrower than the spread among human
experts. More expensive, and typically more capable, models stated higher risks;
newer models showed more uniform, but not higher, risks. More reasoning barely
changed the models' estimates but widened their uncertainty, mostly toward
higher risk. Almost all models named loss of control over AI as the most likely
cause. These answers are not independent judgments; they likely echo training
data, including published expert polls, and choices made in aligning the models.
Still, they document the position that current AI systems state on the risks of
their own technology. Whether this position reflects any genuine understanding
of the systems' own capabilities remains open. Repeated measurements can show
whether it shifts as AI systems advance, and how training and alignment shape it
is a question for both research and public debate.
