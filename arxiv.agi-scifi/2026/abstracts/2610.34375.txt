Title: Whether or Where? Tail Calibration Signals Finite-Budget Reward Hacking in Best-of-N Selection
Authors: Cheng Gui, Yihua Shao, Lei Li, Jingcai Guo, Maolin Wang
ID: 2610.34375
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34375

Abstract:
Best-of-N (BoN) inference returns the candidate with the highest proxy reward,
so a larger $N$ tests the proxy's extreme tail more than its average ranking
quality. We show that the exact BoN value is a Beta-weighted integral of
conditional true reward over within-prompt score quantiles. It follows that BoN
depends on scores only through their ranks. It also follows that BoN can decline
only if the calibration curve in the tail turns downward, and the budget where
the decline appears depends on where that turn lies. Under a bounded additive
model, the error tail sets the limit: Gaussian errors keep improving, Laplace
errors reach a plateau, and heavy-tailed errors return to the prior mean. When
error variance is largest at low true reward, even Gaussian noise can push BoN
below random selection. Synthetic phase maps agree with this taxonomy. On real
candidate pools spanning several verifiable domains, base policies, and learned
reward models, tail failures are rare and all occur in code generation. A held-
out tail calibration statistic reliably predicts where a cross-fitted budget cap
helps. It does about as well as rank accuracy, and the cap is competitive with a
Soft-BoN baseline. Tail calibration therefore does not replace ranking metrics.
It is an audit that is unchanged by any monotone rescaling of the reward scores
and shows which budgets are at risk.
