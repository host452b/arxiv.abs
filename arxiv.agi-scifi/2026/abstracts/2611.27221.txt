Title: ECG-LabWAS: A Laboratory-Wide Association Study Using a Fine-Tuned Single-Lead Electrocardiogram Foundation Model
Authors: Wenhui Liu, Yujie Xiao, Gongzheng Tang, Jianwei Chen, Jun Li, Guangkun Nie, Deyun Zhang, Qinghao Zhao, Shenda Hong
ID: 2611.27221
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27221

Abstract:
Laboratory testing guides clinical diagnosis but requires blood sampling and
processing. The electrocardiogram (ECG) is fast and noninvasive and may carry
information about laboratory abnormalities, yet most AI-ECG models target one
test at a time using 12-lead recordings. We conducted an exploratory ECG
laboratory-wide association study (ECG-LabWAS), fine-tuning an ECG foundation
model pretrained on 10.8 million ECGs to screen 50 laboratory tests from single-
lead waveforms alone. The model was developed in an emergency-department cohort
(MC-MED; 31,826 patients) and applied unchanged to an independent hospital
cohort (MIMIC-IV; 115,921 patients). Of 89 abnormality directions (low or high
values of each test), 33 met the same screening criteria in both cohorts. Areas
under the receiver operating characteristic curve in the two cohorts were 0.841
and 0.838 for elevated NT-proBNP, 0.769 and 0.740 for elevated cardiac troponin,
0.755 and 0.731 for elevated creatinine, and 0.713 and 0.710 for low potassium.
Discrimination changed little when the ECG--laboratory matching window was
widened from $\pm$1 to $\pm$24 hours. Model predictions reproduced the co-
occurrence of observed abnormalities but were about twice as tightly coupled,
largely along one shared component consistent with overall illness severity; low
potassium, elevated NT-proBNP and troponin, and markers of renal function
retained discrimination beyond that of correlated tests. In exploratory
analyses, ECG-assisted versions of the modified Glasgow Prognostic Score and the
systemic inflammatory response syndrome criteria were associated with in-
hospital and one-year mortality. These results identify which laboratory
abnormalities warrant analyte-specific study with single-lead ECG.
