Title: Euclid Data Release 1 (DR1). Learning machine learning: Investigating the complementarity and biases of strong gravitational lens finding networks on Euclid data
Authors: Euclid Collaboration, S. H. Vincken, N. E. P. Lines, S. Schuldt, P. Holloway, T. E. Collett, M. Fogliardi, A. Manjón-García, J. Wilde, A. Verma et al.
ID: 2611.31231
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31231

Abstract:
The Euclid Data Release 1 (DR1) strong lens search presents ECLIPSE, the largest
catalogue of strong gravitational lens candidates to date, with >15 000 systems
identified across 1900 deg2 of sky. It was constructed using five neural
networks applied to approximately 68 million galaxies, followed by citizen
science and expert visual inspection. In this work, we compare how the
independently developed networks use image information to identify their
selection of strong lens candidates. The search combines convolutional neural
networks and a vision transformer, using the same pool of data but with
different image processing and training choices. We investigate why these
networks find different systems and what that means for the completeness of our
catalogue. We evaluate all networks on a 50 deg2 calibration set and their
application to the full DR1 sample. We find that the networks score candidates
very differently, and that combining them recovers over 500 lens candidates
within our calibration set at an inspection budget of 2000 objects. On the full
DR1 sample, we show that individual networks have distinct biases: some networks
favour systems with a larger Einstein radius while others are more sensitive to
compact configurations. We also investigate network preferences in terms of lens
light profile, mass distribution geometry, photometric redshift, and magnitudes.
These findings provide a detailed analysis of how neural network selection
biases have shaped the Euclid DR1 ECLIPSE catalogue. The results inform how
networks should be combined in future Euclid data releases and upcoming wide-
field surveys such as LSST or Roman to maximise both completeness and diversity
of the recovered lens population.
