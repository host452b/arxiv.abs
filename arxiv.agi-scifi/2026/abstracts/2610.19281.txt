Title: Exact Scaling Laws for a Random-Features Bigram Model
Authors: Julian Enoch, Elvis Dohmatob
ID: 2610.19281
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19281

Abstract:
Understanding neural scaling laws is increasingly important for theory and
practice, yet first-principles explanations remain limited even in simple
settings. In this work, we study capacity-limited scaling in a bottlenecked
bigram model with power-law (Zipf) token statistics, viewed as an analytically
tractable toy model of a large language model. Fixed random embeddings serve as
a proxy for representational capacity and induce an explicit bottleneck of
width, allowing us to derive scaling laws for prediction regret as a function of
model width, vocabulary size, and the Zipf exponent. Our results complement
recent theoretical accounts based on optimization dynamics by isolating scaling
due purely to representational constraints. In the appropriate regime, the
theory recovers the functional form of classical neural scaling laws and
suggests that heavy-tailed language statistics may underlie both optimization-
and capacity-driven scaling. Experiments natural language datasets (WikiText2,
OpenWebText) closely match the predicted curves.
