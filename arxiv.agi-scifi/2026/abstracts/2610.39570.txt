Title: LLM Agent-Based Design of RISC-V CPUs with FPGA-in-the-Loop Evaluation
Authors: Leo Itoh, Junichiro Kadomoto
ID: 2610.39570
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39570

Abstract:
Long-run RTL simulation of CPU candidates can take hours, while short diagnostic
runs may miss performance degradation that emerges later in execution. This
evaluation cost can delay design decisions in LLM-agent-based CPU development.
We present a development environment in which LLM agents implement CPU
candidates on an FPGA and use on-board performance measurements to guide design
decisions. We applied it to the human-supervised development of a single-cycle
RV32I core into a three-wide out-of-order RV32IM core. The final design reaches
5.61 CoreMark/MHz at 130 MHz on a Artix-7 FPGA. For the same CPU candidate
running 8,000 CoreMark iterations, FPGA evaluation, including implementation,
was 9.6x faster than single-threaded RTL simulation. In one case, board
measurements revealed long-run performance degradation in a candidate that
improved in a short diagnostic run. The result guided candidate selection and
led to RTL-based root-cause analysis and a fix.
