Title: Recognition of auditory objects from continuous and ultra-sparse spike streams in neuromorphic devices
Authors: Leonardo E. S. Tavares, Kevin D. Sargent, Jon T. Sakata, Dezhe Z. Jin
ID: 2610.38564
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38564

Abstract:
Energy consumption is a major concern for modern artificial intelligence (AI)
applications. The brain is thought to achieve energy-efficient computation
through sparse coding schemes. By emulating the spiking dynamics of the brain,
neuromorphic chips hold the potential to realize energy-efficient AI. In this
work, we use birdsong to demonstrate that auditory object recognition can be
achieved using ultra-sparse coding. Feature-detecting neurons tuned to short
temporal features ($\sim$37 ms), sampled from exemplar syllables, transform
continuous auditory signals into streams of ultra-sparse spikes that mark the
onsets and offsets of features within the signal. Spike patterns elicited by the
exemplars are stored as templates. During recognition, template matching is
performed using axonal delays between feature-detecting and template-detecting
neurons. Recognition is further enhanced by an additional group of specialized
binary neurons trained to discriminate between syllable categories with subtle
differences. When tested on Bengalese finch songs, our approach achieved error
rates below 5% using 10 to 40 exemplars per syllable type. We then implemented
this recognition algorithm using spiking neural networks on Intel's neuromorphic
chip, Loihi, and found that response latencies remain constant (approximately 17
ms per syllable), regardless of the number of templates used. Our results
demonstrate the potential of neuromorphic chips for low-latency, low-energy
recognition of complex sounds, including speech.
