Title: Separatrix splitting in a multiferroic neuromorphic oscillator: toward Arnold diffusion
Authors: D. Maroulakos, Z. Toklikishvili, A. Wal, L. Chotorlishvili
ID: 2611.03297
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03297

Abstract:
Prototype magnetic neuromorphic systems, in essence, are based either on
ferromagnetic or antiferromagnetic materials as key elements for conventional
neuromorphic setups. In this work, we propose a novel neuromorphic system based
on a ferromagnetic disk and a ferroelectric layer coupled via the
magnetoelectric effect. We study a magnetoelectrically coupled ferromagnetic--
ferroelectric oscillator as a candidate element for neuromorphic devices. A
local reduction of conservative Landau--Lifshitz dynamics identifies a magnetic
coordinate--momentum pair, which is coupled to an inertial ferroelectric
polarization mode. We derive the action--angle description of the resulting
double-well--harmonic-oscillator Hamiltonian, obtain the quartic-well frequency
map and Fourier coefficients in terms of elliptic functions, and establish
monotone in-well twist. In the autonomous two-degree-of-freedom model,
isoenergetic nondegeneracy supports KAM confinement on regular compact domains.
Periodic electric forcing introduces a third effective frequency, although the
harmonic magnetic sector leaves the unperturbed resonance loci parallel. An
explicitly specified nonlinear magnetic extension produces transverse resonance
intersections. We calculate leading resonant widths and local homoclinic
splitting, and derive a damping-dependent splitting threshold for a stated
scalar reduction. These results identify analytical structures relevant to
transport and explain possible polarization-based electrical readout.
Establishing Arnold diffusion in the coupled model requires additional
connecting-orbit analysis or controlled long-time evidence; neither a diffusion
law nor Nekhoroshev stability of the driven model is inferred from the present
finite-time calculations.
