Title: Predicting solid-state reaction onset temperatures from high-throughput pairwise synthesis
Authors: Lauren N. Walters, Matthew J. McDermott, Bernardus Rendy, Yuxing Fei, Gerbrand Ceder
ID: 2611.23527
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23527

Abstract:
Solid-state synthesis still relies on heuristics to choose reaction
temperatures. The most widely used, Tammann's rule, places reaction onset at
two-thirds of the lowest precursor melting point, but has never been
systematically tested. Using an automated laboratory, we heated 1,035 pairwise
combinations of 46 common precursors at their Tammann temperatures and
identified the products by x-ray diffraction. Only 25% of pairs formed products
containing both precursors. Combining these outcomes with text-mined literature
syntheses, we trained log-normal accelerated failure time models that treat each
fixed-temperature experiment as a bound on the unknown onset temperature. On 26
reactions with onset temperatures measured by in situ x-ray diffraction, a
three-descriptor model reduced the mean absolute error from 131°C (Tammann's
rule) to 88°C; adding 29 further physicochemical descriptors did not improve the
model. Reaction onset can therefore be predicted from routine pass/fail
synthesis outcomes, and predictions will improve as such data accumulates.
