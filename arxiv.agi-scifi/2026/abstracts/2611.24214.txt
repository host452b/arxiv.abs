Title: Towards Reliable Scaling Laws for Tabular Foundation Models
Authors: Johannes Hog, Neeratyoy Mallik, Steven Adriaensen, Jan Hendrik Metzen, Tom Viering, Danny Stoll, Frank Hutter
ID: 2611.24214
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24214

Abstract:
Scaling laws are widely used to predict foundation model performance and guide
compute allocation. However, tabular foundation models (TFMs) differ from
conventional foundation models in that they are pretrained on virtually
unlimited synthetic data from configurable distributions, while ultimately being
evaluated on real-world datasets. We investigate whether scaling laws for
TabPFNv2-style models reliably extrapolate to larger compute budgets and
generalize across evaluation settings. Our analysis reveals differences between
synthetic and real-world scaling behavior and shows that accurately fitting a
scaling law does not necessarily yield reliable prescriptions on unseen
datasets. In particular, selecting configurations using too few evaluation
datasets leads to systematically over-optimistic performance estimates. We
further demonstrate that compute-optimal scaling requires expanding the range of
synthetic pretraining dataset sizes alongside model size and training compute.
Increasing the maximum numbers of rows and features sampled during pretraining
improves performance and produces more predictable scaling trends. These
insights guide the training of more efficient TabPFNv2-like models, using the
open-source training pipeline of nanoTabPFN.
