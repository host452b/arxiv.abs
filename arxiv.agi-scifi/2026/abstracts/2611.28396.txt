Title: Targeted In-Context Poisoning of Tabular Foundation Models: An Empirical Study
Authors: Adem Alparslan
ID: 2611.28396
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28396

Abstract:
Tabular foundation models (TFMs) predict the label of a query row conditioned on
a context of labelled rows, without training on them. Drawn from operational
data, the context becomes an attack surface. We study targeted in-context
poisoning in its simplest form: an attacker with write access to the context's
source places exact copies of a target row there with the wrong label. On 30
OpenML datasets for binary classification, one such poison row flips on average
53% (TabICL) and 65% (TabPFN) of the predictions correct without attack, and
three poison rows flip almost all. The same copy with the true label flips
almost none, and performance on the remaining rows hardly changes. Predictions
flip by two routes: in TabICL, the target row's embedding rotates towards the
mean of the wrong class; in TabPFN, the poison row pulls genuine rows of the
wrong class along. The TFMs thus infer soundly, but from forged evidence.
Predictions of trained models flip as well: almost always for a random forest,
for CatBoost as often as for the TFMs, hardly for a logistic regression. New
with TFMs is not the kind of vulnerability but the timing: the data take effect
at the moment of prediction, not through training, and a poison row in a live
context can be deleted afterwards. Three established requirements must therefore
cover the context: access control for its source, traceability per prediction,
which can also deter, and validation at prediction time. A detection mechanism
shows how such validation can work.
