Title: Evaluating Instrument-Centered AI Agents for Table-Top Science Experiments
Authors: Jiaqi Cai, Siyu Cheng, William S. Huxter, Li-Qiao Xia, Shuwen Sun, Shuai Yuan, Yifan Zhao, Naiyuan Zhang, Weijie Li, Muxian Xu et al.
ID: 2610.41717
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41717

Abstract:
Reliable evaluation of AI agents for table-top science experiments has remained
elusive because live instruments are hard to reset and share at scale, while
instrument statefulness and safety constraints complicate evaluation. We
introduce instrMCP and q-devBench, an evaluation stack for instrument-centered
AI agents in table-top experiments. instrMCP connects coding agents to
scientific instruments through the Model Context Protocol, serving as the
instrument-access harness. q-devBench reconstructs 37 real experimental tasks
from condensed-matter physics as virtual QCoDeS instruments covering quantum
electronic transport, scanning probe imaging, and calibration workflows.
q-devBench exposes reproducible failure modes in laboratory-agent systems while
preserving realistic instrument-control workflows. Across a range of commercial
coding-agent configurations, current systems already show substantial
operational competence, satisfying 70-83% of measurement-related rubric
criteria. The main bottleneck is scientific analysis, where performance falls to
28-46%. In an analysis-only ablation in which agents receive clean reference
datasets without running the virtual measurement session, analysis performance
improves substantially, closing roughly two thirds of the difference between the
measurement and analysis scores. This suggests that much of the deficit arises
from the measurement-session context, including tool-use overhead, longer
context, measurement-planning decisions, and possible acquisition errors, while
a residual scientific-reasoning gap remains. Together, instrMCP and q-devBench
provide a practical sandbox for benchmarking, diagnosing, and improving
laboratory AI harnesses, and establish a route from controlled virtual
evaluation to future live-hardware validation.
