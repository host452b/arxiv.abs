Title: Scenario-aware Adaptive Neuromorphic Intelligence for multimodal sensor fusion
Authors: Kapil Bhardwaj, Tauseef Ejaz, Sayani Majumdar
ID: 2611.07830
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07830

Abstract:
Multisensory Physical AI systems operating in isolated conditions must make
timely and reliable decisions within strict energy and hardware budgets. In this
context, the present analytical study investigates the feasibility of reliable
multimodal sensor fusion using a neuromorphic approach based on memristor-based
Integrate-and-Fire neuron circuits driven by aggressively compressed
representations of sensory information. Rather than relying on fixed processing
and learning parameters, the proposed framework introduces scenario-dependent
adaptability into individual stages of the neuromorphic decision-making
pipeline, including sensor-data compression, nonlinear spike encoding,
reliability assessment, and memristive learning-forgetting dynamics. By coupling
multiple sensor inputs through adaptive memristive synapses, the framework
investigates how reliable and correlated observations can strengthen learned
associations, while weak or obsolete relationships gradually decay through
nonlinear forgetting. The resulting neuronal responses are evaluated across ten
representative driving scenarios derived from the NuScenes dataset by comparing
their encoded output patterns with predefined synthetic ground-truth codes
representing the expected actuator responses. The simulations demonstrate that
scenario-adaptive activity-vector sizing achieves a 61.5% reduction in retained
sensory information, while improving mean bit-wise agreement from approximately
55% to 61.2% compared with a fixed maximum-resolution configuration. These
findings highlight the potential of introducing scenario-aware adaptability
directly into the building blocks of a neuromorphic system, providing a
foundation for future hardware implementations that could significantly
transform the capabilities of Physical AI systems operating under stringent
resource and environmental constraints.
