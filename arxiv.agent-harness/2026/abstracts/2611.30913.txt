Title: Demystifying Detectors Guarding Agent Skill Hubs
Authors: Kang Yang, Kevin Ying, Xiaojing Liao, Guanhong Tao, Jun Xu
ID: 2611.30913
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30913

Abstract:
Agent Skills has gained widespread adoption as an open standard for extending
agent capabilities. To facilitate skill sharing, public skill hubs have grown in
popularity and increasingly rely on automated security detectors to identify
malicious skills at scale. Yet, the effectiveness and limitations of these
detectors remain insufficiently understood. To fill this gap, we present the
first systematic study of four hub-adopted and two community-provided malicious
skill detectors on 1477 agent skills from seven datasets. Our evaluation reveals
that even the best-performing hub-adopted detector by overall F1, skill-scanner
with Qwen3.6, still misses 4.9% of malicious skills and incorrectly flags 17.8%
of benign skills. To understand these failures, we manually analyze detectors'
findings and decision-making processes, identifying two recurring failure
mechanisms. False positives often arise when detectors flag sensitive
operations, such as credential access, without considering whether they serve
the skill's legitimate purpose. Conversely, false negatives occur when detectors
recognize malicious instructions or behaviors but underestimate their severity
or dismiss valid findings during review, ultimately leaving malicious skills
unblocked. Guided by this analysis, we refine the prompts of a state-of-the-art
detector, skill-vetter, improving its mean F1 score in eight of nine evaluation
settings, with gains of up to 22.7 percentage points. These results highlight
gaps in existing skill-vetting practices and underscore the need for more robust
and context-aware detection mechanisms.
