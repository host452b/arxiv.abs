Title: Seeing Is Not Planning: Spatiotemporal Planning and Plan Repair in Multimodal LLMs
Authors: Siyuan Li, Ethan Lin, Xiang Li, Tianming Liu
ID: 2611.28726
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28726

Abstract:
Planning in a visual world means reading the layout, foreseeing how the state
will change, and following the task rules all at once. When a plan fails, a
model should also use what went wrong to fix it. We call these two abilities
spatiotemporal planning and plan repair, and test them with Railbound-Bench. A
model sees a railway puzzle, adds tracks under a budget, and submits one
complete layout; a simulator then moves all cars at once and decides whether the
layout works. After a failure, the model sees what happened and may submit a new
layout, up to five attempts in total. On the 200-puzzle main set, the best of
nine models solves 59.5% within five attempts, the next 39.5%, and the other
seven at most 10.0%; a 1,000-puzzle extended set with a wider range of
difficulty is far easier, at 90.9% for the best model. Controlled studies on 180
fixed puzzles show that knowing the task is not the same as using it. GPT-6.1
Terra copies 97.2% of boards exactly and, given a correct layout as text,
predicts all four requested future states in 91.3% of cases, yet only 22.8% of
its first layouts work. Planning fails more often as the cars' routes interact,
even among puzzles that a search solver finds equally easy. Visual feedback
removes the reported fault in 175 of 417 first revisions, but only 43 of these
revisions solve the puzzle, and at an equal budget of 40k tokens it does no
better than fresh retries. The code and data are open-sourced on GitHub at
https://github.com/liaoyanqing666/Railbound_Bench.
