Title: Extracting Next Generation Access Control Policies from Natural Language Using LLMs: An Intermediate-Representation Approach with Back-Translation Verification
Authors: Ayesha Habib, Huixiang Zhang, Waiz Haider
ID: 2611.26941
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26941

Abstract:
Organizations write access-control rules in natural language, but NIST's Next
Generation Access Control (NGAC) needs them as a formal policy graph. Large
language models (LLMs) can automate this translation, but their output can break
the format, add rules the text does not state, or drop conditions. We present
NGAC-IR, which follows model-driven engineering (MDE). First, the LLM fills in
an intermediate representation (IR), a JSON template that records who may, must
or must not do what, on which resource and under which condition; a grammar
keeps the output within this template. Second, a compiler that uses no LLM
converts the IR into an NGAC policy graph and links each rule to its source
sentence. Third, a back-translation check writes each rule in English and asks
an LLM whether the source sentence states it. On four public datasets, all
outputs could be compiled, compared with 95.5% without the grammar, and 95.7%
were faithful to their sentence. NGAC-IR outperforms two published methods on
all four datasets: the LLM-based Code4Policy in exact match and faithfulness,
and the rule-based extractor of Abdelgawad et al. in rule type. The back-
translation check sent 10.5% of sentences to human review and found 26 of the 31
errors. To test enforcement, we deployed the compiled policies in NIST's Policy
Machine: they gave the expected decision for all 390 access requests, and the
tested obligations were carried out. On one local server with open-weight
models, all 714 sentences take about 20 minutes and 0.35 kWh, at zero API cost.
