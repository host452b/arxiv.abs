Title: Do Waste Classifiers Recognise Items They Have Never Seen? Object-Disjoint Evaluation on Household and Medical Waste
Authors: Nusrat Jahan, Mst. Eshrat Jahan Esha, Masuma Sadia, Saad Ahmed, Md Zahim Hassan, S M Jahangir Alam
ID: 2611.30911
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30911

Abstract:
Automatic waste classification supports source separation, which enables
recycling and keeps hazardous medical items out of household waste. Waste
classification studies often report near-perfect accuracy on random image
splits, which put photographs of one item in both training and test sets. To
measure how well classifiers recognise items they have never seen, we built
BanglaWaste-23, with 4,160 images of 172 objects in 23 household and medical
waste classes from Bangladesh. Every image also records which physical item it
shows. We evaluated 11 ImageNet-pretrained models and two models trained from
scratch (a compact custom network and MobileNetV3-L) using object-disjoint five-
fold cross-validation and a random split. On unseen objects, the best pretrained
model reached a macro-averaged F1 score of 98.8%, and six models were
statistically tied. The random split overstated from-scratch accuracy by 15 to
19 percentage points. The from-scratch models also missed 11 to 13% of medical
items, against under 1% for pretrained models. On a second dataset from Dhaka,
pretrained accuracy fell by 15 to 22 points. Classifiers should therefore be
evaluated on unseen objects, with random-split results read as an upper bound.
The data are on Zenodo and the code at
https://github.com/saadbaust/BanglaWaste-23.
