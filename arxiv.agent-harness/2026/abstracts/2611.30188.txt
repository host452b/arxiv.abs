Title: Calibrating World Action Models: From Adaptive System 2 Takeover to Self-Evolution
Authors: Wenhao Li, Yu Han, Xiu Su, Dehao Wu, Keke Huang, Shan You, Fei Wang, Chang Xu
ID: 2611.30188
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30188

Abstract:
World action models (WAMs) deliver fast visuomotor control, yet degrade sharply
in challenging scenes and act blindly when about to fail; conversely,
foundation-model-based agents generalize and recover reliably, but incur
prohibitive latency and cost when queried at every step. To achieve both WAM
(System 1)-level speed and agent (System 2)-level reliability--- we propose
\textbf{\oursfull (\ours)}, a framework that calibrates WAMs' confidence---
adaptively triggering System~2 only when necessary, and distilling System~2's
knowledge to form an evolving System~1. First, \ours equips a frozen WAM with
\textbf{calibrated self-assessment}: a single lightweight judgment token reading
internal video--action features is trained via our proposed \textbf{cross-fitted
temporal-difference (TD) learning} using only binary rollout success/failure
signals. It can predict a \textbf{calibrated success probability} at negligible
overhead without affecting action predictions. Second, this calibrated success
probability schedules control chunk by chunk---invoking System~2 to resolve
bottlenecks when confidence drops, and handing control back to the WAM once
confidence recovers, preserving WAM-like speed while approaching System~2
reliability. Third, as System~2 intervenes only when the WAM lacks confidence,
its takeover trajectories directly capture the WAM's capability gaps. JiL
distills these takeovers into the WAM to repair those gaps so System~1 can
operate standalone without System~2 at test time, while our mastered-skill
anchoring and dynamic confidence gating prevent degrading existing capabilities.
