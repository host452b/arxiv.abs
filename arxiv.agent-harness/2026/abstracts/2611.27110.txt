Title: RemoteEvolver: Expanding Earth Observation Capabilities through Agent Harness Evolution
Authors: Shengxiang Xu, Liang Yao, Shuyi Chen, Yishu Wang, Shimin Di, Fan Liu, Bishun Yao, Hanghui Guo, Xinyu Liu, Chengye Liang et al.
ID: 2611.27110
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27110

Abstract:
We are interested in developing a continually self-improving remote sensing
agentic system. It should identify and address its capability gaps, retaining
the resulting improvements to enhance performance on subsequent tasks. However,
existing systems typically rely on fixed toolsets and confine their experience
accumulation to re-orchestrating predefined capabilities, constraining both
capability expansion and the transfer of improvements across tasks. As the
infrastructure for agentic systems, the harness manages tools and workflow
knowledge as editable, persistent resources outside the base model. These
resources can therefore be updated and reused across tasks without additional
post-training. Based on these observations, we propose RemoteEvolver, an
external harness for remote sensing agents that drives tool and workflow
evolution through execution feedback. With the base model parameters fixed,
RemoteEvolver acquires missing specialized tools on demand and distills verified
successful execution experience from individual tasks into agent skills that can
be reused across tasks. The acquired tools and accumulated skills jointly
support planning and execution on subsequent tasks. Experiments on Earth-Bench
and OpenEarthAgent-Bench show that RemoteEvolver achieves average accuracy gains
of 8.18 and 23.86 percentage points over its initial state when tools are fully
available and partially missing. Under the same settings, it also outperforms
the state-of-the-art methods by an average of 6.77 and 17.10 percentage points,
respectively.
