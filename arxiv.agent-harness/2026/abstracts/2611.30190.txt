Title: Same Executions, Different Gaps: Judgment Rules in Web-Agent Evaluation
Authors: Zelong Wang, Weiyue Li, Fei Xue, Junru Zhu
ID: 2611.30190
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30190

Abstract:
Success-rate gains guide the development of web agents. Yet knowing which agent
ranks first does not tell us how much confidence to place in its reported
advantage. We study the comparison itself: how success is defined, what evidence
supports the judgments, and how the difference between agents is estimated. On
fixed web-agent executions, changing a judge from predicting likely completion
to requiring recorded support can remove most of a reported lead without
changing the ranking. This effect persists when unresolved completion is
distinguished from supported failure. Controlled source additions explain how
missing relations between task requirements, objects and observations can
conceal a difference or create an apparent advantage. We also find that making
both agents' score estimates more accurate can make their difference less
accurate, because their original errors partly canceled. To estimate a chosen
outcome difference, we combine judge predictions with reference labels for
sampled task pairs. Across four released execution systems, regression fitted on
the same 50 labeled pairs reduces estimation error relative to direct paired
sampling. The results give agent researchers a practical basis for assessing
claimed improvements: make the comparison target explicit, retain the evidence
needed to check it, and use paired labels to estimate the difference directly.
