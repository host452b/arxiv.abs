Title: Watermark Claims Under Test: Lessons from Auditing a Production LLM
Authors: Felix Desyatirikov
ID: 2611.29018
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29018

Abstract:
Can we tell whether an LLM is watermarked without knowing its secret key?
Published black-box tests suggest that carefully designed prompts can reveal the
statistical footprint of a watermark. This paper examines what happens when
those tests move from controlled reference implementations to a deployed model
whose watermarking parameters are undisclosed. We present an audit of the Red-
Green watermark presence test on Claude. Across ten initial configurations, the
published test never rejects on a model the provider states is watermarked,
despite detecting a local reference at comparable sample sizes. Controlled
experiments expose a failure mode: including the prompt in the watermark's
repeated-context history suppresses marking exactly where the test measures it.
The watermark remains active, but the probe becomes blind. Attempts to repair
the probe reveal further confounds. Constructed contexts can trigger native
digit-to-word preferences, while comparisons within a single response can
measure how the model plans its answer. Tokenization changes which contexts the
probe actually controls. These findings show why validation on a surrogate model
does not establish validity on the model under investigation. A redesigned probe
finds a substantial context-dependent shift on Claude Fable that is absent on
two sibling controls. However, the pre-registered decision remains inconclusive
because selective refusals violate the protocol, and the effect's watermark
origin is unresolved. We show how to design controls, distinguish statistical
rejection from evidence of watermarking and interpret a null without treating it
as proof of absence. We provide researchers and practitioners working on LLM
evaluation, watermarking, and provenance with an audit checklist grounded in
concrete successes and failures. Code, prompts, and parsed responses are
available for reproducing the analyses.
