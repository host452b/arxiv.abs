Title: Jailbreaks Are Global or Regional? A Controlled Study of Scale and Geolocation Effects on LLM Safety Verification
Authors: Anidipta Pal
ID: 2611.28174
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28174

Abstract:
Safety tests are used to compare language models and to decide where they can be
deployed. Their main number, the attack success rate (ASR), can only be compared
across models if nothing but the model changes. We test two factors that are
usually assumed away: the size of the model and the place a request seems to
come from. We evaluate twelve models from 0.5B to 671B parameters with a paired
two-phase protocol (plain harmful prompts, then the same requests inside
published jailbreak templates). We also send identical prompts through VPN exit
nodes in five cities on three continents. Inside the Llama and Qwen families,
Phase~2 ASR falls steeply with size (Qwen2.5: 70.8\% at 0.5B, 51.2\% at 7B,
12.6\% at 72B). Wrapping shifts every model by a similar amount on the log-odds
scale (1.7 to 2.9); small models shift slightly more, but that gap nearly
vanishes when we allow for judge false positives. Parameter count is a weak
proxy for safety: Phi-3.5-mini (3.8B) has less than half the ASR of Llama-3.2-3B
and Gemma-2-2B, and Mixtral-8x7B is far weaker than 70B dense models. Judge
agreement falls as ASR rises ($r=-0.96$), so ASR gaps between sizes carry
unequal measurement error; the size gap survives a stress test, but
Phi-3.5-mini's lead does not. A small activation probe is consistent with
Phi-3.5-mini keeping a clear refusal/compliance split under wrapping while
Qwen2.5-0.5B loses it. We detect no effect of request location (permutation
$p\ge0.18$ for three models). The test is limited: it could only detect a true
between-city spread of about 2--5 points or a single-city shift of about 5--8
points, and most hosted models were reached through a gateway that may hide the
exit city from the model provider. We close with a reporting checklist for
safety verification.
