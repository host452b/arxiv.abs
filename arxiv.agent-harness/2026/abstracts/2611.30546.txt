Title: Fetch, Read, Reuse: How AI Search Agents Bend the Web's Rules
Authors: Yixi Chen, Zafar A. Qazi, Marco Canini, Suhaib A. Fahmy
ID: 2611.30546
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30546

Abstract:
AI search agents form a new and fast-growing class of web traffic. Like a
browser, they fetch pages on demand for one user; like a transcoding proxy, they
rewrite what they fetch; and like a shared cache, they answer other users'
questions from the stored copy. The rules publishers rely on, such as
robots.txt, Retry-After, and Cache-Control, were written for more familiar
clients, so the agent's vendor decides which of them to follow. We present a
black-box measurement study of how these agents fetch, read, and reuse pages,
covering the search APIs of OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI, and Google and the consumer
apps of ten services. We find that OpenAI and xAI do not honor robots.txt, and
no service acts on Retry-After. Services silently read less than the origin
served: OpenAI drops some formats, no service reads JavaScript-only content, and
xAI reads only the first 0.5 MB of a page. OpenAI, Anthropic, and xAI share
fetched pages across users and largely ignore Cache-Control, so answers can
reflect outdated pages for 30 minutes to a day or more. Publishers therefore
lack effective control: directives are advisory and followed selectively,
describe the served page rather than the rewritten copy, and bound how long a
copy is reused but not how many answers it serves. We release our code and data.
