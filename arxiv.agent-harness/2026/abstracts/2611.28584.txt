Title: Right Verdict, Right Reason? An Execution-Grounded Study of Whether LLM Vulnerability Detectors Understand Vulnerabilities
Authors: Leizhen Zhang, Sheng Chen
ID: 2611.28584
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28584

Abstract:
Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly used to detect vulnerabilities,
and their benchmark scores are often read as evidence that they understand the
vulnerabilities they report. We test this reading against program execution. For
515 C and C++ functions from BigVul and PrimeVul whose vulnerability we
reproduce with a proof of concept, we identify the line at which the fault
occurs and the statements on which the faulting run depended. These facts ground
a ladder of five tests, from telling a vulnerable function from its fixed
version, through locating the fault and reacting to its removal, to judging
execution-confirmed edits and predicting the outcome of a concrete run. Each
test is paired with a baseline that needs no understanding, such as a rule that
picks lines with risky calls, and we repeat the study on 195 vulnerabilities
published after the training cutoffs of the models. Four findings emerge. First,
telling a vulnerable function from its fixed version is hard. The open-weight
models separate no more pairs than chance, although five of them show a weak
sensitivity to the fix, and the commercial models still label many screened
fixed versions vulnerable. Second, the commercial models locate the fault beyond
a keyword rule, while removing that evidence changes the verdicts of the
strongest model only on the newer vulnerabilities. Third, given the program
input, the commercial models predict whether a run faults better than a rule
that looks for added checks, and the prediction follows the input. Fourth, on
the newer vulnerabilities this advantage disappears and the lead of the
strongest model narrows, while localization persists. We release the framework,
the per-function evidence, and an execution-confirmed counterfactual benchmark.
