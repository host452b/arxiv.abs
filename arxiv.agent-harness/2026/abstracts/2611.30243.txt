Title: Can Search Agents Correct Their Own Errors? Evaluating Retrieval, Evidence Delivery, and Answer Completion
Authors: Venkata Pagadala, K. Rajasekhara Rao, Pratyay Ganguly
ID: 2611.30243
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30243

Abstract:
A search agent can leave an earlier error unresolved by missing the right
source, overlooking a relevant passage, or failing to revise and deliver the
answer after receiving the evidence. We evaluate these possibilities on
BrowseComp-Plus by replaying each model's own wrong closed-book answer in
conversation and intervening on what evidence reaches the model. In a supplied-
evidence study spanning four open-weight checkpoints, 709 questions and 26,665
test conditions, a confident assistant prior reduced Nemotron's paired accuracy
by 16.1 percentage points despite answer-bearing passages in context. When the
user insisted on the prior, accuracy fell by 32.9 points for Nemotron and 12.6
for gpt-oss. Among gpt-oss responses that lost correctness in that condition,
abstention was more common than repeating the prior. In separate search
interventions with Qwen3.5 and Nemotron, placing a gold page first often left
the decisive source unopened. Delivering a decisive passage in context improved
accuracy over neutral delivery by 51.0 percentage points for Qwen3.5 and 22.4
for Nemotron. Even with the passage in context, Nemotron exhausted its search
budget and returned no answer in 19 of 51 episodes. The tests distinguish
failures of retrieval, passage delivery, correction and answer completion,
giving system builders a way to test which changes resolve a recurring error.
