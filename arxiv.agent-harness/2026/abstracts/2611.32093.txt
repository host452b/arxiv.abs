Title: MARGIN: A Risk-Aware Agent for Robot Program Refinement
Authors: Hongbo Duan, Fan Du, Haipeng Luo, Bin Qian, Zichao Nie, Yilin Mi, Yaopeng Tian, Jiake Tian, Siyuan Liu, Yan Gao et al.
ID: 2611.32093
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32093

Abstract:
Robot-programming agents improve executable control policies through repeated
testing and repair, yet binary success saturates while execution risk persists
across unseen scenes. We introduce MARGIN, a risk-aware agent that converts
successful robot rollouts into actionable evidence for program refinement. Its
near-miss margin measures the fraction of the execution budget remaining in the
slowest successful development rollout. The agent uses this signal to prioritize
fragile programs, guide targeted repairs, and select revisions that preserve
development success while increasing execution headroom. An exact safe-racing
rule accelerates success-based candidate evaluation. We evaluate MARGIN across
LIBERO-Pro, LIBERO-90, and Robosuite. In our 80-task LIBERO-Pro reproduction, 78
programs attain perfect development success while accounting for substantial
failures on unseen seeds. The near-miss margin predicts held-out failures with
an AUC of 0.774, and low-margin programs exhibit 2.97 times higher failure risk.
Across two independent evaluation rounds spanning 37 unique LIBERO-Pro tasks,
MARGIN reduces executed control steps by 10.7% and 15.0%, with reductions of
18.0-20.0% on LIBERO-90 and Robosuite. Safe racing reduces fixed-sequence
candidate-evaluation episodes by 53.5% while retaining identical selections, and
paired execution probes improve acceptance decisions by identifying hidden
regressions. Together, these results establish near-miss evidence as an
effective basis for risk-aware, budget-efficient robot program refinement.
