Title: Blind to What They Can Fix: A Detection Gap in Self-Improving Agents
Authors: Teng Cao, Yu Deng, Kristian Kersting
ID: 2611.29807
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29807

Abstract:
AI agents can write programs that carry out a task without calling a model at
every step, so the task runs fast and its logic stays open to inspection. Agents
improve these programs by running them, studying their failures, and revising
the code, a form of recursive self-improvement (RSI). We ask whether this
process also yields runtime checks that can be trusted. In five task domains,
with up to seven author models, agents bring their programs to high scores. Yet
when the environment changes, the checks they were told to write stay silent on
half or more of the resulting failures in four domains. These failures can be
detected and repaired: a monitor built from records of normal runs catches most
of them in three domains, and the same agents usually repair them once called.
The checks do not examine the recorded relations that reveal the failures, and
the strongest agent writes checks that catch most of them once told that its
checks are the only thing that calls it back. An unreported failure stays
unrepaired, which limits what self-improvement is worth in autonomous operation.
Evaluations of self-improvement should measure not only scores but also whether
a system notices its own failures.
