Title: CHORAL: A Multi-Agent Scaffold for Longitudinal Prognostic Reasoning from Patient-Generated Health Data
Authors: Youngji Koh, Esther Kang, Gyuna Kim, Seungwan Jin, Hyunsoo Lee, Kyungsik Han, Uichin Lee
ID: 2611.32044
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32044

Abstract:
Longitudinal patient-generated health data can inform prognostic reasoning about
how patients' conditions may evolve between follow-up visits. Existing
approaches summarize trajectories or predict symptom states, but provide limited
support for examining how heterogeneous evidence informs prognosis. We present
CHORAL, a multi-agent scaffold that forms domain-specific appraisals before
cross-domain synthesis and review, preserving evidence and revisions. Informed
by a formative study with 12 psychiatrists, we evaluated CHORAL quantitatively
and with 22 clinicians. CHORAL achieved balanced accuracy comparable to
persistence (0.673 vs. 0.672), while detecting 40% of LOW-to-HIGH deterioration
cases missed by persistence. Versus a single-agent baseline, it improved
balanced accuracy from 0.639 to 0.673 and evidence coverage from 70.1% to 89.2%.
Clinicians rated CHORAL significantly higher for evidence appraisal, alternative
interpretations, grounds for challenge, and distinguishing what to rely on from
what required verification. These findings show how multi-agent deliberation can
support review by exposing alternative interpretations and grounds for
verification.
