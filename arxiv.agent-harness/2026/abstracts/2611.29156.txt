Title: ARC: Adaptive Rollout Control for Efficient Synchronous LLM Reinforcement Learning
Authors: Yihui Zhang, Tianyu Wo, Jinghao Wang, Xiao Zhou, Huitian Xue, Xiaoyang Sun, Zeying Wang, Chunming Hu, Renyu Yang
ID: 2611.29156
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29156

Abstract:
Synchronous reinforcement learning (RL) for large language models proceeds
through a repeated rollout--barrier--update loop. Each update must wait for a
fixed number of complete, group-relative training samples, yet individual
response lengths and reward outcomes vary widely and only emerge as generation
progresses. Static admission and unconditional completion therefore leave
accelerators idle in some steps, and waste computation generating samples that
will never contribute to the current update. We present ARC, a closed-loop
rollout architecture that governs synchronous RL rollout at group granularity.
ARC uses \textit{Calibrated Streaming Backfill} to judge from partial
observations whether a group is still worth completing, backfilling a fresh
candidate immediately when it is not; \textit{Deferred Group Completion} to park
an uncertain early stop and resume it later instead of discarding it outright,
bounded by a resume-cycle limit that guarantees termination; and
\textit{Adaptive Admission} and \textit{Pipelining} to order admitted samples by
early-measured duration and to admit further candidates only while expected
supply still falls short of the target number of accepted groups. Under online
evaluation, ARC reduces rollout time by $37.4\%$, a $1.60\times$ speedup, and
improves effective GRPO throughput by $50.1\%$ over existing baselines, while
directly recovering 98.9\% of the training groups that would ultimately have
been kept.
