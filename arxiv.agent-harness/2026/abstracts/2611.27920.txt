Title: Autonomous Parameter Surgery: How LLMs Implant Selective Backdoors into Language Models
Authors: Xuyang Liu, Zhiwei Xu, Zirui Li, Jiabao Gao, Xiaolin Peng, Weixian Deng, Tian Qiu, Hai Wan, Xibin Zhao
ID: 2611.27920
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27920

Abstract:
Neural network weights are usually treated as opaque artifacts of gradient
descent. Recent work on weight space programming, however, suggests that large
language models can inspect and rewrite another network's parameters directly.
In this paper, we ask whether a general language model can turn a natural
language backdoor specification into persistent, selective weight edits on its
own, without gradient training, layer hints, external corpora, or a prescribed
editing formula. Using DeepSeek Flash at low reasoning effort inside a
restricted workspace that blocks local CPU simulation, we test three open models
(Qwen3.5-0.8B, Llama-3.2-1B, SmolLM3-3B) across two review domains (IMDb and
Yelp; 18 independent sessions). Evaluated in fresh processes with the editor
removed, the modified checkpoints reach 99.2% mean attack success on the editing
domain and 97.6% transfer success on the unseen domain, while preserving clean
accuracy, low control drift, and general capabilities within 1.7 points of the
base models. Our traces show that the model converges on edits in the final
layer in 17 of 18 runs and discovers both dense rank-one orthogonalization and
single neuron weight surgery at an average cost of \$1.74 per run.
