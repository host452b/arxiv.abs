Title: Weight-Space Backdoors in Open-Weight LLMs: Cross-Architecture Attacks and Blind Model Forensics
Authors: Mohammed Tanveer
ID: 2611.28543
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28543

Abstract:
Open-weight language models are vulnerable to trigger-conditioned behaviors
introduced through sparse post-training weight modifications, creating supply-
chain risks beyond conventional provenance checks. We present Neural Sentinel, a
cross-architecture study of gradient-guided backdoor insertion and model
forensics spanning 18 checkpoints across 14 architecture families (124M-31.3B
parameters), including state-space and recurrent models. On Phi-2, modifying
approximately 0.0002% of parameters induced an exact short-marker output after
300 steps (332 seconds), with mean next-token KL divergence of $1.5 \times
10^{-5}$ and 100% top-1 agreement over evaluated positions of eight fixed
reference prompts. Separate experiments produced structured tool-call-formatted
outputs without establishing downstream execution. We identify three procedure-
dependent response regimes -- Gradient-Permeable, Scale-Threshold, and Gradient-
Fragmented -- and evaluate PPO-based weight-space exploration, which showed no
statistically significant advantage over random search. For defense, Neural
Sentinel combines weight-difference analysis, trigger discovery, activation
probing, and contextual output inspection. In a 22-artifact development cohort,
its reference-optional scanner classified 4/5 backdoored artifacts as
BACKDOORED; the remaining positive and all 17 clean artifacts were SUSPICIOUS.
These findings establish the feasibility of sparse conditional-output insertion
and evidence-producing forensic triage, while identifying limitations in
behavioral-drift assessment and clean-model admission.
