Title: TurboFHE: An Efficient and Scalable Multi-GPU Framework for Homomorphic LLM Inference
Authors: Honghui You, Haoping Yang, Wenzhe Wang, Lei Ju
ID: 2611.29244
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29244

Abstract:
Fully homomorphic encryption (FHE) enables privacy-preserving large language
model inference but remains computationally intensive. Existing multi-GPU FHE
inference systems rely on expert-crafted parallelization plans that generalize
poorly across hardware platforms and applications. We present \fwName, a
framework that automatically compiles CKKS inference programs into efficient
multi-GPU executables. Its compiler can split different nodes of the same
computation graph at the ciphertext and polynomial level. Guided by a
measurement-based performance model, its hardware-aware planner jointly
optimizes task partitioning, placement, operand residency, and transfer
scheduling. %Critical-path-guided rewrites extract shared computation and
restructure homomorphic sums. Its backend then generates optimized GPU kernels
and executes plans using CUDA graphs and selective point-to-point transfers.
\fwName adapts its plans to diverse hardware configurations and FHE inference
algorithms for large-scale models. On a single A100 GPU, \fwName achieves a
$24.5\times$ speedup over THOR's reported BERT-Base inference latency. For
Cerium's Llama-3-8B workload, \fwName outperforms Cerium's expert-crafted plans
by $1.14\times$, $1.16\times$, and $1.24\times$ on 2, 4, and 8 RTX PRO~6000
GPUs, respectively. On A100 GPUs, \fwName achieves $98.0\%$, $91.8\%$, and
$79.3\%$ parallel efficiency for the same workload on 2, 4, and 8 GPUs,
respectively.
