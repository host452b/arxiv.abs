Title: CLAIR: Consistent LLM Adaptation via Index-free Reprogramming
Authors: Dongkuk Kim, Jungkyu Shin, Yoonhyuk Choi, Jong Wook Kim, Beakcheol Jang
ID: 2611.28721
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28721

Abstract:
Time-series forecasting with pretrained large language models (LLMs) uses
contextualized patch representations, raising the question of how to align them.
Matching patches at the same temporal position is natural, but self-attention
allows each patch state to incorporate information from elsewhere in the input
window, suggesting that useful correspondences can extend across positions. This
flexibility, however, does not necessarily translate into consistent forecasts:
similar patch states may play different roles when used in their original
temporal order. To address both issues, we propose CLAIR (Consistent LLM
Adaptation via Index-free Reprogramming), a teacher-guided framework that
combines patch-level transport alignment (PTA) and forecast-level consistency
(FC). A clean-view exponential moving average (EMA) teacher guides a mildly
perturbed student, with PTA establishing soft, representation-dependent
correspondences and FC encouraging agreement at corresponding future steps. The
forecasting head retains the original patch order. We test this combination
through controlled comparisons: while PTA and index-wise alignment perform
similarly without FC, PTA+FC under EMA supervision reduces horizon-averaged mean
squared error (MSE) by 2.1% on ETTh1 and 2.6% on ETTm1 relative to Index+FC. The
same preference for PTA+FC emerges in comparisons with alternative matching
strategies on Weather and ETTh2. Beyond these controls, CLAIR achieves the
lowest horizon-averaged MSE on five of six long-term benchmarks and the lowest
aggregate overall weighted average (OWA) on M4 among evaluated methods, with
competitive few-shot performance. As the teacher and transport are used only
during training, inference requires only the student.
