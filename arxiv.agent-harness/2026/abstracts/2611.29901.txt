Title: Synapse: An HBM-Coordinated HBF Near-Memory Computing Architecture for Pipelined Agentic AI Execution
Authors: Juhyeong Jin, Hyunjun Cho, Joo-Young Kim
ID: 2611.29901
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29901

Abstract:
During multi-agent video reasoning, model weights and per-agent key-value caches
can exceed high-bandwidth memory (HBM) capacity. High-bandwidth flash (HBF) adds
capacity, but agents still wait for GPU execution. Synapse, an HBM-coordinated
near-memory computing architecture, overlaps HBF attention and expert execution
with GPU computation across agents. It also reduces dispatch delays and
unnecessary refreshes. Across three models, Synapse achieves 48.92-66.60% lower
end-to-end latency, up to $2.98\times$ throughput, and up to 64.48% lower
modeled energy per output token than GPU execution with passive HBF. Wear
simulations project 46% longer HBF lifetime than block-wide refresh at equal
workload rates.
