Title: Beyond Relevance: An Empirical Study of Multimodal LLMs for Diversity-Aware Recommendation Reranking
Authors: Chengkai Liu, Yan Fu, Huiyuan Chen, Sheikh Sarwar, Qilin Qi
ID: 2611.28379
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28379

Abstract:
Large language models (LLMs) have emerged as promising rerankers for
recommendation, yet most existing approaches rely on textual item descriptions
and primarily focus on relevance. In visually oriented domains such as video
streaming, item images provide complementary signals that may also inform
recommendation diversity. This raises two questions: when does visual
information improve recommendation quality, and can multimodal LLMs (MLLMs)
enhance relevance and diversity simultaneously? We investigate these questions
through a training-free, diversity-aware multimodal reranking framework that
jointly incorporates item images, textual metadata, and user interaction
histories. We conduct an empirical study across three public Amazon Review
datasets and an industrial Amazon Prime Video dataset, using both open-source
and proprietary MLLMs. Our results reveal substantial domain-dependent effects:
adding visual information yields up to 121.9% relative improvement in NDCG@5 on
Prime Video, while producing limited or inconsistent gains on product
recommendation datasets. Furthermore, MLLM-based reranking improves both
relevance and category-diversity-sensitive ranking quality over a sequential
recommendation baseline, achieving up to 71.0% improvement in NDCG@5 and 14.1%
in $α$-NDCG@5 on Prime Video. These findings highlight the conditional value of
visual information and the potential of training-free MLLMs for relevance- and
diversity-aware recommendation reranking.
