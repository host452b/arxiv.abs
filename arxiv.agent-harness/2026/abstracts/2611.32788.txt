Title: Keypose or Dense? A Controlled Comparison of Action Representations for Stage-Structured Manipulation
Authors: Haizhou Ge, Mengshuo Wang, Zhixing Chen, Lei Han, Chen Wang, Lu Shi, Guyue Zhou, Ruqi Huang
ID: 2611.32788
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32788

Abstract:
Most imitation policies that predict chunks of future actions use the dense
action representation, in which the chunk holds one control target per step. In
stage-structured manipulation, where a task can be divided into stages with
definable endpoints, the keypose action representation instead sets each step of
the chunk to the endpoint of the stage that step belongs to, and a speed-capped
servo supplies the motion. We compare the two in several simulated manipulation
tasks and on a real robot, with the same chunk length and matched
demonstrations, observations, policy architectures and update budgets; in
simulation, each one's execution rule is selected on tuning scenes. When the
simulated demonstrations are slowed, paused and rerouted, dense success falls
sharply while keypose stays roughly level, across three policy architectures and
with endpoints extracted from the recorded demonstrations; keypose also leads
under object-size and position shift. With clean demonstrations, keypose leads
only when they are scarce and is otherwise close to or behind dense across the
simulated tasks, provided that the demonstrations and execution rule suit its
executor; when they do not, it falls far below dense. The same dependence on the
executor appeared on a real robot learning to open a drawer from human
demonstrations: dense succeeded more often, and keypose improved substantially
only after its executor's rotation and forward speed were adjusted to the
demonstrations. These results suggest that keypose trades sensitivity to such
variation in the demonstrations for sensitivity to execution that departs from
them.
