Title: A Face Robot which Imitates Human Musculoskeletal Skin System and Its Evaluation on Basic Movement of Facial Expression
Authors: Shun Kaneko, Shunnosuke Yoshimura, Akihiro Miki, Kento Kawaharazuka, Kei Okada
ID: 2611.32413
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32413

Abstract:
Facial robots require many coordinated, smallamplitude motions, but
accommodating these motions in a compact face remains difficult. This paper
presents an anatomically inspired facial-robot platform that reproduces the
layered organization of the human facial musculoskeletal system. The platform
comprises bone, muscle, and skin components whose geometry, shape, and
attachment locations are derived from three-dimensional anatomical data. Wire-
driven, compliant muscle components are routed through PTFE tubes, allowing
actuation to be placed away from the face. We describe the selection and
fabrication of 65 muscles (34 muscle types) required to reproduce the 47 target
action units (AUs) in the Facial Action Coding System (FACS). We then evaluate
the reproducibility of individual AUs in two stages: muscle actuation without
skin and actuation after the silicone skin is attached. Forty of the 47 target
AUs were reproduced with the muscle components, and 30 were reproduced after
skin attachment. These results demonstrate the feasibility of reproducing a
broad range of FACS facial expressions using an anatomically inspired
musculoskeletal structure. The proposed platform provides a foundation for the
systematic development and evaluation of future anatomically inspired facial
robots
