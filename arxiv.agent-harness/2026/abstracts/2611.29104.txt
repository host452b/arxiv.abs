Title: When Does Decomposition Help? Empirical Study on Workload-Architecture Affinity in LLM Inference Serving
Authors: Jihui Yang, Wentai Wu, Jiahua Huang, Weiwei Lin, Ligang He, Linghong Tsang
ID: 2611.29104
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29104

Abstract:
Large language model (LLM) inference can be organized as independent monolithic
replicas, tensor- or pipeline-parallel serving groups, or disaggregated prefill
and decode services. Although decomposition is often presented as a route to
higher efficiency, its benefit depends on workload shape, model fit, and whether
phase-specific resources can be independently scaled. Existing comparisons
frequently change several of these factors at once. We isolate the architectural
effect by evaluating five SGLang serving organizations under the same
four-A100-40GB hardware envelope, pinned Qwen3-8B model, software stack,
prompts, and arrival schedules. Our evaluation contains 150 audited conditions
over short-chat, decode-heavy, and 32K prefill-heavy workloads. Four independent
one-GPU replicas achieve the lowest end-to-end latency for short chat and long
output and are the only architecture stable in every condition. Pipeline
parallelism reduces 32K time to first token by 58-59% at non-stress rates, while
hybrid pipeline-based disaggregation achieves the best 32K end-to-end latency at
0.4 requests/s. In contrast, B's single-VM static TP2+TP2 split, without
independent phase scaling, does not improve tested latency over replication and
collapses at higher 32K load. These results reveal a workload-architecture
affinity: pipeline decomposition favors prefill-heavy computation, whereas
replication favors decode-heavy and concurrent traffic. More broadly,
decomposition is not a monotonic optimization; its value depends on matching
parallelism granularity to the dominant phase and preserving queue stability.
