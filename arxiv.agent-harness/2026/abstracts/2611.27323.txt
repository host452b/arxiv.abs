Title: Cruella: A Unified Microscaling and Floating-Point Dot-Product Unit for LLM Inference on FPGAs
Authors: Jinho Yang, Taehyeong Kim, Mincheol Cha, Hyeoncheol Kim, Donggeun Kim, Nahye Kim, Yujin Lee, Ryeowook Ko, Woong Gyu Park, Jiwoong Park et al.
ID: 2611.27323
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27323

Abstract:
The number formats that accelerators execute natively are fixed at fabrication,
yet large language model (LLM) checkpoints are released with MXFP4 weights in
the Microscaling (MX) formats standardized in 2023. FPGAs adopt new formats
through bitstreams. However, to the best of our knowledge, no deployed FPGA LLM
accelerator executes MXFP4 weights alongside BF16 products. We characterize the
decoding of MXFP4 checkpoints, whose layers alternate between MXFP4$\times$BF16
products and BF16$\times$BF16 products of attention and BF16 layers. With a BF16
KV cache, BF16$\times$BF16 products take 56%-77% of the datapath time, during
which a separate MXFP4 unit idles. We present Cruella, whose unified dot-product
unit (UDPU) executes both product types on one DSP array and reduction pipeline.
First, per-group pre-alignment keeps this pipeline at the BF16 width and changes
perplexity by at most 0.7%. Second, pre-adder packing places two signed
MXFP4$\times$BF16 products in one DSP48E2 multiplier with an exact correction.
On an Alveo U55C, the UDPU needs 23% fewer DSP slices and 36% fewer LUTs than
separate MXFP4 and BF16 units of equal throughput. On this board, MXFP4 weights
speed up decoding by 1.15$\times$-1.87$\times$ over BF16 weights, and an NVIDIA
A40 GPU decodes faster but spends 0.92$\times$-2.01$\times$ the board's energy
per token.
