Title: Beyond Autoregression: Diffusion Language Model is All Analog In-Memory Computing Need
Authors: Yuanuo Feng, Yubing Ye, Yizhe Chen, Wenshuai Yao, Ngai Wong, Wenyong Zhou, Wang Kang
ID: 2611.32223
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32223

Abstract:
Analog in-memory computing (AIMC) enables energy-efficient language-model
inference, but hardware non-idealities perturb matrix--vector multiplications
and degrade generation quality. Autoregressive (AR) models are especially
vulnerable: each token is committed to the causal context at birth, so
perturbation-induced errors cascade. We study diffusion language models (DLMs),
which iteratively refine a masked sequence, as an alternative paradigm. Across
scale-matched model pairs and open checkpoints, DLMs degrade more gracefully
than AR counterparts under persistent weight perturbations. Controlled analyses
rule out cross-round noise averaging and support delayed token commitment and
progressive context resolution as the mechanism. A larger denoising-step budget
further shrinks the clean--noisy gap, a DLM-specific latency--robustness knob.
We perform large-scale simulation tests across diverse datasets and models using
a noise model calibrated from real silicon measurements. Furthermore, on a
16-chip 28nm eFlash AIMC cluster, hybrid on-board measurements show DLMs
tolerate $1.4$--$3\times$ larger weight noise, making diffusion-based generation
a promising paradigm for noise-resilient AIMC inference.
