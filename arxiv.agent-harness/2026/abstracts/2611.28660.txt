Title: Persuasion from Within: Model-Based Insider Attacks in Multi-Agent LLM Debate and Consensus
Authors: Bangyan Ju, Xiaolin Sun, Yibin Hu, Zizhan Zheng
ID: 2611.28660
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28660

Abstract:
Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed in multi-agent systems
for collective decision-making, such as multi-agent debate and consensus games,
in which agents exchange messages and update their answers over multiple rounds
and a resolution mechanism aggregates them into a final outcome. Most multi-
agent LLM frameworks assume that every participant shares the system objective.
In practice, however, a malicious agent can join as a legitimate member of the
group and persuade the other agents toward a hidden adversarial goal. We
formalize such attacks as a sequential decision-making problem in which the
insider seeks to degrade the utility of the collective outcome. We propose a
model-based insider attack that learns to persuade a group of agents: the
insider first learns a world model of the benign agents from offline
trajectories and then uses it both to train a persuasion policy and to plan its
messages. In two case studies involving a numerical consensus game and a multi-
agent debate, the learned insider is the most effective and the stealthiest
attack we evaluate, outperforming both prompt-based and trained LLM persuaders
from prior work. The attack also transfers to other target models, larger
groups, and unseen datasets, and remains effective under existing Byzantine-
resilience defenses. The learned insider also enables a new defense:
adversarially training benign agents on the attack data largely removes both our
attack and the strongest prior persuasion attack. These results show that
combining world models with reinforcement learning and planning is an effective
route to both attacking and hardening language-based multi-agent systems.
