Title: PreWAM: Exploring the Design of Latent Future Prediction for World-Action Modeling
Authors: Wan Chi, Sen Li, Zihang Cui, Chuqin Huang, Yunlong Wang, Nanshan Deng, Yanjun Huang
ID: 2611.31901
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31901

Abstract:
World-action models combine action generation with future prediction, allowing
visual transitions to serve as supervision for policy learning. Our controlled
study of prediction spaces shows that spatially structured semantic features
support better overall generalization under visual perturbations and make motion
information more readily recoverable from learned predictive representations.
Building on these observations, we introduce PreWAM, a world-action model that
organizes future supervision around state changes in a prediction space defined
by pretrained DINO features. Specifically, Residual World Prediction jointly
learns source-relative changes and their resulting future states to emphasize
dynamic information while preserving future-state semantics. Temporal-Scale
Prediction conditions world queries on temporal distance to capture both short-
term local motion and longer-horizon state evolution. These predictive
objectives jointly shape a shared vision-language backbone with action
supervision during pretraining and downstream policy learning. Experiments
across the LIBERO benchmark family, SimplerEnv, RoboTwin 2.0, and real-world
robotic manipulation demonstrate strong manipulation performance and improved
generalization and robustness under diverse distribution shifts.
