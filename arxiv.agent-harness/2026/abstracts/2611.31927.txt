Title: (No) Separation of Concerns: Philosophical Concerns about Agent Ethics and Implications for SE Practitioners
Authors: Marc Cheong, Sebastian Baltes, Joanne Byrne, Haoyu Gao, Raula Gaikovina Kula, Sarita Rosenstock, Christoph Treude, Mansooreh Zahedi
ID: 2611.31927
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31927

Abstract:
The use of AI agents in software engineering (SE) are commonplace, with
differing levels of autonomy, a myriad of capabilities, and ability to perform a
variety of tasks. Philosophers have begun to examine the ethics of such agents,
and SE researchers or practitioners could benefit from them; on the other hand,
SE practitioners currently explore most of these concerns through the lens of
principlism. We bridge this gap by reviewing the philosophical literature on AI
agents indexed in PhilPapers, and translating its ethical concerns into a
research agenda for SE researchers and into implications for teams that use
coding agents. A systematic mapping study of PhilPapers -- a major philosophy
index and open access archive -- yielded 33 peer-reviewed papers by professional
philosophers published since 2023. We coded these papers thematically, with
cross-disciplinary review of the emerging themes, resulting in eight themes and
85 codes. From these, we derive seven research questions for SE, covering
developers' reliance on agents, responsibility for agent-written code, ethical
constraints in agent configurations, and benchmark design. For practitioners, we
derive five implications: decide which choices an agent must refer to a human,
keep agent-written pull requests small enough to review, pair configuration-file
instructions with checks, watch for changes to tests, and record who approved
what. We argue that ethical concerns cannot be separated from SE concerns into a
module of their own: they arise inside code review, configuration, evaluation,
and requirements work, and addressing them requires cross-disciplinary work.
