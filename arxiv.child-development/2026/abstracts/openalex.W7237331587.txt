Title: Challenges and Improvement Strategies in Quality-Oriented Standardized Kindergarten Management
Authors: Qin Li
ID: openalex.W7237331587
URL: https://doi.org/10.64103/jsshp.20260017

Abstract:
Standardized management underpins the day-to-day operation of kindergartens and
the quality of the education and care they provide. As legislation and
assessment policy have developed, management has acquired a second task beyond
compliance: attention to children’s daily experience, to how curriculum is
actually delivered, to the interaction between teachers and children, and to the
conditions in which staff work. Drawing on policy documents and published
research, this paper examines standardized kindergarten management through the
lens of process quality, asking how administrative requirements, professional
support, resources and evaluation affect what happens in classrooms. Six
concerns recur in the literature and in practice: reliance on documentation and
inspection as the main form of oversight; pedagogical guidance that begins and
ends with a single event; thin professional support for staff; logistical
services organised apart from educational work; family participation that is
announced rather than arranged; and evaluation findings that are filed rather
than used. Each is connected to how tasks are set, how responsibilities are
divided, what support is available, and why information is collected in the
first place. The paper proposes differentiated requirements, sustained
classroom-based guidance, role-specific support, joint planning of safety and
resources, explicit terms for contact with families, and a clearer use of
process evaluation. The argument is that regulation and professional discretion
are not alternatives: a workable system needs both, calibrated to what children
and teachers actually do during the day.
