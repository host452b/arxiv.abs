Title: How School Leaders Drive Quality Improvement in Early Childhood Education: A Systematic Review of Leadership Practices, Mechanisms, and Contexts
Authors: Tina Alagba, Precious Chinonyerem Ibezim
ID: openalex.W7237521500
URL: https://doi.org/10.66699/dhg9je28

Abstract:
Quality improvement in early childhood education (ECE) depends on more than
resources and curriculum; it also depends on how educational leaders exercise
their role. This systematic review examined how school and ECE leaders drive
quality improvement, the mechanisms through which their influence operates, and
the contextual conditions that enable or constrain it. Following PRISMA 2020,
the review synthesised 28 empirical and scholarly studies published between 2020
and 2026 through narrative thematic synthesis. Effective leadership was
characterised by pedagogical guidance, distributed decision-making, professional
learning, collaboration, relational trust and a shared vision. Its influence on
quality ran chiefly through teacher learning, professional agency,
collaboration, organisational climate, resource coordination and teacher
retention, while limited resources, inadequate preparation, staffing pressures
and unfavourable policy conditions constrained implementation. The evidence
supports pedagogically focused, collaborative and context-responsive leadership,
sustained by adequate workforce, financial and institutional conditions. Stage-
specific search counts were not retained, and the findings should be read with
that limitation in mind.
