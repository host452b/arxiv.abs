Title: Latent profiles of parenting styles and their paradoxical associations with psychological resilience and wellbeing in Chinese adolescents
Authors: Weili Yang, Yanli Lu, Ruonan Shi, BaiHui Hu, Weiquan Wang, Minghui Zhao, Wenqiang Li, Zhaohui Zhang, Juan Li
ID: openalex.W7236367062
URL: https://doi.org/10.3389/fpubh.2026.1912829

Abstract:
Background Parenting styles are significantly associated with adolescent mental
health. However, existing Western typologies may not fully capture the
complexity of Chinese parenting, where warmth and harshness often coexist. This
study aimed to identify latent parenting styles in Chinese adolescents and
examine their associations with adolescent psychological resilience and
wellbeing (affliction and equanimity). Methods A cross-sectional survey was
conducted among three schools in Xinxiang, China in 2024 using convenience
sampling. Participants were 1,772 adolescents (876 boys, 896 girls; M = 13.62,
SD = 1.36). Data were collected using the Egna Minnen Beträffande Uppfostran
(EMBU), the Holistic WellBeing Scale (HWBS), and the Healthy Kids Resilience
Assessment (HKRA). Latent profile analysis was performed, followed by an
associational mediation analysis (with warm-supportive parenting as the
reference group) based on cross-sectional data to examine whether psychological
resilience statistically mediated the associations between parenting styles and
wellbeing. Results Three parenting styles were identified: warm-supportive
(66.08%), harsh-controlling (29.57%), and ambivalent (4.35%). The ambivalent
group showed extremely high emotional warmth combined with high punishment,
rejection, favoritism, and overprotection. Adolescents in this group reported
the highest levels of affliction, equanimity, and psychological resilience.
Psychological resilience statistically mediated these associations. Harsh-
controlling parenting was associated with higher affliction and lower
equanimity, partly through lower resilience, with lower resilience statistically
representing 83% of the association with lower equanimity. Ambivalent parenting
was associated with higher equanimity, partly through its association with
higher resilience (34% mediation). Conclusion Ambivalent parenting (high warmth
+ high harshness) is paradoxically associated with higher resilience and
equanimity alongside higher affliction, challenging the simple “good vs. bad”
dichotomy of parenting.
