Title: Perceived parenting styles, adult attachment, and emotional intelligence in Indian young adults: A parallel mediation approach
Authors: Tanjil Chakarwarti, Madhu Jakhar, Navkiran Kalsi, Rajnish Kumar Gupta
ID: openalex.W7222750335
URL: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actpsy.2026.108003

Abstract:
Emotional intelligence (EI) plays a vital role in how young adults navigate
relationships, academic pressures, and personal growth. While family
environments are widely recognized as foundational to emotional development, the
associations between perceived parenting, adult attachment, and EI in adulthood
remain underexplored, particularly in non-Western contexts. Therefore, this
study investigated the association between perceived parenting styles and EI
among Indian young adults and examined whether adult attachment dimensions
explain these associations. A sample of 151 healthy young adults (aged 18-25
years; 90 females, 61 males) from educational institutions across the Indian
subcontinent completed standardized self-report measures of perceived parenting
style, adult attachment, and EI. Correlation analyses revealed that perceived
authoritative parenting was significantly and positively associated with EI,
while authoritarian and permissive parenting showed weak, non-significant
negative trends. At the attachment level, EI was positively associated with the
close dimension and negatively associated with the anxiety dimension, with no
significant association with the depend dimension. Parallel mediation analyses
indicated that authoritative parenting exerted a strong direct effect on EI; the
combined indirect effect through attachment was non-significant. Notably, the
depend dimension acted as a competitive suppressor, weakening the positive
parenting-EI path. Authoritarian parenting had no significant total or indirect
effects on EI. These findings suggest that growing up with warm, structured, and
communicative parenting lays a meaningful foundation for emotional competence in
adulthood. In collectivist societies like India, where family bonds are central
to identity, promoting authoritative parenting alongside secure relational
patterns may be a particularly powerful way to enhance EI in the next
generation.
