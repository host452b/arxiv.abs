Title: The Relationship Between Parenting Styles and Perceived Stress Among Primary School Students: The Mediating Role of Creative and Critical Thinking Dispositions
Authors: Sevil Momeni Shabanı, Gülten Feryal Gündüz, Levent Vural
ID: openalex.W7214975632
URL: https://doi.org/10.26822/iejee.2026.461

Abstract:
This study aimed to examine the relationship between parenting styles and
perceived stress among primary school students and to determine the mediating
roles of creative and critical thinking dispositions in this relationship. The
study was conducted using a quantitative research approach and employed a
relational survey model. The sample consisted of 598 primary school students and
their parents from the European side of Istanbul. Data were collected using the
Creative Thinking Scale, Critical Thinking Scale, Perceived Stress Scale, and
Multidimensional Parenting Styles Assessment Scale. Descriptive statistics,
independent samples t-tests, ANOVA, and mediation analyses using the PROCESS
macro were conducted for data analysis. The findings indicated that students’
perceived stress levels were at a lower-moderate level, whereas creative and
critical thinking levels were high. The majority of parents demonstrated a
positive reinforcement parenting style. Parenting styles had a limited but
statistically significant effect on perceived stress. However, creative and
critical thinking dispositions did not play a significant mediating role in the
relationship between parenting styles and perceived stress. Overall, the
findings suggest that the relationship between parenting styles and perceived
stress is limited and that cognitive processes such as creative and critical
thinking do not significantly mediate this relationship among primary school
students.
