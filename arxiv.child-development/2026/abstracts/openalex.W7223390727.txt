Title: Symptom linkages between social media addiction and multidimensional stressors across adolescence and young adulthood
Authors: Yukai Zhou, Ted C. T. Fong, PAUL S. F. YIP
ID: openalex.W7223390727
URL: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chbr.2026.101417

Abstract:
Background Social media addiction (SMA) is increasingly prevalent among
adolescents and may persist into emerging and young adulthood. Prior studies
have reported the prevalence of SMA across different age groups, with limited
attention to its symptom-level structure and associations with multiple
stressors within each group. This study applied network analysis to identify the
central symptoms and strongest edges in SMA networks, and the bridge stressors
and their connections with SMA symptoms across three age groups. Methods A total
of 1,472 young people ( M age = 25.12 years, SD = 6.96; 49.7% female) were
recruited via a mobile survey in Hong Kong in 2024. SMA networks and
SMA–stressor bridge networks were estimated separately for adolescents aged
11–17 years ( n = 250), emerging adults aged 18–24 years ( n = 376), and young
adults aged 25–35 years ( n = 846). Networks were compared across age groups
using network comparison tests. Results Across all three age groups, Tolerance
was the most central symptom, and the Tolerance–Salience edge was the strongest
edge in the SMA network. In the bridge network, Academic Stress showed the
highest bridge expected influence among stressors in adolescents and was linked
with Withdrawal and Relapse . In emerging and young adults, bridging edges were
distributed across stressor domains. Network comparison tests detected no
significant differences in global network structure or global strength across
age groups in either network. Exploratory comparisons found that the bridging
role of Academic Stress was stronger in adolescents than in the two adult
groups. Conclusion The core structure of the SMA network was consistent across
the three age groups, whereas the bridge role of Academic Stress was specific to
adolescents. The findings identify both the core and bridge symptoms of SMA
linking to stressors as potential targets for preventive intervention.
