Title: The pedagogy of slow learning in early childhood education: Attention, care, play, and meaning-making in young children’s classrooms
Authors: Sirjon Sirjon, Diana Setyaningsih, Fitriani Fitriani, Erna Olua, Yusuf Hidayat
ID: openalex.W7234046173
URL: https://doi.org/10.37291/jces.1010

Abstract:
Early childhood education systems worldwide are increasingly governed by
accelerated schedules, standardised outcomes, and datafied
accountability—conditions that compress the unhurried time young children need
for deep engagement. This study examined how Indonesian early childhood
educators enact a pedagogy of slow learning and how slowness shapes children's
attention, care relations, play, and meaning-making. Employing a qualitative
multi-site instrumental case study in four early childhood education
institutions in West Java, Indonesia, data were generated through 64 classroom
observations (approximately 96 hours), 32 semi-structured and stimulated-recall
interviews with 16 educators, and 418 pedagogical documentation artefacts, and
were analysed using reflexive thematic analysis. Four themes were constructed:
(1) unhurried attention as deliberate dwelling, noticing, and waiting; (2) care
as the relational infrastructure of slowness; (3) play in slow time, sustained
through unfinished projects and material traces; and (4) meaning-making made
visible through multimodal and multilingual documentation. Educators negotiated
persistent tensions between slow pedagogy and curricular acceleration through
micro-temporal strategies of buffering, weaving, and advocacy. The findings
position slow pedagogy as a learnable professional craft rather than a romantic
luxury and carry implications for teacher education, curriculum policy, and
equitable, child-responsive practice.
