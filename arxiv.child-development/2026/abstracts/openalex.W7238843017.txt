Title: “We Use English to Explain Everything”: Teacher Agency and Ideology in Kindergarten English‐Medium Education
Authors: Menxiang Hu, Seyyed‐Abdolhamid Mirhosseini
ID: openalex.W7238843017
URL: https://doi.org/10.1111/ijal.70429

Abstract:
English‐medium education (EME) has increasingly entered early childhood settings
around the non‐Anglophone world. Yet, little is known about preschool EME
teachers’ engagements within the complex language landscape of their classrooms.
This study examines preschool EME teachers’ language ideologies and agencies in
Hong Kong local kindergartens, where parental, institutional and social
expectations strongly associated English with future academic and social
mobility. Drawing on narrative survey responses from 59 kindergarten teachers
and follow‐up semi‐structured interviews with nine teachers, the study
identifies three ways teachers exercise agency based on their own ideologies:
aligning with English as academic capital, negotiating English‐only ideology,
and reworking the cultural meanings attached to English. The findings show that
teachers mediate between future‐oriented demands for English, multilingual
classroom realities, and local/global cultural resources. However, their agency
remains bounded by school policies, their own native‐speakerist assumptions, and
parental expectations for English immersion. The study contributes to EME
studies and teacher agency research by foregrounding the under‐examined
preschool context and showing how EME is locally enacted through teachers’
everyday pedagogical and ideological negotiations.
