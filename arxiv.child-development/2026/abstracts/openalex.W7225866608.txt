Title: Parental Involvement in Early Childhood Education: A Cross-Sectional Study of Home-School Connections and Socioeconomic Patterns in Indonesia
Authors: Himatul Khoirunnisa, Yuli Kurniawati Sugiyo Pranoto, Sunawan
ID: openalex.W7225866608
URL: https://doi.org/10.14421/jpi.2026.152.315-324

Abstract:
Purpose – This study describes three forms of parental involvement in early
childhood education, examines their interrelationships, and explores differences
across parental education and monthly income groups in an urban Indonesian
sample.Design/methods/approach – A cross-sectional survey included 157 parents
of children aged 5–6 years from 12 kindergartens in Semarang. Data were
collected in January–March 2025 using a 34-item Indonesian Family Involvement
Questionnaire adaptation comprising 10 school-based, 13 home-based, and 11
home–school conferencing items, each rated 1–4. Analyses included descriptive
summaries, Pearson correlations, and approximate one-way comparisons calculated
from rounded group summaries.Findings – The three domains were positively
correlated (r = .363–.532, p < .001). Approximate item means were 1.88 for
school-based involvement, 3.15 for home-based involvement, and 2.52 for
conferencing. Education and income summaries showed increasing group means and
approximate omnibus differences (F = 4.143–6.346, p = .0004–.0074). Scoring and
aggregate inconsistencies in the group summaries limit socioeconomic
inferences.Research Implications – The descriptive patterns support examining
coordinated opportunities for communication and learning at home. The
nonprobability sample, summary-statistic limitations, and lack of adjustment for
kindergarten clustering warrant caution. The cross-sectional findings do not
establish causal mechanisms or child outcomes.
