Title: Wearable Technology in Early Childhood Education: A Systematic Review
Authors: Mariam Alamu, Idowu Tope Aisha Adekanbi
ID: openalex.W7238340154
URL: https://doi.org/10.17605/osf.io/ncb7d

Abstract:
This project registers a systematic review of empirical research on wearable
technology in early childhood education published between 2016 and 2026.
Wearable devices such as activity trackers, accelerometers, smartwatches, worn
sensors, smart glasses, and wearable language recorders are increasingly used
with young children. Yet most studies treat these devices as measurement tools
for physical activity or health. Less is known about how wearables, and the data
they produce, support teaching and learning in early learning settings. Hence,
this review asks how wearable technology has been used pedagogically with
children aged 0 to 8 and their teachers in formal settings such as childcare,
preschool, pre-K, kindergarten, and Grades 1 to 3. Six databases were searched
on 16 November 2026: PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, ERIC, APA PsycInfo, and
SPORTDiscus. After deduplication, 2,004 records remained for screening. Two
reviewers independently screen all records in Covidence against criteria
organized by the Population, Concept, Context (PCC) framework. Data will be
extracted on study characteristics, device types, the pedagogical role of the
device or its data, learning domains, outcomes, and reported challenges.
Methodological quality will be appraised with the Mixed Methods Appraisal Tool
(MMAT), and findings will be synthesized narratively. Reporting follows PRISMA
2020 and PRISMA-S. The review is expected to map the types of wearables used in
early childhood education, the roles they play in instruction, and the outcomes
and implementation issues reported. Thus, it will identify gaps in the evidence
and offer direction for researchers, teacher educators, and designers who seek
to use wearable technology to support young children’s learning.
