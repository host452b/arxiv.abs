Title: Pedagogical Inclusivity: Process, Competence, and Professional Pinnacles of Kindergarten Teachers
Authors: May Manulat, Fray Michael Cangayao, Kaitlin Marie Opingo, Reylan G. Capuno, Cristie Ann Jaca, Margie D. Fulgencio, Regina Sitoy, Jonathan Olores Etcuban
ID: openalex.W7235720635
URL: https://doi.org/10.32996/bjtep.2026.5.11.2

Abstract:
The professional growth, readiness, and career achievements of kindergarten
teachers are essential to the successful implementation of inclusive education
practices in Philippine public elementary schools. Teachers' understanding,
continuing education, and dedication to inclusive teaching strategies will
determine how effectively they can meet the developmental requirements of all
types of learners. This study assessed the professional development,
preparedness, and career milestones of kindergarten teachers, as well as the
level of inclusiveness in their classrooms, within the Liloan District,
Philippines, as a basis for creating a Kindergarten Inclusivity Policy Brief. A
descriptive-correlational method was used to collect data from 31 kindergarten
teachers via a five-part, researcher-constructed quantitative survey. Collected
data were analyzed using frequencies, simple percentages, means, standard
deviations, and ANOVA. The findings indicated that teachers' professional
experience, knowledge/competence, and continuous professional growth form the
foundation upon which teachers can develop responsive and equitable kindergarten
learning environments. Teacher differences also illustrate the need to provide
varying levels of professional assistance rather than uniform interventions.
Furthermore, enhancing teachers' professional capacities may assist them in
developing greater confidence in their teaching abilities, being more responsive
to pedagogy, and meeting the developmental and educational needs of all
learners. It was concluded that the combination of these elements (professional
development, preparedness, and professional career milestones) collectively
impacts teachers' capacity to develop an inclusively oriented classroom
environment. The researchers recommend implementing, monitoring, and evaluating
the proposed Kindergarten Inclusivity Policy Brief to ensure that Kindergarten
education is equitable, accessible, and developmentally appropriate.
