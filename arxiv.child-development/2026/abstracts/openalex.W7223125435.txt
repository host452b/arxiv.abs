Title: Impact of biological nurturing methods on exclusive breastfeeding and maternal attachment: A randomized controlled trial
Authors: longyi hu, Tang Qin, Siqi Li, Leshan Zhou
ID: openalex.W7223125435
URL: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijnss.2026.10.005

Abstract:
Objectives This study aimed to investigate the effects of biological nurturing
(BN) and traditional positions on exclusive breastfeeding rates, infant growth
parameters, and maternal attachment. Methods This randomized, single-blind
controlled trial enrolled 80 mother–infant dyads from the obstetrics department
of a tertiary hospital in Hunan between October 2023 and March 2024; 67
completed the study and were included in the analysis. Participants were
randomly assigned to receive either BN or traditional positioning interventions.
Baseline data were collected using a questionnaire, the Short Form of the
Breastfeeding Self-Efficacy Scale (BSES-SF), and the Maternal Attachment Scale.
Infant growth parameters (weight and length) were also measured. Breastfeeding
self-efficacy and maternal attachment were assessed at baseline, day 7, and day
42; exclusive breastfeeding status was assessed on day 7, day 42, and at 6
months postpartum. Infant weight and length were measured at baseline (at birth)
and day 42 postpartum. Scale outcomes were analyzed using repeated-measures
analysis of variance, whereas individual changes in weight and length from birth
to day 42 postpartum were compared between groups using independent-samples t
-tests. Results Exclusive breastfeeding rates did not differ significantly
between the two groups at 7 days (Wald χ 2 = 0.44, P = 0.506) or 42 days
postpartum (Wald χ 2 = 3.50, P = 0.060), but were significantly higher in the BN
group at 6 months postpartum (Wald χ 2 = 4.79, P = 0.029). The group-by-time
interaction was not significant (Wald χ 2 = 0.89, P = 0.660). At day 7
postpartum, the incidence of breast-related complications was significantly
lower in the BN group than in the control group ( χ 2 = 6.04, P = 0.014).
Breastfeeding self-efficacy scores did not differ significantly between groups
at baseline or day 7 postpartum (all P > 0.05); at 42 days postpartum, the BN
group had significantly higher total, breastfeeding skills, and inner strength
scores ( P = 0.003, P = 0.006, and P = 0.004, respectively). From birth to 42
days, infants in the BN group showed significantly greater gains in weight ( t =
3.14, P = 0.003) and length ( t = 3.63, P = 0.001). Total maternal attachment
scores did not differ significantly at baseline ( t = 0.62, P = 0.538), but were
significantly higher in the BN group at day 7 ( P = 0.005) and 42 days
postpartum ( P = 0.001), with a significant group-by-time interaction ( F =
9.28, P < 0.001). Conclusions At 6 months postpartum, participants receiving BN
had a higher exclusive breastfeeding rate than those receiving traditional
positioning guidance. BN was also associated with fewer breast-related
complications at day 7 postpartum, higher breastfeeding self-efficacy at day 42
postpartum, greater infant weight and length gains from birth to day 42
postpartum, and higher maternal attachment at days 7 and 42 postpartum. These
findings suggest that structured, nurse-led BN guidance may support
breastfeeding continuation and promote early maternal and infant well-being.
