Title: Preschool executive functions and longitudinal school functioning: the incremental contributions of performance-based, parent- and teacher-reported measures
Authors: Grazia Maria Giovanna Pastorino, Antonio Aquino, Piercarla Vitale, Valeria De Simone, Jessyka Marciano, Miriam Olivieri, Rossella Suriano, Noemi Mungo, Francesca Felicia Operto
ID: openalex.W7225347842
URL: https://doi.org/10.3389/frcha.2026.1980617

Abstract:
Introduction: The assessment of executive functioning (EF) in early childhood is
crucial for understanding developmental trajectories and academic outcomes, yet
the convergence and predictive utility of different assessment methods remain
debated. This longitudinal study examined the incremental validity of
performance-based measures, parent-reported, and teacher-reported EF in
predicting later academic functioning. Methods: A sample of 211 preschool
children (M age = 4.34 years) completed performance-based EF tasks (BAFE) and
were rated by parents and teachers using the Behavior Rating Inventory of
Executive Function-Preschool Version (BRIEF-P). Approximately four years later,
academic proficiency across six domains (reading, writing, oral language,
numbers, space and geometry, and science) was evaluated, with complete
longitudinal data available for 191 children. Hierarchical ordinal logistic
regressions were performed. Results: Our results indicated significant but
modest convergence between parent and teacher ratings, as well as limited
associations between performance-based tasks and behavioral ratings.
Hierarchical ordinal logistic regressions revealed that while performance-based
visuospatial working memory remained independently associated with later
mathematics outcomes, informant ratings provided distinct predictive
information. Parent-reported executive difficulties provided incremental
predictive information beyond performance-based measures; however, after
teacher-reported executive functioning was included in the models, teacher-
reported difficulties showed consistent incremental associations with lower
academic proficiency across all six domains, while the associations involving
parent reports were substantially attenuated. Conclusion: These findings support
the complementary value of performance-based and informant-reported measures of
preschool executive functioning. Teacher-reported executive difficulties showed
particularly consistent prospective associations with later school functioning,
although this pattern should be interpreted cautiously given the contextual
overlap between preschool teacher observations and subsequent school-based
academic evaluations.
