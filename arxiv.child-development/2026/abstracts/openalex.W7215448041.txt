Title: Professionals' perspectives on loneliness and mental health among adolescents and emerging adults during educational transitions
Authors: Amanda Jasmin Emilia Sundqvist, Ottar Ness, Pia Nyman-Kurkiala, Jessica Anne Viveka Hemberg
ID: openalex.W7215448041
URL: https://doi.org/10.1080/17482631.2026.2743338

Abstract:
INTRODUCTION: Educational transitions alter academic demands, social
relationships, and access to support. Yet less is known about how professionals
interpret the conditions that shape loneliness and mental health across these
transitions. PURPOSE: This study explored professionals' accounts of factors
that may intensify or mitigate loneliness and mental health challenges among
adolescents and emerging adults during transitions to upper-secondary and higher
education. METHOD: Four focus groups were conducted with 11 school
psychologists, school counsellors, special education teachers, study
supervisors, well-being coaches, project workers, and youth workers in Swedish-
speaking regions of Finland. The interviews were analysed using reflexive
thematic analysis. RESULTS: Professionals described how individual circumstances
and past adversity intersected with disrupted relationships and institutional
arrangements. Transitions could offer autonomy and a fresh start, but fragmented
peer groups, performance demands, weak continuity of support, and limited
communication between institutions could heighten vulnerability. PParticipants
emphasised early preparation, supportive teachers and peers, structured
opportunities for social connection, and stronger collaboration between sending
and receiving institutions. CONCLUSIONS: The findings position transition-
related loneliness as arising through interactions between young people and the
educational environments through which they move.
