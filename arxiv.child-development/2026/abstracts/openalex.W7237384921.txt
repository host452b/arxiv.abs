Title: Preparing Pre-service Teachers for STEAM with Islamic Values: A Multi-Case Study of Indonesian Early Childhood Education Programmes
Authors: Dzikri Rahmat Romadhon, Ratna Faeruz, Rahmatullah Rahmatullah
ID: openalex.W7237384921
URL: https://doi.org/10.47453/hadlonah.v7i2.4191

Abstract:
This study aimed to explore how Indonesian early childhood teacher-education
programmes prepare pre-service teachers to teach STEAM (science, technology,
engineering, arts and mathematics) and whether and how Islamic values are
integrated into that preparation. A multi-site qualitative design was used. Ten
informants (six lecturers, one also a programme head; two further programme
heads; one final-year student; and one recent graduate) were purposively
selected from six programmes: two in Islamic higher-education institutions and
four in general universities. Data were collected in May–June 2022 through
online interviews and written or spoken responses to a common protocol, and were
analysed using codebook thematic analysis with a cross-site case-by-theme
matrix. Four themes were developed. First, staff perceived pre-service teachers’
conceptual understanding of STEAM as stronger than their ability to enact it, a
gap linked to STEAM being taught in separate courses with few opportunities for
supervised enactment; engineering was the least visible component. Second,
constraints cut across programme types: lecturer expertise, a narrow digital-
only conception of technology, pandemic disruption and, at three programmes,
placement teachers who did not model integrated STEAM. Third, proposed
improvements were largely stated intentions. Fourth, expectations for values
integration formed a gradient, from institution-wide mandates in the two Islamic
institutions, through faculty-level expectations in one programme housed in a
general university, to none in the other three general programmes; reported
practices were mostly additive rather than embedded in STEAM pedagogy. The study
offers a 2022 baseline and points to coherent, sequenced STEAM coursework and
pluralism-sensitive approaches to values integration.
