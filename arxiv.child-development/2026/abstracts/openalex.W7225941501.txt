Title: A Phenomenological Study of College Students’ Experiences With Foreign Language Acquisition and Multilingualism in Developing Soft Skills for Employability
Authors: Naomi Elimelech
ID: openalex.W7225941501
URL: https://openalex.org/W7225941501

Abstract:
The purpose of this transcendental phenomenological study was to describe the
lived experiences of the dynamic interplay between multilingualism and soft
skill development among college students from a higher education institution in
Washington, D.C. This study was guided by Larsen-Freeman's Complex Dynamic
Systems Theory (CDST), which explains how a foreign language develops over time
and adapts to changing environments. The central research question was: What are
the lived experiences of foreign language learners in higher education regarding
the development of soft skills for employability? This study employed a
transcendental-phenomenological design to explore college students' lived
experiences of the interplay between multilingualism and the development of soft
skills. The sample included 10 participants who were at least 18 years of age at
the time of the study and were enrolled in, or had completed, an intermediate-
level or higher foreign language course at the selected institution within the
previous three years. The institution was selected for its diverse student
populations and varied institutional characteristics. Data were collected
through semi-structured interviews, observations, and focus groups, and analyzed
using Moustakas' (1994) transcendental phenomenological approach, including
horizontalization, clustering of meaning units, and the development of textural
and structural descriptions to identify the essence of the participants'
experiences. The findings indicated that learning a foreign language is a
dynamic and interconnected process. This process helped students facilitate
communication, collaboration, problem-solving, adaptability, confidence, learner
agency, and cultural awareness concurrently. Participants also described
classroom experiences as contributors not only to soft skills development but
also to enhanced career readiness and, more broadly, to the practical, real-
world application of foreign languages. These findings support Complex Dynamic
Systems Theory, the theoretical framework of this study. The findings show that
language learning, soft skills, and employability all develop through ongoing
interactions among cognitive, social, and experiential factors.
