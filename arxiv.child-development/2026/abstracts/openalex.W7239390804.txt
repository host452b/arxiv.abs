Title: Management and Provision of Early Childhood Education in Public and Private Schools in Chegutu District of Zimbabwe
Authors: Wellington Samkange, Siphehliwe Hove, Ottiliah Jim
ID: openalex.W7239390804
URL: https://doi.org/10.47772/ijriss.2026.101100212

Abstract:
Universal primary education was one of the eight millennium development goals
and was based on the notion that foundation education forms the basis for
sustainable education.2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development focuses on
seventeen goals. Goal number four states that: governments must ensure inclusive
and equitable quality education for all. One of the targets of this goal is to
ensure that all children have access to quality early childhood education. The
Zimbabwe government, enacted laws and policies to support the establishment of
early childhood education. The Secretary’s Circular No. 14 of 2004 and Statutory
Instrument 105 of 2005 regularised the provision of early childhood education in
both public and private schools. The demand for education and the shortage of
schools brought a new dimension in the provision of education in Zimbabwe. The
demand contributed to a rise in the establishment of private schools. Curriculum
reform has incorporated the use of modern technology at all levels of the school
system. The paper aimed at assessing the management and provision of early
childhood education in public and private schools. It used the qualitative
research approach. Data was generated from school heads, deputy heads, teachers-
in-charge, teachers and parents at six purposively selected primary schools in
Chegutu District of Zimbabwe. Data was collected through focus group
discussions, interviews, open-ended questionnaires, observations and document
analysis. The sample comprised forty-five participants. The paper was guided by
the assumption that management and provision of early childhood education has
faced challenges that have compromised quality in both public and private
centres. The paper came up with several findings which included different
perceptions of EC education by stakeholders, lack of understanding of policy
requirements, compromised quality of education and notable gaps in professional
qualifications among ECD teachers. It recommends capacity building for all
stakeholders, a collaborative approach by ministries to intensify the
supervision and monitoring of the centres, intensifying foundation teacher
education. It also recommends the establishment of a regulatory framework for
the concept of a ‘tots class’. Lastly, the provision of early childhood
education should be the responsibility of the state, to ensure that no child is
disadvantaged by the socio-economic status of parents.
