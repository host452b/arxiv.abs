Title: Exploring Parental Health and Nutrition Literacy Practices in Holistic Integrative Early Childhood Education
Authors: Yuni Dwi Suryani, Taruni Suningsih, Akbari Akbari, Yin Yin Septiani, Rina Rahayu Siregar, Izzah Izzah
ID: openalex.W7236624958
URL: https://doi.org/10.37680/absorbent_mind.v6i2.10903

Abstract:
This study explores how health and nutrition information is communicated,
engaged with, and translated into family practices within the school–home
interface. A qualitative case study was conducted at TK Melati Terpadu, located
in Indralaya Utara District, Ogan Ilir Regency, South Sumatra, Indonesia. Data
were collected through participatory observation, semi-structured interviews
with a principal, teacher, and parent, and documentation. Data were analysed
thematically through open, axial, and selective coding, with attention to
patterns connecting institutional services, parental engagement, and home
practices. Three interconnected themes emerged. First, the school provided
diverse health and nutrition services, including nutritious meals, vitamin
supplementation, immunisation, health examinations, growth monitoring, and
collaboration with a community health centre, although implementation was not
yet systematically structured. Second, parental engagement with health and
nutrition information was mainly informal, with limited opportunities for
structured education and discussion. Third, a gap was evident between school-
based services and family practices. Despite nutrition programmes at school, a
parent reported that the child remained a picky eater, illustrating that service
provision did not automatically translate into changes at home. The findings
highlight the school–home interface as an important context for understanding
parental health and nutrition literacy practices. Strengthening Holistic
Integrative Early Childhood Services (PAUD Holistik Integratif) requires
attention not only to service provision but also to the processes through which
health and nutrition information is understood, negotiated, and translated into
everyday family practices.
