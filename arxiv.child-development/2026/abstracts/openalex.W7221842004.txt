Title: Scrolling Through Stress: Physiological Stress Recovery Shapes Risk for Problematic Social Media Use in Young Adults with Depressive Symptoms
Authors: Abigail M. Findley
ID: openalex.W7221842004
URL: https://openalex.org/W7221842004

Abstract:
Depression is among the most consistent correlates of problematic social media
use (PSMU), yet how depressive symptoms confer this risk, and for whom, remains
poorly understood. The present study examined whether stress regulation explains
the link between depressive symptoms and PSMU in young adults, testing
specifically whether stress recovery acts as a mechanism linking the two or as a
vulnerability that determines for whom they are linked, and whether distress
tolerance (DT) shapes these pathways. Participants (N = 77) were young adults
(18–25) with elevated depressive symptoms. Following the Trier Social Stress
Test, participants completed a 15-minute social media "recovery" period using
their preferred platform (TikTok, Instagram, or Facebook). Stress recovery was
assessed across the recovery period using psychological (self-report anxiety,
positive and negative affect) and physiological (respiratory sinus arrhythmia;
RSA) indices, along with the concordance between them. Results indicated that
depressive symptoms were positively but non-significantly associated with PSMU,
and stress recovery did not mediate this association. However, physiological
recovery moderated it, such that among young adults with poorer RSA recovery,
greater depressive symptoms predicted more severe PSMU, whereas among those who
recovered well, depressive symptoms and PSMU were unrelated. The hypothesized
moderated mediation involving DT was not supported, though DT moderated the
association between RSA recovery and PSMU. Contrary to expectations, greater RSA
recovery predicted higher PSMU among young adults with higher DT. Together,
these findings suggest that depressive symptoms relate to PSMU not uniformly,
but conditionally, among young adults who recover poorly from stress,
highlighting stress recovery as a key individual difference factor in
understanding for whom depression confers risk for problematic use.
