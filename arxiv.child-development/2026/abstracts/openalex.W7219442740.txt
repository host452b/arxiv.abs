Title: Selecting and adapting an intervention for policy implementation that aims to promote physical activity and healthy eating in children attending early childhood educational settings in Qatar
Authors: Rita Rizk
ID: openalex.W7219442740
URL: https://openalex.org/W7219442740

Abstract:
Physical activity and healthy eating behaviours established in early childhood
tend to track throughout later childhood and into adulthood. Early childhood
health interventions yield long-term benefits, effectively enhanced when they
are adapted to the contexts in which they are implemented. Although Qatar has
increasingly recognised the importance of promoting physical activity and
healthy eating among young children, significant gaps remain in the development
of policies and the implementation of interventions targeting these behaviours
for this age group. This doctoral research aimed to identify and adapt a health
intervention to promote physical activity and healthy eating among children
attending pre-school nurseries in Qatar. A scoping review revealed the absence
of a child-specific Qatari policy addressing physical activity and healthy
eating. A critical narrative review identified insufficient levels of physical
activity among children in Qatar and highlighted important evidence gaps.
Surveys and interviews with policymakers identified existing and forthcoming
national policy-relevant documents relating to pre-schoolers’ physical activity
and nutrition. Findings also highlighted the lack of accessible national data on
physical activity prevalence among pre-school children, and the limited
availability of programmes promoting physical activity and healthy eating within
pre-schools in Qatar. A systematic review of obesity-prevention interventions
for children aged 2–4 years was undertaken. Using a selective decision-tree
approach, the ToyBox intervention was identified as the most appropriate
programme for contextual adaptation to Qatari pre-schools. The finding was
shared with the previously interviewed policymakers to obtain their perspectives
on how to adapt the ToyBox intervention for implementation in Qatari pre-
schools. Surveys and interviews with pre-school teachers offered further
insights into the formative adaptation of the ToyBox Qatar intervention,
including detailed feedback on the adapted resources. ToyBox Qatar is a
promising health intervention to supporting physical activity and healthy eating
behaviours among pre-school children in Qatar and is ready for pilot testing.
