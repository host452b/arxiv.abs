Title: Blended learning as a tool for enhancing mindfulness skills in early childhood education
Authors: Ali Alelaimat, Reham Al-Almohtadi, Mai Al-Amoush
ID: openalex.W7226016676
URL: https://doi.org/10.3389/feduc.2026.1970309

Abstract:
Introduction This study examined the effectiveness of blended learning in
developing mindfulness skills among kindergarten children in Ma'an, Jordan.
Methods A quasi-experimental study employing a nonequivalent control-group
design was conducted with 90 children from two kindergartens. The experimental
group (n = 45) participated in an eight-week blended learning program, while the
control group (n = 45) followed the regular kindergarten curriculum. Both groups
completed a pictorial mindfulness scale before and after the intervention.
Results After adjusting for pre-test scores, the experimental group scored
significantly higher than the control group at post-test, F (1, 87) = 1342.24, p
< .001, partial η ² = .94. The experimental group also showed a significant
improvement from pre-test to post-test, t (44) = 41.38, p < .001. Discussion The
findings suggest that blended learning may support the development of
mindfulness skills in early childhood education. However, because the
experimental and control groups were drawn from different kindergartens, the
observed effect size should be interpreted as an association rather than
definitive evidence of causality. Further research involving additional
kindergartens and more rigorous experimental designs is needed before
considering the integration of blended learning into the national kindergarten
curriculum.
