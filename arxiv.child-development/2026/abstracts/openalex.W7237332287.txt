Title: A problem-centred case study of kindergarten teachers’ perspectives about the integration of technology in early childhood education
Authors: Tobias Bahr, Bernd Zinn, Jule Kembitzky
ID: openalex.W7237332287
URL: https://doi.org/10.1007/s10798-026-10149-9

Abstract:
Natural science has been integrated into early childhood education (ECE) for
many years. With the growing digitalization of society many experts call for the
integration of technology and computer science (CS) into ECE with the goal of
fostering students’ problem-solving skills. While many research projects
integrate technology and CS into ECE, curricula, e.g. in Germany, stay the same
and so does the current practice. Given this starting point, this study aimed to
explore the reasons behind the lack of technology and CS in ECE from the
perspectives of the teachers. As recent research highlights the lack of this
perspective. A multiple case study consisting of N = 11 kindergarten teachers
(between 25 and 64 years old) was conducted using problem-centred interviews and
applying qualitative content analysis. The results indicate that most of the
teachers have a low interest and self-concept in both fields. Most of the
teachers think that technology and CS education at the preschool level (age 4 to
6) is important. However, several reasons in conformance with prior research are
stated why they do not integrate it. The findings from this case study indicate
that only through systematic changes made in curricula and pre-service teacher
training can a sustainable change reaching all kindergarten students occur. The
findings are discussed in regard to related work in the field of technology and
CS education in the early years.
