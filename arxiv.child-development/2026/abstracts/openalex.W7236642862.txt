Title: Reducing the Risk of Tantrums in Children with Behavioral Problems through Practical Life Activities Using the Montessori Method at Handayani State Kindergarten
Authors: Johanita Retno Purwanti, Ishartiwi Ishartiwi
ID: openalex.W7236642862
URL: https://doi.org/10.63922/ijevss.v5i04.6924

Abstract:
This study aims to determine the application of Montessori's Practical Life
method activities in reducing the risk of tantrums in children with behavioral
problems at Handayani State Kindergarten. The study used Classroom Action
Research (CAR) with the Kemmis and McTaggart model which includes the planning,
action, observation and reflection stages. The research subjects consisted of
two children in group B1 aged 5-6 years who were identified as having a high
risk of tantrums based on initial observations and teacher notes. The study was
conducted through the pre-action stage, Cycle I and Cycle II. Data were
collected through observations of the duration and intensity of tantrums,
observations of Practical Life activities, self-regulation, aggressive behavior,
field notes and documentation. Data analysis was carried out descriptively
quantitatively and qualitatively by comparing changes at each stage of the
action. The results showed that the implementation of Practical Life activities
in a gradual, structured, repetitive manner and supported by a prepared
environment, teacher demonstrations, opportunities for independent work, and a
peace corner can reduce the duration of tantrums. The average duration of
tantrums decreased from 110 minutes in the pre-action to 28.75 minutes in Cycle
I and 6.5 minutes in Cycle II, with a total reduction of 94.09%. Practical Life
skills improved from a score of 4 (BB) to 9 (MB) and then to 16 (BSB). Changes
were also seen in self-regulation skills and a reduction in aggressive behavior
during learning. Therefore, Montessori-style Practical Life activities can be an
alternative learning strategy to help reduce the risk of tantrums while
supporting self-regulation, concentration, and independence in young children.
