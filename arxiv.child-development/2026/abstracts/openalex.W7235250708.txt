Title: Implementation of Deep Learning to Support the Development of Social-Emotional Abilities in Early Childhood Education
Authors: Nurmaulidia Zam Zami, Ayi Najmul Hidayat
ID: openalex.W7235250708
URL: https://doi.org/10.63922/ijevss.v5i04.7455

Abstract:
Deep learning (Pembelajaran Mendalam) is now part of the basic framework of
Indonesia's national curriculum, including early childhood education, yet its
success depends on how the approach is implemented in educational institutions.
This study aims to analyze the implementation of deep learning to support the
development of social-emotional abilities of young children at TKQ Zahrotunnisa
and TKQ Al-Haadii, Karawang Regency. The study employed a qualitative approach
with a multisite case study design. Data were collected through interviews with
2 principals, 6 teachers, and 8 parents, observation of 24 children, and
documentation, and were analyzed using Edward III's four implementation
variables: communication, resources, disposition, and bureaucratic structure.
The results show that both kindergartens implemented deep learning through group
play, role play, sharing, turn-taking, storytelling, and simple conflict
resolution, but with different patterns of support. TKQ Zahrotunnisa relied on
more formal mechanisms, such as scheduled meetings, standard operating
procedures, supervision, and written reflection, whereas TKQ Al-Haadii relied on
direct coordination, daily oral communication, and the use of the surrounding
environment. Observation records showed changes in seven social-emotional
indicators, although some behaviors still required teacher guidance. The main
obstacles were uneven teacher understanding, inconsistent parental involvement,
and limited media and time, which were addressed through internal training,
joint reflection, use of the environment, and stronger communication with
parents. These findings indicate that deep learning can be supported through
both formal and simpler institutional mechanisms as long as communication,
teacher commitment, and collaboration with parents are established
