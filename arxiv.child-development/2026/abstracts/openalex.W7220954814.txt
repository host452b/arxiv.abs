Title: A Bioethical Analysis of a Recent Case Regarding Surrogacy in Russia
Authors: Роман Евгеньевич Тарабрин, A. A. Zobova
ID: openalex.W7220954814
URL: https://doi.org/10.1007/s11673-026-10620-1

Abstract:
This article examines a recent Russian surrogacy case through a bioethical and
socio-legal lens to assess the regulation of commercial surrogacy, the public
response it generated, and the role of bioethics in contemporary Russian debate.
Focusing on a criminal case involving medical professionals accused of child
trafficking in connection with cross-border surrogacy, the article argues that
the controversy exposed deep tensions among law, medicine, religion, public
policy, and child protection. It shows that public discussion centred largely on
legal control, national security, and reproductive market regulation, while key
bioethical questions concerning the commodification of women's reproductive
labour, the objectification of children, and the vulnerability of surrogate
mothers remained largely absent. The article further demonstrates that the
Russian regulatory framework is fragmented and paternalistic, insufficiently
protecting the interests of surrogate mothers, intended parents, and children
alike. By analysing both the case and the surrounding debates, the study
concludes that Russian bioethics remains weakly institutionalized as an
interdisciplinary forum capable of mediating between medical, legal, and moral
perspectives. The article ultimately calls for broader, more inclusive ethical
deliberation that meaningfully incorporates medical professionals, legal
experts, civil society, and religious communities in the governance of
surrogacy.
