Title: Reading Skills and School Readiness of Kindergarten Pupils at Cogon Elementary School
Authors: Maria Gessa B. Rebadeo
ID: openalex.W7225705187
URL: https://doi.org/10.65339/wjsam.v1.i2.17

Abstract:
This study assessed the reading skills and school readiness of kindergarten
pupils at Cogon Elementary School, Monreal, Masbate, and examined the
relationship between these variables. Using a descriptive-quantitative,
correlational design, the study included 34 kindergarten pupils through total
enumeration during School Year 2024–2025. Researcher-developed reading
assessments and teacher observation checklists measured foundational literacy
and cognitive, language, social-emotional, and motor readiness. Data were
analyzed using frequencies, percentages, weighted means, and Pearson
correlation. Most pupils correctly recognized selected letters (97.06% for A, M,
S, and E) and answered simple story-comprehension questions (88.24%–97.06%).
However, performance was weaker in recognizing the sound associated with C
(26.47%) and identifying the words book and school (35.29% each). School-
readiness competencies were generally rated as often demonstrated, with
comparatively lower performance in expressive communication, confidence,
counting, and selected fine-motor activities. Reading skills and school
readiness exhibited a very strong reported positive relationship (r = 0.9882, p
< 0.001). These findings indicate that stronger literacy performance was
associated with greater developmental readiness. Teachers should prioritize
phonological awareness, vocabulary enrichment, integrated developmental
activities, and home-school collaboration. The single-school sample,
correlational design, and limited assessment domains restrict generalizability
and causal interpretation. The study provides localized evidence to guide
responsive kindergarten instruction, strengthen educational sustainability, and
support Sustainable Development Goal 4 (Quality Education), particularly Target
4.2 on early childhood development and readiness for primary education.
