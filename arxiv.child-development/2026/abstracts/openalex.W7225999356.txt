Title: Parenting Styles and Children’s Physical Activity and Sedentary Behavior Among Latino Families
Authors: Paul Marcel Nindenshuti, Rebecca Nissen, Tania Valdez, Kiria Fraga, Patricia Dionicio, John P. Elder, Noé C. Crespo
ID: openalex.W7225999356
URL: https://doi.org/10.1177/15598276261506973

Abstract:
Parenting styles may shape children's physical activity (PA) and sedentary
behavior (SB), yet their associations with these behaviors among Latino families
remain understudied. This study examined associations between parenting styles
and children's PA and SB. We used baseline data from an obesity-prevention
program involving Latino children ages 5-10 and their parents. Parenting style
constructs were assessed with a validated 10-item questionnaire. Children's PA
was measured as the parent-reported number of days per week with at least 60
minutes of moderate-to-vigorous physical activity (MVPA), and SB was assessed as
daily sitting time on a 0-6 scale. Multiple linear regression was used to
examine associations, adjusting for sociodemographic factors. Among 297 parent-
child dyads, parents averaged 35.8 years, and children averaged 8.1 years.
Children engaged in MVPA on approximately 2 days per week and had a mean SB
score of 2.2. Monitoring (β=.28; p<.01) and reinforcement (β=.20; p<.01) were
positively associated with children's MVPA. Limit setting (β=-.24; p<.01) and
monitoring (β=-.15; p=.01) were inversely associated with SB, whereas control
was positively associated with SB (β=.13; p=.04). These findings indicate that
specific parenting styles are associated with children's PA and SB. Longitudinal
and intervention studies are needed to clarify these associations.
