Title: Predicting Authoritarian and Permissive Parenting Styles based on the Factors of Neuroticism and Extraversion in Parents
Authors: Suparna Sarkar, Kalpana Randhawa
ID: openalex.W7220864080
URL: https://doi.org/10.25258/ijddt.16.50s.78

Abstract:
The current study examines the relationship between parental personality traits:
Neuroticism and Extraversion and parenting styles, specifically Authoritarian
and Permissive approaches. Drawing on Eysenck’s personality framework and
Baumrind’s conceptualization of parenting styles, the research aimed to
determine whether Neuroticism is associated with and predictive of Authoritarian
parenting, and whether Extraversion is associated with and predictive of
Permissive parenting. A sample of 150 Bengali and non-Bengali parents of
children aged 10 to 16 years from West Bengal participated in the study. Data
were collected using the NEO Five-Factor Inventory (NEO-FFI) and the Parenting
Styles and Dimensions Questionnaire (PSDQ). The results revealed a significant
positive correlation between Neuroticism and Authoritarian parenting (r = .430,
p < .001), with Authoritarian parenting significantly predicting Neuroticism (β
= .43, p < .001). Similarly, a significant positive correlation was found
between Extraversion and Permissive parenting (r = .450, p < .001), with
Permissive parenting significantly predicting Extraversion (β = .45, p < .001).
Thus, it is determined that stable personality traits meaningfully influence
parenting approaches
