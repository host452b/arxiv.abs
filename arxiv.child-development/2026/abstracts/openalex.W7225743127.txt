Title: Inclusive Practices in Early Childhood Education: The Interplay Between Classroom Management and Learning Skills
Authors: Cres Mariann Son
ID: openalex.W7225743127
URL: https://doi.org/10.32996/bjtep.2026.5.10.5

Abstract:
This study investigated the relationship between teachers' classroom management
practices and learners' learning skills in inclusive early childhood education.
Utilizing a quantitative research approach with a descriptive-correlative
design, data were gathered from 30 participants selected through purposive
sampling. The demographic profile revealed that a majority of the respondents
were female, aged 30–39 years, held ongoing master's units, and possessed 6–10
years of teaching experience. Weighted mean analysis indicated that teachers
highly demonstrated classroom management practices, led by responsiveness and
strategic influence. Concurrently, learners exhibited high proficiency in
baseline learning domains, scoring highest in verbal competence and attention,
though mathematical skills emerged as an area for improvement. Inferential
testing using Spearman’s rank correlation revealed a statistically significant,
strong positive relationship between teachers' classroom management practices
and learners' learning skills leading to the rejection of the null hypothesis.
This demonstrates that proactive, responsive classroom organization directly
drives the development of foundational student competencies. Ultimately, the
output of the study led to the formulation of the CRES Framework. It provides a
comprehensive and transformative model for embedding inclusive practices within
early childhood education by focusing on four interconnected pillars: Cognitive
anticipation, intentional Relationships, Encompassing multi-domain support, and
a Supportive school environment. The CRES Framework serves as a holistic roadmap
for establishing equitable, nurturing, and highly responsive learning
environments where all diverse learners can thrive.
