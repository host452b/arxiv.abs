Title: Positive Mental Health in the Perinatal Period: Biopsychosocial Factors and Intervention Strategies
Authors: Özge Nur Muslu
ID: openalex.W7214894375
URL: https://doi.org/10.62425/esbder.1686834

Abstract:
The aim of this review is to examine the biological, psychosocial, and
environmental factors affecting positive mental health during the perinatal
period in light of current literature, and to identify effective intervention
strategies for promoting positive mental health. This narrative review was based
on a structured literature search conducted in PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science,
and CINAHL for studies published between 2000 and 2025. Keywords related to the
perinatal period, positive mental health, well-being, resilience, social
support, and interventions were used. Eligible studies published in English and
Turkish were grouped by study design and synthesized thematically within a
biopsychosocial framework. It was found that biological (hormonal changes,
physical health conditions), psychosocial (social support, levels of stress and
anxiety, individual resilience), and environmental (cultural norms, access to
healthcare services, economic status) factors influence positive mental health
in the perinatal period in a multidimensional manner. Furthermore,
psychoeducation programs, mindfulness-based interventions, strengthening of
social support systems, and positive psychology approaches were identified as
effective in promoting perinatal mental well-being. Promoting positive mental
health during the perinatal period is a critical priority for the health of both
mother and child, as well as for public health. Interventions should adopt a
biopsychosocial approach and emphasize the importance of early detection and
comprehensive support programs. Additionally, it is recommended to develop
community-based interventions that encourage paternal and family involvement.
