Title: Peer relationships and intraindividual change in school attitudes during adolescence: A longitudinal investigation of trust, communication, and alienation
Authors: Jule Eilts, Tijs Bolz
ID: openalex.W7214190906
URL: https://doi.org/10.1007/s10212-026-01186-y

Abstract:
School attitudes are dynamic during adolescence and are significantly influenced
by peer relationships. While research has established the importance of peer
support for school engagement, the specific mechanisms through which trust,
communication, and alienation shape intraindividual changes in school attitudes
remain understudied. This study examined how specific dimensions of peer
relationships (trust, communication, and alienation) predict intraindividual
changes in students’ attitudes toward school during adolescence, moving beyond
group-level comparisons to focus on within-person developmental processes.
Participants comprised 120 German students aged 10–16 years (M = 12.60, SD =
1.35) from lower secondary schools, with balanced gender distribution (50%
female). A longitudinal design with two measurement points six months apart was
employed. True Intraindividual Change (TIC) modeling was used to examine
baseline levels and change in school attitudes. Peer relationships were assessed
using the Inventory of Parent and Peer Attachment (IPPA), and school attitudes
using the Bielefeld Longitudinal Study questionnaire. School attitudes
demonstrated substantial temporal stability. Communication and trust
significantly predicted higher baseline school attitudes. Unexpectedly,
alienation positively predicted attitude change, while age negatively predicted
change in the alienation model. Peer relationships significantly influence both
baseline school attitudes and their development over time. The counterintuitive
finding that alienation predicted positive attitude change suggests potential
resilience mechanisms, highlighting the complex dynamics of adolescent peer
relationships and school engagement.
