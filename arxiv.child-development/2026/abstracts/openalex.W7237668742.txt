Title: Endurance exercise reduces motivation for physical and cognitive effort without impairing cognitive performance
Authors: Stijn A. A. Massar, Karen Sasmita, Takashi Obana, Weiyan Chee, Jingwen Chai, Ivan C. C. Low, Jason K. W. Lee, Christopher Lee Asplund
ID: openalex.W7237668742
URL: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-80412-5

Abstract:
Prolonged physical exertion can reduce the capacity (physiological fatigue) or
willingness to exert further effort (motivational fatigue). While prior work has
shown that physical exertion diminishes motivation for physical effort, it
remains unclear whether changes in motivation generalize to cognitive effort.
Here we tested whether endurance exercise alters motivation in a domain-general
manner. In two within-subjects crossover experiments ( n = 30, 32), participants
completed 50–60 min of cycling at “somewhat hard” (physical exertion condition)
and “extremely light” (control condition) perceived intensity levels. During
cycling, they completed a sustained attention task and secondary cognitive
tasks, followed by effort-based decision-making tasks that revealed preferences
for additional physical or cognitive effort. Physical exertion led to steeper
discounting of rewards offered for both effort types, despite unchanged
cognitive performance. Motivational declines were observed hypothetical
(Experiment 1) and incentive-compatible scenarios (Experiment 2; participants
enacted a randomly selected choice). Mathematical modeling showed that exertion
lowered the threshold beyond which effort was no longer worthwhile, and heart
rate analysis showed that bodily exertion signals correlated with the magnitude
of this shift. We conclude that physical endurance exercise affects effort
valuation without measurably impairing cognitive performance capacity, reducing
motivation for further effort across both domains.
