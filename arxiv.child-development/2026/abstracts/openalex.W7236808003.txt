Title: A key to unlocking reform in education: autonomy supportive leadership and teacher innovativeness
Authors: Lindsey Devers Basileo, Merewyn Elizabeth Lyons, Michael D. Toth, Barbara Otto, Matteo Carmignola, Franz Hofmann
ID: openalex.W7236808003
URL: https://doi.org/10.1080/13603124.2026.2760749

Abstract:
Declining global trends in student academic proficiency have intensified the
need for effective educational leadership strategies. While many policy
approaches rely on top-down accountability mandates, these strategies often
create controlling environments that limit educational leaders’ and teachers’
ability to implement instructional innovations. This study integrates self-
determination theory, diffusion of innovation theory, and personality systems
interaction (PSI) theory to examine how autonomy-supportive leadership
influences teachers’ implementation of an instructional innovation. Using a
cross-sectional survey of 1,408 teachers from 71 low-performing schools across
11 districts in seven U.S. states, we explored teacher perceptions of leadership
styles, autonomy satisfaction and frustration, and teacher innovativeness.
Findings suggest that PSI leadership styles may influence innovation
implementation through their effects on autonomy satisfaction and frustration.
Additionally, autonomy satisfaction was positively and significantly associated
with implementation, while frustration showed a negative relationship. The self-
directed PSI leadership style had a small but significant direct effect on
implementation, regardless of autonomy satisfaction and frustration. Notably,
teacher innovativeness exerted the largest positive and statistically
significant influence on innovation implementation, even after accounting for
autonomy, experience, and perceptions of principal leadership. These results
underscore the importance of fostering autonomy-supportive leadership and
teacher innovativeness to drive instructional improvements and enhance student
outcomes.
