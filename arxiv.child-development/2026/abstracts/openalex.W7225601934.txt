Title: Relative Strength of Cyberbullying Victimization, Emotion Regulation, and Self-Compassion in Predicting Resilience Among Neurodiverse Adults
Authors: Madison Bosch
ID: openalex.W7225601934
URL: https://openalex.org/W7225601934

Abstract:
Cyberbullying has become a significant social and psychological concern within
digital environments, particularly for neurodiverse individuals who rely on
online platforms for communication, social connection, and community support.
Neurodiverse individuals may be especially vulnerable to cyberbullying, which
can exacerbate social withdrawal, anxiety, and depression. Although prior
research has examined cyberbullying and emotional distress, there is a need to
identify factors that can promote resilience in neurodiverse adults experiencing
cyberbullying. To address this gap, this quantitative study involved determining
the relative strength of cyberbullying victimization, emotion regulation, and
self-compassion in predicting resilience among neurodiverse adults. Informed by
the emotion regulation theory, the study involved using a cross-sectional
correlational survey design, with data collected via SurveyMonkey from English-
speaking adults 18 or older who identified as neurodiverse. Following data
screening, 186 participants were included in the final analysis. Hierarchical
multiple regression analysis revealed emotion regulation and self-compassion
were significant positive predictors of resilience, whereas cyberbullying
victimization was not a significant predictor of resilience. These results
suggested higher levels of emotion regulation and self-compassion were
associated with higher levels of resilience among neurodiverse adults. Findings
have implications for positive social change regarding psychological factors
associated with resilience and may inform mental health interventions, support
services, and strategies that are aimed at strengthening resilience among
neurodiverse adults.
