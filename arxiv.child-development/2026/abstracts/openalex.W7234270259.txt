Title: Parenting Styles and Socio-Emotional Learning Among Junior High School Students: Basis for Development Programs
Authors: Maria Chelo E. Jimenez, Ronie B. Basinang
ID: openalex.W7234270259
URL: https://doi.org/10.47191/ijmra/v9-i11-25

Abstract:
This study examined the relationship between parenting styles and the socio-
emotional learning (SEL) of junior high school students in a selected Catholic
school. Specifically, it determined the respondents’ demographic profiles,
dominant parenting styles, and levels of SEL in terms of self-awareness, self-
management, social awareness, relationship skills, and responsible decision-
making. It also examined the relationships between parenting styles and SEL and
the differences in SEL when respondents were grouped according to demographic
characteristics. Using a quantitative descriptive-correlational research design,
the study involved 112 junior high school students. Data were collected through
a structured questionnaire and analyzed using appropriate descriptive and
inferential statistical tools. The findings showed that students demonstrated
positive levels of the five SEL competencies, although areas for improvement
included seeking help when needed, recognizing the discomfort of excluded peers,
and correcting poor decisions. Significant relationships were found between
specific parenting styles and selected SEL competencies. Authoritative parenting
was significantly associated with four competencies, while authoritarian
parenting was associated with all competencies except self-awareness. Permissive
parenting was significantly associated with self-awareness and relationship
skills, whereas neglectful parenting was associated with self-awareness, self-
management, social awareness, and responsible decision-making. No significant
differences in SEL were found according to age, sex, and family structure.
However, significant differences were observed in social awareness and
relationship skills across grade levels. Based on these findings, a development
plan was proposed to strengthen students’ SEL competencies through targeted
school-based activities, parental engagement, and continuous monitoring and
evaluation. The study underscores the importance of considering parenting styles
and school-based support in promoting students’ socio-emotional development.
