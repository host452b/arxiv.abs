Title: Pain and Mental Health Patterns in Adults With a History of Cancer
Authors: Kyra Hirshey, Laney Goodman, Carsen Chandler, Natasha N. Bray, Micah Hartwell
ID: openalex.W7238814581
URL: https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-11612438/v1

Abstract:
Purpose To examine associations of cancer-related pain, including whether pain
is controlled, with frequent poor mental health and subjective cognitive decline
(SCD) among cancer survivors. Methods We analyzed 2022–2024 Behavioral Risk
Factor Surveillance System data from 24,015 adults with a history of cancer.
Respondents were classified as having no pain, or pain controlled or
uncontrolled, with or without medication. Sex-stratified weighted logistic
regression models, adjusted for age, race and ethnicity, income, and education,
estimated associations with frequent poor mental health (≥ 14 of the past 30
days) and SCD (assessed among adults aged ≥ 45 years). Results Current cancer-
related pain was reported by 11.9% of women and 8.5% of men. Compared with no
pain, uncontrolled pain was associated with the highest odds of frequent poor
mental health (adjusted odds ratio [AOR] up to 5.30 among men and 2.66 among
women) and SCD (AOR up to 4.66 among men and 3.56 among women). Pain controlled
with medication was also associated with elevated odds of both outcomes in both
sexes. Among women, gynecologic cancer was associated with higher odds of both
outcomes. Conclusions Cancer-related pain, particularly uncontrolled pain, was
associated with poorer mental health and SCD, and elevated odds persisted even
when pain was controlled with medication. Implications for Cancer Survivors:
Survivors who report pain, even when it is controlled, may benefit from routine
screening for psychological distress and cognitive concerns, and survivors
reporting these concerns may benefit from assessment of pain and its control.
