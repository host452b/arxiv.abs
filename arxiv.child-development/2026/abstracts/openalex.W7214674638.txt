Title: Shifting academic fates in early adolescence are marked by concurrent behavioural change rather than baseline profiles
Authors: Chihiro Hosoda, Kenchi Hosokawa, Takuto Matsuhashi, Nanase Takahashi, Yu Hayashizaki, Yutaka Matsuzaki, Ryuta Kawashima
ID: openalex.W7214674638
URL: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-72860-w

Abstract:
Academic performance in childhood is highly stable in relative rank, yet the
behavioural characteristics that accompany stable versus changing achievement
standing remain unclear. We longitudinally tracked 16,946 students (grades 4–9;
approximate grade-derived baseline ages 11.9 and 11.4 years) over 3–4 years in
two contrasting Japanese cities, using achievement tests standardized annually
within city and grade, and lifestyle–learning questionnaires. All trajectories
therefore describe changes in students’ relative standing, not absolute learning
growth. Most students’ relative standing was stable and classified well from
baseline questionnaire responses, a distinction that largely reflects baseline
achievement level, with stable high standing accompanied by consistent daily
routines, family support, and active classroom engagement. Approximately 9% of
students rose or fell substantially; these shifts were weakly classified from
Year 1 behavioural profiles but were marked by concurrent changes in study
investment, motivation-related responses, and digital-media use. Behavioural
information available through Year 3 added minimal prospective information about
Year 4 standing beyond prior achievement (ΔR² = 0.002), indicating that these
changes accompany, rather than forecast, shifts in relative standing. Stable and
changing academic standing were thus marked by distinct behavioural
profiles—observable markers whose causal status and modifiability warrant
prospective and experimental study.
