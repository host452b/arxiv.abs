Title: Classroom Interaction Dynamics in Role-Play-Based Learning Oriented Toward Enhancing Speaking Skills and Collaboration at Tk Pertiwi Kendal
Authors: Mila Fitriyah, Harto Nuroso, Aryo Andri Nugroho
ID: openalex.W7235033681
URL: https://doi.org/10.31258/jes.10.11.p.989-1007

Abstract:
This study explored classroom interaction dynamics during the “Book Rescue
Squad” role-play activity designed to provide opportunities to support speaking
skills and collaboration among children aged 4–5 years at TK Pertiwi Kendal,
Indonesia. Using a qualitative phenomenological approach and deep-slice
analysis, the study involved 16 children participating in three rotating roles:
Book Detectives, Book Doctors, and Book Organizers. The activity was conducted
across four sessions, each lasting 90 minutes. Data were collected through video
documentation, field notes, interviews, and research documentation, and analyzed
through phenomenological reduction and thematic interpretation. The findings
revealed that children used language to describe book conditions, ask questions,
give directions, and communicate task-related ideas. Descriptive observations
showed higher scores in confidence, voice clarity, vocabulary, sentence
structure, relevance to assigned roles, and communication of ideas during later
sessions, while fluency remained unchanged. Collaboration was reflected in
sharing materials, negotiating task responsibilities, helping peers, providing
peer support, reaching group consensus, and regulating task participation.
However, participation patterns varied among children, with some demonstrating
physical involvement despite limited verbal responses. The study indicates that
purposeful role-play activities can provide meaningful opportunities for
speaking and collaboration, although the findings are limited to one classroom,
one scenario, and four sessions.
