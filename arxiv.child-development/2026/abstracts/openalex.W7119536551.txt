Title: Cognitive and neural outcomes of executive function training in healthy adults: a systematic review
Authors: Telesmagno Neves-Teles, Ana Paula Schmidt Lourenço, Cristian Zanon, Rosa Maria Martins de Almeida
ID: openalex.W7119536551
URL: https://doi.org/10.1007/s11682-026-01225-8

Abstract:
Cognitive training can strengthen executive functions, which are essential for
adaptive behavior, yet evidence integrating cognitive and neural adaptations in
healthy adults remains limited. This systematic review (PROSPERO:
CRD420251107775), conducted according to PRISMA 2020, synthesized 26
publications examining executive function training and cognitive and neural
outcomes in healthy adults. These comprised 24 randomized controlled trial
publications and two quasi-experimental studies, representing 24 independent
samples (N = 1,303; intervention = 667; control = 636). Cognitive improvements
were most consistently reported for working memory and inhibitory control,
although transfer varied across studies and several trials reported null
findings. Neural evidence showed more consistent training-related changes in
functional than structural measures, particularly involving electrophysiological
dynamics and prefrontal or frontoparietal systems, whereas structural findings
were more heterogeneous. Thirteen publications examined cognitive–neural
associations across structural, functional, hemodynamic, metabolic, and
electrophysiological measures; however, these relationships were heterogeneous
and not consistently observed. Cognitive and neural changes were therefore not
uniformly coupled, limiting mechanistic interpretations. Methodological
constraints included small samples, short interventions, heterogeneous training
and outcome measures, and limited availability of prespecified analysis plans.
Overall, the evidence supports training-related cognitive and neural plasticity
but indicates important limits to broad transfer and consistent brain–behavior
convergence. Larger, longer, prospectively registered studies integrating
cognitive and neural outcomes are needed to establish the durability,
generalizability, and functional significance of these effects.
