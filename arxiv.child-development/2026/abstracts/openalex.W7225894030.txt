Title: Ecoliteracy in Early Childhood Education: A Systematic Literature Review of Construct, Pedagogy, and Assessment
Authors: Hawa Nurul Jannah, Rita Mariyana, Heny Djoehaeni
ID: openalex.W7225894030
URL: https://doi.org/10.23960/jpp.v16i4.pp2244-2267

Abstract:
This review examines how ecoliteracy is defined and dimensioned in early
childhood education research, which learning strategies are used to foster it,
how children are assessed, and what trends and gaps appear. Following PRISMA
2020, Scopus records published between 2016 and 2026 in the Environmental
Science subject area were searched in five steps (1,208 records). Twenty-two
journal articles were read in full text, appraised with the Mixed Methods
Appraisal Tool, and synthesized through structured thematic analysis. No study
takes ecoliteracy as its principal term, and the term is absent from all 144
articles screened before the keyword limit; environmental education is the most
frequent of twelve terms. Eleven studies examine nature-based outdoor learning.
Ten studies do not assess children, and the four that use a scored instrument or
a rubric use four different instruments. A restraint dimension is stated
explicitly in only one study, and three countries contribute 14 studies. Within
the reviewed corpus, the construct is used without a shared name, set of
dimensions, or instrument. This study proposes a framework spanning cognitive,
affective, action, ethical, and restraint dimensions as a basis for early
childhood ecoliteracy instruments. Keywords: early childhood education,
ecoliteracy, environmental education, sustainability, systematic literature
review.
