Title: Intersecting vulnerabilities and the mental health of adolescent mothers in forced displacement: A narrative review and conceptual framework
Authors: Damilola Onietan, Omolayo Anjorin, Belinda Agyapong, Chiranjeev Sanyal, Ejemai Eboreime
ID: openalex.W7214676291
URL: https://doi.org/10.1017/gmh.2026.10298

Abstract:
Adolescent mothers in forced displacement experience intersecting risks related
to adolescent development, displacement-related traumatic experiences and early
motherhood. Despite these risks, their mental health needs remain understudied
in humanitarian research and policy. This narrative review synthesizes evidence
from conflict-affected settings. Drawing on literature published between 2000
and 2025, we propose the Triple Jeopardy Framework, a conceptual model
describing how adolescence, forced displacement and motherhood interact to
influence mental health outcomes, including depression, anxiety, post-traumatic
stress disorder and intergenerational mental health risk. The framework
distinguishes the separate risk and protective factors associated with each
area, then shows how their intersections can create synergistic risk by
weakening protective systems, limiting access to care and increasing
psychological distress. At the same time, displaced adolescent mothers may
exercise agency and draw meaning from motherhood while also experiencing
significant distress and constraint. Effective responses require interventions
that address structural determinants, strengthen community-based support
systems, integrate maternal and mental health care and remain responsive to
young mothers’ capabilities and caregiving realities. The Triple Jeopardy
Framework provides a basis for developing gender-responsive, life-course
approaches to adolescent maternal mental health in humanitarian settings.
