Title: Beyond the Collectivist-Individualist Dichotomy: Parenting Styles and Adolescent Development in South Asian and European Contexts- A Systematic Review
Authors: Syed Imran Haider
ID: openalex.W7202354405
URL: https://doi.org/10.1177/10693971261483500

Abstract:
Parenting style research often relies on Western theories that may not fully
capture parenting in non-Western societies. This review synthesized evidence
from 100 studies examining parenting styles and adolescent development across
South Asia and Europe. The sample included 31 South Asian studies, 33 European
studies, 28 cross-cultural comparisons and 8 meta-analysis/review papers.
Ecological and cultural developmental frameworks guided the analysis. These
included Bronfenbrenner’s ecological systems theory, Kagitcibasi’s family change
model, and the parenting model proposed by Darling and Steinberg. Three
questions guided the review. First, how do parenting styles appear across these
regions? Second, how do they relate to adolescent academic, psychological, and
behavioral outcomes? Third, does the evidence challenge the traditional
collectivist–individualist framework in cross-cultural psychology. Results
showed that parental warmth consistently predicted positive adolescent outcomes
in both regions. Warmth, therefore, appears to function as a pancultural
protective factor. The effects of parental control varied more strongly across
cultural contexts. In South Asian settings, parental authority is often
normative and socially expected. Consequently, negative associations with
control appeared weaker than those reported in European samples. However,
control remained beneficial mainly when combined with warmth and exercised
within culturally valued developmental domains.
