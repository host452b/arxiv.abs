Title: Navigating a Feminized Space: Experiences and Identity-Negotiation Strategies of Male Kindergarten Teachers in Ashanti Region, Ghana
Authors: Veronica Sowah, Dennis Glover, Cindy Sarfo Koduah, Winston Kwame Abroampa, Christopher Sam
ID: openalex.W7234569531
URL: https://doi.org/10.64712/ijeccer.v3i2.1044

Abstract:
This phenomenological study explores how male kindergarten teachers in Ghana’s
Ashanti Region experience and negotiate identity in a predominantly female early
childhood education (ECE) context. Data were collected through semi?structured
interviews with seven male kindergarten teachers and four headteachers from
schools in Atwima Mponua District and Asante Mampong Municipality, and analyzed
using thematic analysis. Findings show male teachers are frequently perceived as
“out of place” and subject to cultural stereotypes that label caregiving as
un?manly, producing parental mistrust, limited peer emotional support, role
concealment, heavier workloads, and constrained career progression. Despite
these barriers, participants demonstrated professional commitment and used
identity?work strategies such as professional pride, transparent communication,
trust?building caregiving, collaboration with school authorities, and pursuit of
further qualifications to counter prejudice and cultivate acceptance. The study
highlights how socio?cultural norms both constrain and motivate male teachers’
career trajectories, fostering resilience and opportunities for more
gender?balanced ECE practice. Recommendations call on policymakers,
headteachers, and teacher educators to amplify positive narratives about male
kindergarten teachers (through media, community engagement, and school
practices), implement mentorship and fair task allocation, and create incentives
to recruit and retain men in ECE.
