Title: Adverse Childhood Experiences, Attachment, Guilt, and Mental Health as Predictors of Positive Dyadic Coping Following Perinatal Loss Among Women
Authors: Rebeca Santamaría‐Gutiez, Francisco González‐Sala, Laura Lacomba‐Trejo
ID: openalex.W7224941158
URL: https://doi.org/10.31083/bp49618

Abstract:
Background: Perinatal loss is a highly stressful life event that can profoundly
affect women’s emotional well-being and couple functioning. This study examined
the role of adverse childhood experiences (ACEs), insecure romantic attachment
(attachment anxiety and attachment avoidance), guilt, and mental health problems
(MHP) in predicting positive dyadic coping (PDC) among women who had experienced
perinatal loss. Methods: A total of 162 Spanish women completed online
questionnaires assessing attachment (experiences in close relationships–revised
scale (ECR-R)), ACEs (adverse childhood experiences questionnaire (ACEQ)), as
well as guilt and MHP using ad hoc Likert-type items. Data were analyzed using
correlation analyses, hierarchical regression, fuzzy-set Qualitative Comparative
Analysis (fsQCA), and mediation models (SPSS, PROCESS). Results: Hierarchical
regression analysis showed that lower attachment anxiety and attachment
avoidance were the strongest predictors of PDC, with the final model explaining
68.8% of the variance. fsQCA indicated that no single condition was necessary
for either high or low PDC; however, several sufficient configurations accounted
for most cases of high PDC, particularly those characterized by low attachment
avoidance combined with low attachment anxiety, low guilt, or the absence of
ACEs. Configurations associated with low PDC were primarily characterized by
higher attachment avoidance and attachment anxiety, often combined with greater
psychological distress or guilt. Mediation analyses revealed that insecure
attachment, particularly attachment anxiety, mediated the association between
ACEs and PDC, suggesting that ACEs influenced coping primarily through indirect
pathways rather than direct pathways. Conclusions: Overall, these findings
highlight the central role of attachment processes and emotional regulation in
promoting PDC following perinatal loss and support the utility of integrative,
non-linear approaches for understanding adjustment to perinatal bereavement.
