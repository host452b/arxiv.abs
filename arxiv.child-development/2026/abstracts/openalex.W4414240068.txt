Title: Associations between breastfeeding duration and blood pressure in children and adolescents: evidence from a cohort study
Authors: Lanling Chen, 程昕然, Shihai Zheng, Muhammad Fahad Tahir, Yuwei Wang, Shunqing Luo, Haiying Zhong, Daochao Huang, Lijing Chen, Xizhou An et al.
ID: openalex.W4414240068
URL: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-77556-9

Abstract:
Breastfeeding is widely recognized as beneficial for child health, yet the
relationship between breastfeeding duration and long-term cardiovascular
outcomes, particularly blood pressure (BP), remains incomplete. This study aimed
to examine the association between breastfeeding duration and blood pressure
(BP) among school-aged children in southwest China. A cohort of 15,007 children
aged 6-13 years from the Chongqing Health Children Cohort was analyzed.
Participants were stratified by breastfeeding duration. Mixed-effects linear
regression models were used to investigate the associations between
breastfeeding duration and BP parameters; while mixed-effects logistic
regression was applied to evaluate the associations with prehypertension and
hypertension, adjusting confounding variables. Longer breastfeeding (> 10
months) was observed to be statistically associated with elevated diastolic
blood pressure (DBP) and mean arterial pressure (MAP), but not systolic blood
pressure (SBP), particularly in males, children under 9.3 (50th percentile)
years old, and those with birth weight below 3000 g or over 3600 g ( P < 0.001).
Further, statistical associations between breastfeeding duration and blood
pressure varied by residential region and sex. Statistically significant
associations were observed for DBP (β(95%CIs) = 1.18(0.40, 1.97), P = 0.044)
among urban children and for SBP (β(95%CIs) = 0.82(0.14, 1.50), P = 0.007) and
DBP (β(95%CIs) = 0.99(0.39, 1.60), P = 0.001) among males. This study
preliminarily identified observed statistical association between longer
breastfeeding duration (> 10 months) and elevated DBP/MAP within certain
pediatric subpopulations, highlighting the need for population-specific
breastfeeding guidance informed by further evidence related to children
cardiovascular health.
