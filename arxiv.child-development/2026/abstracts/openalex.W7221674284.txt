Title: Paternal Parenting Styles and Filial Piety Toward Fathers Among Chinese Adolescents: The Roles of Conscientiousness and Moral Identity
Authors: Cong Peng, Zhenlin Zhang, Yuhua Wang, Zhenyun Zhang, Fang Cheng
ID: openalex.W7221674284
URL: https://doi.org/10.2147/prbm.s640415

Abstract:
Background: Previous studies have demonstrated that caregivers' parenting styles
are associated with children' filial piety toward parents. However, the
underlying mechanisms linking parenting styles to filial piety toward parents
remain largely unspecified. Additionally, prior research commonly classifies
family parenting styles into positive parenting and negative parenting,
neglecting the potential differential impacts and pathways of different types of
paternal parenting styles on filial piety toward fathers. Purpose: Drawing on
theoretical and empirical evidence, this study investigated the relationship
between two types of paternal parenting styles (positive and negative) and
filial piety toward fathers among Chinese adolescents, employing serial
mediation models that included conscientiousness and moral identity as potential
mediators. Methods: A total of 467 Chinese senior high school students aged
14-19 years (male 47.1%) completed anonymous questionnaires assessing
demographics, paternal parenting styles, conscientiousness, moral identity and
filial piety toward fathers. Serial mediation analyses were conducted using
Mplus8.3 and bootstrap procedures. Results: After controlling for gender, age,
and only-child status, the results indicated that both positive and negative
paternal parenting are significantly associated with adolescents' filial piety
toward fathers. Furthermore, positive paternal parenting correlates with
adolescents' filial piety toward fathers through two independent mechanisms: an
indirect pathway mediated by moral identity and the serial mediating pathway of
conscientiousness and moral identity. In contrast, the association between
negative paternal parenting and adolescents' filial piety toward fathers
operates only through a direct pathway. Conclusion: Both positive and negative
paternal parenting were significantly correlated with adolescents' filial piety
toward fathers. However, the serial mediating path from conscientiousness to
moral identity is only significant across the model examining positive paternal
parenting as a predictor of filial piety toward fathers. Conscientiousness does
not function as a viable mediator linking paternal parenting to adolescents'
filial piety toward their fathers. Likewise, moral identity fails to mediate the
association between negative paternal parenting and filial piety toward fathers.
This study verifies that fathers constitute a pivotal figure in the formation of
adolescents' filial piety within the Chinese cultural context and shed light on
the asymmetric dynamic between distinct paternal parenting styles and filial
piety among Chinese adolescents. These findings advance our understanding of the
distinct functions of divergent paternal parenting styles as a critical familial
environmental factor, which shapes adolescents' filial piety through the
intrapersonal pathway of personality traits and moral identity.
