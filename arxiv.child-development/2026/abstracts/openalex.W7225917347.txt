Title: Executive function profiles in Chinese early adolescents: associations with academic achievement and emotional and behavioral difficulties
Authors: Hui Li, Yiting Liang, Jurong Xue, Kaiyu Gan, Sai Ma, Wei Liu, Zijie Qin, shiyu Fang, Xuemin Zhang
ID: openalex.W7225917347
URL: https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-11174665/v1

Abstract:
Executive function (EF) is multidimensional, yet less is known about how its
components are configured within individuals during early adolescence and
whether these configurations relate to academic achievement and emotional and
behavioural difficulties. Using latent profile analysis, this study identified
EF profiles among 489 Chinese fifth-grade students based on sustained attention,
interference inhibition, response inhibition, working memory, and cognitive
flexibility. Three profiles emerged: High EF, Average EF, and Attention-
vulnerable EF. The High EF profile showed higher achievement in Chinese,
English, and mathematics than the other profiles. Compared with the Average EF
profile, the Attention-vulnerable EF profile showed lower mathematics
achievement and greater hyperactivity/inattention, peer problems, and total
difficulties. These findings suggest that EF heterogeneity reflects both
differences in overall functioning and distinct configurations of relative
strengths and weaknesses, highlighting the relevance of attention-centred
vulnerabilities to individual differences in academic and behavioral functioning
in early adolescence.
