Title: Parental Smartphone Phubbing During School-Related Activities: An Integrated Developmental Framework Linking Attachment Security, Emotional Regulation, and Academic Engagement
Authors: Jagadish Mareedu, Saptarshi Sengupta
ID: openalex.W7223040426
URL: https://doi.org/10.65320/jce.vol.1.issue5.48

Abstract:
Smartphones are now embedded in family, educational, and occupational routines,
yet their interpersonal costs are not adequately captured by screen-time
measures alone. This conceptual article examines parental smartphone phubbing
during school-related parent-child interactions and develops an integrated
developmental explanation of how repeated attentional interruptions may become
educationally meaningful. Drawing on a critical interpretive and integrative
synthesis of scholarship on digital parenting, technoference, attachment,
emotion regulation, academic engagement, self-determination, bioecological
development, and family systems, the article positions caregiver responsiveness
as the proximal mechanism connecting parental digital distraction with
children's developmental experiences. The proposed framework suggests a sequence
in which recurrent parental phubbing may reduce perceived caregiver
responsiveness, contribute to less secure expectations of caregiver
availability, constrain opportunities for interpersonal co-regulation, and, in
turn, weaken behavioural, emotional, and cognitive engagement with learning. The
model does not treat smartphones as inherently harmful and does not claim
empirically established causality. Instead, it identifies relational
interruption, developmental accumulation, and contextual moderation as the
conditions under which device use may acquire psychological significance.
School-related interactions are highlighted as a distinctive context because
homework, examination preparation, academic problem solving, and discussions of
school experiences simultaneously involve cognitive scaffolding and emotional
support. The framework integrates six complementary theoretical traditions and
extends prior work by linking family digital behaviour to educational engagement
through a coherent developmental cascade. Implications are developed for
research design, parent education, school counselling, educational psychology,
and culturally responsive work in India. A staged agenda for longitudinal,
dyadic, observational, and intervention research is proposed to test the model.
