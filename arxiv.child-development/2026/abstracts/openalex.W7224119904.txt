Title: Perinatal mental health and reproductive rights in women with severe mental illness
Authors: Rocío Paricio del Castillo, Mónica Díaz de Neira Hernando, María Ángeles Cano Linares, Inmaculada Palanca Maresca
ID: openalex.W7224119904
URL: https://doi.org/10.20935/acadphealth8565

Abstract:
Introduction: Women with severe mental illness and psychosocial disabilities
face significant barriers to exercising their reproductive, maternal, and
parenting rights. Despite growing recognition of perinatal mental health as a
public health priority, international legal obligations concerning motherhood
and early parenting among women with severe mental illness remain insufficiently
examined. Materials and methods: This study employs a doctrinal and
interdisciplinary approach to analyse international human rights obligations
relating to perinatal mental health, psychosocial disability, and reproductive
rights. The analysis focuses on the International Covenant on Economic, Social
and Cultural Rights (ICESCR), the Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of
Discrimination against Women (CEDAW), the Convention on the Rights of the Child
(CRC), and the Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD),
complemented by a narrative review of relevant scientific literature. Results:
International human rights law establishes a coherent framework requiring States
to ensure access to perinatal mental healthcare, protect reproductive autonomy,
recognise legal capacity, and provide support for parenting and family life. The
CRPD, particularly Articles 12 and 23, shifts the focus from paternalistic
models of incapacity and risk management towards supported decision-making and
rights-based approaches to motherhood. However, substantial gaps persist between
international standards and institutional practices, including psychiatric
stigma, mother–infant separation, and insufficient support services.
Conclusions: States have positive obligations to develop integrated, community-
based, and rights-oriented perinatal services that support maternal recovery,
preserve family relationships, and enable women with severe mental illness to
exercise motherhood on an equal basis with others.
