Title: Social Media, STS Engagement, and Information Literacy: A Parallel Mediation Model for Enhancing Critical Thinking in Higher Education
Authors: Ronel Amorin, Gesila M Gallaron
ID: openalex.W7238852870
URL: https://doi.org/10.56059/wyw31656

Abstract:
Understanding the factors that shape students’ cognitive growth, particularly
the impact of social media on critical thinking skills, is essential in today’s
digital learning environment. This study used a cross-sectional, quantitative
design to analyse the mediation of social media usage (SMU) on critical thinking
skills (CTS) through STS (Science, Technology, and Society) learning engagement
(SLE) and information literacy (IL). The study surveyed 300 undergraduate
students from a Philippine state university (selected via stratified random
sampling) who completed the STS course. The study applied a covariance-based
structural equation modeling (CB-SEM) approach to analyse the data. The direct
effect of SMU on CTS was not statistically significant, but both SLE and IL were
positively associated with CTS, and SMU was indirectly associated with CTS
through two parallel paths, one through SLE and the other through IL. Educators
and policymakers should integrate real-world projects, social media, and digital
literacy into the STS curriculum to enhance engagement and critical thinking,
contributing valuable insights for inclusive, future-ready higher education.
