Title: A comparative analysis of play and playful learning in Finnish and Chinese early childhood education and care curricula
Authors: Shuanghong Jenny Niu, Olli‐Pekka Malinen, Inkeri Ruókonen, Tarja-Riitta Hurme, Anitta Melasalmi, Pirkko Siklander, Xinghua Wang, Heyi Zhang, Jaakko H. Moilanen, Xiaowei Li et al.
ID: openalex.W7228196001
URL: https://doi.org/10.1002/curj.70114

Abstract:
Play and playful learning are increasingly emphasised in early childhood
education and care (ECEC), yet limited research has examined how national
curricula shape their role across countries. This study compares Finnish and
Chinese ECEC curricula to explore how play and playful learning are
conceptualised and positioned pedagogically. Eleven researchers from Finland and
China conducted a qualitative content analysis of curricular frameworks. Both
countries recognise play as vital to children's holistic development and link it
to children's rights and learning. Play is integrated across domains in both
systems. However, the Finnish curriculum emphasises child‐initiated, creative
play and digital literacy, fostering autonomy, whereas the Chinese curricula
present a more structured, goal‐oriented approach aligned with developmental
expectations. Both highlight the social dimension of play. These findings
demonstrate how cultural and educational traditions shape curricular
interpretations of play and contribute to global discussions on play‐based
pedagogy that balance child agency with guided learning.
