Title: Cyberbullying and Anxiety Research: A Bibliometric and Science Mapping Analysis of Scopus-Indexed Literature
Authors: Norezlin Sulaiman, Mastura Mahfar, Fauziah Zaiden
ID: openalex.W7237358018
URL: https://doi.org/10.46328/ijemst.8367

Abstract:
Cyberbullying has become a growing concern in educational and psychological
research due to its strong association with anxiety and other mental health
difficulties among young people. This study aims to map the research landscape
of cyberbullying and anxiety by identifying publication trends, key
contributors, influential countries, citation impact and major thematic
patterns. A bibliometric analysis was conducted using 112 Scopus-indexed
publications published between 2013 and 2025. Data were cleaned and analysed
using Microsoft Excel, OpenRefine, biblioMagika® and VOSviewer. The findings
show a steady increase in publications, particularly after 2019, indicating
rising scholarly attention to the psychological effects of online victimisation.
The dataset involved 461 contributing authors, 101 cited papers and 3,470 total
citations, with an h-index of 34, g-index of 56 and m-index of 2.615. The United
States, Spain and Australia emerged as the most productive countries, while key
institutions included Monash University, Tampere University and the University
of Almería. Keyword co-occurrence and thematic interpretation revealed that
cyberbullying and anxiety research is strongly centered on cyber-victimization,
social media use, depression, psychological distress, self-esteem and adolescent
mental health. Keywords related to emotion regulation, loneliness, empathy,
intervention, bystander roles and COVID-19 suggest growing scholarly attention
to protective and prevention-oriented perspectives. Overall, this study provides
a structured overview of the intellectual development of cyberbullying and
anxiety research and highlights future directions for studies on counselling
support, digital well-being and youth mental health interventions.
