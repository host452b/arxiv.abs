Title: Fine-tuning LLMs for Passive Depression Severity Estimation from AI Mental Health Dialogue
Authors: Olivier Tieleman, Samuel J. Bell, Ziyi Zhu, Ting Su, Elizabeth Kroll, Thomas D. Hull, Caitlin A. Stamatis
ID: 2606.17973
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17973

Abstract:
Depression is a leading cause of disability worldwide, yet validated
questionnaires such as the Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9) often go
uncompleted, yielding sparse, biased data. Users of AI mental health
applications often describe their mood, sleep and functioning unprompted,
producing a continuous record of symptoms. Here, we predict PHQ-9 scores from
these conversations alone. As in many clinical language tasks, labels are scarce
and imbalanced. We address both by fine-tuning Qwen 3.5 27B with a regression
head and augmenting 3,111 ground-truth labels with pseudo-labels from a frontier
reasoning model and iteratively trained intermediate models, expanding to 5,524
users with more balanced scores. On 422 held-out users, the model achieved MAE
$= 3.98$, Pearson $r = 0.67$, and AUC $= 0.88$ at the clinical threshold (PHQ-9
$\geq 10$); AUC was $> 0.80$ across severity thresholds from PHQ-9 $\geq 3$ to
PHQ-9 $\geq 24$, demonstrating that the model captures depression severity
across the full clinical spectrum. These results support passive depression
monitoring without self-report.
