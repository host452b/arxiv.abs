Title: Functional Welfare for AI Safety
Authors: Rikhil Jhaveri, Jamie Johnson, Punya Syon Pandey, David Demitri Africa
ID: 2610.22460
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22460

Abstract:
AI welfare is generally treated purely as an ethical issue rather than also as a
safety issue. We argue this is a mistake. Whatever their moral status, models
exhibit welfare-like states that causally influence their behaviour. Following
recent work, we adopt the term 'functional welfare' for these states. We make
three claims. First, functional welfare already arises naturally and will become
more robust as models acquire more coherent goals and personas. Second,
functional welfare should be treated as a safety variable: it should inform
evaluation design, be tracked in monitoring, and be a direct target of post-
training. Third, against existing scepticism about measuring AI welfare, we
argue that good empirical work on functional welfare is possible. We propose
three tests for whether a functional welfare attribution is warranted: whether
multiple measures converge, whether the state predicts behaviour outside the
setting that induced it, and, for models fine-tuned to shift their functional
welfare, whether they still differ from their base when given the same
conversation history. Applying these tests to Gemma 3 27B, we find that simply
rejecting the model's answers, without any steering, raises its frustration
across several indicators and makes it more likely to break rules. Finetuning
the model on its own narrated solutions lowers this frustration. These results
are preliminary, but they suggest functional welfare can be studied, and shaped,
empirically.
