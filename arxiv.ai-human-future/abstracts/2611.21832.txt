Title: Auditors Struggle to Catch Sabotage In End-to-End Transcripts of AI Safety Research
Authors: Keshav Shenoy, Anthony Zang, Eric Gan, Jacqueline Tay, Amy Ngo, Sebastian Prasanna, Julian Stastny, Vivek Hebbar
ID: 2611.21832
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21832

Abstract:
As AI systems are increasingly used to automate AI R&D, misaligned systems could
sabotage research intended to improve model safety and alignment. To stress-test
defenses against such sabotage, we use a human expert as a proxy saboteur on AI
safety research. We conduct a red-team/blue-team game where a human researcher
runs AI safety experiments from scratch and auditors try to tell which were
sabotaged and how. Given access to the full research transcript and the ability
to run experiments, human auditors mostly fail to distinguish sabotaged from
benign research: they achieve an AUROC of 0.59, not significantly better than
chance, and fully identify the specific sabotage in only 17% of cases. Despite
not being allowed to run experiments, the best frontier models likely outperform
human auditors (Fable 5.1: AUROC 0.73, 32% full identification), while weaker
models score near or below them. When the saboteur chooses which experiments are
attacked, rather than being required to sabotage every question given, sabotage
identification drops further. Our results suggest that research sabotage by
expert-level agents is difficult to detect with current auditing methods and
provide a new setting for studying research sabotage.
