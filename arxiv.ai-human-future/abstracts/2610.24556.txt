Title: From Stories to Features: Scaling Concept Vectors for AI Alignment & Interpretability
Authors: Dmitry Scherbakov, Oleg Chernikov, Victoria Khamash, Kirill Afendulev, Alexey Dontsov, Ivan Oseledets, Elena Tutubalina, Anton Korznikov
ID: 2610.24556
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24556

Abstract:
We study feature vectors: directions in a language model's activation space that
correspond to human-interpretable concepts, such as honesty or deception.
Existing unsupervised methods like sparse autoencoders attempt to recover the
whole feature space, often at the cost of feature quality and interpretability.
We take a more pragmatic route: focusing on concepts relevant to AI safety and
alignment, we generate synthetic concept-antagonist story pairs and use the
difference of their mean model activations as a feature vector. We scale this
approach to 1,036 alignment-relevant concepts using 2M synthetic stories across
multiple languages and genres, which we publicly release. Across six models from
three model families, our feature vectors reliably generalize to detecting their
corresponding concepts in chat dialogues and web text. They form an
interpretable geometry shared across models and can causally steer model
behavior when injected into activations. We further use these features to
analyze model representations during jailbreak attacks, mathematical and agentic
reasoning. Our targeted feature construction thus offers a scalable route to
studying concepts in language models, potentially extending to millions of
features across arbitrary domains.
