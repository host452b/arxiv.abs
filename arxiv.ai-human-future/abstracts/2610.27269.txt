Title: African Languages AI Safety Readiness Report
Authors: Israel Abebe Azime, Jesujoba O. Alabi, Tajuddeen Gwadabe, David Ifeoluwa Adelani
ID: 2610.27269
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27269

Abstract:
With the widespread adoption of large language models (LLMs) across languages
and domains, AI safety has emerged as a critical global concern. However, due to
resource constraints, research and development efforts tend to prioritize a
limited number of high-resource languages, leaving many others, especially those
spoken in Africa, underrepresented. While existing research on language
"resource-ness" has largely examined whether sufficient resources are available
to develop language technologies, the question of whether languages are
adequately represented in AI safety research has received attention only more
recently. This report examines the readiness of African languages for inclusion
in AI safety discussions, drawing on existing literature and data. It considers
four factors: the availability of AI-relevant resources, including text data;
national AI policy support; performance on established text benchmarks; and
speaker population. Based on these factors, languages are grouped into four
readiness categories: fully ready, mostly ready, emerging, and foundational
support needed. This classification is intended to inform the inclusion of
African languages in AI safety benchmarks, highlight varying levels of
preparedness, and guide strategic investment in data development, policy, and
community engagement for underrepresented languages.
