Title: Evolutionary Safety of Recursive Self-Improving AI: Taxonomy, Risk Discovery, and Evaluation
Authors: Chang Gong, Jingping Bi, Di Yao, Ran Cheng, Yaodong Yang, Kaile Wang, Xinjian Liang, Chao Xiang, Ruijie Guo
ID: 2609.31186
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31186

Abstract:
Rapid advances in AI are bringing recursive self-improvement (RSI) closer to
practice, with growing progress in model self-training, self-evolving agents,
and automated AI development. Yet the very mechanisms that enable continuous
improvement also introduce new challenges for AI safety. Unlike isolated
failures, safety risks can persist across updates, accumulate through repeated
adaptation, and propagate to successor systems. More importantly, the processes
that generate and select future improvements may themselves evolve, making
safety increasingly difficult to assess and maintain. This raises a fundamental
question, i.e., how can safety be maintained when both AI systems and the
processes that improve them continue to change? Therefore, we introduce
Evolutionary Safety to study how safety properties are preserved, weakened, or
restored throughout persistent and recursive self-improvement. We characterize
six recurring manifestations of evolutionary risk and develop a five-domain
taxonomy that captures how safety-relevant changes arise, persist, and propagate
across evolving systems. Building on this foundation, we establish a framework
for risk discovery and evaluation across states, updates, trajectories, and
lineages, and examine governance principles for controlling modifications,
verifying and authorizing updates, and ensuring traceability and recovery. We
further identify open problems in maintaining safety as both AI systems and
their improvement mechanisms continue to evolve.
