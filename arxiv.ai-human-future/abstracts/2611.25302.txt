Title: Whose Safety? Addressing Anti-Blackness in the Age of AI Safety
Authors: Mírian Silva, Serena Oduro
ID: 2611.25302
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25302

Abstract:
"AI safety" has grown from a niche contingent of actors into the dominant
framing for artificial intelligence (AI) assessment and evaluation,
overshadowing AI ethics. Through political and monetary influence, AI safety has
shifted political focus within the US and abroad. The focus has shifted away
from harms that currently affect the public toward threats framed as
"existential risks". We argue that safety cannot be achieved while anti-
Blackness is left unaddressed. We ground this argument in Sylvia Wynter's
critique of the "overrepresentation" of Man: the modern, Western conception of
the Human that is predicated on the subjugation and othering of Blackness. To
analyze the state of addressing anti-Blackness in the age of AI safety, we
conduct a two-stage, language model-assisted content analysis of 69,290 accepted
main-track papers from ten leading machine learning (ML), natural language
processing (NLP), and AI ethics conferences (2020--2025). Safety research grew
from 0.5% of accepted papers in 2020 to 6.4% in 2025, overtaking ethics. Yet 94%
of safety papers rely on automated metrics, and only 11% of safety papers
mention harms that affect society directly, compared with 78% of ethics papers.
Of the 2,182 safety papers, 8 mention race; none make it central, and none name
Black or African-diaspora communities. Across all 254 identified as race-focused
papers, only 3 describe racial harm in safety terms, and 55% appear at three
ethics venues that publish 2.5% of the corpus. We offer this interdisciplinary
examination of AI safety to argue that a just technological future depends on
confronting anti-Blackness directly.
