Title: Using AI Predictions to Augment Rather Than Replace Surveys
Authors: Lai Wei, Yifei Zhu
ID: 2611.06743
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06743

Abstract:
LLMs can predict human survey responses with nontrivial accuracy. What this
means for the future of social surveys remains hotly contested. Optimists
anticipate a coming era of synthetic respondents; skeptics see a fragile and
overhyped substitute. This article advocates a position: AI predictions cannot
replace surveys, but they can augment them. Using prediction-powered inference
(PPI), a statistical framework rooted in survey methodology and known in
sociology as "mixed subjects design", the study tests whether AI predictions can
improve the statistical efficiency of social surveys when combined with census-
scale auxiliary information. Applying the framework to 83 social outcomes in the
General Social Survey (GSS) and National Health Interview Survey (NHIS), with
American Community Survey (ACS) being the auxiliary file, we find that
supervised predictors yield effective-sample-size gains of about 7-12 percent
when estimating the mean, while LLM gains are smaller but positive.
