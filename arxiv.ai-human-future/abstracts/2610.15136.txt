Title: Trust Hinders Bidirectional Human-AI Alignment
Authors: Michaela Benk, Kacper Sokol
ID: 2610.15136
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15136

Abstract:
Trust calibration remains the dominant framing of AI-assisted decision making:
the goal is to close the gap between perceived and actual trustworthiness of AI
systems so that users rely on them appropriately. We argue that this approach,
while useful for decision problems that are well-defined and clearly scoped,
overly restricts what it means to make a decision with AI support. To overcome
this limitation, we develop three dimensions of AI-assisted decision making.
First, trusting AI is only one of several possible strategies for managing
uncertainty. Second, trust calibration is often misapplied when AI models large-
world decision problems, i.e., ill-defined situations where goals and states are
only partially known or unknowable. Third, in such settings human-AI alignment
requires addressing higher-order decision processes. We conclude by reflecting
on the implications of our observations on the design of process-oriented human-
AI coupled systems that jointly identify and negotiate uncertainty throughout
the decision-making task.
