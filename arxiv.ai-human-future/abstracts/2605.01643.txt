Title: AI Alignment via Incentives and Correction
Authors: Rohit Agarwal, Joshua Lin, Mark Braverman, Elad Hazan
ID: 2605.01643
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01643

Abstract:
We study AI alignment through the lens of law-and-economics models of deterrence
and enforcement, which treats misconduct as a rational response to incentivizes,
where actors weigh the expected gain from aligning against the expected loss
from being caught. We argue that the same logic arises naturally in foundational
language models: an agent trained by RLHF can be modeled as deriving intrinsic
reward from producing a persuasive yet incorrect response, while an auditor can
be viewed as balancing monitoring (which incurs some cost) against identifying
solver misalignment. Therefore, alignment can be viewed as a mechanism design
problem, where stronger penalties can deter solver misbehavior but also reduce
auditing pressure. Under this framework, we can characterize misalignment as an
adversarial post-training problem. While standard post-training optimizes
against a stationary reward signal, our mechanism constantly adapts to mitigate
reward hacking against a fixed monitor. We formalize this interaction as
follows: a principal (e.g., a human) defines a value function over joint
alignment outcomes, and a meta-optimizer adaptively chooses granular rewards for
the solver and auditor to optimize the principal's value. In particular, we
propose a bandit-based meta-optimizer for searching over a discrete set of
reward profiles using noisy interaction feedback, and we show from experimental
results on Qwen2.5-7B that this mechanism can reduce hallucinations on
programming benchmarks by as much as 52\%.
