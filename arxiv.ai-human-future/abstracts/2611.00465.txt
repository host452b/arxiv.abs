Title: The Promises and Limitations of Evaluation-Based AI Governance: Evidence from Expert Interviews
Authors: Lily Stelling, Jacob Charnock, Kevin L. Wei, David Krueger
ID: 2611.00465
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00465

Abstract:
We conducted 37 semi-structured expert interviews to examine how frontier AI
evaluations reduce societal-level AI risks. Evaluations have become central to
AI risk governance, despite their shortcomings being widely critiqued. Still,
the case for how they reduce risks in practice remains largely implicit. Drawing
on expert accounts, we reconstruct this case by identifying the mechanisms
through which evaluations are expected to reduce risk, the assumptions
underpinning those mechanisms, and the challenges those assumptions face. Our
findings suggest that `evaluation-based governance' could contain foundational
gaps which undermine risk-reduction strategies that depend on evaluations. Using
thematic analysis, we identified three key pathways through which evaluations
could reduce risk: by (i) making societal-level risks concrete to decision-
makers and the public (raised in 18 out of 37 interviews), (ii) serving as a
basis for governance commitments and regulation (18), and (iii) incentivizing a
better risk culture within AI companies (16). However, these pathways often
depended on shared assumptions that participants themselves doubted. The main
challenges included, in order of frequency: (i) diminishing informativeness of
evaluation results as model capabilities improve (21), (ii) measurement
limitations that may be inherent to the evaluation paradigm (18), (iii) a lack
of guarantees that evaluation results will be acted upon by companies or
governments (16), and (iv) the degree of control and discretion AI companies
retain over evaluation design, disclosure, and response (12). Participants also
noted that evaluation-based governance could prove counterproductive, by
functioning as a permission structure for developers' continued capability
improvement (4). We finish by discussing the implications of our findings for
evaluation-based governance, and what would improve its prospects.
