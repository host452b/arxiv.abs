Title: The Ethics of Prompt Engineering
Authors: Federico Germani, Giovanni Spitale
ID: 2610.23082
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23082

Abstract:
Prompt engineering is commonly understood as a technical practice aimed at
eliciting useful, accurate, and appropriately structured outputs from large
language models (LLMs). Yet prompts do more than optimize model performance:
they shape what information is elicited, which assumptions are introduced, and
which perspectives are privileged. In this paper, we argue that prompting should
therefore be understood as an epistemic and normative act. We distinguish
between two conceptions of prompting: knowledge generation, in which the model
is implicitly treated as an epistemic authority, and knowledge injection, in
which users provide relevant knowledge, evidence, and context that the model can
organize, articulate, and extend. We contend that the latter provides a more
appropriate model for responsible LLM use, since prompting cannot substitute for
subject-matter expertise or the critical evaluation of model outputs. At the
same time, prompts inevitably inject values, intentions, and assumptions into
the generation process. Building on these premises, we propose five principles
for responsible prompting: epistemic humility, explicit engagement with
uncertainty and epistemic underdetermination, source sensitivity, avoidance of
deceptive framing, and recognition of model limitations and appropriate task
allocation. We further argue that responsibility for AI-generated outputs is
distributed across users, developers, institutions, and societies, and that some
applications cannot be rendered ethical through better prompting alone.
