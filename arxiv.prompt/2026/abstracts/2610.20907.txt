Title: From Detection to Decision: Calibration and Deferral for Prompt-Injection Detection
Authors: Qianlong Lan, Anuj Kaul, Vinothini Pandurangan, Indranil Sanyal
ID: 2610.20907
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20907

Abstract:
Prompt-injection defenses are commonly evaluated as binary detectors, although
deployed LLM applications must make operational decisions about whether to
allow, block, or escalate potentially malicious inputs. Recent probabilistic
decision models aim to support such decisions through calibrated confidence
estimates, but their reliability under distribution shift remains uncertain. We
evaluate a native probabilistic decision model (Jev), two Llama Prompt Guard 2
classifiers (22M and 86M), and an LLM-based judge on 47,611 held-out examples
spanning direct and indirect prompt injections, hard-benign inputs, obfuscation,
and distribution shift. Using separate development and calibration splits, we
assess detection performance, probability calibration, and allow-block-defer
policies under a frozen evaluation protocol. The LLM judge achieves the highest
overall discrimination (AUROC 0.897), followed by Jev (0.871), Prompt Guard 2
86M (0.757), and Prompt Guard 2 22M (0.714). However, Jev's native probabilities
do not consistently outperform post-hoc calibrated classifier scores, and
temperature scaling improves calibration without improving discrimination. At an
empirical false-negative-rate target of 1%, all evaluated gate-reviewer systems
require more than 91.8% escalation and incur substantial false-positive rates.
These findings demonstrate that probabilistic outputs alone do not establish
reliable security decisions under distribution shift. Effective LLM security
systems must jointly evaluate detection, calibration, reviewer behavior, and
operational decision policies.
