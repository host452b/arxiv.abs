Title: Monitorability Is Trainable: Post-Training for Chain-of-Thought Monitorability
Authors: Usman Anwar, Tim Bakker, Christos Louizos, David Krueger
ID: 2610.29734
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29734

Abstract:
Chain-of-thought (CoT) monitoring is one of the few practical tools available
for overseeing capable AI systems, but it is fragile: a model's reasoning is
only useful to a monitor if it remains coupled to what the model actually does.
This coupling can be degraded during training through, for example, CoT
compression, optimization against a monitor, and out-of-context generalization
from synthetic documents. In this work, we propose an unsupervised post-training
objective based on maximizing mutual information between CoT and the output for
improving CoT monitorability. Across three model organisms with degraded CoT, it
recovers most or all of the lost monitorability. Applied to undegraded policies,
it further improves judged monitorability, improves prediction of unsafe
behavior by CoT monitors and activation probes, and increases the prevalence of
consequential reasoning. These results suggest that monitorability should be
treated as a trainable attribute of the policy. A dedicated monitorability post-
training stage can extend current practices that protect CoT from direct
optimization pressure.
