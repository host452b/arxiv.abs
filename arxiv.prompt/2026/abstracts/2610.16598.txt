Title: Detecting Chain-of-Thought Hijacking in Backdoored Multimodal Large Reasoning Models
Authors: Zhifang Zhang, Qi Wei, Weitong Chen, Olaf Maennel, Wei Emma Zhang, zuomin ren, Lei Feng, Miao Xu
ID: 2610.16598
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16598

Abstract:
Chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning enables multimodal large reasoning models
(MLRMs) to solve complex visual problems, but also exposes them to CoT
hijacking: an adversary implants a backdoor through poisoned fine-tuning so that
a visual trigger induces a hijacking turn, redirecting an otherwise faithful
reasoning chain to an attacker-chosen answer. However, since the hijack acts
within the reasoning chain, existing multimodal backdoor defenses designed for
MLLMs that answer directly struggle to detect it. Moreover, inspecting the chain
itself does not reliably expose the hijack, since the hijacking turn can
resemble honest self-correction in form. In this paper, we find that honest
reasoning tends to revise its answer at high-entropy "aha moments", whereas a
hijack can overturn a confident answer through a near-deterministic turn. To
quantify this contrast, we formalize this phenomenon as belief inertia, the
cumulative next-token entropy of a reasoning span minus the answer-belief
revision it induces, with markedly lower values for hijacked spans than for
honest reasoning spans. Since hijacking tokens are also image-dependent, we
further improve detection by restricting the measure to image-dependent spans,
yielding visual belief inertia (VBI). Building on this, we propose InertiaGuard,
a training-free, test-time detector that flags a trace when its VBI falls below
a threshold calibrated on a small set of clean traces. Across various attacks
and benchmarks, InertiaGuard achieves higher detection performance than existing
baselines, while generalizing to different model architectures and trigger
types.
