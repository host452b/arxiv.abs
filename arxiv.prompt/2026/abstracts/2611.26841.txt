Title: Weight-Space Programming Writes In-Context Learning and Matches Fine-Tuning in Language Models
Authors: Konstantinos Skianis, Aristidis Likas
ID: 2611.26841
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26841

Abstract:
Weight-space programming is a frontier language model's recently reported
ability to read another network's weights and edit them without gradient
descent. We investigate how examples can provide a weight update so that a
weaker language model performs the task with nothing in the prompt, and study
what the updates are. Our experiments cover six tasks, four frontier editors and
three families of weaker language models, without the editors being told
anything about in-context learning (ICL). In most runs, the editors measure the
ICL task vector, the shift the examples cause in the residual stream at the
final prompt token, and write it into the weights, at its sparsest into near-
silent MLP units keyed to the prompt format. Results reach the accuracy of ICL
and fine-tuning on the same examples, LoRA included, usually leaving perplexity
untouched, with worse performance under changes of wording. We also qualify the
comparison by counting unfinished runs, observing that one editor falls below
fine-tuning; held only to the seen examples, without task pairs of their own,
only the strongest editor has similar performance. Given a task and unlabelled
inputs, that editor writes its own examples and does nearly as well on unseen
items as with labels.
