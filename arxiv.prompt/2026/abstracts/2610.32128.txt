Title: From Noisy Retrieval to Decision Evidence: Silver Evidence Demonstrations for In-Context Learning
Authors: Masoud Monajatipoor, Fazlolah Mohaghegh, Hamid Reza Hassanzadeh, Akash Uttamrao Bhoite, Robert E. Tillman
ID: 2610.32128
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32128

Abstract:
RAG and retrieval-augmented few-shot ICL can struggle when decision-relevant
evidence is buried in long, noisy context. Retrieved passages and demonstrations
may contain redundant or distracting information. Although expert-labeled
development examples may identify the text supporting each decision, these
annotations are typically used only as answer-side rationales rather than as
supervision for filtering noisy retrieval. We propose silver-evidence
demonstrations, which replace raw retrieved passages in ICL examples with
generated, target-conditioned evidence summaries. For each labeled development
example, an LLM generates silver evidence without access to the expert label or
gold evidence; this evidence is then paired with the expert label and gold
evidence where available. At test time, a single structured call generates
silver evidence from retrieved passages and then predicts the final label and
decision evidence. Across EI 2.0, SciFact, synthetic policy retrieval, and
HICRIC/IMR data, our full method improves over equally sized gold-only ICL
(label-only for policy) on three of four datasets and improves evidence F1 on
all annotated datasets. It also achieves higher mean label accuracy than
InstructRAG-style ICL on all four datasets. These results suggest that replacing
demonstration-side retrieved passages with compact, target-conditioned silver
evidence is particularly useful for decisions based on long and noisy text,
where it reduces distracting context while preserving decision-relevant signals
and expert supervision.
