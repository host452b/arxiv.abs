Title: How Many Examples, and Which Ones? A Study of In-Context Learning for Natural Language Inference
Authors: Georgos Dikaios, Isidoros Perikos, Ioannis Hatzilygeroudis
ID: 2611.09936
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09936

Abstract:
Natural language inference (NLI) is a fundamental task of natural language
processing and crucial for evaluating the strengths and weaknesses of AI
methods. Research has mostly focused on encoder or encoder-decoder
architectures. However, the field of decoder-only Large Language Models (LLMs)
remains underexplored. While prior work has mainly investigated complex sampling
and prompting techniques, the effectiveness of a minimalistic prompting approach
is yet to be examined in decoder-only LLMs. In this work, we examine and provide
a comprehensive benchmark of LLMs (Llama, Mistral, Gemma, and Phi 4) in this
domain, across the MNLI, QNLI and SciTail datasets. The performance of these
models was evaluated using zero-shot and few-shot prompting strategies with
simple and direct prompts. The results of the article indicate that LLMs
generally perform exceptionally well when they are provided with a simple,
straightforward prompt, challenging the notion that complex prompting techniques
are always required for NLI tasks. Additionally, we find that the choice of
demonstration label in one-shot prompting significantly affects performance for
most models, with entailment-primed examples performing best. Our findings also
show that the use of a few representative examples, in the case of few-shot,
produced performance comparable or superior to prompts with higher example
counts, although the efficacy of the prompting techniques is model and dataset-
dependent. Moreover, our simple prompting approach consistently matched or
outperformed more elaborate methods such as ICS and MCQ-based prompting.
