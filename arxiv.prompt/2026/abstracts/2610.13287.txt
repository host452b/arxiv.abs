Title: Do Prompt Optimizers Break Requirements They Are Not Scored On? A Preregistered Study
Authors: Sangdo Han, Moonsoo Kim
ID: 2610.13287
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13287

Abstract:
Prompt optimizers rewrite a prompt to raise one target metric, but prompts also
state requirements the metric does not score, such as an output format,
citations, or abstention. In a preregistered study, we run GEPA, MIPROv2, and
TextGrad on HotpotQA, SQuAD 2.0, and IFEval and count per-item violations of
unscored requirements with deterministic checks. By the preregistered criterion,
all three optimizers produce regressions, substantially on HotpotQA, where net
regression in citation validity reaches 10.7 to 41.6 points while exact match
rises by 21 to 34 points. A post-hoc decomposition changes this reading. The
optimized prompts elicit reasoning before the required JSON object, and under
the 200-token output cap used throughout, 13.9% to 47.0% of responses end before
any answer appears. Among parseable outputs, net regression is within 3 points
of zero. With a 1,024-token cap, extraction failures disappear, content
regression stays below 1 point, and exact match rises further. Two simple gates
fail the preregistered criterion. The side effect is narrower than hypothesized:
optimized prompts are more accurate and break an explicit output contract, and a
lenient target metric does not show the cost.
