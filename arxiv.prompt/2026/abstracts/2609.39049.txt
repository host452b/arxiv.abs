Title: Structure vs. Chain-of-Thought: Evaluating LLM Criteria Extraction for Depression Severity
Authors: Xinkai Chen
ID: 2609.39049
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39049

Abstract:
A large language model (LLM) can rate depression severity directly from a social
media post, or mark which clinical criteria the post shows and let a simple rule
turn the count into a label. The latter is easier to audit because a clinician
can check each marked criterion. We compare these approaches on two Reddit
corpora using three LLMs (from 9B to frontier scale) and two questionnaires
(PHQ-9, BDI-II), and measure agreement with quadratic weighted kappa. For the
two frontier models, criteria extraction scores above chain-of-thought on one
corpus only when its decision thresholds are fitted on labeled data. Neither
model's gain is significant, with or without recalibrating chain-of-thought on
the same labels. With thresholds fixed a priori from PHQ-9's criteria,
extraction shows no gain on either corpus, even where models mark over two
criteria per post. The 9B model behaves differently on a corpus from depression
communities. It labels most posts as severe, whether prompted directly or with
chain-of-thought, while the a priori rule, which uses no labels, beats both.
After chain-of-thought is recalibrated on the same labels, no significant gap
remains, consistent with a calibration effect. Yet higher ordinal agreement does
not ensure better detection of severe cases. PHQ-9 criteria extraction misses
most severe posts, and moving from direct prompting to chain-of-thought and then
to extraction increases misses in nearly all comparisons. On one corpus, a
relabeled stress dataset, a model using that dataset's own features, some
computed from the post text, is not significantly different from frontier
criteria extraction under the a priori rule.
