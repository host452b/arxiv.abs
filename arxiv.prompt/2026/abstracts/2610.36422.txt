Title: What Victims Take Away: Attack Reuse and Defense Revision after Prompt-Injection Attacks
Authors: Zihan Chen, Lei Nico Zheng, Di Zhu
ID: 2610.36422
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36422

Abstract:
Attack logs and red-team reports show targets the attacks used against them, to
help them repair their defenses. Prompt injections against large language models
(LLMs) are plain text, so such records also hand targets a working copy. Drawing
on social learning theory and work on the victim-offender overlap, we study what
targets do with that copy in Tensor Trust, an online game whose players defended
LLM-guarded accounts, attacked one another's, and could read every attack they
received. Using all 563,349 attack records, we compare each received attack with
matched attacks that circulated among similar players at the same moment but
never reached the target. Reuse mainly followed success: within a week, targets
attacked other players with 10.0% of the attacks that had broken their defense,
versus 0.03% of matched attacks (adjusted difference: 8.8-10.0 percentage
points). Near-misses, whose replies began with "access granted" but that scored
as failures, were reused about as rarely as failures: reuse tracked whether an
attack paid off, not how its reply read, consistent with payoff-biased social
learning. Breaches were also followed by defense changes, after which same-
family attacks became 24.5 percentage points less successful against the target,
relative to other defenders. Reuse came mainly from players already attacking,
not newcomers. Previously received texts accounted for 23.2% of the successful
hijacks we analyze. The attack history thus served both repair and reuse; we
derive testable hypotheses for how AI security tools should disclose attacks,
such as describing the technique without the full text.
