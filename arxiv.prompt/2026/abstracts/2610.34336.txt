Title: Improving the Chain-of-Thought Compressibility of Pruned Reasoning Models via Long--Short Contrastive Pruning
Authors: Tao Jing, Chen Kang, Feifei Zhai, Pengyuan Liu, Dong Yu
ID: 2610.34336
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34336

Abstract:
Large reasoning models are costly to serve because they are large and because
they generate long chains of thought (CoT). Weight pruning and CoT compression
address these two costs separately, but they interfere in that pruning adds
redundant reasoning on problems the model still solves and incomplete reasoning
on those it no longer solves, and on harder problems makes the model less
compressible by subsequent length-preference optimization. We trace this to a
blind spot on each side: pruning criteria do not distinguish necessary from
redundant reasoning, and preference-based CoT compression receives no training
signal on problems a pruned model always or never solves. We propose Contrastive
Set-Difference (CSD), which identifies neurons enriched in redundant reasoning
by contrasting their activations on long and short correct solutions of the same
problem and prunes them preferentially, and we extend preference pairs to the
problems a pruned model always or never solves. Pruning 20\% of
DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, the combination uses 8--22\% fewer thinking tokens
on four math benchmarks than pruning with reasoning-aware calibration (RAC)
followed by the same compression, with accuracy within 0.5 points.
