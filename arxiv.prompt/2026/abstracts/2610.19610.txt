Title: CORE: Contrast-and-Reflect for Self-Supervised Prompt Optimization in Anomaly Synthesis
Authors: Haoxiang Rao, Fang Zhao, Zhao Wang, Weijia Li, Chenyang Si, Caifeng Shan
ID: 2610.19610
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19610

Abstract:
Industrial anomaly detection (AD) is bottlenecked by the scarcity of anomalous
samples, making high-fidelity anomaly synthesis crucial for improving downstream
performance. However, existing methods either rely on scarce real anomaly
references or auxiliary data, while lacking complex reasoning and iterative
optimization capabilities for generating realistic defects in open-set
scenarios. To address these challenges, we propose CORE, a cost-efficient and
training-free prompt optimization framework based on Large Multimodal Models
(LMMs), which unifies zero-shot and one-shot anomaly synthesis. CORE first
introduces an Anomaly Context Grounding module to transform sparse user context
into defect-aware generation context, which is subsequently instantiated as
structured anomaly prompts that disentangle normal-region preservation from
anomaly-specific variation, enabling controllable and faithful defect
generation. Furthermore, CORE autonomously refines prompts through a self-
supervised contrast-and-reflect mechanism. This design empowers general-purpose
LMMs to synthesize realistic anomalies under both zero-shot and one-shot
settings without task-specific training. Extensive experiments on MVTec-AD and
VisA demonstrate that CORE synthesizes realistic and diverse anomalies, yielding
superior improvements in downstream AD performance. Furthermore, its model-
agnostic and plug-and-play paradigm enables effortless integration with diverse
LMMs, facilitating adaptation to unseen open-set scenarios.
