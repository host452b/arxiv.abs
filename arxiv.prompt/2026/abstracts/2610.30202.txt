Title: MPIDetector: Detecting Multi-modal Prompt Injection via LLM-based Reasoning
Authors: Yiheng Li, Jingyue Li
ID: 2610.30202
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30202

Abstract:
With the rapid integration of Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) into
real-world applications, Prompt Injection (PI) has emerged as a critical
security threat. Attackers can conceal malicious instructions within textual
narratives or visual inputs to bypass safety alignment. Existing approaches,
such as signature- and perplexity-based methods, rely heavily on pattern
matching, making them insufficient to detect sophisticated, cross-modal PI
payloads. In this paper, we propose a novel detection framework, called
MPIDetector, that leverages LLMs' intrinsic reasoning capabilities via few-shot
In-Context Learning (ICL). Instead of fine-tuning, we provide the model with
specific prompt templates and representative attack examples, enabling it to
scrutinize the semantic intent of input across modalities. We constructed a
comprehensive evaluation benchmark comprising eight textual and seven visual
malicious categories, supplemented with benign datasets to rigorously test for
false positives. Our evaluation across multiple leading LLMs, including
Gemini-2.0-Flash, Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct, and Doubao-seed-1.6-flash, demonstrates
the efficacy and generalizability of this approach. The proposed method achieves
up to 90.91% accuracy and 100% recall on textual tasks. In the visual domain, it
achieves up to 99.28% accuracy while maintaining 100% precision across all
evaluated models. Crucially, when evaluated against 500 highly obfuscated
environmental web-agent injections (WebInject), MPIDetector achieved a detection
rate up to 99.80%. Comparisons with two baseline approaches for detecting
textual PI payloads, along with two additional methods for visual input
detection, demonstrate MPIDetector's superior accuracy. These results show that
LLM-driven reasoning provides a robust, cross-modal defense against advanced
prompt injections.
