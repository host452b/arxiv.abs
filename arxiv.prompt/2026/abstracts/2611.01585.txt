Title: StateGate: A Behavioral-Fidelity Prompt Optimization Method for Long-Chain LLM Agents
Authors: Shicheng Huang, Cai Fu, Jianqiang Lyu, Yifan Zhang, Xianmao Ji
ID: 2611.01585
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01585

Abstract:
Currently, long-chain LLM agents commonly suffer from behavioral degradation in
complex tasks, where prompts are written in full but steps are not executed in
full. To address this issue, existing approaches face three major challenges.
First, constraints are strongly bound to their textual carriers; once natural-
language constraint sentences are removed, their binding force vanishes. Second,
the execution process lacks independent verification, as the evidence for
judgment is often supplied by the executing party itself, leading to the failure
of self-referential validation. Third, existing remediation schemes are
difficult to generalize, and hard-coded mechanisms cannot be transferred across
tasks. In this paper, we propose StateGate, a novel prompt-optimization scheme
that transforms semantic constraints into symbolic gates enforced by an external
deterministic runtime. By decoupling the content of a constraint from its
carrier of efficacy, StateGate achieves behavioral fidelity in long-chain tasks
without losing the binding force of the prompt. Specifically, we first propose
four independent compression strategies (deduplication, generalization,
externalization, and text-to-gating) together with their corresponding decision
chains, enabling principled prompt symbolization. Second, we design a three-
component architecture consisting of a state file, a validator, and an
orchestrator, which thoroughly separates judgment authority from execution
authority. Third, we formally prove that the compliance of symbolic constraints
is independent of context size. Finally, we validate the framework on a
vulnerability-discovery task in the security domain; the results show that
StateGate achieves 100% execution closure and accurate vulnerability detection.
