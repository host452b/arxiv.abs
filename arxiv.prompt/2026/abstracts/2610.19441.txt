Title: Integrating Simulation and Chain-of-thought Reasoning in Multimodal-Language Models For Physical Reasoning
Authors: YingQiao Wang, Eric Bigelow, Tomer Ullman, Yujin Tang, Sebastian Risi
ID: 2610.19441
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19441

Abstract:
Multimodal language models often need to reason about the physical dynamics of
the world in order to answer questions appropriately. Various methods have
emerged for solving physical reasoning tasks, and in particular, two leading
approaches are Chain-of-Thought reasoning and physics-grounded simulation.
However, relatively little is known about which particular problems can be
solved by each approach. Some tasks may benefit from computationally expensive
generative video simulations, whereas others may be solved equally well (or even
better) with language-based reasoning. Our work addresses this by systematically
comparing simulation and language reasoning across a variety of multimodal tasks
involving physical reasoning, such as answering questions about fluid dynamics
and elasticity. We draw a parallel with this to resource-rational theories of
human cognition, which propose that people optimally trade off simulation and
reasoning depending on task demands. We present a novel cognitively-inspired
hybrid model which optimally trades off simulation and reasoning to improve
model performance and computational efficiency. Our model is able to decide the
appropriate method to solve a physical reasoning problem at test time, and finds
an optimal balance between accuracy and cost. Further, our model can be trained
on a smaller vision-language model that is only 4B parameters and deployed to
improve performance on much bigger base models with $>$3T parameters like Gemini
3 Pro.
