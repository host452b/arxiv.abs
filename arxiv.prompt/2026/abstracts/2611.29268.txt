Title: Benchmarking Web Agent Defenses Against Adaptive Visual Prompt Injection
Authors: Haichuan Zhang, Zhiyuan Zhong, Shih-Chieh Dai, Xinyu Sun, Guanhong Tao
ID: 2611.29268
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29268

Abstract:
Multimodal web agents automate website interactions but remain vulnerable to
visual prompt injection (VPI), where malicious webpage content manipulates their
visual processing and induces unintended actions. Although several defenses
focus on detecting injected instructions in screenshots, malicious destinations
such as phishing URLs embedded in HTML can remain hidden yet accessible through
agent interactions. Existing evaluations have also not sufficiently examined
adaptive attackers who know the defense mechanisms. To address these
limitations, we introduce AdaptiveVPI, the first benchmark for systematically
evaluating adaptive VPI threats in web agents. Specifically, we present a
dataset of 360 webpage instances spanning 6 distinct hijack strategies across 10
real-world websites, with malicious destinations not visible in screenshots. We
design an adaptive attack framework that iteratively refines malicious
instructions based on feedback from the defense mechanism being evaluated. We
also introduce three metrics to measure the extent of agent hijacking at three
different levels. Across 10 defense configurations for computer-use agents and
11 defense configurations for browser-use agents, our adaptive attacks achieve
average success rates of 91.87% and 91.99%, respectively. These results reveal a
gap between current VPI defenses and realistic threats and highlight the need
for defenses designed with adaptive attackers in mind.
