Title: Controllable Chains of Thought Can Reveal Less, Even Without Evasion Training
Authors: Zheng-Xin Yong, Benjamin Arnav, Miles Wang, Tomek Korbak, Olivia Watkins
ID: 2611.21799
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21799

Abstract:
Chain-of-thought (CoT) monitoring is a promising tool for detecting hidden
goals, off-task behavior, and other failures in AI agents. One risk is that
models could deliberately shape their reasoning to make it opaque to monitors.
We stress-test this risk through persona-control training: supervised fine-
tuning on mathematical reasoning traces rewritten in the voices of diverse
personas. This induces a broader ability to control CoT, even though training
contains no monitor-evasion examples. In covert side-task evaluations, traces in
which a gpt-oss-20b model organism completes the hidden side task contain less
monitorable evidence than the base model's. Averaged across evaluations and CoT
lengths, weighted by side-task-success counts, the CoT monitorability metric
g-mean$^2$ falls from 0.94 for the base model to 0.68 for the model organism
without a hiding instruction, and to 0.22 when explicitly instructed to hide. In
intervention evaluations, explicit hiding often drives monitorability near zero.
The effect extends to a Kimi-K2.6 model organism in agentic environments, though
hidden objectives requiring extended reasoning or longer trajectories remain
more monitorable than simple single-step side tasks. We further discover that
targeted transparency prompts can substantially recover monitorability,
sometimes surpassing the base model. These model organisms provide a testbed for
studying monitorability failures and potential mitigations. Our code can be
found on https://github.com/yongzx/cot-control-model-organism.
