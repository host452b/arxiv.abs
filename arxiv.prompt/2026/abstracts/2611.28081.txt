Title: TaskVICL: Task-Conditioned Decoder States for Visual In-Context Learning
Authors: Chenyang Wang, Jiamo Hu, Chengrui Wu, Lingyue Chen, Jianguo Chen, Xun Xu, Xulei Yang
ID: 2611.28081
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28081

Abstract:
The usefulness of an in-context demonstration for a vision-language model (VLM)
depends on both the query image and the task posed about it. Common retrievers
rank examples by image similarity and ignore the task, while task-aware
alternatives require answer generation, external encoders or retriever training.
We ask whether the frozen target VLM's own decoder states, read before it
answers, can select demonstrations directly. TaskVICL represents each image and
task by a task-conditioned processing signature: the hidden states at the final
input position across all decoder layers. Its training-free variant, TaskVICL-
Raw, averages cosine similarities between corresponding layer states and passes
the retrieved examples with their reference answers to the same VLM. Across two
VLMs and eight classification and visual question answering datasets, Raw
achieves the highest average accuracy among training-free retrievers, exceeding
the strongest baseline by 3.30 points on Qwen3-VL and 1.41 on InternVL3.5, with
gains carrying over to Gemma-3-12B. When the same NICO++ image is asked about
its object or its context, Raw retrieves largely different demonstrations for
the two questions and outperforms an image retriever given task-matched
demonstrations. A lightweight shared projection, TaskVICL-Proj, adds further
gains on classification. Controls show that task text at extraction drives most
of the gains, and attention analysis describes how it shifts attention toward
the classified object. These results support the target VLM's internal states as
a practical basis for task-aware demonstration retrieval.
