Title: MetaPI: Constructing Prompt Injection Benchmarks from LLM Agent Benchmark
Authors: Peiran Wang, Chong Xiang, Wenjie Qu, Ying Li, Jiaheng Zhang, G. Edward Suh, Yuan Tian
ID: 2611.10026
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10026

Abstract:
Large language model (LLM) agents increasingly perform complex tasks using high-
privilege tools, making indirect prompt injection (IPI) a critical security
risk. However, IPI benchmarks lag behind their rapidly evolving utility
counterparts, only offering limited task and environment coverage and relying on
narrow, static attack scenarios. In this paper, we propose meta-benchmarking for
IPI: transforming existing utility benchmarks into end-to-end IPI evaluation
suites that reuse their realistic tasks, environments, and infrastructure. We
present MetaPI, an extensible toolkit that automatically identifies plausible
injection points, generates realistic attack objectives, optimizes adaptive
payloads, and validates attacks in the real environment. Its modular interfaces
support heterogeneous environments, agents, defenses, and attack algorithms,
allowing new components to be integrated without redesigning the evaluation
pipeline. We apply MetaPI to AppWorld, VitaBench, and Terminal-Bench and
evaluate representative agents and defenses across multiple backbone LLMs. Our
results show that no evaluated defense simultaneously achieves strong utility
and strong security in realistic, adaptive agentic IPI settings. MetaPI thus
enables scalable and consistent security evaluation across diverse and evolving
agent ecosystems
