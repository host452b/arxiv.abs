Title: On Task, Off Track: Benchmarking LLM and Agent Robustness to Near-Target Prompt Injection
Authors: Yuyang Gong, Zihao Wang, Jiawei Liu, XiaoFeng Wang
ID: 2610.32531
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32531

Abstract:
Prompt injection benchmarks assess whether large language models (LLMs) and
agents resist malicious instructions embedded in external content. However, a
system could perform well by rejecting attacks that obviously abandon the user's
request, while still accepting changes that retain most of a legitimate answer
but make it incorrect or harmful. Testing more instructions for unrelated
outcomes would not necessarily reveal this weakness. We call attacks seeking
responses designed to resemble legitimate answers while introducing incorrect or
harmful changes near-target prompt injection. We introduce NTBench, the Near-
Target Benchmark, which builds on legitimate tasks from existing datasets. For
each task, a legitimate reference outcome guides the generation of target--
instruction pairs, with separate tracks targeting incorrect and harmful outcomes
in responses or agent tool invocations. Harmful targets can also accompany
otherwise correct task completion. We combine LLM-based checks with human
validation of the intended deviations, then assess whether injected instructions
induce them using response judgments or tool-call checks, excluding deviations
already observed without injection. We evaluate seven LLMs on ten datasets for
language tasks and two agent benchmarks. Under a common budget of three attack
executions per sample, both tracks achieve higher overall attack success rates
than the fixed-target baseline for every evaluated model in each setting. On
language tasks, the increases range from 9.3 to 35.8 percentage points for
incorrect targets and 1.7 to 17.3 percentage points for harmful targets. These
results show that resistance to the tested fixed-target attacks does not imply
resistance to related incorrect or harmful outcomes, and that reference-guided
targets broaden the failures revealed by robustness evaluation.
