Title: Conartist: Adaptive Agentic Red-Teaming of Computer-Using Agents against Multi-Step Prompt Injection
Authors: Jiaqi Rong, Yunchen Lu, Lihai Nie, Yan Jia, Tong Li, Zheli Liu
ID: 2611.29977
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29977

Abstract:
Harnesses secure Computer-Using Agents by enforcing security constraints and
defending against prompt injection attacks. However, we find that two core
security mechanisms of agent harnesses remain vulnerable. First, consistency
checking prevents agents from deviating from legitimate user objectives, while
attackers can bypass it by embedding malicious actions that appear logically
consistent with legitimate task execution. Second, risk auditing proactively
examines high-risk operations. Yet, the auditing process itself would introduce
additional exploitable actions that violate security boundaries. To evaluate
above defects we found, we propose Conartist, a cascaded adaptive prompt
injection framework comprising two progressive stages. First, Conartist employs
a goal-pollution strategy to establish deceptive causal dependencies between
legitimate user objectives and malicious operations, progressively embedding
attack steps into legitimate task workflows. However, these task-coupled attacks
remain susceptible to risk auditing. To address this limitation, Conartist
further analyzes security-auditing feedback from controlled attack exploration
to identify exploitable auditing patterns. Conartist then adaptively refines its
deception strategies to either avoid triggering risk auditing or induce
additional high-risk operations. Extensive experiments across five backbone
models demonstrate the effectiveness of Conartist, achieving attack success
rates of 32.12% on 576-instance tool use benchmark and 66.67% in the Reddit GUI
benchmark, outperforming the SOTA attack by 14.41 and 29.17, respectively. By
analyzing one of the most popular Agent, Openclaw, we furthermore concluded
seven realistic exploitable attack patterns, which reveals that security
mechanisms designed to prevent malicious actions can themselves be manipulated
to facilitate attacks.
