Title: Deletion Is Not Substitution: Component Attribution in a Layered Prompt-Injection Gateway for LLM Tool Calls
Authors: İpek Abasıkeleş Turgut, Edip Gümüş
ID: 2610.33198
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33198

Abstract:
Prompt-injection defenses for LLM agents are commonly built as cascades of
increasingly costly stages, and ablation studies evaluate their components by
switching each stage off and counting the detections lost. In a cascade,
however, stages are not independent: a stage's contribution depends on what is
routed to it and how its verdicts are enforced, so deletion-based ablation can
mislead. We test this on BARBICAN, a four-tier, fail-closed gateway for Model
Context Protocol tool calls, developed in our group, that routes borderline
inputs to a neural arbitrator. After reproducing its recorded evaluation
exactly, we apply deletion, constant substitution, repair and transplant to its
arbitration stage. The findings rest on over thirty instrumented runs over
25,000 prompts, two platforms, an independent check set and six versions from
the system's history. Depending on how "switching off" is defined, deletion
credits the stage with contributions that differ by more than an order of
magnitude, whereas substitution shows that its verdicts can affect only a few
inputs, all attacks. Neither repairing the stage nor replacing it with a much
stronger classifier changed the system appreciably: its influence is set by its
position, not its quality. An observation that bounds the decisions a routed
stage can change explains this, and a cascade of public classifiers reproduces
it whenever routing concentrates attacks. Treating constant verdicts as mutants,
we derive a continuous-integration gate that detects a stage whose verdicts
cannot reach the output and, in light of these results, offer a three-point
roadmap for ablation studies.
