Title: In-Context Learning Can Help Vision-Language Models Overcome Training Priors
Authors: Kun Wang, Xindi Wu, Sanghyuk Chun, Olga Russakovsky, Esin Tureci
ID: 2610.12942
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12942

Abstract:
Vision-language models (VLMs) learn strong statistical regularities during
training, which can make them fail to properly respond to visual evidence when
input images violate those regularities. It is challenging to disambiguate these
prior-induced errors from errors caused by the model missing the underlying
visual capabilities. In this work, we use controlled visual in-context learning
to encourage the model to focus on the visual evidence, and demonstrate that
this alone is frequently sufficient to correct the errors -- suggesting that the
models in fact do possess the relevant visual capabilities. Specifically, we
show that across five vision-centric tasks, demonstrations yield large gains on
prior-conflicting counterfactual examples ($+27.2\%$ for Qwen3-VL 235B and
$+15.1\%$ for Qwen3-VL 32B) while leaving accuracy on prior-aligned examples
nearly unchanged. These findings generalize across five VLM families with gains
from $+9.7\%$ to $+30.3\%$. We analyze visual representations and find that
demonstrations indeed redirect attention toward grounding cues while reducing
reliance on cues that support the learned prior. Finally, we find that in-
context learning is limited when the underlying visual capability is weak or
absent. Together, these results show that current VLMs possess visual grounding
capabilities that standard evaluations may overlook, underscoring the need for
controlled evaluation design in diagnosing and improving model capability.
