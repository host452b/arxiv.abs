Title: Explore More, Validate Less: Efficient Reflective Prompt Optimization
Authors: Xiaofan Zhou, Lu Cheng
ID: 2610.27367
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27367

Abstract:
Reflective prompt optimization improves instructions using task feedback, but
coupling candidate generation with full-validation-based selection can make
search costly and sequential. We propose a two-stage framework that decouples
reflective candidate-pool construction from validation allocation. During
construction, sampled parents first answer training questions, and groups of
their saved responses guide parallel reflection and revision. Each child is
screened on its group's questions against the corresponding parent scores.
Improving, nonduplicate prompts enter the pool and can become parents without
full validation. After construction, an exploration policy selects candidates
from the frozen pool under a fixed full-validation budget, and the prompt with
the highest measured validation score is returned. This separation allows
candidate-pool size and validation budget to be set independently. We evaluate
random selection and a multilayer perceptron with similarity-based exploration
(MLP + similarity) on five benchmarks using Qwen3.5-9B and
Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct. With 100 retained candidates and 20 full validations, our
framework improves over the initial solver by up to 11.22 percentage points. MLP
+ similarity improves over the initial solver in all ten comparisons, although
neither selection policy consistently outperforms the other. These results
support separating reflective search from budgeted validation while leaving room
for stronger candidate-selection policies.
