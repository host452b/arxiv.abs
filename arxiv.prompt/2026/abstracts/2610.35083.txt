Title: Ask Before You Answer: A Question-Driven Evidence Reasoning Architecture (QERA) for In-Context Learning
Authors: Jinhu Qi, Minda Hu, Wentao Zhang, Zixing Song, Irwin King
ID: 2610.35083
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35083

Abstract:
AI assistants are increasingly asked to reason over a user's own web traces:
group chats, community threads, activity logs and revision histories. On CL-
bench Life, a benchmark of such real-life context learning, frontier LLMs solve
fewer than one task in five. We find that the request is not the specification:
over 80% of the benchmark's rubrics share less than 60% of their content words
with the final request, as for a rule set two turns earlier or every record that
meets the user's criterion, and LLMs answering directly pass only 67% of these
implicit rubrics against 81% of the others. We propose QERA, a Question-driven
Evidence Reasoning Architecture that asks before it answers. QERA first turns
the request into a requirement checklist and typed information needs, filtered
by code for faithfulness to the user's words; it then answers from the whole
conversation, asks the same questions again with quotes verified by code, and
applies a guarded local repair. It needs no training or rubrics and makes about
four LLM calls per task. On all 405 tasks with four frontier LLMs, QERA attains
the highest solving rate on every LLM (21.0% on average, against 18.2% for the
strongest of three inference-time baselines and 14.6% for direct answering) and
leads every baseline significantly when pooled across LLMs, while using fewer
tokens than PSCI and 4-5% of the tokens of Ctx2Skill. Ablations on GPT-6-Luna
show that the gain is settled before the answer, by a fixed answer protocol
(+3.8 rubric points) and the request-specific questions (+1.6), and that it is
concentrated on implicit rubrics, with checking afterwards refining mainly
numeric requirements.
