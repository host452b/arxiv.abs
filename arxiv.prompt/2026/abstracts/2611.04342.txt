Title: Beyond Fine-Tuning: Evaluating Prompt Engineering and Prompt Tuning for Roman Urdu Hate Speech Detection
Authors: Toneema Zubair, Muhammad Junaid Asif, Faisal Kamiran, Hafiz Hassan Saeed, Rana Fayyaz Ahmad
ID: 2611.04342
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04342

Abstract:
Low Resource Languages (LRLs) pose significant challenges for hate speech
detection because of a scarcity of readily available annotated data, language
variation, non-standard spelling, and context-dependent ambiguity. Beyond the
problem of informal conventions of writing in Roman Urdu, there is also the
issue of code-mixing. After working on the previous project about zero-shot
learning and parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT), this research
systematically compares prompt engineering and prompt tuning for Roman Urdu hate
speech detection. To study the effect of in-context examples, we will compare
the zero-shot and few-shot prompting approaches. To understand the advantage of
structured prompting and learnable prompt representations, we will conduct
studies of manual vs. mixed prompt-based adaptation strategies. Experimentations
are carried out on the PURUT dataset (~72,771 social media comments) with
GPT-3.5, LLaMA-3, Mistral-7B, and multilingual BERT in several settings.
Experimental results show that few-shot prompting always outperforms zero-shot
prompting, with the Mistral-7B model scoring 0.67 F1 compared to 0.56 of the
zero-shot models. Based on this observation, prompt-based adaptation is proposed
to introduce additional improvements, with the proposed mixed prompt
configuration able to yield a F1-score up to 0.87 which further surpasses the
performance of regular manual prompts. Despite the larger size of the model, its
performance trade-off between inference-based prompting, prompt optimization,
and parameter-efficient model adaptation is compared to the previously reported
PEFT-based F1-score, which is ~0.93.
