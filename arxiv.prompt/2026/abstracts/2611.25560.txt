Title: Is a Description Worth a Thousand Demonstrations? Specificity and Reliability of Task Descriptions in In-Context Learning
Authors: Bruce Nguyen, Bei Jiang, Linglong Kong
ID: 2611.25560
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25560

Abstract:
A prompt for in-context learning (ICL) can describe a task in addition to, or
instead of, demonstrating it through examples. Yet how task descriptions work
remains poorly understood theoretically. Through the Bayesian view of ICL, we
model descriptions as informative priors characterized by their specificity and
reliability. We adapt the effective sample size (ESS) from Bayesian statistics,
quantifying a description's predictive value as an equivalent number of
demonstrations. For in-context linear regression, we show that a reliable
description's ESS grows with its specificity and with the task's dimension. A
description that may be irrelevant, however, gains little from its specificity
unless demonstrations are given. In practice, do in-context learners use
descriptions as a Bayes-optimal predictor would? Our experiments show that meta-
trained Transformers come close to the Bayes-optimal ESS when tested on their
training distribution. Pretrained language models, however, make too little use
of highly specific descriptions when answering without reasoning; most of them
closely match the Bayes-optimal predictor only when reasoning is enabled. These
results extend the Bayesian analysis of ICL from demonstrations to descriptions.
