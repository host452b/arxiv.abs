Title: Prompt Injection Through Forensic Evidence: A Taxonomy and Proof of Concept of Anti-Forensic Attacks on LLM-Assisted Digital Investigations
Authors: Frank Breitinger, Jan Gruber, Christopher Hargreaves, Jan-Niclas Hilgert, Gaëtan Michelet, Mark Scanlon
ID: 2610.29747
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29747

Abstract:
Large language models (LLMs) are entering digital forensics as examination
assistants and autonomous agents. Digital forensics inverts the usual setting
for indirect prompt injection: the evidence under analysis is attacker-
controlled by definition, and the examiner is obliged to read it closely. This
makes the reasoning layer a new anti-forensic target, next to the classical ones
of data and tools. Classical anti-forensic taxonomies do not cover it, and
prompt injection defences do not anticipate it. We extend the anti-forensic
model to reflect the manipulation of reasoning, and present a taxonomy of
attacks carried through adversarial evidence: an attacker selects an objective,
a technique, a payload location, and a concealment method. We observe a
perception gap, i.e., the fact that deterministic tools, the human examiner, and
the model perceive the same evidence differently, which is what lets an
injection reach the model unseen by the examiner. We demonstrate three attacks
against an LLM-assisted analysis pipeline, changing a reported conclusion,
writing attacker-chosen content to the analyst's workstation, and causing
outbound network traffic, each driven only by text planted on the seized device.
Mitigations to these attacks are also proposed.
