Title: The ROI of Small Examples: Optimizing In-Context Learning Under Token Budgets
Authors: Anxo Perez, Jorge Gabín, Javier Parapar
ID: 2611.24971
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24971

Abstract:
In-context learning (ICL) improves large language model performance at inference
time without updating model parameters. However, these gains are not free: in
practical deployments, longer prompts directly increase inference cost and
latency. Under a fixed token budget, prompt construction therefore becomes a
resource-allocation problem. While prior work has often focused on how many
examples to include in ICL, we study the complementary question of whether the
available budget is better spent on a few long demonstrations or on a larger
number of shorter ones. We study this aspect in question answering (QA), by
organizing candidate demonstrations into length-based bins and constructing
prompts from examples within the same length range under fixed budgets.
Experiments on two QA benchmarks (\textit{Beat the AI} and \textit{Natural
Questions}), using six instruction-tuned LLMs, show that shorter demonstrations
are generally the most effective use of prompt space. This effect is especially
strong on \textit{Beat the AI}, and remains visible on NQ, although gains are
smaller and saturate earlier in the long-context setting. An additional natural
language inference experiment on ANLI R3 finds that one of the two shorter bins
performs best for five of six models, although longer demonstrations remain
competitive. Overall, our results highlight demonstration granularity as an
important factor in prompt design.
