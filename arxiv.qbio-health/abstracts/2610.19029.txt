Title: Deep learning-based virtual perturbation reveals increasing heterogeneity of gene regulatory responses with aging
Authors: Yongjian He, Julia Hanna Haddad Guez, Xu-Wen Wang, Orr Levy
ID: 2610.19029
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19029

Abstract:
A central hypothesis in aging biology proposes that the gradual accumulation of
stochastic molecular damage drives transcriptional deregulation (TD), ultimately
leading to functional decline. However, existing single-cell analyses largely
rely on static expression measurements and provide limited resolution of how
regulatory responses change at the level of individual cells. Here, we leverage
DKO, a deep learning framework that predicts cell-specific virtual gene
perturbation effects from unperturbed transcriptomes, capturing cell-to-cell
differences in regulatory context without requiring a reconstructed gene
regulatory network or matched perturbation data. Applying DKO to single-cell
transcriptomic datasets from humans, mice, Drosophila, and C. elegans revealed
that the transcriptional consequences of perturbing the same gene became
increasingly heterogeneous across cells with age, indicating that gene
regulatory responses become less consistent and more dependent on cellular
context. Furthermore, we find that cells with higher somatic mutation burdens
have greater knockout-response variability, linking accumulated molecular damage
to regulatory inconsistency. Within the same cellular populations, higher impact
variability was also associated with higher senescence scores, linking
transcriptional dysregulation to senescence-associated states. Our findings
identify a progressive loss of regulatory robustness at both the gene and cell
levels as a conserved feature of aging, linking stochastic molecular damage to
increasing heterogeneity in cellular regulatory responses.
