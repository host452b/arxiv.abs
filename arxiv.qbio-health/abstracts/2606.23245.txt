Title: Predator-associated cues promote host riding, and coupling to mobile hosts improves survival in an epizoic limpet
Authors: Ryo Nakayama, Katsushi Kagaya, Keita Harada, Tomoyuki Nakano
ID: 2606.23245
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23245

Abstract:
A host with multiscale movements may be difficult for a small animal to
intercept or predict. Boarding turns that moving target into a source of
locomotion. The snail Tegula nigerrima previously showed statistical signatures
consistent with near-critical locomotor dynamics over limited ranges. We
examined how the facultative epizoic limpet Lottia tenuisculpta approaches and
boards this host, and whether host mobility improves rider survival. Crab-
associated waterborne cues increased riding (19/80 without cues versus 42/76
with cues) and visible host--limpet distance closure. Paired trajectories of
eventual riders captured both animals' projected movement toward one another
during a nominal ten-minute window ending at the movement-analysis reference
frame. Normalised by each animal's shell size, the limpet's projected
contribution exceeded the host's in 22/58 pairs. A complementary exploratory
assay compared each limpet's cumulative path length on an assigned surface with
its no-mucus baseline at a common observation time. Log-transformed ratios
varied less among individuals in gastropod-mucus groups than in the no-mucus
group. Across the observed frequency range, a matched-trial comparison estimated
a 1.21-fold cue-present to cue-absent amplitude ratio (95% recording-date
bootstrap interval: 0.97--1.59); the treatment difference in high-frequency
spectral slope remained uncertain. Survival was higher on mobile than fixed
hosts, with shells occupied in both treatments (posterior median hazard ratio,
fixed versus mobile: 2.1). These findings connect the behavioural formation of
host--rider coupling to a survival benefit from host mobility, illustrating how
one animal gains access to the multiscale locomotion of another.
