Title: Arginine metabolism drives tumor immune escape by shaping an immunosuppressive microenvironment
Authors: Chenghang Li, Haifeng Zhang, Yipu Qu, Jinzhi Lei
ID: 2611.19270
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19270

Abstract:
The tumor microenvironment evolves through the ``elimination-equilibrium-
escape'' paradigm, yet the quantitative mechanisms by which metabolic
reprogramming contributes to immune evasion remain unclear. We develop a
mathematical model of tumor-immune interactions to investigate how arginine
metabolism shapes an immunosuppressive microenvironment. We establish the
positivity and boundedness of solutions and analyze the existence and stability
of equilibrium states. The model is calibrated using tumor growth data from
three mouse cohorts and reproduces tumor growth patterns under distinct immune
conditions. The calibrated model predicts faster tumor growth in immunocompetent
mice than in immunodeficient controls, with macrophage dynamics accounting for
this difference. It further predicts that arginine administration promotes tumor
progression by enhancing macrophage polarization and weakening T cell-mediated
tumor control. To account for interindividual heterogeneity, we develop the
TVInfABC algorithm to generate a virtual mouse cohort. Moreover, our analysis
indicates that tumor-associated macrophages can promote transitions from
immunologically ``hot'' to ``cold'' states. Systematic pharmacodynamic
simulations further uncover a synergy between arginine depletion (anti-ARG1) and
immune checkpoint blockade (anti-PD-1/PD-L1). These results provide a
mathematical basis for studying metabolic-immune interactions and for evaluating
mechanism-based combination therapies.
