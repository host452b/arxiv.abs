Title: Predicting fitness from genomic offset: a multivariate latent-trait framework
Authors: Olivier François
ID: 2611.09748
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09748

Abstract:
Genomic offset methods quantify the genetic consequences of environmental change
using genotype environment associations (GEA), but the conditions under which
they predict fitness remain poorly understood. Here, we develop a theoretical
framework linking genomic offset to relative fitness loss through multivariate
latent adaptive traits under stabilizing selection. We show that squared genomic
offset is linearly related to expected log relative fitness loss when averaged
over genetic architectures, provided that direct environmental effects are
absent. However, this relationship does not necessarily hold for a realized
genetic architecture. We identify two complementary mechanisms that can explain
high predictive accuracy of genomic offset statistics. First, when effective
adaptive dimensionality is sufficiently large, the genetically mediated fitness
response concentrates around its marginal expectation, reducing the dependence
on the particular genetic architecture. Second, even with few adaptive traits,
accurate predictions can arise from geometric alignment between the complete
fitness loss matrix and the GEA metric. This second mechanism also encompasses
direct environmental effects and their interactions with genetically mediated
responses, which may preserve or weaken predictive accuracy depending on their
alignment. We further derive bounds on predictive accuracy that characterize
these effects. Our results clarify the assumptions and limitations underlying
genomic offset predictions and suggest that genomic offset is best interpreted
as a measure of genetically informed adaptive mismatch, rather than a direct
predictor of realized fitness or any particular phenotypic trait.
