Title: Keep on growing or change direction? Dependence of clonal plant survival on the probability of side-branching
Authors: Róbert Juhász, Beáta Oborny
ID: 2611.02700
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02700

Abstract:
In clonal organisms, the genetic individual (genet) may consist of several
physiological individuals (ramets). Clonal plants typically grow horizontally by
means of stolons or other clonal growth organs. Some species grow linearly for
several ramet generations, while others produce side branches frequently. We
hypothesize that the probability of side-branching ($b$) affects genet survival
($G$) depending on the ramets' ratio of birth to death rates ($λ$). We used a
spatially explicit model of ramet population dynamics to compare three growth
strategies. In the neutral case, growing forward and side-branching were equally
probable, whereas forward-growing and side-branching plants had lower and higher
$b$, respectively. We found that the genet's survival threshold (the critical
$λ_c$) strongly depended on the branching probability. The lowest threshold, and
hence the broadest range of survival, occurred in moderately forward-growing
plants, in which the average length of linear (non-branching) intervals was
$1.89$ units. Above the threshold, the $G(b)$ relationship strongly depended on
$λ$. Genet survival was promoted by forward growth in sparse ramet populations
(low $λ$). However, this advantage gradually disappeared as $λ$ increased, and
branching probability became effectively neutral across a broad parameter range
encompassing both forward-growing and side-branching plants. This suggests
strong selection for low branching intensity in unfavorable habitats, but weak
or negligible selection in favorable habitats.
