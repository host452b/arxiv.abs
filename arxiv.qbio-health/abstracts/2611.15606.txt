Title: Optogenetic Modulation Fraction: A Robust Opsin-Presence Effect and an Unresolved Cell-Type Question, by Censoring-Aware Survival Analysis of Public Neuropixels Data
Authors: Siddhardha Nanda
ID: 2611.15606
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15606

Abstract:
Expressing channelrhodopsin-2 (ChR2) in an interneuron population substantially
raises the fraction of locally recorded units whose spike timing is altered by
light, relative to no-opsin controls, under every method tried. Establishing
that result rigorously, and retracting two secondary claims that did not survive
the same rigor, is this paper's actual content. We reanalyze eight public Allen
Institute Neuropixels sessions (4,112 units), modeling light-to-first-spike
latency as a right-censored time-to-event outcome, with a Cox model, pooled FDR
correction, and a small-cluster-robust test (logistic regression plus an exact
wild cluster bootstrap, since only eight session clusters exist). We report an
unrestricted modulation fraction and a restricted direct-responder fraction
(evoked latency under 10 ms, an explicit, literature-grounded threshold).
ChR2-expressing Sst/Pvalb sessions show a large, bootstrap-confirmed elevation
over control under both metrics. Two further claims, each initially suggested by
a standard test, did not survive the exact bootstrap or a direct formal check,
and we report both retractions as results in their own right. Vip-ChR2's
apparent elevation over control is not confirmed by the bootstrap and becomes
non-significant once restricted to direct responders. A second pattern, that
among modulated units Vip sessions had a higher proportion of direct responses
than Sst/Pvalb, opposite the raw ordering, evaporated under the same bootstrap
test: no genotype contrast approaches significance, and control has the highest
proportion of all three, which cannot be biological. Per-session rates vary
enormously within one genotype. With two earlier corrections (a session-
composition artifact and a standard-versus-exact-bootstrap discrepancy), this is
a four-correction paper: pooled estimates and secondary patterns deserve the
same scrutiny as a headline claim.
