Title: Neural Computation Runs on Dissipative Structures Under Cyclic Calibration: A Systems Perspective on Sleep
Authors: Javier Diaz
ID: 2610.21486
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21486

Abstract:
Biological function operates far from thermodynamic equilibrium, sustained by
metastable structures that continuously cycle through synthesis, function
execution, and degradation--consuming energy and exporting entropy in the
process. Neural computation exemplifies this dissipative organization, relying
on low-entropy structures engaged in dynamic processes that define key
information transfer and processing parameters such as gain, threshold, and
integration time. This perspective proposes that sleep serves as a systemic
recalibration phase required to reset crucial signaling parameters that drift
during dissipative processing. During wakefulness, neural activity progressively
consumes and disrupts the organization of the very substrate from which it
emerges, leading to parameter drift and reduced computational precision. Sleep
maintains computational accuracy across activity cycles by recalibrating
function-structure coupling--that is, by counterbalancing entropic drift. Rather
than explaining the experimentally observed sleep need through homeostatic
regulation of unknown physiological variables, this perspective reframes it as
an emergent property of neural computation under thermodynamic constraints.
