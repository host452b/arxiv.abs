Title: Stochastic predator-prey compartment model with cyclic and non-cyclic transition pathways and mortality
Authors: Thomas M. Michelitsch, Ayten D. Baher, Michael Bestehorn, Andrzej F. Nowakowski, Alejandro P. Riascos, Bernard A. Collet
ID: 2610.32535
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32535

Abstract:
We study variants of a stochastic SEIRS-SEID compartmental model incorporating
presymptomatic infectiousness and disease-related mortality, with independent
compartmental sojourn times drawn from arbitrary distributions, in particular
Weibull distributions. We consider a large population of agents that occupy the
states (compartments) S (susceptible to infection), E (exposed: infected but
asymptomatic during the latency period and already infectious, including
diseases with presymptomatic infections), I (infected and infectious, with
symptoms), R (recovered, immune, and non-infectious), or D (dead). We consider
(i) cyclic transmission pathways, where compartmental sojourn times are finite
and immunity is temporary, and (ii) non-cyclic pathways, where immunity is
permanent. In the absence of mortality, cyclic transmission pathways support an
endemic equilibrium above the spreading threshold which is either globally
stable or oscillatory (Hopf) unstable, whereas for non-cyclic pathways a
stationary disease-free state is emerging. From a stochastic perspective, we
elucidate the connection between compartmental sojourn-time distributions and
the appearance of fractional operators in the evolution equations: fat-tailed
compartment waiting-time distributions necessarily lead to fractional
derivatives. For cyclic transition pathways we obtain an explicit expression for
the expected number of infection cycles that an agent survives. Our model is
validated by a combined Monte Carlo -- Runge -- Kutta simulation scheme and
random-walk simulations on Barabasi-Albert networks. It has cross-disciplinary
applications beyond epidemiology, including wildfire spreading, certain classes
of chemical reactions, population dynamics, finance, economics, and biology.
