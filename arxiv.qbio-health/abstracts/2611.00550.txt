Title: Governing Equations of Human Sleep Macro-Structure:Symbolic Discovery from 1,106 Nights
Authors: Junhui Gao
ID: 2611.00550
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00550

Abstract:
Human sleep is organized into a small number of cycles per night: deep sleep
dominates early cycles, REM lengthens toward morning, and light sleep clusters
at cycle boundaries. These regularities have long been described qualitatively
but never distilled into closed-form laws from large-scale data. A total of
1,106 nights (Sleep-EDF 153 + SHHS 953) are segmented into REM-anchored cycles,
and four cycle-indexed statistics follow simple closed-form equations: cycle
length declines nearly linearly (R^2=0.996), REM duration follows an inverted U
(R^2=0.873), deep-sleep (N3) run length decays exponentially (R^2=0.987) with a
time constant of 2.94 cycles, consistent with the homeostatic Process S of the
two-process model, and light-sleep (N1) run length follows a U shape
(R^2=0.956), confirming a "long-short-long" pattern across the night. The laws
are reproduced by per-night differentiable fitting (142/142 and 773/773 nights
converge), by unconstrained symbolic regression (PySR), and by a 500-night
generative synthesizer built from them. Their robustness is established against
the two main methodological threats: bootstrap CIs computed by resampling whole
nights (1,000 replications) exclude zero for every shape term, and a five-level
scan of the REM-merge segmentation threshold preserves every qualitative form.
These appear to be the first closed-form laws governing the macro-structure of
sleep, distilled and tested across an age-mixed sample (healthy young adults and
community-based older adults).
