Title: Uncertainty-aware integration of deuterium metabolic imaging with genome-scale metabolic modeling reveals diet-associated feasible-space remodeling in mouse skeletal muscle
Authors: Tin Yau Pang, Samia Maouhoub, Maruf Khan, Andreas Korzowski, Michael Roden, Ulrich Flögel, Daniel Dörr
ID: 2610.15175
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15175

Abstract:
Deuterium metabolic imaging (DMI) measures glucose-derived labeling in vivo, but
noisy time courses complicate network-level interpretation. We developed an
uncertainty-aware workflow that integrates dynamic DMI data with genome-scale
metabolic modeling (GEM) through soft flux constraints. After injection of
[6,6'-2H2]glucose, dynamic spectra were acquired from gastrocnemius, soleus, and
tibialis anterior muscles of control and high-fat diet (HFD) mice. A
hierarchical four-state kinetic model estimated labeling parameters while
accounting for shared plasma input, tissue-specific kinetics, voxel-level
fluxes, isotope effects, and measurement uncertainty. Because glucose labeling
was more reliable than lactate and glutamate/glutamine signals, only two DMI-
derived quantities were used as GEM anchors: glucose processing mapped to
hexokinase flux, and glucose-derived oxidative entry mapped to mitochondrial
pyruvate dehydrogenase flux. Two-sided soft target intervals with weighted
penalties defined DMI-compatible feasible regions for each mouse and muscle.
Flux variability analysis and sampling assessed changes across reactions and
metabolic subsystems. The constraints were compatible with the muscle network
and affected feasible activity in glycolysis, lactate metabolism, pyruvate
oxidation, the TCA cycle, oxidative phosphorylation, and redox pathways. HFD-
associated changes differed by muscle: tibialis anterior and soleus showed
reduced glucose-linked feasible activity, whereas gastrocnemius showed mixed
changes with preserved or increased TCA and redox activity. This framework
connects noninvasive tracer imaging with network-level metabolic inference while
avoiding strong claims from poorly identified signals.
