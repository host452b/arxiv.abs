Title: Do agents need to sleep?
Authors: Ido Haber, Giulio Tononi
ID: 2611.02216
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02216

Abstract:
LLM-based agents, which increasingly operate persistently across sessions and
accumulate memory through interaction, already exhibit the familiar symptoms of
unchecked plasticity: semantic drift, degraded retrieval precision, propagation
of stored errors, and saturation of memory capacity. Drawing on the selectionist
"ringing, resonance and renormalization" (RRR) account of sleep, we propose that
each of its three phases maps onto an operation feasible in digital memory
architectures. Engineers building stateful agents, meanwhile, are independently
rediscovering the problems sleep evolved to solve: what to forget, and when to
do the forgetting. From this we derive the falsifiable prediction that
selectionist consolidation will outperform instructionist alternatives as memory
stores grow large. The core claim concerns the nature of continuous learning
itself: plasticity has a price, and for any system that learns continuously
under finite capacity while coupled to its environment, that price is sleep.
