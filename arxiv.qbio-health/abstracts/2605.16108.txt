Title: Estimating marginal association between paired outcomes in clustered data with an application to analyzing caries and periodontal disease data from the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey
Authors: Owen Visser, Steven Levy, Bruce Dye, Shaoping Zhang, Victor Agboli, Somnath Datta
ID: 2605.16108
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16108

Abstract:
The within-cluster subgroup composition of units can inform on the marginal
distribution of those units outcomes, denoted informative subgroup size (ISS).
Moreover, within-cluster subgroup composition of paired outcomes can allow one
outcome to inform upon the marginal distribution of the other. If this type of
ISS ignored, it can bias marginal association estimates by allowing subgroup
composition to influence how clusters contribute to the analysis. We develop two
weighting approaches for estimating marginal association between paired outcomes
in this setting. The weights are derived using within-cluster resampling
arguments and extend conventional cluster weighting to paired response
subgroups. We adapt an existing test of marginal dependence to assess ISS by
examining this subgroup structure in both directions. In simulated data
exhibiting this bidirectional ISS, pair-based weighting reduced bias when
association was primarily carried through the paired observations. These weights
also allow scientifically or clinically meaningful dichotomizations to enter
directly into the weighting scheme, with the choice of subgroup definition
further improving estimation in several simulated settings. Application to
NHANES oral-health data showed strong evidence of ISS for filled-surface
outcomes across subgroups defined by clinical attachment loss and pocket depth,
with the corresponding periodontal--caries associations generally small and
positive. These methods provide a practical approach for estimating marginal
association when paired subgroup composition is informative.
