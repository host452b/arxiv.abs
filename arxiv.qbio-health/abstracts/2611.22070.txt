Title: Why do continual physical learners need sleep?
Authors: Nada Elmeligy, Nidhi Pashine, J. M. Schwarz
ID: 2611.22070
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22070

Abstract:
Sleep is widespread across the animal kingdom, including in organisms without a
centralized nervous system. Does learning create a need for sleep, even without
biology? To explore this question, we study a disordered spring network trained
to perform a sequence of mechanical allosteric tasks through local changes in
bond stiffness. We find that continual learning progressively reduces the
network's ability to acquire new tasks as individual bonds become increasingly
specialized. We identify an exact algebraic relationship between the
polarization of bond dominance and the network's remaining learning capacity,
providing a quantitative measure of this loss of adaptability. We then
demonstrate that random internal perturbations allow individual bonds to
estimate their mechanical dominance and locally adjust their stiffnesses to
restore learning capacity. However, the forces associated with active learning
bias these local measurements and interfere with restoration. Separating
learning and repair into wake-like and sleep-like phases removes this
interference and improves continual-learning performance. Our results suggest
that the need for offline restorative phases can emerge from the mechanics of
physical learning, even in systems without biological components.
