Title: Language processing in the autistic brain: A meta-analysis of neuroimaging studies across modalities and age
Authors: Hanlin Wu, Bei Xiao, Qingxi He
ID: 2611.11927
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11927

Abstract:
Language is central to social communication, which is altered in autism, and
language abilities vary widely among autistic individuals. How the autistic
brain processes language is central to understanding autistic communication. Yet
neuroimaging studies report inconsistent findings on how autistic brain activity
is altered during language processing. We synthesized 45 fMRI and PET studies
(753 autistic and 777 non-autistic participants) with seed-based d mapping with
permutation of subject images (SDM-PSI). We estimated autistic and non-autistic
activation and their difference with meta-regressions adjusted for each study's
language modality and mean age. Autistic and non-autistic brains activated a
similar left-lateralized language network. Autistic brains showed reduced
activity throughout the left perisylvian cortex, from the inferior frontal gyrus
to the mid superior temporal gyrus and the posterior superior temporal sulcus,
and increased activity in the right temporal pole. Spoken language showed a
larger autistic reduction than written language in the left mid superior
temporal gyrus. Adults, but not children and adolescents, showed increased
autistic activity in the right superior temporal gyrus and cuneus. Autistic
language processing thus relies on the typical left-lateralized network but
shows reduced leftward lateralization.
