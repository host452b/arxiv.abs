Title: The Shape of Individual Fitness Distributions and the Dynamics of Ecological Communities
Authors: Julie Rowlett, Leo Tingqvist
ID: 2611.24047
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24047

Abstract:
Developing a mechanistic understanding of biogeography and biodiversity that
predicts species abundances, distributions, and fluctuations is a central goal
of community ecology. We develop a model, that can be seen as an extension of
Hubbell's neutral theory, in which a focal species competes with the aggregate
of all other species in a local community of fixed size. Each individual has its
own competitive ability, and the shape of the distribution of these abilities is
incorporated in the model, represented by distributions rather than single
species-level values. Under complete mixing, symmetric competitive encounters
without ties recover Hubbell's neutral model. We further incorporate immigration
from a larger metacommunity and derive a diffusion approximation for the focal
species' relative abundance. The model shows that competitive ability
distributions with the same mean and variance can nevertheless yield different
patterns of local abundance. Thus, species that appear equivalent when
summarized by trait statistics need not be ecologically equivalent. Our
framework links individual-level variability to population-level persistence and
abundance and provides a way to examine how competition and immigration jointly
shape ecological outcomes.
