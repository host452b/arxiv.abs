Title: Boundary inference for the Makeham term in mortality models
Authors: Silvio C. Patricio
ID: 2610.03685
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03685

Abstract:
Whether a mortality model needs a Makeham term, the constant representing
background mortality, is commonly decided with a likelihood-ratio test. The
constant cannot be negative, so testing its absence places it on a boundary,
where the usual chi-squared calibration fails. Assuming independent Poisson
death counts and standard regularity conditions, we show that when the constant
is the only parameter on a boundary the statistic converges to an equal mixture
of a point mass at zero and a chi-squared distribution with one degree of
freedom: at the 5% level the critical value is 2.71, not 3.84. We state
conditions under which the correction holds for Makeham models, verify them for
Gompertz--Makeham and gamma-Gompertz--Makeham, and quantify the information
about the constant, which fixes the local power, the smallest detectable term,
and the loss of detectability as the age window narrows. When a gamma-frailty
variance is estimated at a true value of zero, the limit is no longer a chi-
squared mixture and the correction rejects too often. Monte Carlo experiments
show the conventional cutoff rejecting at half the nominal level, a correctly
calibrated test missing a term at its detection threshold in most samples, and
excess rejections persisting as exposure grows.
