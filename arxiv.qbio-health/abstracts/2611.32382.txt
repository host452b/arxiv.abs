Title: Cancer multi-omics joint dimension reduction leads to reliable and interpretable survival predictions
Authors: Vincent Le Goff, Vincent Guillemot, Cathy Philippe, Wiam Ahdidou, Gwendoline Mendes, Jean-François Deleuze, Edith Le Floch, Arnaud Gloaguen
ID: 2611.32382
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32382

Abstract:
Multi-omics studies are becoming more and more frequent, and are especially
suited to decrypt the complexity of dysregulation happening in cancer. In this
context, improving prognostication through interpretable methods could provide
better patient stratification and help identify new prognostic biomarkers and
therapeutic targets. Multi-omics data are however very often high-dimensional
(few patients/high number of variables). A possible solution to high-
dimensionality is Joint Dimension Reduction (JDR) methods, which compute a low-
dimensional latent space from multi-omics data. In this work, we benchmark 12
JDR methods on 18 different TCGA cancer datasets. Survival prediction is
achieved through a two-step process: the estimation of a low-dimensional multi-
omics latent space followed by survival prediction with or without additional
clinical data. We had to equip 4 methods with the ability to predict the
survival of new patients. Results reveal that JDR methods can improve survival
prediction with clinical data, and match the predictive performance of clinical
data alone, with a high variability between different cancers. Among JDR
methods, JIVE and RGCCA show superior performance. In a subsequent bootstrap
analysis, JDR methods identify reliable prognostic biomarkers across cancers. We
propose a clinically relevant interpretation of such markers on Low-Grade
Glioma. Reproducible codes are available on
https://github.com/vlegoff/MultiOBenchmark.
