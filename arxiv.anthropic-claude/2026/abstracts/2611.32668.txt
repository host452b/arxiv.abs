Title: The Elephant-Goldfish Model for software design review: what a fresh reviewer can see
Authors: Sabry E. Farrag
ID: 2611.32668
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32668

Abstract:
A design can be easy to understand and still specify the wrong system. Google's
Elephant-Goldfish Model asks fresh agents to review a design without the
conversation that produced it. We examined how this choice affects detection of
omissions, agreement violations and internal defects. We seeded nine defects in
each of 47 public software design documents and supplied each with a scripted
drafting conversation. A fresh reviewer given the conversation as its own
earlier exchange detected 94.8% of omitted-decision instances, compared with
62.4% for the same model given the document alone. It detected 98.3% of
violations of conversation-only requirements. On document-visible defects,
recall was 87.5% with the conversation and 88.4% without it, equivalent within a
prespecified 15-percentage-point margin. Three model judges scored detection and
were calibrated on constructed controls. In document-only runs, the plugin's
five-stage review took a geometric mean of 9.03 minutes against 1.81 for one
reviewer. Its estimated recall was lower under both judging procedures, with a
difference distinguishable from zero under one. A later generic-prompt
comparison gave a similar result. The experiment separates two objectives for
automated design review: testing whether a new reader can reconstruct the design
and checking whether the design preserves the decisions that govern
implementation.
