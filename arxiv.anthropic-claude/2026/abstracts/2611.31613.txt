Title: Training decision models for simulating human respondents
Authors: Davood Wadi, Yu Ma
ID: 2611.31613
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31613

Abstract:
Researchers increasingly use large language models (LLMs) as synthetic
respondents in market research, conjoint analysis, and randomized experiments.
However, synthetic choice data from off-the-shelf generative LLMs bias
downstream econometric inference. Neither proprietary models nor higher
reasoning effort reliably reduces this bias. We investigate decision models
(System One architectures) for silicon sampling. Using choice training, we
update KEV model weights on historical respondent-level choice microdata by
conditional maximum likelihood, before inference and without target-study data.
Across three model scales, training on two discrete choice experiments lowers
treatment-effect error by 22% to 89% on four held-out studies from new domains
(vaccines, nutrition labels, consumer rights, and AI) and new response formats.
A small choice-trained model outperforms a much larger off-the-shelf model on
three of the four studies. Although training on single studies lowers cross-
entropy, it can exacerbate treatment-effect error. Choice training thus
complements post-inference calibration by providing a stronger starting point
for estimating human preferences.
