Title: ViTacLab: Physically Consistent Visuo-Tactile Simulation, Benchmarking, and Representation Learning for Robotic Manipulation
Authors: Linjing You, Zeming Yang, Zhenhao Shen, Shengqiang Xu, Guanda Li, Wenxuan Li, Jiyu Guo, Jasper Lu, Kyle Xu, Shawn Xie et al.
ID: 2611.32476
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32476

Abstract:
Tactile sensing is essential for contact-rich robotic manipulation, but existing
visuo-tactile simulators trade physical fidelity for scalability, and benchmarks
largely focus on two-finger grippers. We present ViTacLab, a framework
comprising ViTacSim, ViTacBench, and ViTacPolicy. ViTacSim uses a tri-modal
tactile primitive: a global sparse contact-force lattice, a local dense force
array, and tactile images. Anchored to PhysX contact-solver outputs, it corrects
normal inconsistency through local stiffness alignment and reconstructs
tangential forces under decoupled slip-stick regimes while preserving GPU
parallelism. A joint projection preserves the sparse wrench and enforces local
friction cones. ViTacBench spans parallel grippers, dexterous hands, and
bimanual systems, with tasks ranging from vision-dominant to tactile-dominant
regimes. ViTacPolicy aggregates variable numbers of heterogeneous tactile inputs
using spatial-pose embeddings and a Transformer, and aligns force-array and
tactile-image modalities through a two-stream VQ-VAE with a shared codebook.
Controlled Hertzian-reference indentation and real-sensor sliding tests reduce
normal and tangential force RMSE from 2.31 and 1.85 N to 0.42 and 0.53 N,
respectively. Throughput reaches 2,482.9 tactile-pipeline FPS at 256 parallel
environments. Simulation and real-world experiments show gains on several
contact-rich tasks, while low absolute success on tactile-dominant tasks
highlights the remaining difficulty of purely tactile control.
