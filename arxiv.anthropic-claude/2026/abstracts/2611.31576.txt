Title: Lit Up but Not Needed: Scoped Lesions Show Where Multimodal Mixture-of-Experts Depend on Their Experts
Authors: Zhixuan Ge, Anqi Li, Kexin Geng, Wei Qiu
ID: 2611.31576
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31576

Abstract:
Cognitive neuroscience learned that an activation map is not a map of necessity.
Regions that light up during a task are not always needed for it, and only
lesions reveal which ones are. Mixture-of-experts (MoE) vision-language models
come with an activation map for free, since the router records which experts
respond to image and text tokens, and recent work reads modality-specialized
experts off this map. We bring the lesion. In Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B, Kimi-VL-A3B and
Gemma-4-26B-A4B, 3 to 12% of the experts are vision-exclusive: image tokens
route to them at up to five times the uniform rate and text tokens almost never.
In Qwen3-VL they receive a quarter of all image-token routing, and lesioning all
of them costs less than one percentage point on average over eight perception
benchmarks and free-form OCR, no more than lesioning a random set of the same
size. In Kimi-VL and Gemma-4 the lesion also costs no more than a random set.
Scoped lesions, which remove an expert set only at image positions or only at
text positions, then show where the models do depend on their experts. In
Qwen3-VL, silencing every expert at every image position costs 3 points, against
45 at text positions, where the dependence sits in dual-use experts that text-
only tasks need as well. Across models the image-side dependence is 2 to 3
points in Qwen3-VL and Kimi-VL and 26 in Gemma-4. Reading text in images is the
one ability that needs image-position experts in every model. The exclusive
experts receive informative patches, produce outputs of ordinary size, and stay
inert under sensory deprivation: text-only training recruits them only when load
balancing forces it. In Kimi-VL the balancing bias itself manufactures most of
them. Load balancing draws the modality map in the part of the computation the
models depend on least. Expert selection, pruning and skipping should follow the
lesion, not the map.
