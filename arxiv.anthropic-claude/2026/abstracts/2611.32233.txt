Title: Roboclock: A Unified Benchmark for Time-Aware Robotic Intelligence
Authors: Yujie Zhao, Anthony L. Hu, Zimeng Huang, Zhijing Wu, Luobin Wang, Richard Li, Shrivas Sudharsan, Henrik I. Christensen, Jishen Zhao
ID: 2611.32233
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32233

Abstract:
Time is a fundamental dimension of robotic intelligence: a robot may fail
despite physically correct actions if it acts outside a valid time window,
responds too slowly to an event, or fails to meet required throughput. Yet
existing robotic benchmarks primarily emphasize spatial correctness and logical
task completion, largely overlooking the temporal constraints of real-world
applications. We present RoboClock, a unified benchmark for time-aware robotic
manipulation featuring 60 tasks across five application domains. Mirroring these
failure modes, RoboClock structures tasks around three types of temporal
constraints: Temporal Window, Event-Reactive, and Throughput. Furthermore,
RoboClock incorporates a Latency-Aligned evaluation protocol that continuously
advances environment time during model inference and control-interface delays,
paired with time-sensitive metrics. Evaluating over 30 methods, we find that the
best-performing approach is the trained VLA, yet it achieves only 8.3% temporal
success even on Easy-level tasks, with persistent challenges in generalization
and transfer. These findings reveal fundamental limitations in current robotic
systems and highlight the need to jointly optimize physical competence, temporal
reasoning, and execution latency.
