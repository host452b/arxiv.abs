Title: Adapt, Label, Distill: Foundation-Model Supervision for Efficient Object Detection
Authors: Zhengye Yang, Anthony Wang, Yuyang Li, Mehmet Kerem Turkcan, Beizhan Yan, Zoran Kostic
ID: 2611.31604
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31604

Abstract:
Object detectors often transfer poorly to specialized domains, where box
annotations are scarce and deployment requires compact, real-time models. Large
vision foundation models can provide offline supervision, but their zero-shot
predictions may be poorly aligned with the target domain. We present an adapt-
label-distill pipeline that uses limited target annotations to specialize a
foundation teacher and transfer its supervision to compact detectors. We adapt
SAM3 on the labeled subset, generate pseudo-labels for the remaining unlabeled
images, and train YOLOv11 students with confidence-guided supervision. Teacher
confidence weights pseudo-label losses, while zero-gating retains uncertain
hypotheses during assignment without applying their associated training losses.
Experiments across six target domains and three student capacities show
substantial gains in several domains, while revealing limited or negative
transfer in others. Teacher comparisons under a common hard-label training
recipe support the choice of SAM3, and comparisons with an EfficientTeacher
adaptation provide additional evidence against an online SSOD baseline. With
YOLOv11m, our pipeline improves over STAC-style self-training by up to 15.3 AP
and reaches 51.6 AP on Garbage, compared with 54.1 under full supervision. On
NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin, the deployed student achieves 188 FPS versus 2.9 FPS for
SAM3, benefiting from foundation-model supervision without incurring the
teacher's deployment cost. Code is available at
https://github.com/zk2172-columbia/ProjectAdaptLabelDistill_ObjectDetection.
