Title: Fix How It Acts, Not How It Thinks: Surgical Fine-Tuning of Frontier LLMs
Authors: Sandeep Singh Sandha, Prann Bansal, Arvind Sundararajan
ID: 2611.32064
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32064

Abstract:
Open-weight LLMs are competent software engineers, but they sometimes fail at
tasks they are otherwise able to do. Often it is not a failure of reasoning. It
is a few small missteps over a long trajectory (repeating a step that has
already failed, or declaring the task done while a requirement is still unmet)
that make the difference. Correcting this calls for a surgical weight update
that steers the model toward the right actions without interfering with its
reasoning. Such updates, typically learned with LoRA adapters, are often too
small to survive merging into a released checkpoint. In our BF16 release, the
median weight change is only 1.5% of a single rounding step, and merging loses
roughly two thirds of the update's norm. When the same adapter is merged into
the mixed FP8/NVFP4 release of the same model, 93% of the update disappears.
Instead of increasing the size of the update, we directly train on the surviving
part. We introduce merge-aware fine-tuning. Standard LoRA rounds the merged
weights to the storage format only at export, so the loss never sees the rounded
weights. We instead round the sum of base weights and adapter update to the
release format at every training step, keeping the vendor's quantization scales,
and compute the loss on these weights. The forward pass matches the serving
engine numerics, and the gradient passes straight through the rounding, so the
model we train is the model we ship. The loss optimizes only the model's actions
and keeps its reasoning close to the base model. Applied to Nemotron-3.5, the
recipe improves agentic coding in both the BF16 and the NVFP4 release, by up to
8.4 points on Terminal-Bench 2.1, and we publicly release both models.
