Title: The Fingerprinting Paradox: Why Sensitivity Undermines Black-Box Model Integrity Verification
Authors: Shahinul Hoque, Farhin Farhad Riya, Jinyuan Sun, Fnu Suya
ID: 2611.31455
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31455

Abstract:
Black-box integrity verification aims to detect unauthorized modifications to
outsourced models using only prediction APIs. Existing frameworks seek to
improve verification efficiency through sensitive fingerprints: queries whose
predictions are more likely to change under tampering than those of genuine
inputs. We show that this sensitivity can also help dishonest providers
recognize verification queries and selectively conceal tampering. We prove that
excess sensitivity relative to genuine inputs lower-bounds statistical
distinguishability. We evaluate five integrity verification frameworks across
three model architectures, three datasets, and three adversarial knowledge
settings. When the deployed framework is known, our fingerprint detectors
achieve AUCs at least 0.989, and selective filtering makes all five frameworks
require significantly larger estimated query budgets than genuine inputs,
reversing their intended efficiency advantage. A query-efficiency advantage
remains marginally in a small subset of evaluated settings with poor detection
transfer. We further design verification methods under stronger concealment
constraints but find that reduced detectability comes with losses in sensitivity
or fingerprint-generation success. Our results expose a sensitivity-
detectability tension and show that sensitivity alone does not ensure efficient
black-box verification against adaptive malicious providers.
