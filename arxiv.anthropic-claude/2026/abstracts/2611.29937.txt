Title: The Answer Is Not the State: Trajectory-Level Corrigibility in Diffusion Language Models
Authors: Junchi Liao, Wentao Li
ID: 2611.29937
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29937

Abstract:
Diffusion language models generate tokens at many positions on a shared canvas,
making unfinished responses appear revisable. We find a counterexample: after
the final answer is reopened, previously generated rationale can reconstruct the
old decision despite newly available evidence. We isolate this effect by
comparing reopening the answer alone with reopening both rationale and answer,
and by exchanging rationales between paired trajectories. On a 150-question
source-disjoint benchmark, reopening both regions makes decisions follow the
evidence currently present--whether correct or deliberately corrupted--across
Dream, LLaDA, and iLLaDA. On a new 100-question free-form short-answer
evaluation, the same effect remains strong in LLaDA and iLLaDA but is negligible
in Dream. Coherent transplanted rationales steer rebuilt decisions, while token-
level attribution improves which positions to reopen without producing a compact
repair. Corrigibility therefore depends on retained rationale state, not answer
openness alone.
