Title: A Well-Posed Fourth-Order Navier-Stokes Model for Image Inpainting: Theory, Order Selection, and Stripe Decomposition
Authors: Yiwen Dou, Yiqiang Ding, Zhongyun Bao, Senyan Zhao
ID: 2611.31777
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31777

Abstract:
The Navier-Stokes (NS) inpainting model of Bertalmío, Bertozzi and Sapiro is the
only transport-type paradigm among PDE-based methods, yet it has never received
a systematic higher-order extension. We show that the obvious fourth-order
correction is ill-posed, and we construct the corrected, well-posed model. In
the vorticity-streamfunction formulation we add a gated fourth-order term whose
sign is decisive: the backward form is a Perona-Malik-type anti-diffusion,
Hadamard ill-posed and consistently harmful in experiments, whereas the forward
form is uniformly parabolic, energy-stable and consistently beneficial. An
order-dissipation principle, stating that odd orders are dispersive and even
orders dissipative, singles out the fourth order as the lowest admissible one.
Large missing blocks pose a separate problem, because the correction gate stays
silent there; we answer it with stripe decomposition, a Telea base fill followed
by interleaved slit-wise NS passes, which converts block damage into the
structural-slit regime where the model excels. The device is base-agnostic, and
its gains add to those of the forward correction. On three datasets, namely
BSDS500, DIV2K and a classical Chinese painting benchmark, and on three damage
types, namely cracks, blocks and text occlusion, the training-free model beats
every classical baseline on every sample of structure-dominated damage, gains
+1.02 dB PSNR over Telea on the full 42-image painting test set with a 42/42
per-sample win rate, matches the strongest recent PDE solvers within 0.4 dB
while attaining the best perceptual quality of the entire PDE family, and runs
at 4-16 s per image on a consumer GPU.
