Title: RumiFold: Teaching an LLM to Natively Fold Proteins and Reason About Their Function
Authors: Kevin Lu, David Bau, Wengong Jin
ID: 2611.29444
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29444

Abstract:
Can we teach a general-purpose language model to fold proteins? And if so, what
does learning to fold enable beyond structure prediction itself? We introduce
RumiFold, a pretrained chat model that learns to predict protein structures
directly from its own hidden states. RumiFold processes proteins through
repeated folding passes, then uses these folded representations to answer
questions in natural language. It achieves folding accuracy comparable to
dedicated single-representation models such as SimpleFold, alongside state-of-
the-art performance on Gene Ontology protein function prediction and free-text
function description. We find that folding pretraining organizes the model's
hidden states around protein function, creating an internal per-residue
workspace that is biologically grounded. Causal interventions show that this
folded workspace links structure and language when RumiFold predicts function,
providing rich representations for biomolecular reasoning. We see RumiFold as a
step toward native biomolecular intelligence: where protein-based computations
and language-based reasoning become integrated capabilities of general-purpose
language models. Code, data, and models are available at:
https://rumifold.baulab.info
