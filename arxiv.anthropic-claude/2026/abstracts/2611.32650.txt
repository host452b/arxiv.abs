Title: LaWanle: Why Can't Leading Driving Models Make a Simple Turn?
Authors: Chengzhi Gao, Junzhe Gao, Yongzhi Liu, Weichao Zhuang, Weilong Hu, Taonian Liang, Guodong Yin
ID: 2611.32650
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32650

Abstract:
We were surprised to find that leading end-to-end driving models across major
paradigms, despite scoring close to human drivers on NAVSIM, cannot reliably
make a simple 90-degree turn in photorealistic closed-loop simulators. To
investigate why, we build LaWanle, a benchmark of turns drawn from three such
simulators. Driving each turn from several starts, we find that the same model
passes it from one start and fails it from another, and that the two drives look
alike until they enter the junction. Rotating the car by a few degrees at the
junction entry can fail a turn it otherwise passes. Conversely, a slight heading
correction to the plans can rescue about half of the failed turns we tested.
Such errors arise when a model drives by itself, so we post-train an open-source
lane-keeping model with closed-loop DAgger on its lateral path. Training 5% of
its parameters is enough to raise its turn pass rate from 19% to 76%. The gain
transfers to the two other benchmarks, where the policy also scores among the
leading models. Further fine-tuning on recovery data repairs the deviations it
is trained on but, against a control trained equally long, improves turns on
none of the three benchmarks, and every variant stays far below a human driver.
