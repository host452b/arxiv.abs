Title: CaLA: Causal Latent Adjustment for World Models under Adaptive Policies
Authors: Robert Ness, Sofiane Ennadir, Oleg Smirnov
ID: 2611.31431
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31431

Abstract:
World models is answering ``imagine" answers to ``what-if'' questions via
imagined rollouts. But rollouts collected by adaptive policies induce treatment-
outcome confounding in imagined rollouts. We study this problem in Match-3
difficulty adjustment, where latent player skill influences both assigned
difficulty and churn. We build a fully specified Match-3 SCM with sequential
mechanics, adaptive assignment, and paired ground-truth interventions, on which
unadjusted models fit to the logged trajectories select difficulty settings that
raise churn. We then introduce Causal Latent Adjustment (CaLA), a DAG-
constrained world model that infers a variational player representation from
pretreatment history. Across our evaluations, CaLA provides the most reliable
population-level intervention estimates and competitive individualized
decisions, although rich handcrafted histories can remain advantageous for
individual-level adjustment. We further find that residual correction can
degrade causal decision quality despite improving predictive fit, highlighting
the need to distinguish predictive accuracy, confounding adjustment and the
behavior of downstream causal estimators.
