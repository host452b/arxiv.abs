Title: Beyond Binary Correctness: A Surrogate for Confidence Learning in Large Language and Vision-Language Models
Authors: Chen Li, Xiaoling Hu, Chao Chen
ID: 2611.30059
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30059

Abstract:
Confidence estimation for large language models (LLMs) and vision-language
models (VLMs) aims to assess how likely a particular generated response is to be
correct. We investigate how learning this assessment can benefit from the
model's predictive reliability on the underlying query. Binary correctness
supervision, however, assigns identical labels to occasional errors and frequent
failures, as well as isolated successes and consistent success. These outcomes
can arise from substantially different predictive behavior under stochastic
decoding. We propose a confidence-learning framework that supplements realized
response correctness with query-level predictive reliability. We estimate
reliability through repeated generations under a fixed model and decoding
configuration, then combine the empirical success rate with individual
correctness to construct a graded confidence surrogate. The surrogate
differentiates responses sharing the same correctness label while preserving the
average predictive reliability of the sampled response pool. To learn this
target, we condition an independent confidence estimator on the query and its
full generated response, and optimize backbone LoRA adapters together with a
projection and scalar confidence head using mean squared error. Repeated
sampling is required only for supervision construction; inference scores an
individual response without additional generations. We establish ordering and
mean preservation properties of the surrogate and derive a selective-risk bound
connecting population surrogate prediction error to the error rate among
accepted responses. We evaluate the framework across language and vision-
language models, examining confidence calibration and selective prediction.
