Title: Scalable Placement for Disaggregated LLM Serving on Heterogeneous Edge Fleets
Authors: Truong-Thanh Le, Hoang-Loc La, Amir Taherkordi, Frank Eliassen, Phuong Hoai Ha
ID: 2611.32881
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32881

Abstract:
Serving large language models (LLMs) at the edge is increasingly desirable for
privacy-sensitive applications and scenarios that require data to remain on
local devices. However, edge environments are constrained by limited
computational resources and exhibit substantial device heterogeneity, making
efficient LLM serving challenging. These limitations become particularly
pronounced under bursty demand, where congestion and queue buildup can
significantly increase response latency. Replicating the model into multiple
pipeline instances can improve serving capacity and reduce congestion. Yet,
deciding how devices should be grouped into replicas, assigning replicas to
prefill or decode functions, and partitioning model layers across heterogeneous
devices jointly creates a large combinatorial optimization problem. We present
Spade, a scalable and runtime-efficient placement planner for disaggregated LLM
serving with pipeline replication across heterogeneous edge clusters. Spade
introduces a dominance-based pruning technique that discards inferior replica
candidates and recursively eliminates larger candidates that contain them,
dramatically shrinking the search space. It further combines a water-filling
approach for layer partitioning with a bounded multidimensional knapsack
formulation that optimizes placement under a congestion-aware objective. On a
synthetic fleet of 30 devices spanning five hardware classes, Spade finds the
optimal placement in 441ms. In contrast, a genetic-search baseline augmented
with dynamic-programming partitioning requires approximately two hours and still
produces solutions with a 19.7% worse congestion objective. When evaluated on
fleets containing ten device types, Spade's pruning strategy eliminates 99.6% of
candidate placements while yielding the same surviving candidate set as
exhaustive enumeration.
