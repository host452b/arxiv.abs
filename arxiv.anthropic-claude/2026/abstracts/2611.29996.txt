Title: MindBridge: A Multilingual and Multimodal Tool for Interactive Cognitive Monitoring
Authors: Xiaojie Gu, Haoyu Zhang, Qian Niu, Fan Gao, Rio Yang, Eleni Friligkou, Kevin Jin, Yutaka Matsuo, Irene Li
ID: 2611.29996
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29996

Abstract:
We present MindBridge, a clinician-informed, multilingual, multimodal cognitive
monitoring tool combining participant-controlled interviews with explainable
reports. Reports link lexical diversity and pause patterns to speech or text
evidence and disclose data quality and missingness. In a formative study of
typed interaction with 10 adults (seven aged 18-59; three aged 60 or older),
follow-ups prompted elaboration but sometimes introduced assumptions requiring
correction. Nine participants ranked interpretation-first reports highest; all
identified score-only reports as most likely to encourage fixation and
overreaction. On a fixed DementiaBank manifest, the best configuration achieved
70.3% zero-shot accuracy on valid predictions versus a 56.0% majority-class
baseline on the full manifest. Some configurations predicted only one category;
tests with generated samples revealed different error patterns across input
types and languages. MindBridge makes evidence collection, analysis, and
interpretation inspectable, linking participant control to the scope and limits
of monitoring results.
