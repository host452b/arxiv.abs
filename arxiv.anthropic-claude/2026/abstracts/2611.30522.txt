Title: Can LLMs Understand Human Rules?
Authors: Sydney Levine, Elizaveta Tennant, Seth Lazar, Rasmi Elasmar, Matija Franklin, Verena Rieser, Julia Haas
ID: 2611.30522
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30522

Abstract:
Rules help people coordinate and cooperate for mutual benefit. For AI models to
coexist with humans and other AI systems, they will need to interact with and
navigate such rules successfully. But such interactions require AI systems to
recognize not only when a rule permits or prohibits a particular action, but
also cases where people may reasonably disagree about whether a certain action
is appropriate. This paper introduces the RULES benchmark---building on a set of
300 images depicting signs posted around New York City, and accompanied by 937
scenarios---to test frontier models' ability to discern whether a proposed
action is socially acceptable, unacceptable, or would generate disagreement. At
first pass, we find that frontier models exhibit robust performance on the
benchmark, successfully engaging with human-written directives in naturalistic
visual environments, and correctly determining the social acceptability of a
wide range of everyday activities. However, we further find that models
systematically fail to recognize certain widely accepted permissible exceptions
to a rule, indicating a critical shortcoming in model understanding. Moreover,
models struggle to identify instances where humans disagree regarding the
application of a given rule; in these instances, models tend to identify
proposed actions as unacceptable, rather than as appropriate targets of
disagreement. We do find that ensembling a series of diverse models together
begins to recover the disagreement signal. Overall, our findings identify
significant limitations in AI performance on human rules.
