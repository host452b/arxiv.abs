Title: Explainable Emotion and Mental Health Reasoning in Large Language Models Using Theories of Emotion
Authors: Tadesse Destaw Bela, Iqra Ameer, Helena Gómez Adorno, Alexander Gelbukh, Eusebio Ricardez Vázquez, Olga Kolesnikova, Grigori Sidorov, Seid Muhie Yimam
ID: 2611.29628
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29628

Abstract:
Advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) have enabled a wide range of
applications. However, their ability to simulate human-like behavior and
reasoning remains unclear. While emotion detection is a better-established
Natural Language Processing (NLP) task, existing approaches primarily focus on
directly classifying text into predefined emotion classes, often overlooking the
appraisal process that explains why an individual experiences a particular
emotion in a given situation. In this work, we propose Psychology-based Chain-
of-Thought (PsyCoT) reasoning, a theory-driven prompting strategy grounded in
the Schachter-Singer and Cognitive Appraisal theories of emotion. We propose and
evaluate PsyCoT for subjective NLP tasks, including emotion analysis, emotion
intensity prediction, stress analysis, and depression severity identification.
Our evaluation covers four levels of classification complexity: binary,
multiclass, multi-label, and ordinal scaling, spanning 11 languages. We assess
the performance of five state-of-the-art LLMs across these selected subjective
NLP tasks. Experimental results demonstrate that PsyCoT prompt variants improve
the standard chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting across the evaluated tasks. Across
all evaluated tasks, PsyCoT yields F1 score gains ranging from 0.5% to 28.1%,
depending on the model, task, and language configuration.
