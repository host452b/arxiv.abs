Title: Language models seek help despite guaranteed score losses in question answering
Authors: Jiahao You, Ziye Jia, Chao Dong, Fuhui Zhou, Qihui Wu
ID: 2611.29981
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29981

Abstract:
At the highest waiting cost in a multiple-choice question-answering task, all 7
tested open-weight language models still consult an expert even though a correct
expert answer earns fewer points than an incorrect independent answer. Pooling
over expert accuracy and fixed-fee combinations gives help-seeking rates of
20.8% to 94.1% across models. We test 2,291 questions across 54 combinations of
expert accuracy and consultation costs, comparing observed decisions with an
expected-score baseline based on independently measured and calibrated model
confidence. Help-seeking rates vary across conditions with the same expected
consultation score. Fine-tuning reduces decision regret by 59% to 87% on held-
out questions within the training range but increases inconsistency at matched
expected consultation scores. In a separate task, external routing improves
scores by 0.180 to 0.273 points per question with candidate answers held fixed.
