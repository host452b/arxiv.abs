Title: Perception Is Enough, at the Right Scale: Scale-Adaptive Token Pruning for Vision-Language-Action Models
Authors: Yifeng Lin, Liuxiang Qiu, Yunfeng Chen, Tiesong Zhao
ID: 2611.31954
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31954

Abstract:
Vision-language-action (VLA) models must process continuous streams of visual
observations, and the resulting computational cost limits real-time robotic
control. Token pruning reduces this cost, but a fixed semantic scale of
perceptual representation can overlook information needed at different stages of
manipulation. We show that perceptual semantics can guide effective token
selection without relying on internal attention when the selection scale adapts
to the scene's evolving semantic structure. Based on this observation, we
introduce TauPrune-VLA, a training-free method that controls the scale of the
retained perceptual representation through a single perception temperature $τ$.
To adapt this scale as manipulation proceeds, TauPrune-VLA updates $τ$ online
using trends in perceptual semantics, with robot-state changes and action
magnitude regulating the adjustment strength. While these semantics evolve over
the course of manipulation, they remain relatively stable between consecutive
frames. This continuity allows retention indices computed from the current
observation to be applied at the next policy invocation, enabling token
selection to run asynchronously with inference and reducing pruning overhead.
Evaluated across diverse VLA models and simulation benchmarks, TauPrune-VLA
achieves up to a $1.86\times$ inference speedup while maintaining near-baseline
task success rates. We further validate its effectiveness on two real-world
robotic platforms.
