Title: Parsing Visual Latents for Multimodal Spatial Reasoning
Authors: Zhuoying Yang, Yichen Guo, Kathy Jia, Zichang Su, Ayesha Kashif, Nange Hai, Jeffrey Ming Han Li, Zhewen He, Silvio Kempf, Ni Jiachen et al.
ID: 2611.31021
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31021

Abstract:
Spatial reasoning across multiple views requires inferring the 3D relations
between objects and the camera from 2D observations, which depends on cross-view
correspondence and scene geometry that vision-language models (VLMs) do not
reliably extract. Pretrained vision encoders already capture this information in
their latent features, with CLIP linking objects to image regions, DINOv3
matching semantics across views, and VGGT encoding multi-view geometry. However,
VLMs do not benefit from these features when we present them directly in context
as grayscale feature images. We therefore propose LatentParse, a training-free
framework that parses latent features into explicit spatial evidence for VLMs.
In LatentParse, an analyst VLM probes the features of all three encoders with
predefined tools that locate objects, track them across views, and measure
motion between views. It then distills the results into a concise text report,
which is provided together with the images to the VLM to answer the question. We
evaluate LatentParse on MMSI-Bench, MV-RoboBench and SPAR-Bench, where it
improves all five VLMs over the photo-only baseline by 0.7-2.4 percentage points
on average. On MMSI-Bench, it raises Qwen3.8-27B from 34.4% to 39.5%, with the
largest improvements of 19.2 percentage points on the camera-related questions.
These results suggest that latent features become useful for spatial reasoning
once they are parsed into relation evidence, without training. Code is available
at https://github.com/Harvard-AI-and-Robotics-Lab/LatentParse.
