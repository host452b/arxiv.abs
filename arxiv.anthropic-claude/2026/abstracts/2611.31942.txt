Title: Beyond "AI Can Make Mistakes": How Risk Communication Shapes Trust and Decision Confidence for LLMs in Health Information Seeking
Authors: Jiawei Zhou, Mengting Tu, Shravika Mittal, Sowmya Krishna, Q. Vera Liao, Munmun De Choudhury
ID: 2611.31942
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31942

Abstract:
Adoption of LLMs for health information has outpaced public understanding, but
whether risk communication can work as a corrective remains unknown. In a
preregistered experiment (N=500), we compared a standard disclaimer against
structured risk communication varying in contextual relevance and mechanistic
explanation. Without intervention, users understanding LLMs less reported higher
trust and confidence in deciding when to rely. Structured risk communication
significantly reduced trust, more so with health-specific framing, but did not
improve confidence in reliance decision. Risk disclosure was associated with
higher decision confidence among those with stronger AI knowledge, but lower
confidence among those less preferring analytical thinking. Effects were also
task-dependent: disclosure shifted preferences for medical queries and
impairment assistance, while LLMs remained preferred for emotional companionship
regardless of exposure. These findings demonstrate that risk communication can
narrow trust gaps across AI-literacy levels, but turning disclosure into
judgment requires adapting to usage context and users' prior knowledge.
