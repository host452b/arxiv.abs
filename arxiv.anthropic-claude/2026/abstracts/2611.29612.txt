Title: Subsurface Model Programming: Frontier Large Language Models Natively Interpret and Edit Dynamic Reservoir Models
Authors: Yanchen Song, Huizhu Xiang, Zhengdong Lei, Yuezhong Wang, Xingbang Liu
ID: 2611.29612
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29612

Abstract:
Multiphase flow simulation in heterogeneous porous media supports decisions in
hydrocarbon recovery, geological carbon sequestration, and geothermal
extraction. We introduce Subsurface Model Programming, a workflow in which large
language models (LLMs) read and edit reservoir simulation decks directly without
numerical gradients or external optimization algorithms. We evaluate four
benchmarks using geometric checks for connectivity and open-source simulators
for dynamic tasks. In one text-only response, the LLM correctly classified all
six reservoir-layer well-pair labels and all three flow-path labels between open
completions. On PUNQ-S3, a single proposal modified 54 of 1,761 active cells
(3.1%) and reduced well-balanced water-cut RMSE by 71.9%. On the SPE10 sector
model, the best of five LLM proposals yielded 1.795 million standard cubic
meters of cumulative oil over ten years, 8.9% more than the best plan from 25
particle swarm optimization evaluations. On the 533,403-cell UNISIM-IV-2026
compositional model, five rounds of script-based editing reduced combined field-
and-well NRMSE from 37.98% to 11.62% (a 69.4% reduction) and field production
error by 73.9%. Replacing exhaustive stochastic search with physically auditable
causal interventions in just one to five runs provides a generalizable
foundation for autonomous reservoir development, rapid history matching, legacy
model diagnosis, and coupled multiphysics engineering.
