Title: Interpretable Orthogonal Latent Diffusion with Scale-Selective Uncertainty for Medical Image Super-Resolution
Authors: Onkar Jadhav, Tim French
ID: 2611.32135
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32135

Abstract:
Diffusion models are increasingly used for medical image reconstruction and
super-resolution, but their samples may contain plausible fine-scale
hallucinations that are difficult to identify from the reconstructed image
alone. We propose Med-PODiff, a patch-wise proper orthogonal decomposition (POD)
diffusion framework for brain MRI super-resolution that performs generative
reconstruction in compact and interpretable local coefficient spaces. Unlike
pixel-space or learned-latent diffusion models, Med-PODiff uses the POD
structure to separate low-rank anatomical variation from fine-detail residual
variation, enabling predictive uncertainty to be decomposed into total, coarse,
and fine components. Building on this decomposition, we define a pixel-wise
hallucination-risk score, $H_p$, that emphasizes regions where fine-detail
uncertainty dominates coarse structural uncertainty. On brain MRI, Med-PODiff
outperforms representative diffusion baselines in reconstruction quality while
producing well-calibrated predictive intervals, with total uncertainty detecting
high-error regions at AUROC 0.954. More importantly, the decomposed uncertainty
is scale-selective: coarse and fine uncertainty preferentially predict errors at
the corresponding scales, with $H_p$ localizing fine error at AUROC $0.906$.
Controlled injections and retrospective Cartesian $k$-space undersampling
further support fine-detail localization and degradation robustness. The
discarded POD tail is only $3.8\%$ of the retained fine-error magnitude, and
Med-PODiff improves downstream segmentation consistency while requiring
substantially less inference time than pixel-space diffusion.
