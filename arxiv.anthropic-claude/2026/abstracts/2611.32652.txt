Title: Power Side-Channel Evaluation using Multi-FPGA Systems
Authors: Yaswanth Tavva, Geng Luo, Trevor E. Carlson, Li-Shiuan Peh
ID: 2611.32652
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32652

Abstract:
Complex out-of-order processors are difficult to evaluate for power side-channel
leakage before tapeout. A single FPGA often lacks sufficient capacity, whereas
conventional CAD-based analysis is too slow to support the large numbers of
traces required by practical attacks. Multi-FPGA emulation provides greater
capacity, but existing partitioning schemes are not security-aware and thus
results in poor attack performance. This work presents Scissors, a security-
aware multi-FPGA partitioning approach for pre-silicon power side-channel
evaluation of complex out-of-order processors. Scissors uses side-channel
vulnerability factor (SVF) analysis to identify signals correlated with the
secret key. It then keeps strongly key-correlated signals confined to one FPGA
partition (measured) while moving rest of the signals to the other FPGAs
(support), improving the observability of the side-channel information. We
evaluate Scissors through RTL simulations and physical measurements on a
two-U200 FPGA testbed, using frequency-domain CPA with a Hamming-weight leakage
model. RTL ablation shows that only 68-71 high-SVF signals dominate key recovery
process. For a single-core processor (SingleBOOM), Scissors recovers the key
with 12,000 traces, compared with 49,000 for the unpartitioned design and 43,000
for TritonPart, while improving correct-key correlation by $2.83\times$. For a
quad-core processor (QuadBOOM), which exceeds the capacity of the CW340 single-
FPGA platform, Scissors reduces the required traces from 111,000 to 66,000 and
improves correlation by $1.48\times$ over TritonPart. Scissors also reduces
QuadBOOM RTL-to-bitstream generation from 61 h 39 min to 6 h 40 min, a
$9.25\times$ speedup. These results show that security-aware partitioning
improves leakage observability and attack performance while substantially
reducing implementation time for complex processors against the baseline method.
