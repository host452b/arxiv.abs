Title: AI, Bias, and Inclusion: A Two-Phase Mixed-Methods Study of Gender Bias, Awareness, and Trust in Artificial Intelligence
Authors: MD Junayed Talukdar, Khosro Salmani
ID: 2611.32869
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32869

Abstract:
Artificial Intelligence (AI) systems are widespread across fields such as
healthcare, finance, employment, and criminal justice, with a substantial impact
on the lives of individuals and society. However, it has been established that
AI systems perpetuate existing social inequalities, particularly through biases
that are not easily discernible. Such biases are embedded in the technical and
social structures of artificial intelligence systems, making them difficult to
detect. These biases challenge the concept of Equity, Diversity, and Inclusion
(EDI), which recognizes diversity as the acknowledgment of individual
differences, equity as fairness and equal access, and inclusion as the value
placed on all participants. This study argues that fairness in artificial
intelligence systems cannot be achieved by focusing solely on their technical
aspects, necessitating a holistic approach. This study employs a two-phase
mixed-methods design. Phase 1 applies computational content analysis to 360
occupational narratives generated by ChatGPT across four countries (Canada,
Germany, India, and Bangladesh) using gender-neutral prompts to examine whether
professions are predominantly associated with one gender and whether these
patterns remain consistent across regions. Phase 2 examines the extent to which
students recognize potential bias in AI, their trust in AI for consequential
decision-making, and how these perceptions relate to themes in EDI and AI ethics
scholarship. By combining output-level bias analysis with user perception data,
this study identifies the empirical foundations necessary for future EDI-AI co-
design frameworks, outlining the sociotechnical dimensions that such frameworks
must address to be effective.
