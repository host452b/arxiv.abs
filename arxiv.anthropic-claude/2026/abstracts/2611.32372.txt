Title: From the ELBO to Maximum Likelihood: Convergence of Variational Autoencoders for Nonlinear Mixed-Effects Modeling
Authors: Jan Rohleff, Johannes Schropp
ID: 2611.32372
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32372

Abstract:
Nonlinear mixed-effects (NLME) models are the central statistical models of
pharmacometrics, where they support dose selection, individualized dosing and
regulatory decisions in drug development. Statistically, NLME modeling is a
marginalized likelihood problem. Variational autoencoders (VAEs) solve such
problems with a neural network encoder that amortizes the approximation of the
intractable posterior distribution, and allow the integration of AI components
into NLME modeling. This paper provides the convergence analysis of the VAE-NLME
algorithm. First, its version for the evidence lower bound (ELBO) is proven to
converge almost surely to the set of stationary points of the ELBO, without
assuming that the encoder is trained exactly. Second, the final VAE-NLME
algorithm trains the encoder on the ELBO by gradient-based optimization and uses
it as proposal for importance-weighted sufficient statistics. It converges
almost surely to the set of stationary points of the marginal log-likelihood,
without any assumption on the training of the encoder. Thus, it combines the
fast gradient-based training of VAEs with the limit of maximum likelihood
estimation.
