Title: Multimodal View Training: Learning More Through Different Modalities
Authors: Devang Kulshreshtha, Priyanka Kaswan, Minh Pham, Chinmay Hegde, Niv Cohen
ID: 2611.30165
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30165

Abstract:
Can a language model learn more from the same information simply by encountering
it in different forms? We answer this in the affirmative by introducing
Multimodal View Training (MVT), a simple training approach that presents
identical information through text, speech, and images, without requiring new
facts or supervision. Starting from a text sample, MVT generates corresponding
audio and rendered-text image views, and trains a multimodal language model to
produce the same target output across all three views. To further bring these
benefits to text-only large language models (LLMs), we train lightweight
connector modules to pretrained audio and vision encoders, allowing the LLM to
learn from multimodal inputs while retaining its text-only architecture at
inference. Across question answering, machine unlearning, and knowledge editing,
adaptive MVT improves QA recall by +15.2 percentage points, forget quality by
+40.7 points, and editing generality by +25.2 points over text-only training.
Combining MVT with standard augmentation further improves QA and editing
generality. Our theoretical analysis illustrates how training across modalities
can shift weight updates toward shared factual content and reduce their reliance
on features specific to each view.
