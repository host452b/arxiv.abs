Title: RecurSciBench: Benchmarking Recursive Self-Improvement in Data-Driven Scientific Research
Authors: Wenxi Xu, Chuan Qin, Zihan Wang, Zixing Chen, Le Zhang, Jingbo Zhou, Hao Wang, Yuanchun Zhou, Hengshu Zhu
ID: 2611.32508
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32508

Abstract:
Large language model (LLM)-based agents are advancing the automation of data-
driven scientific research. Recent advances in recursive self-improvement (RSI)
offer the potential for these agents to continuously improve through experience,
but evaluating transfer across scientific tasks requires executable,
scientifically verifiable tasks and independent assessment of self-improvement
gains. We introduce RecurSciBench, a scalable benchmark for evaluating RSI in
data-driven scientific research. Its RecurSciPipe framework automatically
constructs scientific modeling and data analysis tasks from raw datasets, with
execution-based verification and scientific validity checks. RecurSciBench spans
four scientific domains, multiple data modalities, and varied scientific
objectives. We evaluate four RSI systems by comparing their initial and evolved
states on independent tasks after evolution on source tasks, covering within-
type, cross-type, and cross-domain transfer. Experiments show that the benefits
of self-improvement vary across agent systems and scientific domains. Positive
overall gains can coexist with regressions on individual tasks. Experience can
transfer across task types and benefit other domains even without measurable
gains in the source domain.
