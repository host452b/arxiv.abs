Title: Decision Models for Definition-Driven GitHub Actions Workflow Smell Detection
Authors: Rohit Gheyi, Anderson Uchôa, Wesley Assunção, Francisco Sousa, Baldoino Fonseca, Márcio Ribeiro
ID: 2611.31421
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31421

Abstract:
GitHub Actions workflows automate continuous integration and delivery (CI/CD),
and workflow smells are recurring configuration patterns that may weaken
security, hinder maintenance, or waste resources. Extending static analyzers to
new smells requires encoding constructs, applicability conditions, and
exceptions in detection rules. We investigate definition-driven detection, in
which models assess workflows against reusable natural-language smell
definitions, focusing on decision-oriented models that return scores for bounded
questions. We compare them with generative models and a static analyzer, and
examine their score reliability, repeatability, and robustness. We evaluate
three decision-oriented and two generative configurations on 1,710 manually
validated workflow-smell pairs covering 19 smells, 903 workflows, and 559
repositories. Effectiveness depends strongly on the decision policy: JEV 1.13
achieves 79.69% micro-F1 at threshold 0.5 and 85.43% with thresholds selected on
separate repository folds, but global thresholds selected on the benchmark lower
micro-F1 on a historical comparison set. Generative GPT-6 LUNA achieves
comparable effectiveness on the pairs it classifies but leaves 247 unresolved.
Repeatability does not ensure robustness: two decision models agree across three
identical-input runs yet change more than 3% of their predictions after label-
preserving transformations, despite nearly unchanged aggregate F1. On 46
reconstructed workflows, a static analyzer outperforms JEV 1.13 (87.8% versus
76.4% micro-F1). With a median latency of 0.675 seconds per workflow and about
US$0.22 in API charges for the benchmark, JEV 1.13 makes frequent model-assisted
checks operationally plausible, but deployment requires validated decision
policies, and effectiveness on new criteria remains to be evaluated.
