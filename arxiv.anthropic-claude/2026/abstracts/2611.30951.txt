Title: Evaluable Language-Model Treatment Policies for Sepsis Resuscitation via Offline Value-Guided Selection
Authors: Yue Pei, Shining Bo, Yingying Zhang, Qinggang Ge, Zhiling Zhao, Jian Cheng, Haogang Zhu
ID: 2611.30951
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30951

Abstract:
Offline treatment learning must balance imitation, which stays close to logged
evidence but ignores outcomes, against value maximisation, which uses outcomes
but drifts toward treatments the records cannot verify. Numerical reinforcement
learning strikes this balance during training. For large language models (LLMs),
directly using sparse retrospective outcomes for fine-tuning raises challenges
of delayed credit assignment and limited evidence for alternative treatments. We
present R-LLMC, which strikes the balance at inference for sepsis resuscitation.
A behaviour-supervised LLM, decoded into an exact distribution over 25
vasopressor-fluid actions, proposes clinician-anchored candidates; an offline
critic selects among them by estimated long-term value. The policy has a closed
form for off-policy evaluation, and its rationale is written after selection, so
explanation cannot steer treatment. In experiments on held-out MIMIC-III
patients, R-LLMC exceeded the clinician reference in doubly robust value by
+0.214 (95\% CI 0.091-0.300), with fitted Q evaluation agreeing.
