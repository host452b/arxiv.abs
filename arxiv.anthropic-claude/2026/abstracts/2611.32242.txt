Title: Can You Find the Edit When Nothing Looks Wrong? A Paired Benchmark for Text-Driven Image Manipulation Localization Beyond Semantic Abnormality
Authors: Peiyu Zhuang, Jianquan Yang, Zhuoying Cai, Yi Jiang, Yingquan Chen, Xinle Wang, Zeqin Yu, Dongdong Lin, Ruitao Xie, Kaige Li et al.
ID: 2611.32242
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32242

Abstract:
Instruction-driven image editing can alter a photograph while keeping the result
coherent with the scene. Existing benchmarks lack matched plausible and
anomalous edits of the same target, making it difficult to measure how
localization performance changes with semantic plausibility. We construct a
paired benchmark by generating semantically plausible and anomalous replacements
of the same target in each source photograph. Both edits use the same editor,
language, and generation settings. We estimate a separate mask for each output
and retain pairs with sufficient mask overlap, enabling direct comparison of
localization performance under the two semantic conditions. The release contains
47,747 bilingual triplets from 6,500 photographs, spanning four editors, three
source domains, and six intended violation types. Across nine public localizers,
editor-averaged IoU is lower on plausible edits in 26 of 27 model-dataset
comparisons. This gap persists under pixel-change controls and region-size
analyses. These findings reveal a semantic-plausibility shortcut: localization
often weakens when an edited result looks ordinary. The benchmark provides a
controlled setting for measuring this gap and developing localizers that remain
effective on plausible edits.
