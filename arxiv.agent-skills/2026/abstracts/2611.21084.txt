Title: CompCert4Lean: Porting a Verified C Compiler to Lean in Six Weeks with Parallel Coding Agents
Authors: Cong Wang, Zhiyang Chen, Fan Long, Martin Rinard
ID: 2611.21084
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21084

Abstract:
We present CompCert4Lean, a large-scale port of CompCert 3.17's verified
compiler core from Rocq to Lean, completed in approximately six weeks by one
expert directing parallel LLM agents. Our migration workflow partitions the
development along its dependencies, audits translated definitions and theorem
statements before large-scale proof reconstruction, and then reconstructs proofs
in parallel against stable interfaces. The resulting compiler covers typed
CompCert C through x86-64 assembly, discharges all 4,908 recorded proof
obligations without project-specific axioms, and produces executable Linux
binaries. The workflow enabled the expert to concentrate on organizing the work
rather than supplying technical solutions. Of 1,071 human interactions, 88.8%
concerned workflow and coordination, while only three supplied technical
direction. Optimization campaigns further demonstrate the benefit of the Lean
port for agent-driven verified development. Under matched budgets of 24 active
hours, GPT-6-astra produced a Lean candidate with 11.7% lower generated-program
runtime across 29 small-tests programs, compared with 2.3% lower for its Rocq
candidate, both relative to original CompCert 3.17 and while preserving the end-
to-end correctness theorem. These results show that parallel LLM agents can
support large-scale migration with limited human technical direction and
subsequently improve the migrated compiler while retaining its formal
guarantees.
