Title: $R^2$: Recognizing and Reducing Redundant Visual Tool Use
Authors: Yilin Zhao, Jinyu Wang, Wenming Yang
ID: 2611.27070
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27070

Abstract:
Recent studies have shown that visual tools can improve multimodal reasoning.
However, the effects of tools vary substantially across samples, and benchmark
accuracy alone does not reveal whether tool interactions are beneficial or
redundant. We present $R^2$, a framework that recognizes and reduces redundant
visual tool use in vision-language models. We first introduce a sample-level
Category Analysis of Tool Utility (CAT), which categorizes samples according to
whether a model can benefit from tools. This analysis reveals that benchmark
scores may obscure a model's tool-use ability. Across seven benchmarks, samples
benefiting from tools account for at most 12.44% of each benchmark, while the
majority gain little from tool interaction or are even harmed by it. To assess
individual calls, we propose the Visual Feedback Dependency (VFD) metric, which
compares the likelihood of the same subsequent reasoning under actual and
alternative visual feedback without an external evaluator or trajectory
regeneration. Redundancy-Aware GRPO (RA-GRPO) combines this dependency signal
with a category-conditioned base call cost to assign relative efficiency
advantages among correct trajectories, supplementing outcome-based supervision.
Across the seven benchmarks, RA-GRPO reduces the mean number of tool calls by
12.39% relative to Vanilla GRPO at comparable benchmark accuracy. Evaluated by
tool-utility category, RA-GRPO improves category macro-average accuracy over
Vanilla GRPO by 3.35 percentage points.
