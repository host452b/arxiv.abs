Title: Strategically Robust Multi-Agent Control
Authors: Boris Velasevic, Nicolas Lanzetti, Eric Mazumdar
ID: 2611.16388
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16388

Abstract:
A key source of uncertainty in multi-agent systems is the behavior of other
agents. Game-theoretic control approaches ignore this uncertainty, while robust
control approaches treat others as fully adversarial. In this paper, we
introduce strategically robust multi-agent control to populate the spectrum
between these two paradigms. Specifically, in dynamic games, we propose that
players are strategically robust and seek protection against misspecified
control policies of the other players. This formulation induces a novel
equilibrium concept, strategically robust dynamic equilibrium, that interpolates
between game-theoretic control and robust control. We study this concept across
canonical open- and closed-loop settings and show that accounting for strategic
robustness also has computational benefits over classic approaches in game-
theoretic control. Numerical experiments on vehicle platooning, inventory
management, and network control illustrate resilience to misspecification, and
also exhibit a "free-lunch" phenomenon in which strategic robustness can improve
performance or social welfare.
