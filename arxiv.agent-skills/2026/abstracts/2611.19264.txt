Title: Read Boundaries in Agent Memory: Consistency, Completeness, and Repair
Authors: Haoting Qiu, Qibai Chen
ID: 2611.19264
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19264

Abstract:
Agent-memory services assemble evidence through several reads across connections
or stores. An update between those reads can pair an assertion with source
content from another revision. We protect the complete recall, including
enrichment and materialization, and evaluate the resulting evidence and service
behavior in Hindsight and Cognee. A 600-run Cognee study compares unprotected
recall, a repeated recall, a read/write lock, and optimistic epoch validation
under default incremental updates and full rebuilds. At approximately 20
recalls/s, unprotected default updates yield 199 conflicting responses among
12,010 scheduled recalls with spaced writes and 284 among 12,010 with bursts.
The lock and epoch validation each deliver complete, compatible evidence for all
24,020 default-update recalls. With default bursts, epoch validation reduces
update p95 by 109 ms while adding 290 ms to recall p95 relative to the lock. A
separate 160-run incremental-failure study distinguishes mixed revisions from
complete new derived content whose canonical source record remains old. Both
protected policies refuse service after failure; the first default retry
restores all 80 fault runs through native full-rebuild fallback. In Hindsight, a
shared PostgreSQL snapshot removes the mismatch in ten controlled pairs, with
recall p95 rising from 19 ms to 29 ms. These results identify the read boundary
needed for coherent evidence and the publication and recovery conditions
governing service after an update fails.
