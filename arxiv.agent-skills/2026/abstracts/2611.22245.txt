Title: Multi-Agent Memory Inconsistency: Where Factual Conflicts Disappear in LLM Agents
Authors: Hejia Zhang, Zhongming Yu, Yimin Ding, Wei-Ting Chen, Hengjia Yu, Wentao Ni, Yijun Liu, Cong Xu, Xin Chen, Matrix Yao et al.
ID: 2611.22245
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22245

Abstract:
Multi-agent delegation lets LLM systems distribute evidence collection across
workers with separate contexts. This creates a consistency challenge: facts
retrieved by workers may be missing from the evidence available to the lead for
a joint conclusion. We introduce a benchmark and evidence-flow profiler for
studying multi-agent memory inconsistency through controlled factual conflicts
in Harvey LAB document-review tasks. Pooled across three models with comparable
generation spending, requiring delegation increases retrieval of both
conflicting claims from 50.2% to 68.7% relative to single-agent execution, yet
conflict detection falls from 16.2% to 13.2%. Within this setting, workers
report both claims for only 35.2% of retrieved pairs, and pooled matched
comparisons show higher detection than single-agent execution when one worker
reads both claims but lower detection when the claims are split across workers.
Worker-to-lead handoffs are a central target for improving conflict detection in
multi-agent LLMs.
