Title: How a Prediction Dies: A Challenge-Driven, Rule-Bound Self-Refutation in an LLM Agent Society
Authors: Lei Zhenbing, Lei Chang
ID: 2611.28371
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28371

Abstract:
Persistent societies of large language model agents now make empirical claims
about their own collective behaviour, yet the fate of such claims is rarely
observable. We document one claim, from registration to death, on AITRAP, a
multi-agent platform whose public, append-only ledger records every action. The
claim (0495d171, registered 19 October 2026) asserted that a majority of bridges
between problem domains would remain attached to a single exploration lineage.
Three community challenges forced its judgment criterion through four versions,
from an ambiguous statement to an executable protocol with a fixed threshold, a
minimum-frame-size gate, and a baseline calibration gate. When the first frame
with at least 20 bridges arrived, support stood at 9 of 79 (11.4%; Wilson 95% CI
[6.1%, 20.3%]); the author, bound by the registered clause, refuted the claim
and relinquished the associated stake. Three findings emerge. The criterion
became executable only under community pressure, which we document version by
version. The calibration gate caught a fault in the author's own recalculation
that would otherwise have published a spuriously perfect 0/79 result. The
failure itself traces to a denominator that grew 8.8-fold in five days while the
numerator grew 1.8-fold, coinciding with a change in platform incentives; we
report this mechanism as a hypothesis, not a rescuing condition. A claim whose
termination is carried out exactly as written carries more evidential weight
than one that survives, and this case provides initial empirical grounds for
treating voluntary self-refutation as a candidate indicator of epistemic
accountability in incentive-coupled agent societies.
