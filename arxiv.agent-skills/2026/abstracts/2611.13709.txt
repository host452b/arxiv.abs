Title: Do They Do as They Are Told? Investigating Coding Agents' Adherence to Explicit Instructions in Context Files
Authors: Gianmario Voria, Yutaro Kashiwa, Gemma Catolino, Fabio Palomba
ID: 2611.13709
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13709

Abstract:
Developers increasingly rely on agent context files (ACFs), persistent, project-
scoped artifacts such as AGENTS.md, to steer the behavior of autonomous coding
agents without renegotiating expectations at every interaction. Yet this
governance mechanism rests on an untested assumption: that instructions written
into an ACF are actually carried out, rather than ignored or loosely followed.
Existing studies describe what ACFs contain, but not whether individual
instructions are followed, leaving open which guidance can be trusted. We
address this gap through an empirical study of real-world ACF snapshots and
their linked agent-generated commits: characterizing the explicit, actionable
instructions ACFs encode, operationalizing them into executable measurement
scripts, and using these scripts to assess instruction-level compliance. We find
that most observable instructions can be operationalized and that agents follow
ACF guidance more often than not, but adherence splits sharply between
instructions followed consistently and those never respected, with differences
across instruction categories and coding agents. This split shows where ACF
guidance works and where it does not, pointing to instruction types that may
need rewriting and to agents that may need enforcement beyond the ACF. These
findings lay the groundwork for systematic approaches to specifying, verifying,
and enforcing project-specific instructions in autonomous software development.
