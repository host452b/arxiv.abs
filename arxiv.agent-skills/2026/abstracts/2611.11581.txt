Title: Same Error, Different Recovery: How Delivery Changes LLM Agent Behavior
Authors: Sunghyeun Woo, Javier Conde, Pedro Reviriego
ID: 2611.11581
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11581

Abstract:
A tool-using language model must decide whether to retry a failed call, correct
it, or stop and tell the user. The error text alone may not be enough: the same
feedback can lead to different actions depending on where it appears in the
conversation. We isolate this delivery problem by varying placement, guidance,
retained history and turn structure. Two studies with plans fixed before data
collection, across 13 open models from 3B to 70B parameters, compare the next
action and subsequent task completion, with exact deterministic reruns. A new
user turn that retains the failed exchange avoids the severe stopping failure
observed in Llama models when that exchange is removed. It is non-inferior to
history rewriting in pooled synthetic comparisons, but is not best for every
model. We implement this form as an opt-in recovery layer within a deterministic
inference engine and evaluate it on public benchmarks and a coding agent. On
ToolMaze, it raises success on explicit errors by 14.2 points with 7-8B models
and 10.5 points with 32-70B models; Loud/Quiet, 24 multi-turn tasks with visible
failures and silent corruption, scored by final-state agreement, shows no
measurable gain. A client given the exact fault kind performs better still. The
findings support delivery as a distinct factor in recovery, not an engine-
exclusive solution or a universal retry policy.
