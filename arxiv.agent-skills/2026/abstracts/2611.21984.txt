Title: From Apology to Action: Apologies, Compensation, and Cooperation in LLM Agents
Authors: Grace Ibukunoluwa Ufeoshi, Alessandro Di Stefano, Ndidi Bianca Ogbo, The Anh Han, Zhao Song
ID: 2611.21984
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21984

Abstract:
Large language models (LLMs) can make decisions that affect other agents, but
what happens after a harmful or unsuccessful interaction remains poorly
understood. We investigate whether apologies by LLM agents are associated with
subsequent cooperative behaviour, using controlled Prisoner's Dilemma
experiments with four commercial models (Claude Opus 4.8, GPT-5.5, Gemini 3.1
Pro, and Mistral Large 3). Across payoff conditions, we examine when agents
apologise, the reasons they provide, and the actions that follow. We also
examine how access to one round of interaction history affects behaviour and, in
a second experiment, whether and how agents offer compensation alongside their
apologies. Apology behaviour differed substantially across models and preceding
interaction outcomes, and verbal expressions of cooperation did not always align
with subsequent actions. Notably, Claude Opus 4.8 apologised even after mutual
cooperation, while Mistral Large 3 apologised after being exploited but
continued to defect even when its opponent cooperated. After unilateral
defection, positively compensated apologies from GPT-5.5 and Gemini 3.1 Pro were
followed by mutual cooperation, unlike those from Claude and Mistral. Our
findings suggest that apologies, compensation, and strategic behaviour are
distinct: conciliatory messages may precede continued defection, while costly
apologies may go unanswered. This has important implications for the study of
machine behaviour and human-AI interactions, as verbal signals alone may not
reliably reflect an AI system's subsequent actions.
