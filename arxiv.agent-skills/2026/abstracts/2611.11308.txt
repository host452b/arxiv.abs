Title: SkillCODER: Towards Auditing and Attribution of Copyright Infringement in LLM Agent Skills
Authors: Enhao Huang, Chunshu Xia, Yiming Li, Yuchen Yang, Bingrun Yang, Zhan Qin, Dacheng Tao, Kui Ren
ID: 2611.11308
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11308

Abstract:
Agent skills package reusable workflows and task-specific procedures into
deployable natural-language artifacts for LLM agents. Their scale and
readability make them valuable but vulnerable to unauthorized redistribution and
repackaging. Existing prompt copyright auditing methods struggle with long
skills and introduce token-level perturbations that compromise readability. We
propose SkillCODER for copyright auditing and buyer attribution in a black-box,
content-only setting. During embedding, SkillCODER parses a skill into a
section-aware intermediate representation, SkillIR, to identify structural
anchors. It distributes conditional instructions across these anchors to form an
Artifact-Gated Capsule, designed to activate under verification queries and
remain dormant otherwise. Controlled-Vocabulary Error-Correcting Codes encode
buyer-specific fingerprints through controlled lexical choices within the
capsule. During verification, the defender issues paired positive and negative
probes, extracts capsule evidence, and recovers ownership and buyer identity
through error-correcting decoding. Across three domains, six models, and three
agent frameworks, SkillCODER achieves 99.2% owner verification and 99.3% buyer
Top-1 attribution accuracy while preserving task fidelity, substantially
outperforming prompt-level baselines. Embedded signals remain robust under
paraphrase, compression, clause deletion, and reorganization. Under five
adaptive attacks, including a method-aware LLM sanitizer and multi-buyer
collusion, ownership evidence remains reliably detectable while buyer
attribution degrades gracefully.
