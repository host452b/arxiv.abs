Title: Repeating Work Can Lower Energy per Successful Goal in LLM Agents
Authors: Deepak Panigrahy, Stephen Abkin, Aakash Tyagi, David Kebo Houngninou, Rabi Mahapatra
ID: 2611.10315
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10315

Abstract:
When a temporary call failure interrupts a tool-using LLM agent, the framework
must choose how much completed work to repeat. Repeating less completed work
intuitively saves computation, but recovery mechanisms also change subsequent
execution and the chance of success. We compare recovery mechanisms by energy
per successful goal (EpG), which divides the energy of all task runs (including
failed runs) by the number that finish correctly. In controlled vLLM experiments
on one node, we measure board energy above idle for eight recovery options on
reliably solved multi-turn tasks, with selected comparisons across three model
families. For the model tested across all eight options, with the prefix cache
retained, starting over has 22 percent lower EpG than continuing after early
failures. Under the same cache condition, continuing has 24 percent lower EpG
than starting over after late failures. Among all eight recovery options,
repeating only the interrupted turn has the lowest observed EpG after early and
late tool failures on this model. Holding recovery fixed, retaining the prefix
cache reduces prompt tokens needing fresh computation by 86 percent but reduces
recovery energy by only 8 percent at contexts up to 7,333 tokens. These
measurements and a condition relating recovery energy and success show when
repeating work lowers EpG, helping framework developers choose how their agents
recover on their intended models and tasks.
