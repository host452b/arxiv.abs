Title: Multi-Agent Coordination Through Stigmergy for Canal Network Formation on Real Terrain
Authors: Jordan Kennedy, Federico Oliva, Amir Degani, Karen-Lee Bar Sinai, Nare Karapetyan
ID: 2611.32966
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32966

Abstract:
Terrain-modifying robot swarms operating in unstructured outdoor environments
must coordinate under uncertain dynamics despite unreliable communication and
incomplete global information. Stigmergy offers a decentralized alternative by
allowing agents to coordinate indirectly through persistent environmental
modifications, yet its application to large-scale terrain construction on real
landscapes remains largely unexplored. Inspired by beaver canal construction, we
present a minimal stigmergic framework for multi-agent systems in which
decentralized agents follow local wetness cues, excavate terrain, and interact
through a closed agent--environmental feedback loop. The framework couples
agents with seasonal hydrology on a real digital elevation model (DEM). Across
110 simulations on an 862m x 1167m region spanning agent count and hydrological
recession rate, the model produces emergent canal-like networks whose morphology
varies predictably with environmental constraints. An alignment-invariant
morphological evaluation against a field-annotated reference network identifies
parameter regimes that reproduce key canal characteristics, while failure cases
reveal over-canalization and fragmentation. These results demonstrate that
simple local terrain-modifying behaviors can generate plausible landscape-scale
structures through environmental feedback, providing a minimal framework for
studying decentralized terrain construction and bio-inspired swarm coordination.
