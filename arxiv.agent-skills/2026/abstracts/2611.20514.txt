Title: Beyond the Patch: Reconstructing an Incomplete-Fix Patch Bypass in LLM Agent Frameworks with Control-Gated, Benign Probes
Authors: Chaitanya Garware
ID: 2611.20514
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20514

Abstract:
Replaying a vulnerability's original proof-of-concept after patching tests
whether its reported trigger remains reproducible, but does not establish that
the underlying security invariant holds on other code paths. We present a
benign, control-gated reconstruction method and a pilot study across four LLM
agent frameworks and four invariant classes. For PraisonAI, we compare original-
trigger replay (B0) with a distinct sibling-path probe (M). We also perform five
before/after reconstructions across Langroid, Agno, and CrewAI. Each run reports
VIOLATION, NO_VIOLATION_OBSERVED, or INCONCLUSIVE, gated by a positive
authorized control. Probes use inert markers or builtins-reachability checks. At
PraisonAI 4.6.31, the original tool-loading trigger is blocked while the
tool_override path still violates the invariant. This reproduces the publicly
documented CVE-2026-44334 bypass of CVE-2026-40287; neither tested path shows a
violation at 4.6.32. For CrewAI, we observe a violation in tested release 1.11.0
and confinement in 1.11.1, without claiming the earliest fixed release. Fourteen
harness-validation scenarios produced their expected verdicts. Only one same-
version B0-versus-M divergence is demonstrated. The PraisonAI probes invoke
loader functions directly and establish boundary behavior under the supplied
setup, not a full end-to-end exploit. A non-violation verdict applies only to
the tested probe, version, and configuration and is not a proof of safety. This
pilot reconstructs a known incomplete fix; it neither introduces a new testing
technique nor estimates vulnerability prevalence.
