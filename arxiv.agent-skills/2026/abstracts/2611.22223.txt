Title: GapLeak: When Privacy-Preserving LLM-based Web Agents Become a Privacy Risk
Authors: Chaklam Cheong, Tiancong Cao, Qi Li, Zelin Zhang, Jianbing Ni, Rongxing Lu, Mohammad Zulkernine
ID: 2611.22223
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22223

Abstract:
Large Language Model (LLM)-based web agents rely on data minimization and least
privilege to protect user privacy. We show that these principles are necessary
but insufficient: they constrain authorized sources, but not higher-sensitivity
sources that remain reachable. A website can request a field unavailable from
authorized sources, creating an information gap. To complete the task, a goal-
driven agent may escalate to reachable but unauthorized sources and disclose
accumulated sensitive information. We formalize this vulnerability as GapLeak,
where an adversary exploits a legitimate web form without prompt injection or
other malicious instructions. We evaluate GapLeak on three web-agent frameworks
and six LLMs from Anthropic, Google, and OpenAI across recruitment, e-commerce,
travel, and healthcare. GapLeak induces an average unauthorized disclosure of
approximately 37%. The information gap is the primary driver: eliminating it
reduces disclosure by more than 50%. Existing instruction-level defenses based
on least privilege partially mitigate the vulnerability but often reduce task
success. We propose submission-time verification, which filters unauthorized
content before form submission, eliminating over 96% of unauthorized disclosures
while preserving task success.
