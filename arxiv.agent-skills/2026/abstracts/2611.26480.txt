Title: Held While Waiting: What LLM Agent Sessions Reserve on Serverless and Agent Runtimes
Authors: Mekhala Hithyshini Sreenivasa
ID: 2611.26480
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26480

Abstract:
LLM agents spend nearly all their time waiting for the model. On Amazon Bedrock
AgentCore, our agents waited a median of 2.7 seconds for each model reply, then
computed for an estimated 5 to 40 milliseconds. AgentCore does not charge for
CPU during the wait. But it reserves a separate environment, a virtual machine
with its own memory, for every conversation, and keeps it reserved until the
conversation is closed. So 1,000 conversations reserve 1,000 environments to do
the work of 2 to 15 processor cores. A service runs out of environments long
before it runs out of processing power. The shortage has two causes, and each
has a fix. An environment stays reserved after its conversation ends: in the
customer-service benchmark we ran, a conversation lasts 33 seconds on average,
but its environment is held for 15 minutes more unless the client closes it. And
an environment serves only one conversation, which is mostly waiting: in our
simulation, 64 conversations sharing one environment need 59 times fewer
environments, and 99 of every 100 replies are delayed by at most 40
milliseconds. The milliseconds are an estimate because they are hard to measure:
a stubbed model, a timer around the model call, and steps run back to back each
gave a plausible number that was too low. We show how to avoid all three. Of the
two fixes, closing needs only the client. Sharing needs the platform, and of the
agent runtimes we surveyed only one allows it, in part. They keep conversations
apart because an agent runs tools, and sometimes code, chosen by a model. The
open problem is to keep them apart without reserving an environment for each
while it waits.
