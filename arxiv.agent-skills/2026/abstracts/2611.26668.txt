Title: How developers split roles among them and a coding agent and what "collaboration" means
Authors: Linus Ververs, Helya Dadkhah, Lutz Prechelt
ID: 2611.26668
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26668

Abstract:
Background: Coding agents such as Claude Code become more and more competent, so
that their role in the development process can grow. Objective: How do
developers characterize the role-split between them and their agent? How would
they ideally want to collaborate with their agent? Methods: We perform a 3-step,
mixed-methods study. We apply qualitative open coding to 2025 early adopters'
blog articles; perform a quantitative survey of 2026 practitioners; develop and
apply a method for coding-agent-supported open coding of survey freetext
responses. Results: The 2025 practitioners have the agent work in supporting
roles only. Accountability was firmly human. In 2026, both are fundamentally
still true. Gradually, however, the role-split is now different, with much more
agent involvement. The developers' notion of collaboration has three facets:
Predominantly a separation into human-only, agent-only, and collaborative work
steps, with defined conditions and goals. Second, accommodating personal
preferences without engineering justification. Third, a holistic view of
collaboration covering the entire development process. Conclusions: Defining
one's role relative to the agent will need to be a recurring activity. The
different views on collaboration are likely a source of friction and conflict
for teams; future work should find out how to cope with that.
