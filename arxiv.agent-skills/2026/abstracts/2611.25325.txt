Title: Pandora's Toolbox Or Why Agent Safety Cannot Be Verified
Authors: Mario Franco, Carlos Gershenson
ID: 2611.25325
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25325

Abstract:
Present artificial intelligence agents are constructed by means of a coupling
between a fixed language model with a general computing device. We show that
this coupling makes safety undecidable. In particular, we show that given some
dangerous program, no algorithm can predict whether an agent will run such a
program that is semantically equivalent. In general, guardrails, blacklists,
whitelists and certifications can only lead to imperfect safety solutions. Under
finite constraints (bounded context, values and running time) safety is
decidable but still intractable. Specifically, the problem is
$\mathsf{PSPACE}$-complete even when the description of the agent is known.
Finally, safety is not a compositional property, i.e., agents that can be proven
safe on their own can still produce unsafe behavior when grouped together. We
also discuss relaxed notions of safety that could be used in practice.
