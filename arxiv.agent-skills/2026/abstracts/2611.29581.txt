Title: Social Interaction Breaks Replicate Independence in Controlled-Clone LLM Agents
Authors: Yandan Zheng, Haoran Luo, Anh Tuan Luu
ID: 2611.29581
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29581

Abstract:
Multi-agent LLM evaluations and social simulations create many agents from one
model and treat them as independent replicates, even after the agents interact.
This assumption remains untested with matched controls. We introduce SocCogEval,
a controlled-clone assay for Social Cognitive Transfer (SCT). Clones share the
base model, system prompt, and decoding parameters and differ only in a private
brief, and a factorial protocol separates the brief, 30 rounds of interaction,
and initialization noise. On Qwen3-122B-FP8, interaction with the briefs held
fixed raises off-topic BFI-10 response-profile drift by $\barΔ=+0.917$ and
$+0.912$ in two topically disjoint scenarios (exact $p=0.0008$ and $0.0039$).
The drift is larger than under the brief alone, persists under temperature-zero
re-probing, and stays positive without typed memory extraction in exploratory
tests. Matched solo generation reproduces part of it, and social interaction
remains highest. Across five more model variants, every scenario-averaged
interaction contrast is positive. Each interacting population is therefore one
replicate, and treating its agents as independent requires paired no-interaction
controls.
